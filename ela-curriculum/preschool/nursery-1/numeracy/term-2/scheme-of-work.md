# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 2 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Comparing**: Which group has more; Which group has fewer; Matching one to one | Children will say which of two groups has more or fewer. Children will match objects one to one to check. | Which group has more, which has fewer, and matching one to one to check. | Compare groups by matching objects one to one in two rows before counting; use the words more, fewer and the same; use real objects children care about such as sweets pictures, toy cars and fruit; avoid judging by how spread out a group looks. | Say which of two groups has more or fewer and match objects one to one to check. | Counters in two colours; Toy cars and garages; Plates and fruit; Number cards; Two baskets | Observe whether each child says which group has more or fewer and checks by matching. | Says which group has more. Matches groups one to one to check. | Social Habits: sharing fairly; Literacy: comparison words; Science: comparing amounts |
| 2 | **Counting**: One more; One less; Number lines to 10 | Children will find one more and one less than a number to 5. Children will use a number line to 10. | One more, one less, and number lines to 10. | Act out one more and one less with real objects and children themselves; use a number line to show that one more is the next number and one less is the number before; use stories such as one more child comes to the door; keep numbers to 5 at first. | Say one more and one less than a number up to 5 and use a number line to 10. | Counters; A floor number line 0 to 10; A bus made of chairs; Numeral cards; A puppet | Observe whether each child says one more and one less than numbers up to 5. | Says one more than a number to 5. Says one less than a number to 5. | Literacy: number stories; Physical Development: moving along a line; Social Habits: counting children |
| 3 | **Counting**: Saying numbers 11 to 15; Counting groups of 11 to 15; Counting in twos | Children will say numbers to 15 in order. Children will count groups of up to 15 objects. | Saying numbers 11 to 15, counting groups of 11 to 15, and counting in twos. | Use a ten frame or two rows of five to show that eleven is ten and one more; say teen numbers slowly; count out groups of 11 to 15 in tens and ones with ten frames; count in twos with pairs such as shoes and gloves. | Say numbers to 15 in order, count groups of up to 15 objects and count in twos. | Ten frames; Counters; A number line to 15; Pairs of shoes and gloves; Egg boxes | Observe whether each child says numbers to 15 and counts groups beyond 10. | Says numbers to 15 in order. Counts up to 12 objects accurately. | Literacy: number rhymes; Physical Development: hopping in twos; Science: counting natural objects |
| 4 | **Numerals**: Putting numerals 1 to 10 in order; Missing numbers; Before and after | Children will put numeral cards 1 to 10 in order. Children will find a missing number. | Putting numerals 1 to 10 in order, missing numbers, and before and after. | Use number cards that can be moved; ask "What comes before?" and "What comes after?"; hide one card and ask which is missing; link order to the number line; use songs that count forward and backward. | Put numeral cards 1 to 10 in order and find a missing number. | Large numeral cards 1 to 10; A washing line and pegs; A floor number line; Number jigsaws; A blanket to hide cards | Observe whether each child puts numerals in order and finds a missing number. | Orders numerals 1 to 10. Finds a missing number from 1 to 5. | Literacy: before and after words; Physical Development: pegging; Creative Development: number songs |
| 5 | **Consolidation**: Fingers and parts of 5; Two groups that make 5; Number bond games | Children will show 5 in different ways with fingers and objects. Children will find two groups that make 5. | Fingers and parts of 5, two groups that make 5, and number bond games. | Use fingers, two-colour counters and five frames to show that five can be split into two parts; always say the whole and the parts; accept all finger patterns; keep language simple: "two and three make five". | Show 5 in different ways and find two groups that make 5. | Two-colour counters; Five frames; Bead strings; Cups for hiding counters; Number cards | Observe whether each child shows 5 in two different ways and says how many more to make 5. | Shows 5 in two different ways. Says how many more to make 5 with support. | Literacy: number sentences in words; Physical Development: finger movement; Social Habits: sharing five sweets |
| 6 | **Adding**: Putting two groups together; Counting all; Joining stories | Children will join two small groups and count all. Children will tell a simple joining story. | Putting two groups together, counting all, and joining stories. | Use real objects and stories first; model "two and three make five" in words; let children push two groups together and count all; do not introduce the plus sign until children are secure with the idea; keep totals to 5 at first and to 10 for confident children. | Join two small groups and count all, and tell a simple joining story. | Counters; Toy animals and a farm; Two baskets; Fruit pictures; A puppet | Observe whether each child joins two groups and counts all correctly to 5. | Joins two groups and counts all to 5. Tells a joining story with objects. | Literacy: story words; Social Habits: working with a partner; Science: counting animals and plants |
| 7 | **Subtracting**: Taking some away; How many are left; Taking away stories | Children will take objects away from a group and say how many are left. Children will tell a simple taking away story. | Taking some away, how many are left, and taking away stories. | Use objects that can really be removed, such as sweets pictures, counters and children leaving a line; say "five take away two leaves three"; use stories children enjoy; start with totals to 5 and take one or two away. | Take objects away from a group of up to 5 and say how many are left. | Counters; Toy ducks; A pond made from a blue cloth; A bus of chairs; Number cards | Observe whether each child takes away from a group and says how many are left. | Takes away from a group of up to 5. Says how many are left. | Literacy: story language; Social Habits: sharing; Science: counting animals leaving a pond |
| 8 | **Shape**: Rectangles; Ovals; Shape sorting | Children will name rectangle and oval. Children will sort shapes by name. | Rectangles, ovals, and shape sorting. | Compare a rectangle with a square (two long and two short sides) and an oval with a circle (an egg shape); use real objects such as books, doors, eggs and mirrors; trace shapes with fingers; sort and name all five shapes. | Name rectangle and oval and sort shapes by name. | Card shapes: circle, square, triangle, rectangle, oval; Books, doors and plates; Eggs (pictures); A feely bag; Sorting hoops | Observe whether each child names rectangle and oval and sorts five shapes by name. | Names rectangle and oval. Sorts five shapes by name. | Creative Development: shape pictures; Science: shapes in nature; Literacy: shape words |
| 9 | **Patterns**: AB patterns; AAB and ABB patterns; Making my own pattern | Children will continue AB and AAB patterns. Children will make their own pattern. | AB patterns, AAB and ABB patterns, and making my own pattern. | Start with body patterns (clap, stamp) and sound patterns before objects; say the pattern aloud as you build it; use colour, shape and size; ask "What comes next?"; let children make their own and explain them. | Continue AB and AAB patterns and make their own pattern. | Coloured cubes and bottle tops; Beads and string; Shapes; A drum; Leaves and stones | Observe whether each child continues an AB pattern and makes a pattern of their own. | Continues an AB pattern. Makes an AB or AAB pattern. | Creative Development: beads and printing; Literacy: rhythm and rhyme; Science: patterns in nature |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising comparing and counting; Revising joining and taking away; Revising shapes and patterns | Children will show Term 2 learning through play stations. Teachers will gather evidence for every child’s Term 2 record. | Revising comparing and counting, joining and taking away, and shapes and patterns, with end-of-term records. | Keep every activity familiar and joyful; observe quietly during play; record how far each child counts and how well they join and take away; share records with parents in plain language with a strength, a next step and a home idea. | Show learning from the term through play stations and a celebration. | Counters; Number lines; Toy animals; Shapes; Beads and cubes; Stickers; Class record sheets | Observe and record each child’s Term 2 numeracy learning. | Completes familiar tasks with growing independence. Each child has an observation record for Term 2. | Literacy: songs and rhymes; Social Habits: celebration; Creative Development: patterns |
