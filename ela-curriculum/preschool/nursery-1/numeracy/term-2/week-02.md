# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 2, Week 2: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** One more; One less; Number lines to 10

## Learning objectives
- Children will find one more and one less than a number to 5.
- Children will use a number line to 10.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, one less, add one, take one, number line, next

**Content:** One more, one less, and number lines to 10.

**Teaching activities:** Act out one more and one less with real objects and children themselves; use a number line to show that one more is the next number and one less is the number before; use stories such as one more child comes to the door; keep numbers to 5 at first.

**Learner activities:** Say one more and one less than a number up to 5 and use a number line to 10.

**Resources:** Counters; A floor number line 0 to 10; A bus made of chairs; Numeral cards; A puppet

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says one more and one less than numbers up to 5.

## Expected outcomes
- Says one more than a number to 5.
- Says one less than a number to 5.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: number stories; Physical Development: moving along a line; Social Habits: counting children

## Day 1 (Monday): One more

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** One more

### Learning objectives
- Children will find one more than a number up to five.

### Success criteria
- Adds one more object
- Says the next number

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say which group has more or fewer and match one to one.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, one less, add one, take one, number line, next

**Instructional materials:** Counters; Number cards 1 to 6; A puppet

### Starter
Say "Three children are in the line. One more child joins. How many now?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put three counters down and add one more. Children: Children count the new group.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Three and one more is four." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

Step 3. Teacher: Try different starting numbers. Children: Child adds one and says the number.

Step 4. Teacher: Act out with children in a line. Children: Children join the line one at a time.

### Practical activity: One more game
1. Take a group of counters.
2. Add one more.
3. Say the new number.

**Safety:** Use large counters and supervise closely. Keep the line of children safe and orderly.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use groups of one to three and count each time.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say one more than seven.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is one more than three?
  **Expected response:** Four.
- **Ask:** What is one more than five?
  **Expected response:** Six.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds one more than a number
- Says the next number

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say one more.

### Home connection
Add one more spoon to the table and say the number.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find one more than a number up to five" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): The number line

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Number lines to 10

### Learning objectives
- Children will use a number line to find one more and one less.

### Success criteria
- Moves forward one for one more
- Moves back one for one less

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say which group has more or fewer and match one to one.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, one less, add one, take one, number line, next

**Instructional materials:** A floor number line 0 to 10; A beanbag; Numeral cards

### Starter
Show a number line on the floor and say "We can walk forward for more and back for less."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Stand on three and step forward to four. Children: Children watch and say four.

Step 2. Teacher: Step back to two and say "One less." Children: Children say one less.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child stand on a number and step. Children: Child steps forward or back.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Where are you now?" Children: Child says the number.

### Practical activity: Walk the number line
1. Stand on a number.
2. Step forward for one more.
3. Step back for one less.

**Safety:** Keep the number line flat and secured so children do not trip. Move one at a time.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk the line holding the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to take two steps forward and say the number.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is one more than six?
  **Expected response:** Seven.
- **Ask:** What is one less than six?
  **Expected response:** Five.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses a number line for one more and less
- Says the number after a step

### Conclusion
Walk to ten and back to zero together.

### Home connection
Draw a number line with chalk and step on it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use a number line to find one more and one less" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): More and less stories

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** One more

### Learning objectives
- Children will solve small stories about one more and one less.

### Success criteria
- Solves a one more or one less story
- Tells their own story

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say which group has more or fewer and match one to one.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, one less, add one, take one, number line, next

**Instructional materials:** Toy animals; A farm picture; Counters

### Starter
Tell a story: "Three ducks are in the pond. One more duck comes. How many ducks?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Act out the story with toy ducks. Children: Children count the ducks.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell a one less story: "One duck goes away." Children: Children count what is left.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children tell their own story. Children: Child tells a story with toys.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How many now?" Children: Child says the number.

### Practical activity: Duck stories
1. Put toy ducks in a pond.
2. Add or take one.
3. Say how many now.

**Safety:** Use large toy animals only and supervise play. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Tell the story with one to three toys.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a story with more than five.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many ducks now?
  **Expected response:** The number now.
- **Ask:** Did we add or take away?
  **Expected response:** Add or take away.

### Expected outcomes
- Solves a one more or one less story
- Tells a story with toys

### Conclusion
Put the animals away and say the numbers.

### Home connection
Tell a number story with toys at bedtime.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 one more and one less check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** One less

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child finds one more and one less.

### Success criteria
- Says one more than a number to 5
- Says one less than a number to 5

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say which group has more or fewer and match one to one.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, one less, add one, take one, number line, next

**Instructional materials:** Counters; Number line; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the number song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: one more, one less and number line. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is one more?" and watch. Children: Child answers with a number.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: One more and less stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep the floor line flat. Use large counters.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use numbers to three.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to say one more and one less than ten.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says one more; says one less; uses a number line; solves a story.

- **Ask:** What is one more than four?
  **Expected response:** Five.
- **Ask:** What is one less than four?
  **Expected response:** Three.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Finds one more and one less with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep practising one more and one less at home.
