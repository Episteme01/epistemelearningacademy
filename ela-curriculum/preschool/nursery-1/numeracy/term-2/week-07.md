# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 2, Week 7: Subtracting

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Taking some away; How many are left; Taking away stories

## Learning objectives
- Children will take objects away from a group and say how many are left.
- Children will tell a simple taking away story.

**Key vocabulary:** take away, left, fewer, gone, remove, minus

**Content:** Taking some away, how many are left, and taking away stories.

**Teaching activities:** Use objects that can really be removed, such as sweets pictures, counters and children leaving a line; say "five take away two leaves three"; use stories children enjoy; start with totals to 5 and take one or two away.

**Learner activities:** Take objects away from a group of up to 5 and say how many are left.

**Resources:** Counters; Toy ducks; A pond made from a blue cloth; A bus of chairs; Number cards

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child takes away from a group and says how many are left.

## Expected outcomes
- Takes away from a group of up to 5.
- Says how many are left.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: story language; Social Habits: sharing; Science: counting animals leaving a pond

## Day 1 (Monday): Taking some away

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Taking some away

### Learning objectives
- Children will take some objects away from a group.

### Success criteria
- Takes objects away
- Says gone

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join groups and count all.

**Key vocabulary:** take away, left, fewer, gone, remove, minus

**Instructional materials:** Five counters; A plate; A cup

### Starter
Show five counters and say "I will take some away. They are gone."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put five counters on a plate and take two away. Children: Children watch what is gone.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Five take away two." Children: Children repeat the words.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child take away from their own group. Children: Child takes away counters.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How many did you take away?" Children: Child says the number.

### Practical activity: Take away
1. Count five counters.
2. Take some away.
3. Say how many you took.

**Safety:** Use large counters and supervise closely. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Start with three counters and take one away.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to start with eight and take three away.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many did you take away?
  **Expected response:** The number taken.
- **Ask:** What do we say?
  **Expected response:** Take away.

### Expected outcomes
- Takes objects away from a group
- Says take away

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say take away.

### Home connection
Take away spoons from a group and say how many.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "take some objects away from a group" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): How many are left?

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** How many are left

### Learning objectives
- Children will say how many are left after some are taken away.

### Success criteria
- Counts what is left
- Says left

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join groups and count all.

**Key vocabulary:** take away, left, fewer, gone, remove, minus

**Instructional materials:** Counters; Toy ducks; A blue cloth pond

### Starter
Put five ducks in the pond, take two out and say "How many ducks are left?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Take two ducks out of a group of five. Children: Children count the ducks left.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Five take away two leaves three." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

Step 3. Teacher: Try different starting numbers. Children: Child counts what is left.

Step 4. Teacher: Check by adding back. Children: Child counts all again.

### Practical activity: Ducks in the pond
1. Put ducks in the pond.
2. Take some out.
3. Count how many are left.

**Safety:** Use large toy ducks and supervise closely. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Start with three ducks and take one away.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find two take-aways that leave two.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many are left?
  **Expected response:** The number left.
- **Ask:** How do you check?
  **Expected response:** Count again.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts what is left
- Says left after taking away

### Conclusion
Put the ducks away and say left.

### Home connection
Take away some objects and count what is left.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Bus stories

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Taking away stories

### Learning objectives
- Children will act out taking away stories on a pretend bus.

### Success criteria
- Acts out a taking away story
- Says how many are left

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join groups and count all.

**Key vocabulary:** take away, left, fewer, gone, remove, minus

**Instructional materials:** Chairs as a bus; A puppet driver; Counters

### Starter
Say "Five children are on the bus. Two get off at the market. How many are left?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Act out the story with children or toys on chairs. Children: Children watch and count.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "How many are left on the bus?" Children: Child says the number.

Step 3. Teacher: Tell new stories and let children act them out. Children: Child acts the story.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child be the driver and tell a story. Children: Child tells a story.

### Practical activity: Bus stories
1. Sit on the bus.
2. Some children get off.
3. Count who is left.

**Safety:** Keep children seated and move slowly. Supervise the bus carefully and keep chairs steady.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Act the story with three toys on the bus.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a story with eight children.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many are left?
  **Expected response:** The number left.
- **Ask:** Who got off?
  **Expected response:** The children who left.

### Expected outcomes
- Acts out a taking away story
- Says how many are left

### Conclusion
Put the chairs away and say left.

### Home connection
Tell a bus story at bedtime.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Eating the sweets

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Taking away stories

### Learning objectives
- Children will solve a taking away story about picture sweets.

### Success criteria
- Solves a taking away story
- Tells their own story

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join groups and count all.

**Key vocabulary:** take away, left, fewer, gone, remove, minus

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards of fruits; A plate; Counters

### Starter
Tell a story: "There are four oranges. We eat one. How many are left?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Act out the story with picture oranges. Children: Children count what is left.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell another story with different fruit. Children: Children solve it.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child tell their own story. Children: Child tells a story.

Step 4. Teacher: Draw the story with pictures. Children: Child draws the story.

### Practical activity: Fruit stories
1. Put pictures on a plate.
2. Take some away.
3. Say how many are left.

**Safety:** Use only picture cards or toy fruit. If real fruit is used, check allergies and supervise eating.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two or three picture fruits.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to write a number sentence in words.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many oranges are left?
  **Expected response:** The number left.
- **Ask:** What happened?
  **Expected response:** We ate some.

### Expected outcomes
- Solves a taking away story
- Tells their own story

### Conclusion
Put the pictures away and say well done.

### Home connection

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 taking away check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** How many are left

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child takes away and says how many are left.

### Success criteria
- Takes away from a group to 5
- Says how many are left

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join groups and count all.

**Key vocabulary:** take away, left, fewer, gone, remove, minus

**Instructional materials:** Counters; Toy ducks; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the counting song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: counters, ducks and bus stories. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How many are left?" Children: Child takes away and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Take away stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use large objects. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use groups of three.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to take away from ten.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: takes away from a group; says how many are left; acts a taking away story; checks by counting.

- **Ask:** What is five take away two?
  **Expected response:** Three.
- **Ask:** How many are left?
  **Expected response:** The number left.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Takes away with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep taking away at home.
