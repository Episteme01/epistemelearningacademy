# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 2, Week 3: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Saying numbers 11 to 15; Counting groups of 11 to 15; Counting in twos

## Learning objectives
- Children will say numbers to 15 in order.
- Children will count groups of up to 15 objects.

**Key vocabulary:** eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, twos

**Content:** Saying numbers 11 to 15, counting groups of 11 to 15, and counting in twos.

**Teaching activities:** Use a ten frame or two rows of five to show that eleven is ten and one more; say teen numbers slowly; count out groups of 11 to 15 in tens and ones with ten frames; count in twos with pairs such as shoes and gloves.

**Learner activities:** Say numbers to 15 in order, count groups of up to 15 objects and count in twos.

**Resources:** Ten frames; Counters; A number line to 15; Pairs of shoes and gloves; Egg boxes

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says numbers to 15 and counts groups beyond 10.

## Expected outcomes
- Says numbers to 15 in order.
- Counts up to 12 objects accurately.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: number rhymes; Physical Development: hopping in twos; Science: counting natural objects

## Day 1 (Monday): Saying 11 to 15

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Saying numbers 11 to 15

### Learning objectives
- Children will say the numbers 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 in order.

### Success criteria
- Says numbers 11 to 15 in order
- Says what comes after ten

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 10 and say one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, twos

**Instructional materials:** A number line to 15; Numeral cards 11 to 15; A drum

### Starter
Walk along a number line saying "nine, ten, eleven, twelve."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say the new numbers slowly with the drum beat. Children: Children say them with the beat.

Step 2. Teacher: Show numeral cards 11 to 15 and say each name. Children: Children repeat each name.

Step 3. Teacher: Walk along the number line from 10 to 15. Children: Children walk and count.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What comes after twelve?" Children: Child says thirteen.

### Practical activity: Walk to fifteen
1. Stand at ten.
2. Walk along the line.
3. Say each number.

**Safety:** Keep the number line flat and secured. Walk slowly and one at a time.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count from nine to thirteen only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count to twenty.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What comes after ten?
  **Expected response:** Eleven.
- **Ask:** What comes before fifteen?
  **Expected response:** Fourteen.

### Expected outcomes
- Says numbers 11 to 15 in order
- Says what comes next

### Conclusion
Count from one to fifteen together.

### Home connection
Count up to fifteen steps with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say the numbers 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15 in order" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Ten and some more

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Counting groups of 11 to 15

### Learning objectives
- Children will show numbers 11 to 15 as ten and some more on a ten frame.

### Success criteria
- Fills a ten frame
- Counts the extra counters

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 10 and say one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, twos

**Instructional materials:** Ten frames; Counters; Numeral cards 11 to 15

### Starter
Show a full ten frame and say "This is ten. Eleven is ten and one more."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Fill a ten frame and put one counter beside it. Children: Children count eleven.

Step 2. Teacher: Add one more and say twelve. Children: Children count twelve.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children make 11 to 15 on a ten frame and extras. Children: Child fills and adds counters.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How many in all?" Children: Child says the number.

### Practical activity: Ten frame numbers
1. Fill a ten frame.
2. Add some more counters.
3. Say how many in all.

**Safety:** Use large flat counters and supervise placing. Keep ten frames on a mat.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Fill the ten frame together and add up to three.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a number beyond fifteen.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many counters in all?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.
- **Ask:** How many in the ten frame?
  **Expected response:** Ten.

### Expected outcomes
- Shows numbers 11 to 15 on a ten frame
- Counts ten and more

### Conclusion
Put the frames away and say the numbers.

### Home connection
Count out ten and then a few more objects.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show numbers 11 to 15 as ten and some more on a ten frame" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Counting groups to fifteen

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Counting groups of 11 to 15

### Learning objectives
- Children will count groups of 11 to 15 objects.

### Success criteria
- Counts up to 15 objects accurately
- Moves objects as counted

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 10 and say one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, twos

**Instructional materials:** Baskets of objects; Two trays; Number cards 11 to 15

### Starter
Show a basket and say "How many are in this basket? Let us count carefully."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count a group of twelve moving each into a tray. Children: Children count aloud.

Step 2. Teacher: Say how many and show the numeral. Children: Children say twelve.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each pair a basket to count. Children: Pairs count and agree.

Step 4. Teacher: Match baskets to numeral cards. Children: Child matches a card.

### Practical activity: Count the basket
1. Take a basket.
2. Move each object as you count.
3. Put the matching card.

**Safety:** Use large safe objects only and supervise closely. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a basket of eleven or twelve and count in two steps.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count a group of twenty.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many are there?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.
- **Ask:** How did you not lose count?
  **Expected response:** Move each one.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts groups to fifteen
- Moves objects as counted

### Conclusion
Put the baskets away and say the numbers.

### Home connection
Count up to fifteen objects at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count groups of 11 to 15 objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Counting in twos

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Counting in twos

### Learning objectives
- Children will count pairs of objects in twos.

### Success criteria
- Counts pairs in twos
- Says two, four, six

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 10 and say one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, twos

**Instructional materials:** Pairs of shoes; Pairs of gloves; Egg boxes

### Starter
Show a pair of shoes and say "One pair is two shoes. Two pairs are four shoes."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put shoes in pairs and count in twos. Children: Children count two, four, six.

Step 2. Teacher: Use gloves or cups to count in twos. Children: Children count with the teacher.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children make pairs and count them. Children: Child makes pairs and counts.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Two, four, six, eight, ten." Children: Children chant in twos.

### Practical activity: Count the pairs
1. Put things in pairs.
2. Touch each pair.
3. Count in twos.

**Safety:** Use clean shoes and wash hands after. Keep walkways clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count two pairs first and say the numbers together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count to twenty in twos.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many shoes in two pairs?
  **Expected response:** Four.
- **Ask:** What comes after six in twos?
  **Expected response:** Eight.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts pairs in twos
- Says two, four, six

### Conclusion
Put the pairs away and count in twos.

### Home connection
Count socks and shoes in twos at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 counting beyond ten check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Saying numbers 11 to 15

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child counts beyond ten.

### Success criteria
- Says numbers to 15
- Counts groups beyond 10

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 10 and say one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, twos

**Instructional materials:** Ten frames; Counters; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the counting song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: number line, ten frame and counting baskets. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How many?" and watch. Children: Child counts and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and highest number counted. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Beyond ten stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use large counters. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and count to twelve.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to count to thirty.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says numbers to 15; counts groups beyond 10; uses a ten frame; counts in twos.

- **Ask:** What comes after twelve?
  **Expected response:** Thirteen.
- **Ask:** Count these.
  **Expected response:** The number counted.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Counts beyond ten with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep counting beyond ten at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child counts beyond ten" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
