# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 2, Week 8: Shape

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Rectangles; Ovals; Shape sorting

## Learning objectives
- Children will name rectangle and oval.
- Children will sort shapes by name.

**Key vocabulary:** rectangle, oval, long, round, side, corner

**Content:** Rectangles, ovals, and shape sorting.

**Teaching activities:** Compare a rectangle with a square (two long and two short sides) and an oval with a circle (an egg shape); use real objects such as books, doors, eggs and mirrors; trace shapes with fingers; sort and name all five shapes.

**Learner activities:** Name rectangle and oval and sort shapes by name.

**Resources:** Card shapes: circle, square, triangle, rectangle, oval; Books, doors and plates; Eggs (pictures); A feely bag; Sorting hoops

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names rectangle and oval and sorts five shapes by name.

## Expected outcomes
- Names rectangle and oval.
- Sorts five shapes by name.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Creative Development: shape pictures; Science: shapes in nature; Literacy: shape words

## Day 1 (Monday): Rectangles

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Rectangles

### Learning objectives
- Children will name a rectangle and say it has two long and two short sides.

### Success criteria
- Names a rectangle
- Counts four sides and four corners

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name circle, square and triangle and count and join groups.

**Key vocabulary:** rectangle, oval, long, round, side, corner

**Instructional materials:** Card rectangles; A book; A door picture; A square card

### Starter
Hold up a book and say "This book is a rectangle. It has two long sides and two short sides."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a rectangle and trace its sides. Children: Children trace the shape.

Step 2. Teacher: Count four sides and four corners. Children: Children count with the teacher.

Step 3. Teacher: Compare a rectangle with a square. Children: Child points to the long sides.

Step 4. Teacher: Find rectangles in the room. Children: Child finds a rectangle.

### Practical activity: Find rectangles
1. Look at the shape.
2. Trace the sides.
3. Find one in the room.

**Safety:** Use smooth card shapes and supervise the walk. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Compare a rectangle and a square side by side.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a rectangle with straws.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is the book?
  **Expected response:** A rectangle.
- **Ask:** How many long sides?
  **Expected response:** Two.

### Expected outcomes
- Names a rectangle
- Says it has four sides

### Conclusion
Put the shapes away and say rectangle.

### Home connection
Find rectangles at home such as doors and books.

- Which children could show "name a rectangle and say it has two long and two short sides" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Ovals

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Ovals

### Learning objectives

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name circle, square and triangle and count and join groups.

**Key vocabulary:** rectangle, oval, long, round, side, corner

**Instructional materials:** Card ovals; A mirror; Egg pictures; A circle card

### Starter
Show a picture of an egg and say "An egg is an oval. It is like a stretched circle."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show an oval and trace its edge. Children: Children trace the shape.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "An oval has no corners." Children: Children touch the edge.

Step 3. Teacher: Compare an oval with a circle. Children: Child says which is longer.

Step 4. Teacher: Find ovals in pictures. Children: Child finds an oval.

### Practical activity: Find ovals
1. Look at the shape.
2. Trace the edge.
3. Find an oval in a picture.

**Safety:** Use smooth card shapes and an unbreakable mirror. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation

- **Extension:** Ask the child to draw an oval in the sand.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is an egg?
  **Expected response:** An oval.
- **Ask:** Has an oval got corners?

### Expected outcomes

### Conclusion
Put the shapes away and say oval.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Sorting five shapes

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Shape sorting

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort shapes into groups by name.

### Success criteria
- Sorts shapes by name
- Names each shape

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name circle, square and triangle and count and join groups.

**Key vocabulary:** rectangle, oval, long, round, side, corner

**Instructional materials:** Card shapes in five kinds; Sorting hoops; A feely bag

### Starter
Show a mixed pile of shapes and say "Let us sort them into groups."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sort a few shapes and name each group. Children: Children say the shape names.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child take a shape from the bag and name it. Children: Child names the shape.

Step 3. Teacher: Put each shape in the correct hoop. Children: Child sorts the shape.

Step 4. Teacher: Count each group. Children: Children count the shapes.

### Practical activity: Sort the shapes
1. Take a shape.
2. Name the shape.
3. Put it in the right hoop.

**Safety:** Use smooth shapes with no sharp edges and keep them out of mouths. Supervise sorting.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sort three kinds of shapes.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sort the shapes by size as well as kind.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is this?
  **Expected response:** The shape name.
- **Ask:** Which hoop has most?
  **Expected response:** The hoop with most.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts shapes by name
- Names five shapes

### Conclusion
Put the shapes away and say their names.

### Home connection
Sort shapes cut from paper at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort shapes into groups by name" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Shape train

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Shape sorting

### Learning objectives
- Children will make a train from shapes and describe it.

### Success criteria
- Makes a train from shapes
- Names the shapes used

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name circle, square and triangle and count and join groups.

**Key vocabulary:** rectangle, oval, long, round, side, corner

**Instructional materials:** Rectangles, circles and triangles; Glue sticks; Paper; A sample picture

### Starter
Show a train made of rectangles with circle wheels and say "Today we make a shape train."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the sample train and name the shapes. Children: Children name the shapes.

Step 2. Teacher: Help children choose shapes for the train. Children: Child chooses shapes.

Step 3. Teacher: Stick the shapes on paper. Children: Child sticks the shapes.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Which shapes did you use?" Children: Child names the shapes.

### Practical activity: Shape train
1. Choose shapes.
2. Arrange them as a train.
3. Stick them down.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic glue sticks and supervise so that no glue goes in mouths. Wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Arrange the shapes together with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count the wheels and windows.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which shapes did you use?
  **Expected response:** Rectangles and circles.
- **Ask:** How many wheels?
  **Expected response:** The number of wheels.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a train from shapes
- Names the shapes used

### Conclusion
Hang the trains and say well done.

### Home connection
Make a shape picture with paper at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make a train from shapes and describe it" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 shapes check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Shape sorting

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names and sorts five shapes.

### Success criteria
- Names rectangle and oval
- Sorts five shapes

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name circle, square and triangle and count and join groups.

**Key vocabulary:** rectangle, oval, long, round, side, corner

**Instructional materials:** Shapes; Hoops; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the shape song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: feely bag, sorting and shape train. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What shape is this?" Children: Child names the shape.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Shape stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use smooth shapes only. Keep glue and small items away from mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use three shapes.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to describe the sides and corners.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names rectangle; names oval; sorts five shapes; describes sides and corners.

- **Ask:** What shape is this?
  **Expected response:** The shape name.
- **Ask:** How many sides has a rectangle?
  **Expected response:** Four.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Names and sorts shapes with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep spotting shapes at home.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
