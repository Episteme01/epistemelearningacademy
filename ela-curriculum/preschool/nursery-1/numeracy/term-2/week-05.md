# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 2, Week 5: Consolidation

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W05 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Fingers and parts of 5; Two groups that make 5; Number bond games

## Learning objectives
- Children will show 5 in different ways with fingers and objects.
- Children will find two groups that make 5.

**Key vocabulary:** five, parts, whole, make, fingers, together

**Content:** Fingers and parts of 5, two groups that make 5, and number bond games.

**Teaching activities:** Use fingers, two-colour counters and five frames to show that five can be split into two parts; always say the whole and the parts; accept all finger patterns; keep language simple: "two and three make five".

**Learner activities:** Show 5 in different ways and find two groups that make 5.

**Resources:** Two-colour counters; Five frames; Bead strings; Cups for hiding counters; Number cards

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child shows 5 in two different ways and says how many more to make 5.

## Expected outcomes
- Shows 5 in two different ways.
- Says how many more to make 5 with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: number sentences in words; Physical Development: finger movement; Social Habits: sharing five sweets

## Day 1 (Monday): Five fingers

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Fingers and parts of 5

### Learning objectives
- Children will show five in different ways with their fingers.

### Success criteria
- Shows five with fingers in two ways
- Says the two parts

**Previous knowledge:** Children can order numbers and find one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** five, parts, whole, make, fingers, together

**Instructional materials:** Number cards; A puppet; A rhyme about five fingers

### Starter
Hold up one hand and say "Five fingers. Can you show me three on this hand and two on that?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show five fingers as 5 and 0, then 3 and 2. Children: Children copy the finger patterns.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Three and two make five." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children find other ways to show five. Children: Child shows five another way.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How many fingers are down?" Children: Child says how many.

### Practical activity: Finger partners
1. Show five fingers.
2. Fold some down.
3. Say the two parts.

**Safety:** Keep the game gentle and seated. Accept every correct finger pattern.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Show and count the fingers together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to show five in four different ways.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many fingers are up?
  **Expected response:** The number up.
- **Ask:** How many are down?
  **Expected response:** The number down.

### Expected outcomes
- Shows five in different ways
- Says the two parts of five

### Conclusion
Wave five fingers goodbye.

### Home connection
Show five fingers in different ways at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show five in different ways with their fingers" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Two colour counters

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Two groups that make 5

### Learning objectives
- Children will split five counters into two groups.

### Success criteria
- Makes five with two colours
- Says the two parts

**Previous knowledge:** Children can order numbers and find one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** five, parts, whole, make, fingers, together

**Instructional materials:** Two-colour counters; A five frame; Number cards

### Starter
Show five red-and-yellow counters and say "Let us see how many red and how many yellow."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Shake five counters and drop them on the mat. Children: Children count red and yellow.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Two red and three yellow make five." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child shake and say the parts. Children: Child shakes and says the parts.

Step 4. Teacher: Record the parts with counters. Children: Child puts counters in a five frame.

### Practical activity: Shake and spill
1. Shake five counters.
2. Count red and yellow.
3. Say the two parts.

**Safety:** Use large two-colour counters and drop them over a tray. Keep floors clear of loose counters.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use counters of three and four with clear colours.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find all the ways to make five.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many red?
  **Expected response:** The number red.
- **Ask:** How many in all?
  **Expected response:** Five.

### Expected outcomes
- Splits five into two groups
- Says the two parts

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say five.

### Home connection
Split five objects into two groups.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 3 (Wednesday): How many more to make five?

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Number bond games

### Learning objectives
- Children will find how many more are needed to make five.

### Success criteria
- Says how many more make five
- Checks by counting

**Previous knowledge:** Children can order numbers and find one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** five, parts, whole, make, fingers, together

**Instructional materials:** Counters; A five frame; Cups

### Starter
Show three counters in a five frame and say "How many more do we need to make five?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show counters in a five frame and ask how many more. Children: Children say the number.

Step 2. Teacher: Check by adding counters. Children: Children count to five.

Step 3. Teacher: Try other starting numbers. Children: Child says how many more.

Step 4. Teacher: Play the game with partners. Children: Pairs ask each other.

### Practical activity: Fill the five frame
1. Put counters in the frame.
2. Say how many more.
3. Check by counting.

**Safety:** Use large counters and supervise closely. Keep frames on the mat.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use counters of three or four in the frame.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to try making ten.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many more make five?
  **Expected response:** The number needed.
- **Ask:** How do you check?
  **Expected response:** Count to five.

### Expected outcomes
- Says how many more to make five
- Checks by counting

### Conclusion
Put the frames away and say five.

### Home connection
Ask how many more to make five at home.

## Day 4 (Thursday): Hidden counters

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Number bond games

### Learning objectives
- Children will play a hiding game that finds the hidden part of five.

### Success criteria
- Finds how many are hidden
- Says the two parts

**Previous knowledge:** Children can order numbers and find one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** five, parts, whole, make, fingers, together

**Instructional materials:** Five counters; A cup; A mat

### Starter
Show five counters and say "I will hide some. How many are hidden?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show five counters then hide some under a cup. Children: Children count what is left.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "How many are hiding?" Children: Child says the number.

Step 3. Teacher: Lift the cup to check. Children: Children cheer.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children hide counters for a friend. Children: Child hides some counters.

### Practical activity: Hide and find
1. Count five counters.
2. Hide some under a cup.
3. Say how many are hidden.

**Safety:** Use large counters and keep the game seated and gentle. Supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use three counters instead of five.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to play with six or seven counters.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many are hiding?
  **Expected response:** The number hidden.
- **Ask:** How many can you see?
  **Expected response:** The number seen.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds how many are hidden
- Says the two parts of five

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say five.

### Home connection
Play a hiding game with five objects.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 making five check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T2-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Number bond games

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child makes and splits five.

### Success criteria
- Shows five in two ways
- Says how many more to make five

**Previous knowledge:** Children can order numbers and find one more and one less.

**Key vocabulary:** five, parts, whole, make, fingers, together

**Instructional materials:** Counters; Five frames; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the five song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: fingers, counters and hidden counters. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How many more make five?" Children: Child answers with a number.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Making five stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use large counters. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use numbers to four.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to make six in different ways.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: shows five in different ways; splits five into two parts; says how many more to make five; finds hidden parts.

- **Ask:** How many more to make five from two?
  **Expected response:** Three.
- **Ask:** What two parts make five?
  **Expected response:** Two and three.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Makes five with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep playing making five games at home.
