# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 3, Week 2: Capacity

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Full and empty; Half full; Pouring and filling

## Learning objectives
- Children will say full, half full and empty.
- Children will fill and pour with cups and jugs.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, half, pour, fill, cup

**Content:** Full and empty, half full, and pouring and filling.

**Teaching activities:** Use water, sand and rice outdoors or over a tray; mark half with a line or a sticker; compare tall and wide containers; let children pour, fill and empty at will; say "full", "half full" and "empty"; talk about which holds more.

**Learner activities:** Say full, half full and empty and fill and pour with cups and jugs.

**Resources:** Clear plastic cups and jugs; Water tray; Sand and rice; Funnels and spoons; Stickers or tape to mark half

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says full or empty and finds a half-full container.

## Expected outcomes
- Says full or empty for a container.
- Finds a half-full container.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: water and materials; Physical Development: pouring; Literacy: comparing words

## Day 1 (Monday): Full and empty

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Full and empty

### Learning objectives
- Children will say full and empty for containers.

### Success criteria
- Says full or empty correctly
- Fills a cup

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare weights and use a balance.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, half, pour, fill, cup

**Instructional materials:** Clear plastic cups; A water tray; A jug of water

### Starter
Show a full cup and an empty cup and say "This cup is full. This cup is empty."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pour water into a cup until it is full. Children: Children watch and say full.

Step 2. Teacher: Pour the water out and say empty. Children: Children say empty.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child fill and empty a cup. Children: Child fills and empties a cup.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Is it full or empty?" Children: Child says full or empty.

### Practical activity: Fill and empty
1. Take a cup.
2. Fill it with water.
3. Pour it out.

**Safety:** Use shallow water in a tray and wipe spills at once. Supervise closely and use a towel on the floor to prevent slipping.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Fill the cup together and say full.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to fill three cups to full.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is this cup full?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no.
- **Ask:** Is it empty?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no.

### Expected outcomes
- Says full and empty correctly
- Fills and empties a cup

### Conclusion
Dry the tray and say full and empty.

### Home connection
Fill and empty cups in the bath or sink.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say full and empty for containers" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Half full

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Half full

### Learning objectives
- Children will find containers that are half full.

### Success criteria
- Says half full
- Fills a cup to a line

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare weights and use a balance.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, half, pour, fill, cup

**Instructional materials:** Cups marked with a line at half; A water tray; A jug

### Starter
Show a cup filled to the line and say "This cup is half full. It is not full and not empty."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pour water to the line and say half full. Children: Children say half full.

Step 2. Teacher: Compare full, half full and empty cups. Children: Children point to each.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child fill a cup to the line. Children: Child fills to the line.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Is it half full?" Children: Child says yes or no.

### Practical activity: Fill to the line
1. Take a marked cup.
2. Pour water to the line.
3. Say half full.

**Safety:** Use shallow water in a tray and wipe spills at once. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Fill to the line together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find half full without a line.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the cup full or half full?
  **Expected response:** Full or half full.
- **Ask:** What does half mean?
  **Expected response:** In the middle.

### Expected outcomes
- Says half full
- Fills a cup to a line

### Conclusion
Dry the tray and say half full.

### Home connection

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Pouring and filling

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Pouring and filling

### Learning objectives
- Children will pour from one container to another and say which holds more.

### Success criteria
- Pours without spilling much
- Says which holds more

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare weights and use a balance.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, half, pour, fill, cup

**Instructional materials:** Tall and wide containers; A tray of rice or water; Funnels and spoons

### Starter
Show two containers and say "Which holds more? Let us pour and find out."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pour from a small cup into a big jug and count the cups. Children: Children count the cups.

Step 2. Teacher: Compare how many cups fill each container. Children: Child says which holds more.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children pour rice or water between containers. Children: Child pours carefully.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Which holds more?" Children: Child points to the container.

### Practical activity: Pour and compare
1. Pour from a cup into a jug.
2. Count the cups.
3. Say which holds more.

**Safety:** Use a tray for spills and wipe up at once. Keep rice away from mouths and supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Pour with a funnel and count together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to predict before pouring.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which holds more?
  **Expected response:** The bigger container.
- **Ask:** How many cups fill the jug?
  **Expected response:** The number of cups.

### Expected outcomes
- Pours and compares containers
- Says which holds more

### Conclusion
Dry the tray and say holds more.

### Home connection
Pour water between containers at bath time.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "pour from one container to another and say which holds more" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Water play measuring

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Pouring and filling

### Learning objectives
- Children will use cups to fill a bucket and count how many cups it takes.

### Success criteria
- Counts cups poured
- Says how many

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare weights and use a balance.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, half, pour, fill, cup

**Instructional materials:** A bucket; A cup; A tray; Number cards

### Starter
Say "How many cups of water will fill the bucket? Let us count as we pour."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pour one cup at a time into the bucket and count. Children: Children count the cups.

Step 2. Teacher: Stop when the bucket is full. Children: Children say full.

Step 3. Teacher: Compare with a smaller bucket. Children: Child says which needs more.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "It took ten cups." Children: Children repeat the number.

### Practical activity: Fill the bucket
1. Take a cup.
2. Pour into the bucket and count.
3. Stop when full.

**Safety:** Use a shallow bucket outdoors or on a tray and wipe spills. Supervise closely near water.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Pour with the child and count together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find which bucket needs fewer cups.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many cups did it take?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.
- **Ask:** Which bucket needed more?
  **Expected response:** The bigger one.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts cups to fill a bucket
- Says how many

### Conclusion
Empty the bucket outside and say well done.

### Home connection
Count cups of water to fill a bowl.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use cups to fill a bucket and count how many cups it takes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 capacity check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Half full

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child talks about full, half and empty.

### Success criteria
- Says full, half full and empty
- Pours and compares

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare weights and use a balance.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, half, pour, fill, cup

**Instructional materials:** Cups; Water tray; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the full and empty song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: full and empty, half full and pouring. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Is it full?" and watch. Children: Child answers and pours.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Capacity stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and wipe spills at once. Use shallow water and towels on the floor.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two cups.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to order three containers by capacity.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says full and empty; finds half full; pours carefully; says which holds more.

- **Ask:** Is it full?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no.
- **Ask:** Which holds more?
  **Expected response:** The bigger container.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Talks about capacity with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep playing with water and containers at home.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
