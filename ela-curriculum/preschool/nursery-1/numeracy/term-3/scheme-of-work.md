# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 3 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Weight**: Heavy and light; Heavier and lighter; Using a balance | Children will compare two objects by weight. Children will use a balance to check. | Heavy and light, heavier and lighter, and using a balance. | Let children lift real objects and feel the difference before using a balance; teach that size does not always tell weight (a big balloon is light); use a simple pan or seesaw balance; say "the heavier side goes down". | Compare two objects by weight and use a balance to check. | A balance or seesaw; Stones, sponges, balloons and books; Bags of sand and cotton wool; Baskets; Pictures of heavy and light animals | Observe whether each child says which of two objects is heavier and uses a balance with support. | Says which of two is heavier. Uses a balance with support. | Science: forces and materials; Physical Development: lifting and carrying; Literacy: comparing words |
| 2 | **Capacity**: Full and empty; Half full; Pouring and filling | Children will say full, half full and empty. Children will fill and pour with cups and jugs. | Full and empty, half full, and pouring and filling. | Use water, sand and rice outdoors or over a tray; mark half with a line or a sticker; compare tall and wide containers; let children pour, fill and empty at will; say "full", "half full" and "empty"; talk about which holds more. | Say full, half full and empty and fill and pour with cups and jugs. | Clear plastic cups and jugs; Water tray; Sand and rice; Funnels and spoons; Stickers or tape to mark half | Observe whether each child says full or empty and finds a half-full container. | Says full or empty for a container. Finds a half-full container. | Science: water and materials; Physical Development: pouring; Literacy: comparing words |
| 3 | **Time**: Morning, afternoon and night; Days of the week; Yesterday, today and tomorrow | Children will name parts of the day and days of the week. Children will use yesterday, today and tomorrow. | Morning, afternoon and night, days of the week, and yesterday, today and tomorrow. | Link time words to the school day and family routines; use a daily timetable with pictures; sing the days of the week song; use a calendar and turn a page each day; keep each idea concrete (what we did yesterday). | Name parts of the day and days of the week and say yesterday, today and tomorrow. | A daily picture timetable; Days of the week cards; A calendar; A sun and moon picture; Pictures of morning, afternoon and night routines | Observe whether each child names parts of the day and says today and tomorrow correctly with support. | Names parts of the day. Says today and tomorrow correctly with support. | Literacy: the days song; Science: day and night; Social Habits: routines |
| 4 | **Ordinal numbers**: First and last; Second and third; Race and queue games | Children will say first, second, third and last. Children will describe the order of a line. | First and last, second and third, and race and queue games. | Use real lines and races where children can see who is first; say "first, second, third" aloud; distinguish counting numbers (one, two) from ordinal numbers (first, second); make sure every child gets a turn to be first. | Say first, second, third and last and describe the order of a line. | Toy cars for a race; A line of children; Ordinal cards: 1st, 2nd, 3rd; Ribbons or medals made of card; A start and finish line | Observe whether each child says first and last and second and third with support. | Says first and last in a line. Says second and third with support. | Physical Development: running and queueing; Social Habits: taking turns; Literacy: ordinal words |
| 5 | **Consolidation**: Tally marks; Counting in fives and tens; Counting games | Children will make tally marks and count them. Children will count to 20 with support. | Tally marks, counting in fives and tens, and counting games. | Introduce tally marks through a real need such as counting votes or visitors; draw four lines and a cross-line for five; count in fives along tally groups; use a hundred-square or number line for counting to 20; play counting games to build fluency. | Make tally marks for up to 5 and count to 20 with support. | Chalk and boards; Sticks or lollipop sticks; A number line to 20; Counters in tens; Counting game cards | Observe whether each child makes tally marks for up to 5 and counts to 20 with support. | Makes tally marks for up to 5. Counts to 20 with support. | Literacy: marks and symbols; Social Habits: voting and fairness; Physical Development: drawing lines |
| 6 | **Data**: Collecting information; Making a picture graph; Reading the graph | Children will collect information such as favourite fruit. Children will make and read a simple picture graph. | Collecting information, making a picture graph, and reading the graph. | Choose a question children care about (favourite fruit, how we come to school); collect answers with real objects first, then pictures, then a graph; keep the graph to three or four columns; always ask "which has most?" and "which has least?"; accept each child’s choice without comment. | Add a picture to a class graph and say which column has most. | A large graph on the floor or wall; Pictures of fruits; Sticky notes or stickers; Counters; A puppet | Observe whether each child adds to a graph and says which column has most. | Adds a picture to a class graph. Says which column has most. | Literacy: question words; Science: favourite foods and plants; Social Habits: voting fairly |
| 7 | **Money**: Coins and notes; Buying with play money; Giving change with objects | Children will recognise Nigerian coins and notes in play. Children will buy items with play money. | Coins and notes, buying with play money, and giving change with objects. | Use play coins and notes or school-made tokens that look like Nigerian naira; never use real money with children at this age; keep prices to small numbers (1 to 10 play naira); link to market and tuck-shop experiences; respect that families have different incomes and never ask about money at home. | Name coins and notes used in play and buy one item with play money. | Play coins and notes; A play market stall with toy goods; Price labels; Baskets; A till or box | Observe whether each child names play coins and notes and buys one item with play money. | Names coins and notes used in play. Buys one item with play money. | Social Habits: manners at the market; Literacy: market words; Understanding the World: shops and markets |
| 8 | **Position**: Over, under and next to; Left and right; Giving directions | Children will use position words. Children will follow and give simple directions. | Over, under and next to, left and right, and giving directions. | Use the children’s own bodies and real objects; play games such as hide the toy; teach left and right with a wristband on the right hand and a song; keep directions to two steps; let children give directions to friends. | Use position words and follow and give simple directions. | A teddy and a box; A bench and a chair; A wristband or ribbon; Cones; A treasure map picture | Observe whether each child places an object on, under and next to something and follows a two-step direction. | Places an object on, under and next to. Follows a two-step direction. | Physical Development: movement and balance; Literacy: position words; Social Habits: giving and following directions |
| 9 | **Consolidation**: Adding story problems; Taking away story problems; Making up our own story | Children will solve simple number stories with objects. Children will make up a number story. | Adding story problems, taking away story problems, and making up our own story. | Use stories with real objects and children’s own lives (market, school, farm); read each story twice; let children act it out before solving; ask "Do we join or take away?"; keep totals to 5 and extend to 10 for confident children; celebrate different ways of solving. | Solve simple number stories with objects and make up a number story. | Counters and toy animals; Story cards with pictures; A puppet; A farm, market and school set; A number line | Observe whether each child solves a story problem to 5 with objects and tells a simple story. | Solves a story problem to 5 with objects. Tells a simple number story. | Literacy: story language; Social Habits: working with a partner; Science: animals and plants |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising number and counting; Revising shape and measures; Celebration and end-of-year record | Children will show year learning through games and a maths fair. Teachers will complete every child’s end-of-year record and transition note. | Revising number and counting, shape and measures, and celebrating the year with end-of-year records and a transition note. | Keep every activity familiar and joyful; observe quietly; record how far each child counts and how well they solve small problems; invite parents to the celebration; complete the transition note honestly for the Nursery 2 teacher. | Show learning from the year through games and a small maths fair. | Counters and number lines; Shapes, patterns and beads; Balance and cups; Play market; Graph; Certificates or stickers; End-of-year record sheets | Observe and record each child’s end-of-year numeracy learning and complete the transition note. | Takes part confidently in familiar maths games. Each child has an end-of-year observation record. | Literacy: songs and stories; Social Habits: celebration and thanks; Creative Development: shapes and patterns |
