# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 3, Week 8: Position

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Over, under and next to; Left and right; Giving directions

## Learning objectives
- Children will use position words.
- Children will follow and give simple directions.

**Key vocabulary:** over, under, next to, left, right, between

**Content:** Over, under and next to, left and right, and giving directions.

**Teaching activities:** Use the children’s own bodies and real objects; play games such as hide the toy; teach left and right with a wristband on the right hand and a song; keep directions to two steps; let children give directions to friends.

**Learner activities:** Use position words and follow and give simple directions.

**Resources:** A teddy and a box; A bench and a chair; A wristband or ribbon; Cones; A treasure map picture

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child places an object on, under and next to something and follows a two-step direction.

## Expected outcomes
- Places an object on, under and next to.
- Follows a two-step direction.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Physical Development: movement and balance; Literacy: position words; Social Habits: giving and following directions

## Day 1 (Monday): Over, under and next to

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Over, under and next to

### Learning objectives
- Children will use the words over, under, next to, on and in.

### Success criteria
- Places a toy over, under and next to a box
- Says the position word

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt counting, comparing, shapes, time, graphs and money.

**Key vocabulary:** over, under, next to, left, right, between

**Instructional materials:** A teddy; A box and a chair; A bench

### Starter
Put a teddy under a chair and say "The teddy is under the chair."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Move the teddy over, under, in and next to a box. Children: Children say each position word.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Where is the teddy?" Children: Child says where it is.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children place the teddy where you say. Children: Child places the teddy.

Step 4. Teacher: Move their own bodies over and under the bench. Children: Children crawl under and step over.

### Practical activity: Where is the teddy?
1. Take the teddy.
2. Put it where the teacher says.
3. Say where it is.

**Safety:** Keep benches low and crawling areas clear and soft. Supervise closely and never let children climb on furniture.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two position words and move together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give position directions to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is the teddy?
  **Expected response:** Under the chair.
- **Ask:** Is it on the box or in the box?
  **Expected response:** On or in.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses position words
- Places an object where told

### Conclusion
Put the teddy in a box and say goodnight.

### Home connection
Play hide the toy at home and say where it is.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use the words over, under, next to, on and in" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Left and right

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Left and right

### Learning objectives
- Children will learn left and right using their own hands.

### Success criteria
- Shows their left and right hands
- Follows a left or right instruction

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt counting, comparing, shapes, time, graphs and money.

**Key vocabulary:** over, under, next to, left, right, between

**Instructional materials:** A ribbon for the right hand; Cones; A left and right song

### Starter
Put a ribbon on each child’s right hand and say "This is your right hand. The other is your left."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show right and left hands and say the words. Children: Children raise the right and left hand.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing a left and right song with actions. Children: Children sing and move.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Step to the left" or "Step to the right". Children: Children step left or right.

Step 4. Teacher: Remove the ribbons and try again. Children: Child tries without ribbons.

### Practical activity: Left and right moves
1. Hold up your right hand.
2. Step left.
3. Step right.

**Safety:** Leave plenty of space between children and keep the floor clear. Accept that left and right take time to learn.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use the ribbon on the right hand throughout.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to point to something on their left.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which is your right hand?
  **Expected response:** The right hand.
- **Ask:** Step to the left.
  **Expected response:** The child steps left.

### Expected outcomes
- Shows left and right hands
- Follows left and right instructions

### Conclusion
Wave with your right hand and then your left.

### Home connection
Play left and right games at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "learn left and right using their own hands" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Giving directions

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Giving directions

### Learning objectives
- Children will follow and give simple directions around the classroom.

### Success criteria
- Follows a two-step direction
- Gives a direction to a friend

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt counting, comparing, shapes, time, graphs and money.

**Key vocabulary:** over, under, next to, left, right, between

**Instructional materials:** Cones; A toy to find; A simple map picture

### Starter
Say "Walk forward two steps and turn right. What do you find?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give a two-step direction and let a child follow it. Children: Child follows the direction.

Step 2. Teacher: Let the child find the hidden toy. Children: Child finds the toy.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children give directions to friends. Children: Child gives a direction.

Step 4. Teacher: Show a simple map and follow the arrows. Children: Child follows arrows.

### Practical activity: Treasure hunt
1. Listen to the directions.
2. Walk and turn.
3. Find the toy.

**Safety:** Keep the route clear and supervised. Check for hazards and use a safe indoor or outdoor space.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Give one-step directions and walk together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give three-step directions.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where did you go first?
  **Expected response:** Forward.
- **Ask:** Where did you find the toy?
  **Expected response:** Where the map said.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows two-step directions
- Gives a direction to a friend

### Conclusion
Put the cones away and say well done.

### Home connection
Give simple directions at home to find a hidden toy.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Position stories

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Over, under and next to

### Learning objectives
- Children will use position words in a simple story and in play.

### Success criteria
- Uses position words in play
- Retells where things were

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt counting, comparing, shapes, time, graphs and money.

**Key vocabulary:** over, under, next to, left, right, between

**Instructional materials:** A toy house, toy animals and furniture; A story with position words; A puppet

### Starter
Tell a story about a mouse that goes under the table, over the box and next to the cat.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Act out the story with toys. Children: Children watch and say the position words.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Where did the mouse go?" Children: Child says where.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children make their own story with toys. Children: Child plays and tells.

Step 4. Teacher: Retell the story together. Children: Children retell with position words.

### Practical activity: Toy story
1. Choose toys.
2. Make them move.
3. Say where they go.

**Safety:** Use large toys only and supervise sharing. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two or three position words only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a story with four position words.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where did the mouse go?
  **Expected response:** Under the table.
- **Ask:** What was next to the cat?
  **Expected response:** The mouse.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses position words in play
- Retells where things were

### Conclusion
Put the toys away and say well done.

### Home connection
Tell position stories with toys at bedtime.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 position check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Giving directions

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child uses position words and directions.

### Success criteria
- Uses over, under and next to
- Follows a two-step direction

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt counting, comparing, shapes, time, graphs and money.

**Key vocabulary:** over, under, next to, left, right, between

**Instructional materials:** Toys; Cones; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the left and right song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: position words, left and right and directions. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Where is it?" and watch. Children: Child answers and moves.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Position stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use soft and low equipment only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to give directions to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: uses position words; shows left and right; follows directions; gives directions.

- **Ask:** Where is the toy?
  **Expected response:** Under or next to something.
- **Ask:** Which is your left hand?
  **Expected response:** The left hand.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Uses position words and directions with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep using position words at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child uses position words and directions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
