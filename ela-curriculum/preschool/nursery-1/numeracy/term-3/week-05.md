# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 3, Week 5: Consolidation

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W05 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Tally marks; Counting in fives and tens; Counting games

## Learning objectives
- Children will make tally marks and count them.
- Children will count to 20 with support.

**Key vocabulary:** tally, mark, five, ten, twenty, count

**Content:** Tally marks, counting in fives and tens, and counting games.

**Teaching activities:** Introduce tally marks through a real need such as counting votes or visitors; draw four lines and a cross-line for five; count in fives along tally groups; use a hundred-square or number line for counting to 20; play counting games to build fluency.

**Learner activities:** Make tally marks for up to 5 and count to 20 with support.

**Resources:** Chalk and boards; Sticks or lollipop sticks; A number line to 20; Counters in tens; Counting game cards

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child makes tally marks for up to 5 and counts to 20 with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Makes tally marks for up to 5.
- Counts to 20 with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: marks and symbols; Social Habits: voting and fairness; Physical Development: drawing lines

## Day 1 (Monday): Tally marks

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Tally marks

### Learning objectives
- Children will make tally marks to count things.

### Success criteria
- Makes tally marks for up to 5
- Counts the marks

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 15, order numbers and use ordinal words.

**Key vocabulary:** tally, mark, five, ten, twenty, count

**Instructional materials:** Chalk and boards; Sticks; A bucket of balls

### Starter
Drop balls into a basket one at a time and say "Let us make a mark each time. These are tally marks."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to draw one line for each ball. Children: Children watch and draw a line.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the fifth line crossing the four. Children: Children copy the cross-line.

Step 3. Teacher: Count the marks in fives. Children: Children count the marks.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children tally another action such as jumps. Children: Child tallies jumps.

### Practical activity: Mark and count
1. Do an action.
2. Make a tally mark.
3. Count the marks.

**Safety:** Use chalk outdoors or on boards and wash hands afterwards. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Tally to three and count the marks together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tally to ten.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many marks?
  **Expected response:** The number of marks.
- **Ask:** What is the fifth mark?
  **Expected response:** It crosses the four.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes tally marks for up to five
- Counts the marks

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say tally.

### Home connection
Tally how many cars you see on a walk.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make tally marks to count things" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Counting in fives

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Counting in fives and tens

### Learning objectives
- Children will count groups of five using tally marks and fingers.

### Success criteria
- Counts in fives
- Says five, ten, fifteen

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 15, order numbers and use ordinal words.

**Key vocabulary:** tally, mark, five, ten, twenty, count

**Instructional materials:** Tally boards; Counters in fives; A number line to 20

### Starter
Show a hand and say "One hand is five. Two hands are ten."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count five counters in a group and say five. Children: Children say five.

Step 2. Teacher: Put another five and say ten. Children: Children say ten.

Step 3. Teacher: Count tally groups in fives. Children: Children count five, ten, fifteen.

Step 4. Teacher: Check by counting one by one. Children: Child counts one by one.

### Practical activity: Fives and tens
1. Make groups of five.
2. Count them in fives.
3. Check one by one.

**Safety:** Use large counters and supervise closely. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count five and ten only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count to fifty in fives.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many in two groups of five?
  **Expected response:** Ten.
- **Ask:** What comes after ten in fives?
  **Expected response:** Fifteen.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts in fives
- Says five, ten and fifteen

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say fives.

### Home connection
Count your fingers and toes in fives.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count groups of five using tally marks and fingers" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Counting to twenty

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Counting games

### Learning objectives
- Children will say the numbers from 1 to 20 in order with support.

### Success criteria
- Says numbers to 20 with support
- Counts objects to 20

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 15, order numbers and use ordinal words.

**Key vocabulary:** tally, mark, five, ten, twenty, count

**Instructional materials:** A number line to 20; Counters; A drum

### Starter
Walk along a number line to 20 and say "Let us count to twenty together."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count to 20 with a drum beat. Children: Children count with the beat.

Step 2. Teacher: Count 20 counters in two tens. Children: Children count ten then ten more.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children count in pairs. Children: Pairs count to twenty.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Well done, you can count to twenty." Children: Children clap and cheer.

### Practical activity: Walk to twenty
1. Stand at the start.
2. Walk along the number line.
3. Say each number.

**Safety:** Keep the number line flat and secured. Walk slowly and one at a time.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count to fifteen with the number line.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count backwards from ten.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What comes after nineteen?
  **Expected response:** Twenty.
- **Ask:** Count to twenty.
  **Expected response:** The child counts.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts to twenty with support
- Counts objects to twenty

### Conclusion
Count to twenty together and wave.

### Home connection
Count up to twenty during daily routines.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say the numbers from 1 to 20 in order with support" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Counting games

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Counting games

### Learning objectives
- Children will play games that practise counting, tallying and numerals.

### Success criteria
- Plays a counting game
- Takes turns and counts correctly

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 15, order numbers and use ordinal words.

**Key vocabulary:** tally, mark, five, ten, twenty, count

**Instructional materials:** Dice with dots; Counters; Number cards; A game board

### Starter
Show a dice and say "We will throw the dice and count the dots."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to throw the dice and count the dots. Children: Children watch and count.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child throw the dice and collect counters. Children: Child throws and counts.

Step 3. Teacher: Make a tally of the dice throws. Children: Child makes a tally mark.

Step 4. Teacher: Count the counters at the end. Children: Child counts the counters.

### Practical activity: Dice and count
1. Throw the dice.
2. Count the dots.
3. Collect that many counters.

**Safety:** Use large foam dice and keep play on a mat so that nothing rolls away. Supervise turns closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a dice with up to three dots.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to add two dice.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many dots?
  **Expected response:** The number of dots.
- **Ask:** How many counters now?
  **Expected response:** The total.

### Expected outcomes
- Plays a counting game
- Takes turns and counts correctly

### Conclusion
Put the games away and say well done.

### Home connection
Play a dice game at home and count the dots.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 tally check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Counting games

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child tallies and counts to twenty.

### Success criteria
- Makes tally marks for up to 5
- Counts to 20 with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to 15, order numbers and use ordinal words.

**Key vocabulary:** tally, mark, five, ten, twenty, count

**Instructional materials:** Tally boards; Counters; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the counting song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: tally, fives and dice game. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How many?" and watch. Children: Child counts and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and highest number counted. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Tally stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use large counters and foam dice.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use numbers to ten.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to count to thirty.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: makes tally marks; counts in fives; counts to 20; plays counting games.

- **Ask:** How many tally marks?
  **Expected response:** The number of marks.
- **Ask:** What comes after fifteen?
  **Expected response:** Sixteen.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Tallies and counts with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep counting and tallying at home.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
