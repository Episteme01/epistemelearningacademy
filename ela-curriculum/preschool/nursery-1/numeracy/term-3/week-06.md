# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 3, Week 6: Data

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Collecting information; Making a picture graph; Reading the graph

## Learning objectives
- Children will collect information such as favourite fruit.
- Children will make and read a simple picture graph.

**Key vocabulary:** graph, favourite, most, least, row, picture

**Content:** Collecting information, making a picture graph, and reading the graph.

**Teaching activities:** Choose a question children care about (favourite fruit, how we come to school); collect answers with real objects first, then pictures, then a graph; keep the graph to three or four columns; always ask "which has most?" and "which has least?"; accept each child’s choice without comment.

**Learner activities:** Add a picture to a class graph and say which column has most.

**Resources:** A large graph on the floor or wall; Pictures of fruits; Sticky notes or stickers; Counters; A puppet

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child adds to a graph and says which column has most.

## Expected outcomes
- Adds a picture to a class graph.
- Says which column has most.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: question words; Science: favourite foods and plants; Social Habits: voting fairly

## Day 1 (Monday): Collecting information

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Collecting information

### Learning objectives
- Children will ask and answer a simple question and collect the answers.

### Success criteria
- Answers a favourite question
- Puts an object in a group

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, count to 20 and make tally marks.

**Key vocabulary:** graph, favourite, most, least, row, picture

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of mango, orange and banana; Three baskets; A puppet

### Starter
Ask "Which fruit do you like best? Mango, orange or banana?" and hold up the pictures.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the three fruit pictures and the three baskets. Children: Children look and name the fruits.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask each child to choose a favourite and place a counter in the basket. Children: Child places a counter in a basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Count the counters in each basket. Children: Children count each basket.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Which has the most counters?" Children: Child points to the basket with most.

### Practical activity: Favourite fruit
1. Look at the fruits.
2. Choose your favourite.
3. Put a counter in a basket.

**Safety:** Use only pictures or toy fruit for this activity. Never judge a child’s choice and check allergies if real fruit is shown.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two fruits and help the child choose.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count each basket and say how many more.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which fruit do you like?
  **Expected response:** A fruit name.
- **Ask:** Which has the most?
  **Expected response:** The fullest basket.

### Expected outcomes
- Answers a favourite question
- Counts the answers

### Conclusion
Put the baskets away and say well done.

### Home connection
Ask family members their favourite fruit and count the answers.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "ask and answer a simple question and collect the answers" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Making a picture graph

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Making a picture graph

### Learning objectives
- Children will make a picture graph from the answers.

### Success criteria
- Places pictures in a column
- Lines pictures up neatly

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, count to 20 and make tally marks.

**Key vocabulary:** graph, favourite, most, least, row, picture

**Instructional materials:** A large graph with three columns; Fruit picture cards; Sticky tack

### Starter
Show an empty graph and say "Today we put our choices on a graph."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the columns with a fruit at the bottom of each. Children: Children name each column.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a picture to place in a column. Children: Child places a picture.

Step 3. Teacher: Line the pictures up from the bottom. Children: Child lines up the pictures.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Now we can see the answers." Children: Children look at the graph.

### Practical activity: Our graph
1. Choose a fruit picture.
2. Stick it in the right column.
3. Line it up neatly.

**Safety:** Use sticky tack that is safe and keep it away from mouths. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Place the picture together in the column.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a graph about something else.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which column did you choose?
  **Expected response:** Mango, orange or banana.
- **Ask:** Why do we line them up?
  **Expected response:** To see which is most.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a picture graph
- Places pictures in the correct column

### Conclusion
Look at the finished graph and say well done.

### Home connection
Make a graph at home with pictures or objects.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Reading the graph

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Reading the graph

### Learning objectives
- Children will read the graph and say which column has most and least.

### Success criteria
- Says which column has most
- Says which column has least

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, count to 20 and make tally marks.

**Key vocabulary:** graph, favourite, most, least, row, picture

**Instructional materials:** The class graph; Counters; A pointer

### Starter
Point to the graph and say "What can we learn from our graph?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count the pictures in each column together. Children: Children count each column.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which has the most?" Children: Child points to the tallest column.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which has the least?" Children: Child points to the shortest column.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How many more mangoes than bananas?" Children: Child counts the difference.

### Practical activity: Read the graph
1. Look at the graph.
2. Count each column.
3. Say most and least.

**Safety:** Use a pointer carefully and keep the group seated. Keep the graph at child height.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Compare two columns only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say how many more in one column than another.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which has the most?
  **Expected response:** The tallest column.
- **Ask:** Which has the least?
  **Expected response:** The shortest column.

### Expected outcomes
- Reads a graph for most and least
- Counts each column

### Conclusion
Say the answers together and wave.

### Home connection
Read a graph in a book or magazine with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "read the graph and say which column has most and least" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Graph of how we come to school

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Collecting information

### Learning objectives
- Children will make and read a graph about how they come to school.

### Success criteria
- Adds to a new graph
- Reads the graph with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, count to 20 and make tally marks.

**Key vocabulary:** graph, favourite, most, least, row, picture

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a car, a bus, a bicycle and walking; A large graph; Sticky tack

### Starter
Ask "How do you come to school? Do you walk, or come by bus, car or bicycle?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the four pictures and name each way. Children: Children name the ways.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child place a picture for how they come. Children: Child places a picture.

Step 3. Teacher: Count and compare the columns. Children: Children count each column.

Step 4. Teacher: Say which way is the most common. Children: Child says the most common way.

### Practical activity: Travel graph
1. Choose how you come to school.
2. Place your picture.
3. Say which has most.

**Safety:** Be sensitive to different family circumstances and never comment on how a child travels. Use pictures only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two ways to choose from.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say how many fewer come by bicycle than by bus.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How do you come to school?
  **Expected response:** Walk, bus or car.
- **Ask:** Which way has the most?
  **Expected response:** The tallest column.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a travel graph
- Reads the graph with support

### Conclusion
Look at the graph and say well done.

### Home connection
Talk about how your family travels.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 graph check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T3-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Reading the graph

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child contributes to and reads a graph.

### Success criteria
- Adds to a graph
- Says which column has most

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, count to 20 and make tally marks.

**Key vocabulary:** graph, favourite, most, least, row, picture

**Instructional materials:** Graphs; Pictures; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the counting song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: collect, make and read. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Which has most?" and watch. Children: Child answers with a point or word.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Graph stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use safe sticky tack.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two columns.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to ask a question and make a graph.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: answers a question; adds to a graph; says most and least; counts columns.

- **Ask:** Which has the most?
  **Expected response:** The tallest column.
- **Ask:** Which has the least?
  **Expected response:** The shortest column.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Contributes to and reads a graph with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep making simple graphs at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child contributes to and reads a graph" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
