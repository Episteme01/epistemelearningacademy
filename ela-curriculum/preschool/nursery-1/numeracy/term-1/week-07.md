# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 1, Week 7: Size

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Big and small; Bigger and smaller; Ordering three things by size

## Learning objectives
- Children will compare two objects by size.
- Children will order three objects from small to big.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, bigger, smaller, biggest, smallest

**Content:** Big and small, bigger and smaller, and ordering three things by size.

**Teaching activities:** Always compare two objects side by side; use the words bigger and smaller; introduce the -est words with three objects; use everyday objects such as cups, balls, stones and children’s shoes; keep a fair comparison (same starting line).

**Learner activities:** Compare two objects by size and order three objects from small to big.

**Resources:** Cups in three sizes; Balls in three sizes; Stones; Shoes; Three bears picture set

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child compares two objects by size and orders three objects.

## Expected outcomes
- Says which of two is bigger.
- Orders three objects by size with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: comparing natural things; Literacy: comparing words; Creative Development: drawing big and small

## Day 1 (Monday): Big and small

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Big and small

### Learning objectives
- Children will say big and small for different objects.

### Success criteria
- Says big or small correctly
- Finds a big and a small thing

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour and size and find pairs.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, bigger, smaller, biggest, smallest

**Instructional materials:** A big and a small ball; A big and a small cup; Pictures of a big and a small animal

### Starter
Hold up a big and a small ball and say "This is big. This is small."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show pairs of objects and say big or small. Children: Children repeat the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which is big?" for each pair. Children: Children point to the big one.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children find a big and a small thing in the room. Children: Child finds objects.

Step 4. Teacher: Make big and small shapes with arms and bodies. Children: Children stretch big and curl small.

### Practical activity: Big and small hunt
1. Look around the room.
2. Find a big thing.
3. Find a small thing.

**Safety:** Keep the walk slow and supervised. Do not let children lift heavy objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two very different sizes first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find something bigger than their hand.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the elephant big or small?
  **Expected response:** Big.
- **Ask:** Is the ant big or small?
  **Expected response:** Small.

### Expected outcomes
- Says big and small correctly
- Finds big and small things

### Conclusion
Make a big shape and a small shape and wave.

### Home connection
Talk about big and small things you see.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say big and small for different objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Bigger and smaller

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Bigger and smaller

### Learning objectives
- Children will compare two objects and say which is bigger or smaller.

### Success criteria
- Says bigger or smaller
- Compares side by side

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour and size and find pairs.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, bigger, smaller, biggest, smallest

**Instructional materials:** Pairs of similar objects in different sizes; Cups; Stones; Shoes

### Starter
Put two stones side by side and say "This one is bigger than that one."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to put two objects side by side to compare. Children: Children watch and say bigger.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which is bigger?" for different pairs. Children: Child points and says bigger.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which is smaller?" Children: Child points and says smaller.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children compare their own shoes. Children: Child compares two shoes.

### Practical activity: Compare two
1. Pick two objects.
2. Put them side by side.
3. Say which is bigger.

**Safety:** Use clean objects with no sharp edges and wash hands after. Keep shoes tidy.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Compare objects that are very different in size.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find something bigger than a stone.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which is bigger?
  **Expected response:** The larger object.
- **Ask:** Which is smaller?
  **Expected response:** The smaller object.

### Expected outcomes
- Compares two objects by size
- Says bigger and smaller

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say bigger and smaller.

### Home connection
Compare two fruits at home and say which is bigger.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Biggest and smallest

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Ordering three things by size

### Learning objectives
- Children will order three objects from smallest to biggest.

### Success criteria
- Puts three objects in order
- Says biggest and smallest

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour and size and find pairs.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, bigger, smaller, biggest, smallest

**Instructional materials:** Three cups; Three balls; Three bears picture set

### Starter
Show three bears and say "Baby Bear, Mummy Bear, Daddy Bear. Which is the biggest?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put three cups in a row from smallest to biggest. Children: Children watch and say biggest.

Step 2. Teacher: Mix them up and ask a child to order them. Children: Child orders the cups.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which is smallest? Which is biggest?" Children: Child points to each.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child order a set of three. Children: Child orders objects.

### Practical activity: Order three
1. Take three objects.
2. Put them in order.
3. Say biggest and smallest.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable cups and supervise closely. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Order two objects first and then add a third.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to order five objects.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which is the biggest?
  **Expected response:** The largest object.
- **Ask:** Which is the smallest?
  **Expected response:** The smallest object.

### Expected outcomes
- Orders three objects by size
- Says biggest and smallest

### Conclusion
Put the sets away and say biggest and smallest.

### Home connection
Order three spoons or cups from smallest to biggest.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "order three objects from smallest to biggest" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Three bears

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Ordering three things by size

### Learning objectives
- Children will use size words to retell part of the story of the three bears.

### Success criteria
- Uses big, middle and small
- Matches objects to the bears

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour and size and find pairs.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, bigger, smaller, biggest, smallest

**Instructional materials:** Three bowls, chairs and beds in three sizes; Three bear toys; A short story

### Starter
Say "Today we meet three bears: Daddy Bear, Mummy Bear and Baby Bear."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the story slowly with the three sizes of bowls. Children: Children listen and point.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which bowl is for Daddy Bear?" Children: Child points to the biggest bowl.

Step 3. Teacher: Match chairs and beds to each bear. Children: Child matches objects to bears.

Step 4. Teacher: Retell the story together. Children: Children retell with objects.

### Practical activity: The three bears
1. Look at the three bowls.
2. Match each to a bear.
3. Say big, middle and small.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable bowls and keep the play gentle. Supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Match two bowls to two bears first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to add more things in three sizes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which bowl is for Baby Bear?
  **Expected response:** The smallest bowl.
- **Ask:** Which is the middle size?
  **Expected response:** Mummy Bear’s bowl.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses big, middle and small
- Matches objects to the bears

### Conclusion
Put the bears away and say good night.

### Home connection
Tell the story of the three bears at bedtime.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 size check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Ordering three things by size

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child compares and orders by size.

### Success criteria
- Compares two objects by size
- Orders three objects

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour and size and find pairs.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, bigger, smaller, biggest, smallest

**Instructional materials:** Cups; Balls; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the big and small song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: compare, order and three bears. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Which is bigger?" and watch. Children: Child answers with a word.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Size stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use unbreakable objects. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and compare two objects only.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to order five objects.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says big and small; says bigger and smaller; orders three objects; uses biggest and smallest.

- **Ask:** Which is bigger?
  **Expected response:** The larger object.
- **Ask:** Which is the smallest?
  **Expected response:** The smallest object.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Compares and orders by size with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep comparing sizes at home.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
