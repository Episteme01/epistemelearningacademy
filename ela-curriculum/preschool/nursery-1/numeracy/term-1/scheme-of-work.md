# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 1 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Counting**: Counting 1 to 5 with objects; Counting rhymes; Numerals 1 to 5 | Children will count objects to 5 touching each one once. Children will recognise the numerals 1 to 5. | Counting objects to 5, counting rhymes, and the numerals 1 to 5. | Always count real objects that children can touch and move; model touching each object once and moving it aside; say the last number as how many; link numerals to quantities; use the first week to observe how far each child counts. | Count up to 5 objects touching each once and match numerals 1 to 5 to quantities. | Counters such as bottle tops, stones or seeds; Number cards 1 to 5; Counting rhyme pictures; Baskets and plates; Chalk or sand tray | Observe whether each child counts up to 5 objects accurately and matches a numeral to a group. | Counts up to 5 objects accurately. Matches numerals 1 to 5 to quantities. | Literacy: number rhymes; Social Habits: sharing and turns; Science: counting natural objects |
| 2 | **Counting**: Saying numbers to 10; Number rhymes to 10; Counting steps and claps | Children will say the number names from 1 to 10 in order. Children will count steps, claps and jumps to 10. | Saying numbers to 10, number rhymes to 10, and counting steps and claps. | Use rhythm and movement for number names 6 to 10; make a number line on the floor; count actions as well as objects; say numbers slowly and clearly; practise both forwards and, when ready, backwards. | Say the number names from 1 to 10 in order and count actions to 10. | A floor number line 1 to 10; Counting rhyme chart to 10; A drum; Large numeral cards 1 to 10; Beanbags | Observe whether each child says numbers to 10 in order and counts actions to 10. | Says numbers to 10 in order. Counts actions to 10 with support. | Physical Development: hopping and jumping; Literacy: rhymes; Music: drumming and counting |
| 3 | **Counting**: One touch for each object; Counting in a line and a circle; How many are there? | Children will touch each object once as they count to 10. Children will say the last number as how many. | Touching each object once, counting in a line and a circle, and saying how many there are. | Teach the one-to-one rule: touch, move and say; arrange objects in a line first, then a circle, then a scatter; always ask "How many are there?" after counting; use tricks such as moving counted items into a box. | Count up to 10 objects with one-to-one touch and say how many. | Counters; Egg boxes or ten frames; Pegs and a line; Stones, seeds and bottle tops; Baskets | Observe whether each child counts up to 10 objects accurately and says how many. | Counts up to 10 objects with one-to-one touch. Says how many in a group. | Science: counting natural objects; Social Habits: sharing equally; Literacy: how many words |
| 4 | **Numerals**: Looking at numerals 6 to 10; Tracing numerals in sand and air; Matching numerals and groups | Children will recognise the numerals 6 to 10. Children will trace numerals in sand and in the air. | Looking at numerals 6 to 10, tracing numerals in sand and air, and matching numerals and groups. | Link each numeral to a quantity; model the starting point and direction of each numeral; use sand, clay, large air movements and pipe cleaners before pencils; accept reversed numerals gently; keep sessions short. | Recognise the numerals 1 to 10 and trace them in sand and air. | Large numeral cards 1 to 10; A tray of sand or flour; Clay or playdough; Pipe cleaners; Counters | Observe whether each child names numerals 1 to 10 and traces some of them. | Names numerals 1 to 10 with support. Traces three or more numerals. | Literacy: letter-like shapes and fine motor skills; Physical Development: finger strength; Creative Development: clay |
| 5 | **Consolidation**: Number hop and jump; Number hunt; Counting bags | Children will play games that practise counting and numerals to 10. Children will show how many in a group with fingers or objects. | Number hop and jump, number hunt, and counting bags. | Use games to practise rather than teach new ideas; keep rules simple; let children play in pairs; watch for children who guess and not count; note children who need more practice. | Play counting games with confidence and count out a number of objects when asked. | Numeral cards 1 to 10; Counting bags; A hopscotch grid; Beanbags; Counters | Observe whether each child counts out a number of objects when asked and plays games with confidence. | Plays counting games with confidence. Counts out a number of objects when asked. | Physical Development: hopping and throwing; Social Habits: taking turns; Literacy: following instructions |
| 6 | **Sorting**: Sorting by colour and size; Matching pairs; Odd one out | Children will sort objects by colour and size. Children will find pairs and the odd one out. | Sorting by colour and size, matching pairs, and finding the odd one out. | Let children choose their own sorting rule at first; name the rule aloud ("these are all red"); use real objects such as bottle tops, leaves and fruit; ask "How did you sort?"; accept any sensible rule. | Sort objects by colour and size, find pairs and spot the odd one out. | Mixed bottle tops and buttons; Sorting hoops or baskets; Pairs of socks or shoes; Picture cards for odd one out; Leaves and stones | Observe whether each child sorts by colour or size and says how they sorted. | Sorts a set of objects by colour or size. Finds the odd one out with support. | Science: grouping natural things; Literacy: describing words; Social Habits: sharing materials |
| 7 | **Size**: Big and small; Bigger and smaller; Ordering three things by size | Children will compare two objects by size. Children will order three objects from small to big. | Big and small, bigger and smaller, and ordering three things by size. | Always compare two objects side by side; use the words bigger and smaller; introduce the -est words with three objects; use everyday objects such as cups, balls, stones and children’s shoes; keep a fair comparison (same starting line). | Compare two objects by size and order three objects from small to big. | Cups in three sizes; Balls in three sizes; Stones; Shoes; Three bears picture set | Observe whether each child compares two objects by size and orders three objects. | Says which of two is bigger. Orders three objects by size with support. | Science: comparing natural things; Literacy: comparing words; Creative Development: drawing big and small |
| 8 | **Length**: Long and short; Longer and shorter; Measuring with hands and steps | Children will compare two objects by length. Children will measure with hand spans or steps. | Long and short, longer and shorter, and measuring with hands and steps. | Compare lengths by lining objects up at the same starting point; use ropes, ribbons, pencils and children’s own bodies; introduce non-standard units such as hand spans and cubes; stress that we start at the same line and count the units without gaps. | Compare two objects by length and measure a short object with hands or cubes. | Ropes and ribbons of different lengths; Pencils; Cubes or paper clips; String; Pictures of long and short animals | Observe whether each child says which of two objects is longer and measures with cubes or hands. | Says which of two is longer. Measures a short object with hands or cubes. | Science: comparing things; Physical Development: stretching; Literacy: describing words |
| 9 | **Shape**: Circles and squares; Triangles; Shapes around us | Children will name circle, square and triangle. Children will find these shapes in the classroom. | Circles and squares, triangles, and finding shapes around us. | Handle real 3D and flat shapes; talk about sides and corners; draw shapes in the air and sand; look at shapes in the environment such as plates, windows and roofs; accept shapes in different sizes and positions. | Name circle, square and triangle and find them in the classroom. | Flat wooden or card shapes; Plates, books and roof pictures; Sand tray; Straws and playdough; A feely bag | Observe whether each child names circle, square and triangle and finds them around the room. | Names circle, square and triangle. Finds each shape in the room. | Creative Development: shape pictures; Science: shapes in nature; Literacy: shape words |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising counting and numerals; Revising sorting and measures; Revising shapes | Children will show Term 1 learning through play stations. Teachers will gather evidence for every child’s Term 1 record. | Revising counting and numerals, sorting and measures, and shapes, with end-of-term records. | Keep every activity familiar and joyful; observe quietly during play; record what children can do and how far each child counts; share records with parents in plain language with a strength, a next step and a home idea. | Show learning from the term through play stations and a celebration. | Counters; Numeral cards; Sorting baskets; Ropes and cubes; Shapes; Stickers; Class record sheets | Observe and record each child’s Term 1 numeracy learning. | Completes familiar tasks with confidence at play stations. Each child has an observation record for Term 1. | Literacy: songs and rhymes; Social Habits: celebration; Creative Development: shape pictures |
