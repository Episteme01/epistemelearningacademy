# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 1, Week 6: Sorting

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Sorting by colour and size; Matching pairs; Odd one out

## Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects by colour and size.
- Children will find pairs and the odd one out.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, match, pair, same, different, odd one out

**Content:** Sorting by colour and size, matching pairs, and finding the odd one out.

**Teaching activities:** Let children choose their own sorting rule at first; name the rule aloud ("these are all red"); use real objects such as bottle tops, leaves and fruit; ask "How did you sort?"; accept any sensible rule.

**Learner activities:** Sort objects by colour and size, find pairs and spot the odd one out.

**Resources:** Mixed bottle tops and buttons; Sorting hoops or baskets; Pairs of socks or shoes; Picture cards for odd one out; Leaves and stones

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child sorts by colour or size and says how they sorted.

## Expected outcomes
- Sorts a set of objects by colour or size.
- Finds the odd one out with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: grouping natural things; Literacy: describing words; Social Habits: sharing materials

## Day 1 (Monday): Sorting by colour

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Sorting by colour and size

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects by colour and say how they sorted.

### Success criteria
- Puts objects of one colour together
- Says the colour name

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count objects to 10, name numerals 1 to 10 and play counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, match, pair, same, different, odd one out

**Instructional materials:** Bottle tops in red, blue, green and yellow; Four baskets; Colour cards

### Starter
Hold up a red bottle top and say "This is red. Where do the red ones go?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to put red bottle tops into a red basket. Children: Children watch and name the colour.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a handful of mixed bottle tops. Children: Child looks at the colours.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children sort them into the baskets. Children: Child sorts by colour.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How did you sort?" Children: Child says by colour.

### Practical activity: Colour baskets
1. Put colour cards on the mat.
2. Take a bottle top.
3. Put it in the matching basket.

**Safety:** Use bottle tops larger than a fist or check they are not small enough to swallow. Supervise closely and keep tops out of mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sort two colours only and name each colour together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sort by colour and then count each group.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What colour is this?
  **Expected response:** The colour name.
- **Ask:** How did you sort?
  **Expected response:** By colour.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts objects by colour
- Says how they sorted

### Conclusion
Look at the full baskets and say the colours.

### Home connection
Sort socks or spoons by colour at home.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Sorting by size

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Sorting by colour and size

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects by size into big and small.

### Success criteria
- Puts big and small objects in groups
- Says big or small

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count objects to 10, name numerals 1 to 10 and play counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, match, pair, same, different, odd one out

**Instructional materials:** Big and small leaves; Big and small stones; Two hoops labelled big and small

### Starter
Hold up a big and a small leaf and say "Which one is big? Which one is small?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to sort leaves into big and small hoops. Children: Children watch and say big or small.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each pair a bag of mixed leaves and stones. Children: Pairs look at the objects.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children sort them into hoops. Children: Child sorts by size.

Step 4. Teacher: Check the hoops together. Children: Children say which hoop has more.

### Practical activity: Big and small hoops
1. Look at the objects.
2. Decide big or small.
3. Put them in the hoop.

**Safety:** Check leaves and stones are clean with no sharp edges and wash hands after. Use only large safe objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer one big and one small object at a time.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sort three sizes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is this big or small?
  **Expected response:** Big or small.
- **Ask:** Which hoop has more?
  **Expected response:** The hoop with more.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts objects by size
- Says big or small

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say big and small.

### Home connection
Sort spoons or shoes by size at home.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Matching pairs

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Matching pairs

### Learning objectives
- Children will find matching pairs of objects.

### Success criteria
- Finds a pair
- Says same

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count objects to 10, name numerals 1 to 10 and play counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, match, pair, same, different, odd one out

**Instructional materials:** Pairs of socks; Pairs of shoes; A matching card game

### Starter
Hold up one sock and say "Where is the other one? It must be the same."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a sock and find its pair from a pile. Children: Children watch and say same.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child one sock to match. Children: Child finds the matching sock.

Step 3. Teacher: Play the matching card game. Children: Child turns cards to find a pair.

Step 4. Teacher: Count the pairs. Children: Children count the pairs.

### Practical activity: Find the pair
1. Take one item.
2. Look for its pair.
3. Put them together.

**Safety:** Use clean items and wash hands after handling socks and shoes. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer three items with one match.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find pairs that are almost the same.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is this the same?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no.
- **Ask:** How many pairs?
  **Expected response:** The number of pairs.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds matching pairs
- Says the word same

### Conclusion
Put the pairs away and count them.

### Home connection
Match socks and shoes with your child at home.

## Day 4 (Thursday): Odd one out

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Odd one out

### Learning objectives
- Children will find the odd one out in a group of objects.

### Success criteria
- Finds the odd one out
- Says why it is different

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count objects to 10, name numerals 1 to 10 and play counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, match, pair, same, different, odd one out

**Instructional materials:** Groups of four objects with one different; Picture cards; A tray

### Starter
Show three red bottle tops and one blue and say "One is different. Which one?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a group with one different and ask which is odd. Children: Children point to the odd one.

Step 2. Teacher: Say why it is different. Children: Children say different colour.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children find the odd one in other groups. Children: Child points and says why.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children make an odd one out for a friend. Children: Child makes a group.

### Practical activity: Odd one out
1. Look at four objects.
2. Find the odd one.
3. Say why it is different.

**Safety:** Use large safe objects and supervise the game. Keep objects away from mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use groups where the difference is a clear colour.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find an odd one out by shape.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which is the odd one out?
  **Expected response:** The different object.
- **Ask:** Why is it different?
  **Expected response:** Colour, size or shape.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds the odd one out
- Says why it is different

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say well done.

### Home connection
Make an odd one out game with household objects.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 sorting check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Sorting by colour and size

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child sorts and matches.

### Success criteria
- Sorts by colour or size
- Finds pairs and odd one out

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count objects to 10, name numerals 1 to 10 and play counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, match, pair, same, different, odd one out

**Instructional materials:** Sorting baskets; Pairs; Odd one out groups; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the sorting song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: colour sort, size sort and odd one out. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How did you sort?" Children: Child answers with a word.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Sorting stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use large objects only. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two colours.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to sort by two features.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: sorts by colour; sorts by size; finds pairs; finds the odd one out; says the sorting rule.

- **Ask:** How did you sort?
  **Expected response:** By colour or size.
- **Ask:** Which is the odd one?
  **Expected response:** The different one.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Sorts and matches with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep sorting things at home.
