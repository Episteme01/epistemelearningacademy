# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 1, Week 2: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Saying numbers to 10; Number rhymes to 10; Counting steps and claps

## Learning objectives
- Children will say the number names from 1 to 10 in order.
- Children will count steps, claps and jumps to 10.

**Key vocabulary:** six, seven, eight, nine, ten, number

**Content:** Saying numbers to 10, number rhymes to 10, and counting steps and claps.

**Teaching activities:** Use rhythm and movement for number names 6 to 10; make a number line on the floor; count actions as well as objects; say numbers slowly and clearly; practise both forwards and, when ready, backwards.

**Learner activities:** Say the number names from 1 to 10 in order and count actions to 10.

**Resources:** A floor number line 1 to 10; Counting rhyme chart to 10; A drum; Large numeral cards 1 to 10; Beanbags

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says numbers to 10 in order and counts actions to 10.

## Expected outcomes
- Says numbers to 10 in order.
- Counts actions to 10 with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Physical Development: hopping and jumping; Literacy: rhymes; Music: drumming and counting

## Day 1 (Monday): Saying numbers to ten

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Saying numbers to 10

### Learning objectives
- Children will say the number names from one to ten in order.

### Success criteria
- Says numbers 1 to 10 with support
- Points to numerals as they say them

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to 5 objects and recognise numerals 1 to 5.

**Key vocabulary:** six, seven, eight, nine, ten, number

**Instructional materials:** A floor number line from 1 to 10; Numeral cards 1 to 10; A pointer

### Starter
Walk along a number line on the floor and say "Let us walk the numbers: one, two, three."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Walk along the number line saying each number. Children: Children walk and say the numbers.

Step 2. Teacher: Point to each numeral and say it slowly. Children: Children repeat each number.

Step 3. Teacher: Stop at a number and ask "What comes next?" Children: Child says the next number.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk the line again and count to ten. Children: Children walk and count to ten.

### Practical activity: Walk the line
1. Stand at one.
2. Walk to ten saying each number.
3. Stop and say what comes next.

**Safety:** Keep the number line flat and secured so children do not trip. Walk slowly and one at a time.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk the line holding the child’s hand and say the numbers softly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to walk backwards from ten.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What comes after five?
  **Expected response:** Six.
- **Ask:** What is the last number?
  **Expected response:** Ten.

### Expected outcomes
- Says numbers to ten in order
- Points to numerals while counting

### Conclusion
Count to ten together and wave.

### Home connection
Count stairs or steps up to ten with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say the number names from one to ten in order" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Number rhymes to ten

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Number rhymes to 10

### Learning objectives
- Children will sing and act number rhymes that count to ten.

### Success criteria
- Sings a rhyme with number actions
- Shows ten fingers

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to 5 objects and recognise numerals 1 to 5.

**Key vocabulary:** six, seven, eight, nine, ten, number

**Instructional materials:** A rhyme chart: ten little fingers; Finger puppets; A drum

### Starter
Sing "Ten little fingers" and show ten fingers.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Teach the rhyme line by line with finger actions. Children: Children copy the actions.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing again and count the fingers from one to ten. Children: Children count their fingers.

Step 3. Teacher: Add a drum beat for each number. Children: Children clap with each beat.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children act the rhyme in a circle. Children: Children act and count.

### Practical activity: Ten fingers rhyme
1. Sing the rhyme.
2. Count ten fingers.
3. Clap the numbers.

**Safety:** Keep the movement seated and gentle. Make sure the drum is not too loud for sensitive children.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sing slowly and count on fingers beside the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count ten fingers backwards.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many fingers?
  **Expected response:** Ten.
- **Ask:** What comes before ten?
  **Expected response:** Nine.

### Expected outcomes
- Sings a ten rhyme
- Counts ten fingers

### Conclusion
Sing the rhyme once more and wave.

### Home connection
Sing ten little fingers together at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sing and act number rhymes that count to ten" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Counting steps and claps

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Counting steps and claps

### Learning objectives
- Children will count actions such as steps, claps and jumps to ten.

### Success criteria
- Counts ten claps
- Counts steps to ten

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to 5 objects and recognise numerals 1 to 5.

**Key vocabulary:** six, seven, eight, nine, ten, number

**Instructional materials:** A drum; A rope line for steps; Beanbags

### Starter
Clap your hands and say "Let us count my claps. One, two, three."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Clap up to ten times slowly and ask children to count. Children: Children count the claps.

Step 2. Teacher: Walk ten steps across the room counting aloud. Children: Children walk and count steps.

Step 3. Teacher: Jump ten times together. Children: Children jump and count.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How many jumps did we do?" Children: Child says ten.

### Practical activity: Count the action
1. Clap and count.
2. Walk and count.
3. Jump and count.

**Safety:** Clear the floor of hazards and leave space between children. Jump on a soft mat only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count to five and then add on with the teacher.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose an action and count to ten.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many claps?
  **Expected response:** Ten.
- **Ask:** What action did we count?
  **Expected response:** Claps, steps or jumps.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts actions to ten
- Says how many actions

### Conclusion
Sit quietly and count slow breaths to five.

### Home connection
Count claps, hops or jumps with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count actions such as steps, claps and jumps to ten" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Number hop

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Number rhymes to 10

### Learning objectives
- Children will hop or jump along numerals 1 to 10 saying each number.

### Success criteria
- Jumps from number to number in order
- Says each number

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to 5 objects and recognise numerals 1 to 5.

**Key vocabulary:** six, seven, eight, nine, ten, number

**Instructional materials:** Large numerals 1 to 10 on the floor; A bell; A soft mat

### Starter
Put large numerals on the floor and say "Today we hop along the numbers."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to jump from one to two to three. Children: Children watch and copy.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children take turns to jump along the numerals. Children: Child jumps and says each number.

Step 3. Teacher: Ring the bell and call a number. Children: Child jumps to that number.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise each child for counting. Children: Children clap and cheer.

### Practical activity: Number hop
1. Stand at the start.
2. Hop to each numeral.
3. Say each number.

**Safety:** Use a soft non-slip mat and keep the numerals flat. Supervise each hop and let one child go at a time.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk along the numbers holding hands.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to hop in twos.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where do you hop first?
  **Expected response:** To number one.
- **Ask:** Where is ten?
  **Expected response:** At the end.

### Expected outcomes
- Jumps along numerals in order
- Says each number

### Conclusion
Pick up the numerals and say them aloud.

### Home connection
Chalk numbers on the ground and hop along them.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 counting to ten check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Saying numbers to 10

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how far each child counts in order.

### Success criteria
- Says numbers to 10 in order
- Counts actions to 10

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to 5 objects and recognise numerals 1 to 5.

**Key vocabulary:** six, seven, eight, nine, ten, number

**Instructional materials:** Number line; Drum; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the number rhyme and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: walk the line, clap count and numeral order. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask the child to count and watch. Children: Child counts and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and highest number counted. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Counting stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Keep the drum volume gentle.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and count to five.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to count to fifteen.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says numbers to 10 in order; counts actions; sings number rhymes; says what comes next.

- **Ask:** What comes after eight?
  **Expected response:** Nine.
- **Ask:** Count to ten.
  **Expected response:** The child counts.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Counts to ten with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep counting to ten in daily life.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how far each child counts in order" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
