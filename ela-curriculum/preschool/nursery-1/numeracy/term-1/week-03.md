# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 1, Week 3: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** One touch for each object; Counting in a line and a circle; How many are there?

## Learning objectives
- Children will touch each object once as they count to 10.
- Children will say the last number as how many.

**Key vocabulary:** how many, count, touch, all, line, group

**Content:** Touching each object once, counting in a line and a circle, and saying how many there are.

**Teaching activities:** Teach the one-to-one rule: touch, move and say; arrange objects in a line first, then a circle, then a scatter; always ask "How many are there?" after counting; use tricks such as moving counted items into a box.

**Learner activities:** Count up to 10 objects with one-to-one touch and say how many.

**Resources:** Counters; Egg boxes or ten frames; Pegs and a line; Stones, seeds and bottle tops; Baskets

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child counts up to 10 objects accurately and says how many.

## Expected outcomes
- Counts up to 10 objects with one-to-one touch.
- Says how many in a group.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: counting natural objects; Social Habits: sharing equally; Literacy: how many words

## Day 1 (Monday): Touch each one

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** One touch for each object

### Learning objectives
- Children will touch each object once while counting to ten.

### Success criteria
- Touches each object once
- Does not miss or repeat objects

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say numbers to 10 and count up to 5 objects.

**Key vocabulary:** how many, count, touch, all, line, group

**Instructional materials:** Ten counters in a line; A plate; A basket

### Starter
Show ten counters in a line and say "We touch each one once. We do not miss any."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Touch each counter and move it to a plate as you count. Children: Children count aloud with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Show what happens if you count one twice. Children: Children say oops and laugh.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child count a line of counters. Children: Child touches and counts.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How many?" after each count. Children: Child says the last number.

### Practical activity: Touch and move
1. Put counters in a line.
2. Touch and move each one.
3. Say how many.

**Safety:** Use counters too large to swallow. Keep children seated and supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count up to five with the child touching and moving.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count ten counters and then fifteen.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many counters?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.
- **Ask:** What do we do to each one?
  **Expected response:** Touch it once.

### Expected outcomes
- Touches each object once
- Counts up to ten objects

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say "Ten."

### Home connection
Count ten pegs or spoons together, touching each once.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Counting in a circle

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Counting in a line and a circle

### Learning objectives
- Children will count objects arranged in a circle.

### Success criteria
- Counts objects in a circle
- Starts and stops at the same place

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say numbers to 10 and count up to 5 objects.

**Key vocabulary:** how many, count, touch, all, line, group

**Instructional materials:** Ten counters in a circle; A coloured marker; A string circle

### Starter
Make a circle of counters and say "In a circle, where do we start? We need a marker."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put a coloured counter as a marker and count from there. Children: Children count with the teacher.

Step 2. Teacher: Show how to stop at the marker. Children: Children watch and say stop.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child count a circle of counters. Children: Child counts and stops at the marker.

Step 4. Teacher: Compare a circle count and a line count. Children: Child says the numbers are the same.

### Practical activity: Circle count
1. Put a marker counter.
2. Count round the circle.
3. Stop at the marker.

**Safety:** Use large counters and supervise counting. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count a circle of five with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count a circle of twelve.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where do we start?
  **Expected response:** At the marker.
- **Ask:** How many in the circle?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts in a circle
- Starts and stops at the marker

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say the numbers.

### Home connection
Count items arranged in a circle at home.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): How many are there?

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** How many are there?

### Learning objectives
- Children will count groups and say how many are there.

### Success criteria
- Says how many in a group
- Checks by recounting

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say numbers to 10 and count up to 5 objects.

**Key vocabulary:** how many, count, touch, all, line, group

**Instructional materials:** Baskets of mixed objects; Numeral cards; A sorting tray

### Starter
Show a basket of objects and say "How many are there? Let us find out."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count the objects together and say how many. Children: Children count aloud.

Step 2. Teacher: Put a numeral card by the group. Children: Child matches a card.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each child a basket to count. Children: Child counts the basket.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap baskets and count again. Children: Child counts a new basket.

### Practical activity: Count the basket
1. Take a basket.
2. Count the objects.
3. Put the matching numeral card.

**Safety:** Use only large objects and keep small items out of reach. Supervise baskets.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a basket of five or fewer objects.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a basket with more than ten.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many are there?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.
- **Ask:** How do you check?
  **Expected response:** Count again.

### Expected outcomes
- Says how many in a group
- Checks by recounting

### Conclusion
Return the baskets and say the numbers.

### Home connection
Count a group of objects and say how many.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Counting scattered things

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** One touch for each object

### Learning objectives
- Children will count scattered objects by moving them as they count.

### Success criteria
- Moves each object as counted
- Says how many

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say numbers to 10 and count up to 5 objects.

**Key vocabulary:** how many, count, touch, all, line, group

**Instructional materials:** Scattered stones or seeds; Two baskets; A tray

### Starter
Scatter stones on the mat and say "Now they are everywhere. How can we count them?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to move each stone into a basket as you count. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children count the scattered stones. Children: Child moves and counts each stone.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "How many?" and compare. Children: Child says the number.

Step 4. Teacher: Count again to check. Children: Child counts again.

### Practical activity: Scatter and count
1. Look at the scattered stones.
2. Move each one to a basket.
3. Say how many.

**Safety:** Use stones larger than a child’s fist and wash hands afterwards. Check for sharp edges first.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count five stones together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count two baskets and say the total.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many stones?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.
- **Ask:** How do you not lose count?
  **Expected response:** Move each stone.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts scattered objects
- Moves objects as they are counted

### Conclusion
Put the stones away and wash hands.

### Home connection
Scatter small safe objects and count them.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 counting objects check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** How many are there?

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how accurately each child counts objects.

### Success criteria
- Counts up to 10 objects accurately
- Says how many

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say numbers to 10 and count up to 5 objects.

**Key vocabulary:** how many, count, touch, all, line, group

**Instructional materials:** Counters; Baskets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the counting rhyme and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: line, circle and scatter. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How many?" and watch. Children: Child counts and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and highest accurate count. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Counting stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use large counters. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use groups up to five.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to count to twenty.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: touches each object once; counts in a line; counts in a circle; counts scattered objects; says how many.

- **Ask:** How many are there?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.
- **Ask:** How do you check?
  **Expected response:** Count again.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Counts objects with one-to-one touch

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep counting objects at home every day.
