# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 1, Week 1: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Counting 1 to 5 with objects; Counting rhymes; Numerals 1 to 5

## Learning objectives
- Children will count objects to 5 touching each one once.
- Children will recognise the numerals 1 to 5.

**Key vocabulary:** one, two, three, four, five, count

**Content:** Counting objects to 5, counting rhymes, and the numerals 1 to 5.

**Teaching activities:** Always count real objects that children can touch and move; model touching each object once and moving it aside; say the last number as how many; link numerals to quantities; use the first week to observe how far each child counts.

**Learner activities:** Count up to 5 objects touching each once and match numerals 1 to 5 to quantities.

**Resources:** Counters such as bottle tops, stones or seeds; Number cards 1 to 5; Counting rhyme pictures; Baskets and plates; Chalk or sand tray

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child counts up to 5 objects accurately and matches a numeral to a group.

## Expected outcomes
- Counts up to 5 objects accurately.
- Matches numerals 1 to 5 to quantities.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: number rhymes; Social Habits: sharing and turns; Science: counting natural objects

## Day 1 (Monday): Counting to five

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Counting 1 to 5 with objects

### Learning objectives
- Children will count up to five objects touching each once.

### Success criteria
- Touches each object once while counting
- Says the last number as how many

**Previous knowledge:** Children arrive able to count a little, usually to about five, and to use words such as big and small.

**Key vocabulary:** one, two, three, four, five, count

**Instructional materials:** Counters such as bottle tops; Five plates; A basket; Number cards 1 to 5

### Starter
Show five bottle tops in a line and say "Let us count them together. One, two, three, four, five."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count five bottle tops slowly, touching and moving each one. Children: Children count aloud with the teacher.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "How many?" and point to the last bottle top. Children: Children say five.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each child a small group of up to five counters. Children: Child counts their counters.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk around and ask each child "How many do you have?" Children: Child says the number.

### Practical activity: Count and move
1. Put counters in a line.
2. Touch each one and move it to a plate.
3. Say how many.

**Safety:** Use counters larger than a child’s fist so they cannot be swallowed and supervise closely. Do not let children put counters in their mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count one to three objects with the child, guiding the touching hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count out exactly five and then say one more.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many counters do you have?
  **Expected response:** The number of counters.
- **Ask:** What do we say last?
  **Expected response:** The last number is how many.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts up to five objects accurately
- Says how many in a group

### Conclusion
Hold up five fingers and say "Five. Well done."

### Home connection
Count five spoons or five stones with your child at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count up to five objects touching each once" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Counting rhymes to five

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Counting rhymes

### Learning objectives
- Children will sing counting rhymes and show the numbers with fingers.

### Success criteria
- Sings a counting rhyme with actions
- Shows the right number of fingers

**Previous knowledge:** Children arrive able to count a little, usually to about five, and to use words such as big and small.

**Key vocabulary:** one, two, three, four, five, count

**Instructional materials:** Rhyme pictures: five little monkeys, five fat sausages; Finger puppets; A rhyme chart

### Starter
Sing "One, two, three, four, five, once I caught a fish alive" with finger actions.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Teach the rhyme line by line with finger actions. Children: Children copy the lines and fingers.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing the rhyme again and count down from five. Children: Children hold up the right number of fingers.

Step 3. Teacher: Use finger puppets to act out a rhyme. Children: Child moves puppets and counts.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How many are left?" at each verse. Children: Child says the number.

### Practical activity: Rhyme and fingers
1. Sing the rhyme.
2. Show fingers for each number.
3. Count down together.

**Safety:** Keep the movement small and seated to avoid collisions. Make sure children have space around them.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sing slowly and show the fingers beside the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead a verse and say the numbers backwards.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many fingers on one hand?
  **Expected response:** Five.
- **Ask:** What comes after three?
  **Expected response:** Four.

### Expected outcomes
- Sings a counting rhyme with actions
- Shows numbers with fingers

### Conclusion
Sing the rhyme once more and wave.

### Home connection
Sing counting rhymes at bedtime and count fingers.

### Teacher reflection (private)

- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Numerals 1 to 5

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Numerals 1 to 5

### Learning objectives
- Children will recognise and name the numerals 1 to 5.

### Success criteria
- Names numerals 1 to 5
- Matches a numeral to a group

**Previous knowledge:** Children arrive able to count a little, usually to about five, and to use words such as big and small.

**Key vocabulary:** one, two, three, four, five, count

**Instructional materials:** Large numeral cards 1 to 5; Counters; A tray

### Starter
Hold up the numeral 3 and say "This is the numeral three. It tells us how many."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each numeral card and say its name. Children: Children say each numeral.

Step 2. Teacher: Place the matching number of counters under each card. Children: Children count the counters.

Step 3. Teacher: Mix the cards and ask children to find a numeral. Children: Child points to the numeral.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child match a card to a group. Children: Child places the card by a group.

### Practical activity: Match numeral and group
1. Look at a numeral card.
2. Count out that many counters.
3. Put the card by the group.

**Safety:** Use counters too large to swallow and supervise matching. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Match numerals 1 to 3 only with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put the cards in order from 1 to 5.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this numeral?
  **Expected response:** The numeral name.
- **Ask:** How many counters go here?
  **Expected response:** The same number.

### Expected outcomes
- Names numerals 1 to 5
- Matches numerals to groups

### Conclusion
Hold up each numeral and say it together.

### Home connection
Look for numerals 1 to 5 on doors, clocks and calendars.

## Day 4 (Thursday): Counting out

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Counting 1 to 5 with objects

### Learning objectives
- Children will count out a number of objects when asked.

### Success criteria
- Counts out the number asked for
- Checks by counting again

**Previous knowledge:** Children arrive able to count a little, usually to about five, and to use words such as big and small.

**Key vocabulary:** one, two, three, four, five, count

**Instructional materials:** Baskets of counters; Plates; Number cards 1 to 5

### Starter
Say "I will ask you for some counters. Can you give me three?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Ask a child to count out three counters. Children: Child counts out three counters.

Step 2. Teacher: Show a numeral card and ask for that many. Children: Child counts out the number.

Step 3. Teacher: Check by counting together. Children: Children count again to check.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children ask each other for a number. Children: Child asks a friend for a number.

### Practical activity: Give me counters
1. Take a card.
2. Count out that many counters.
3. Check by counting again.

**Safety:** Use large counters and supervise to avoid putting them in mouths. Keep the floor tidy.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count out two or three counters together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count out five and then take one away.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you give me four?
  **Expected response:** Counts out four.
- **Ask:** How do you check?
  **Expected response:** Count again.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts out a number of objects
- Checks by counting again

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say the numbers.

### Home connection
Ask your child for four spoons or five plates at mealtimes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count out a number of objects when asked" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 1 counting check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Counting 1 to 5 with objects

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how far each child counts and recognises numerals.

### Success criteria
- Counts up to 5 objects accurately
- Matches numerals 1 to 5 to groups

**Previous knowledge:** Children arrive able to count a little, usually to about five, and to use words such as big and small.

**Key vocabulary:** one, two, three, four, five, count

**Instructional materials:** Counters; Numeral cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the counting rhyme and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: counting out, numeral match and rhyme. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How many?" and watch. Children: Child counts and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and how far they count. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Counting stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use counters too large to swallow. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use only up to three.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to count to ten.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: counts to 5 with one-to-one touch; matches numerals 1 to 5; sings counting rhymes; says how many.

- **Ask:** How many are there?
  **Expected response:** The number.
- **Ask:** What numeral is this?
  **Expected response:** A numeral from 1 to 5.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Counts to 5 with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep counting small groups at home every day.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
