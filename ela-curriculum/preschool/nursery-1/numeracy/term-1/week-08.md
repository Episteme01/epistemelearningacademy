# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 1, Week 8: Length

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Long and short; Longer and shorter; Measuring with hands and steps

## Learning objectives
- Children will compare two objects by length.
- Children will measure with hand spans or steps.

**Key vocabulary:** long, short, longer, shorter, longest, measure

**Content:** Long and short, longer and shorter, and measuring with hands and steps.

**Teaching activities:** Compare lengths by lining objects up at the same starting point; use ropes, ribbons, pencils and children’s own bodies; introduce non-standard units such as hand spans and cubes; stress that we start at the same line and count the units without gaps.

**Learner activities:** Compare two objects by length and measure a short object with hands or cubes.

**Resources:** Ropes and ribbons of different lengths; Pencils; Cubes or paper clips; String; Pictures of long and short animals

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says which of two objects is longer and measures with cubes or hands.

## Expected outcomes
- Says which of two is longer.
- Measures a short object with hands or cubes.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: comparing things; Physical Development: stretching; Literacy: describing words

## Day 1 (Monday): Long and short

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Long and short

### Learning objectives
- Children will say long and short for different objects.

### Success criteria
- Says long or short correctly
- Finds a long and a short object

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare and order objects by size.

**Key vocabulary:** long, short, longer, shorter, longest, measure

**Instructional materials:** A long rope and a short rope; A long and a short pencil; Pictures of a snake and a worm

### Starter
Hold up a long rope and a short rope and say "This is long. This is short."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show pairs of objects and say long or short. Children: Children repeat the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which is long?" for each pair. Children: Children point to the long one.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children find long and short things in the room. Children: Child finds objects.

Step 4. Teacher: Stretch long and curl short with bodies. Children: Children stretch and curl.

### Practical activity: Long and short hunt
1. Look around the room.
2. Find a long thing.
3. Find a short thing.

**Safety:** Keep ropes short enough that they are not a strangling risk and supervise closely. Do not let children wrap ropes around necks.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two very different lengths first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find something as long as their arm.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the snake long or short?
  **Expected response:** Long.
- **Ask:** Is the worm long or short?
  **Expected response:** Short.

### Expected outcomes
- Says long and short correctly
- Finds long and short things

### Conclusion
Stretch long and curl short and wave.

### Home connection
Talk about long and short things you see.

- Which children could show "say long and short for different objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Longer and shorter

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Longer and shorter

### Learning objectives
- Children will compare two objects and say which is longer or shorter.

### Success criteria
- Says longer or shorter
- Lines up objects at the same start

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare and order objects by size.

**Key vocabulary:** long, short, longer, shorter, longest, measure

**Instructional materials:** Pairs of ropes and ribbons; Pencils; A line on the table

### Starter
Line up two ribbons at the same place and say "This one is longer. This one is shorter."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to line up two objects at the same start. Children: Children watch and say start.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which is longer?" Children: Child points to the longer one.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which is shorter?" Children: Child points to the shorter one.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children compare pairs of objects. Children: Child compares and says longer.

### Practical activity: Line up and compare
1. Put two objects at the same start.
2. Look at the ends.
3. Say which is longer.

**Safety:** Keep ribbons short and supervise closely. Never let children tie ropes round themselves or friends.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Compare objects that are very different in length.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find something longer than a pencil.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which is longer?
  **Expected response:** The longer object.
- **Ask:** Which is shorter?
  **Expected response:** The shorter object.

### Expected outcomes
- Compares two objects by length
- Lines up objects at the same start

### Conclusion
Put the ribbons away and say longer and shorter.

### Home connection
Compare two pencils or spoons at home.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Longest and shortest

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Longer and shorter

### Learning objectives
- Children will order three objects by length.

### Success criteria
- Puts three objects in order by length
- Says longest and shortest

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare and order objects by size.

**Key vocabulary:** long, short, longer, shorter, longest, measure

**Instructional materials:** Three ropes; Three pencils; A line on the floor

### Starter
Show three ropes and say "Which is the longest? Which is the shortest?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Line three ropes at the same start and order them. Children: Children watch and say longest.

Step 2. Teacher: Mix them up and ask a child to order them. Children: Child orders the ropes.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which is the shortest?" Children: Child points to the shortest.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child order a set of three. Children: Child orders objects.

### Practical activity: Order by length
1. Take three objects.
2. Line them up at the same start.
3. Say longest and shortest.

**Safety:** Use short pieces of soft rope and supervise closely. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Order two objects first and then add a third.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to order five objects.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which is the longest?
  **Expected response:** The longest object.
- **Ask:** Which is the shortest?
  **Expected response:** The shortest object.

### Expected outcomes
- Orders three objects by length
- Says longest and shortest

### Conclusion
Put the sets away and say longest and shortest.

### Home connection

## Day 4 (Thursday): Measuring with cubes and hands

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Measuring with hands and steps

### Learning objectives
- Children will measure an object with cubes or hand spans and say how many.

### Success criteria
- Measures with cubes or hands
- Says how many

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare and order objects by size.

**Key vocabulary:** long, short, longer, shorter, longest, measure

**Instructional materials:** Cubes or paper clips; Objects to measure such as a book or a table; Number cards

### Starter
Say "How long is our book? Let us measure it with cubes."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to line cubes along the book with no gaps. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Count the cubes together. Children: Children count the cubes.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children measure other objects. Children: Child measures and counts.

Step 4. Teacher: Compare: "Which is longer, the book or the shoe?" Children: Child compares the counts.

### Practical activity: Measure with cubes
1. Line cubes along the object.
2. Leave no gaps.
3. Count the cubes.

**Safety:** Use cubes too big to swallow and supervise closely. Keep the floor clear of cubes after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Measure with hand spans and count together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to measure with two different units.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many cubes long?
  **Expected response:** The number of cubes.
- **Ask:** Which is longer?
  **Expected response:** The one with more cubes.

### Expected outcomes
- Measures with cubes or hands
- Says how many

### Conclusion
Put the cubes away and count them.

### Home connection
Measure a spoon or a shoe with your hand.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 length check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Long and short

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child compares and measures lengths.

### Success criteria
- Says which is longer
- Measures with cubes or hands

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare and order objects by size.

**Key vocabulary:** long, short, longer, shorter, longest, measure

**Instructional materials:** Ropes; Cubes; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the long and short song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: compare, order and measure. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Which is longer?" and watch. Children: Child answers and measures.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Length stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use soft short ropes and large cubes. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and compare two objects.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to measure and compare three objects.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says long and short; says longer and shorter; orders three by length; measures with cubes or hands.

- **Ask:** Which is longer?
  **Expected response:** The longer object.
- **Ask:** How many cubes long?
  **Expected response:** The number of cubes.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Compares and measures length with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep comparing lengths at home.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
