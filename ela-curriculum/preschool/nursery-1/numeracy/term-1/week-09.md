# Nursery 1 Numeracy — Term 1, Week 9: Shape

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Circles and squares; Triangles; Shapes around us

## Learning objectives
- Children will name circle, square and triangle.
- Children will find these shapes in the classroom.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, round, corner, side

**Content:** Circles and squares, triangles, and finding shapes around us.

**Teaching activities:** Handle real 3D and flat shapes; talk about sides and corners; draw shapes in the air and sand; look at shapes in the environment such as plates, windows and roofs; accept shapes in different sizes and positions.

**Learner activities:** Name circle, square and triangle and find them in the classroom.

**Resources:** Flat wooden or card shapes; Plates, books and roof pictures; Sand tray; Straws and playdough; A feely bag

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names circle, square and triangle and finds them around the room.

## Expected outcomes
- Names circle, square and triangle.
- Finds each shape in the room.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Creative Development: shape pictures; Science: shapes in nature; Literacy: shape words

## Day 1 (Monday): Circles and squares

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Circles and squares

### Learning objectives
- Children will name circle and square and say how they are different.

### Success criteria
- Names circle and square
- Says a circle has no corners

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, compare size and length, and count.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, round, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** Card circles and squares; A plate and a book; A feely bag

### Starter
Hold up a plate and a book and say "The plate is a circle. The book is a square."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each shape and trace the edge with a finger. Children: Children trace the shape.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "A circle is round and has no corners." Children: Children touch the edge of a circle.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "A square has four sides and four corners." Children: Children count the corners.

Step 4. Teacher: Play feel and name in the feely bag. Children: Child feels a shape and names it.

### Practical activity: Feely bag shapes
1. Put a hand in the bag.
2. Feel a shape.
3. Say circle or square.

**Safety:** Use smooth card or wooden shapes with no sharp edges and keep them out of mouths. Supervise the bag game.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sort two shapes only and name each with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find shapes with exactly four corners.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this shape?
  **Expected response:** A circle or a square.
- **Ask:** How many corners has a square?
  **Expected response:** Four.

### Expected outcomes
- Names circle and square
- Says how they are different

### Conclusion
Put the shapes away and say circle and square.

### Home connection
Find circles and squares at home, such as plates and tiles.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Triangles

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Triangles

### Learning objectives
- Children will name a triangle and say it has three sides and three corners.

### Success criteria
- Names a triangle
- Counts three sides and three corners

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, compare size and length, and count.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, round, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** Card triangles; Straws; Playdough; Pictures of roofs and tents

### Starter
Show a roof picture and say "This roof is a triangle. It has three sides."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a triangle and count its three sides. Children: Children count the sides.

Step 2. Teacher: Count the three corners. Children: Children point to each corner.

Step 3. Teacher: Make a triangle with three straws and playdough. Children: Child builds a triangle.

Step 4. Teacher: Find triangles in the pictures. Children: Child points to a triangle.

### Practical activity: Straw triangles
1. Take three straws.
2. Join them with playdough.
3. Count sides and corners.

**Safety:** Use short soft straws and keep them away from mouths. Wash hands after using playdough.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Build the triangle together with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make triangles of different sizes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many sides has a triangle?
  **Expected response:** Three.
- **Ask:** Where do you see a triangle?
  **Expected response:** A roof or a tent.

### Expected outcomes
- Names a triangle
- Counts its sides and corners

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say triangle.

### Home connection
Look for triangles on signs, roofs and food.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Shapes around us

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Shapes around us

### Learning objectives
- Children will find circles, squares and triangles around the school.

### Success criteria
- Finds each shape in the environment
- Names the shape

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, compare size and length, and count.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, round, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** Shape cards; A clipboard; A basket

### Starter
Show three shape cards and say "Today we go on a shape hunt."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the three shape cards and say each name. Children: Children say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Walk around the room and look for the shapes. Children: Children walk and look.

Step 3. Teacher: Point to a shape and name it. Children: Child names a shape.

Step 4. Teacher: Tally the shapes found. Children: Children count the shapes.

### Practical activity: Shape hunt
1. Look around.
2. Find a circle, square or triangle.
3. Say its name.

**Safety:** Walk slowly with an adult and keep to safe areas. Check the area before the walk.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hunt for one shape only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a shape inside another shape.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is the clock?
  **Expected response:** A circle.
- **Ask:** What shape is the window?
  **Expected response:** A square or rectangle.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds shapes in the environment
- Names the shapes

### Conclusion
Sit together and say the shape names.

### Home connection
Hunt for shapes on a walk with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find circles, squares and triangles around the school" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Shape pictures

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Circles and squares

### Learning objectives
- Children will make a picture using circles, squares and triangles.

### Success criteria
- Makes a picture from shapes
- Names the shapes used

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, compare size and length, and count.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, round, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** Paper shapes; Glue sticks; Paper; A sample picture

### Starter
Show a house made of a square, a triangle and a circle and say "I made this from shapes."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the picture and name each shape. Children: Children name the shapes.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child choose shapes and arrange them. Children: Child arranges shapes.

Step 3. Teacher: Help children stick the shapes down. Children: Child sticks shapes on paper.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What did you make?" Children: Child says what they made.

### Practical activity: Shape house
1. Choose shapes.
2. Arrange them.
3. Stick them down.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic glue sticks and supervise so that no glue goes in mouths. Wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Arrange the shapes together with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a picture with ten shapes and count each kind.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you make?
  **Expected response:** A house or a person.
- **Ask:** Which shapes did you use?
  **Expected response:** Circle, square or triangle.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a picture from shapes
- Names the shapes used

### Conclusion
Hang the pictures and say well done.

### Home connection
Make a shape picture with paper at home.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 9 shapes check

**ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM-T1-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 25 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Shapes around us

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names and finds shapes.

### Success criteria
- Names circle, square and triangle
- Finds the shapes around the room

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort, compare size and length, and count.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, round, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** Shapes; Feely bag; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the shape song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: feely bag, shape hunt and shape pictures. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What shape is this?" Children: Child names the shape.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Shape stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use smooth shapes only. Keep glue and small items away from mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two shapes.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to describe the sides and corners.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names circle; names square; names triangle; finds shapes in the environment; says sides and corners.

- **Ask:** What shape is this?
  **Expected response:** Circle, square or triangle.
- **Ask:** How many sides has a triangle?
  **Expected response:** Three.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Names and finds shapes with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep spotting shapes at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child names and finds shapes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
