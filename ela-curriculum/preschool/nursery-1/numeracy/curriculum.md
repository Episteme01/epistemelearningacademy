# Nursery 1 Numeracy

**Programme ID:** ELA-PS-N1-NUM · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft (not yet approved by an ELA reviewer)

## Subject philosophy
Numeracy in Nursery 1 is learnt by handling, counting, comparing and talking about real things: stones, seeds, bottle tops, fruit, blocks and the children themselves. Every idea starts with objects, moves to pictures and songs, and only then to written numerals. ELA’s Nursery 1 Numeracy keeps lessons short, active and playful, and builds a firm, confident feel for numbers to 10 before children meet formal sums.

## Rationale
Children who arrive in Nursery 1 can usually count to about five. This programme takes them to counting 10 objects accurately, recognising and forming numerals 1 to 10, comparing sizes, lengths, weights and amounts, naming common shapes, making patterns and solving small real-life problems by joining and taking away. It uses everyday Nigerian contexts such as the market, naira coins, fruit, school bags and games so that mathematics makes sense to children.

## Aims
- To count objects to 10 accurately and say the number names in order.
- To recognise and begin to write the numerals 1 to 10.
- To compare and order objects by size, length, weight and capacity using everyday words.
- To sort, match, make patterns and name common shapes.
- To understand more, fewer, one more and one less, and to join and take away small groups.
- To talk about time, position, money and simple data in daily life.

## Learning outcomes by the end of the year
- Counts to 10 and counts up to 10 objects, touching each once.
- Recognises numerals 1 to 10 and matches them to quantities.
- Sorts and matches objects by one or two features such as colour and size.
- Compares size, length, weight and capacity with words such as bigger, longer, heavier and fuller.
- Names circles, squares, triangles, rectangles and ovals and finds them around the class.
- Says which group has more or fewer and finds one more and one less.
- Joins two small groups and takes away from a group with objects, up to 5.
- Continues and makes simple repeating patterns.
- Uses everyday words for time, position and money.

## Developmental expectations
- Children learn to count by moving objects as they say each number, not just by saying the number names.
- Counting a group of more than five accurately is a major milestone and needs plenty of practice.
- Children grasp "how many in all" after they can count each object once; do not rush.
- Writing numerals is hard for small hands; use sand, air tracing, large pencils and clay before paper.
- Concentration is short; each lesson has several short activities and plenty of movement.

## Key competencies
- Counting with one-to-one correspondence.
- Recognising and writing numerals.
- Comparing and ordering.
- Naming and sorting shapes.
- Solving simple practical problems.

## Curriculum strands
- **Number and counting** (NC): Counting, numerals, more and fewer to 10 and beyond.
- **Early operations** (OP): Joining, taking away and number stories with objects.
- **Shape and pattern** (SH): Shapes, sorting, matching and patterns.
- **Measures** (MS): Size, length, weight, capacity, time and money.
- **Position and data** (PS): Position words and simple picture graphs.

## Prior knowledge
Children are expected to count to about five with objects, recognise a few shapes and use words such as big and small. Observe in the first two weeks how far each child counts and record it as the baseline.

## Progression across the year
- **Term 1:** Term 1 builds counting and numerals to 10, sorting and matching, size and length comparisons, and the shapes circle, square and triangle.
- **Term 2:** Term 2 builds more and fewer, one more and one less, counting beyond 10, numeral order, making 5, joining and taking away, rectangles and ovals, and patterns.
- **Term 3:** Term 3 builds weight and capacity, time and ordinal numbers, tallying, picture graphs, money, position, and number stories.

## Assessment principles
- Assessment is by watching, listening and recording what children do and say with real objects. There are no written tests.
- Levels Emerging, Developing, Secure and Exceeding follow the lesson success criteria (see ASSESSMENT_FRAMEWORK.md).
- Check counting by asking a child to count out a number of objects, not only to recite numbers.
- Share with parents in plain language, always with a strength, a next step and a home idea.

## Inclusion and differentiation
- Every lesson has a support and an extension version.
- Use large, tactile objects for children who need them and let children count at their own pace.
- Use home-language number names alongside English and celebrate them.
- Offer movement, song and games for children who find sitting hard.

## Cross-curricular connections
- Literacy: number rhymes, stories and vocabulary.
- Science: sorting, measuring and comparing natural materials.
- Social Habits: turn-taking, sharing equally and working in pairs.
- EYFS mapping: Mathematics, with Communication and Language and Physical Development (see STANDARDS_ALIGNMENT.md).

## Parent connection
- Every lesson ends with a short home idea using safe household objects.
- Parents are invited to count with their child in daily life: steps, stairs, fruit and coins.
- Families are invited to share number words in their home language.
