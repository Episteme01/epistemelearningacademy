# Toddler Literacy

**Programme ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft (not yet approved by an ELA reviewer)

## Subject philosophy
Literacy begins with listening, talking, singing and sharing stories. Toddlers learn language by hearing it used warmly and often, by joining in rhymes and songs, by handling books and by making marks. ELA’s Toddler Literacy builds a strong oral foundation and early sound awareness, so that later reading and writing rest on secure listening, vocabulary and confidence. It does not rush letters or handwriting.

## Rationale
Children who arrive at formal phonics with rich vocabulary, sharp listening and a love of stories learn to read more easily. This programme gives toddlers daily experiences of environmental sounds, rhythm, rhyme, syllables and first sounds, together with picture books, Nigerian stories and songs, and plenty of large, playful mark-making. It follows ELA’s phonics sequence, where Toddler is the listening phase and the first four sounds are heard and said, not written.

## Aims
- To build listening skills and attention through sounds, songs and stories.
- To grow vocabulary and spoken sentences in English while valuing children’s home languages.
- To develop awareness of rhythm, rhyme, syllables and first sounds.
- To share books and stories with enjoyment, naming and talking about pictures.
- To strengthen the large and small movements that lead to writing, and to encourage confident mark-making.

## Learning outcomes by the end of the year
- Listens to a short story or song and responds with words, gestures or actions.
- Names familiar people, objects, foods and animals, and uses simple sentences of two to four words.
- Joins in action rhymes and songs and fills in missing words.
- Tells sounds apart: loud and quiet, high and low, animal and instrument sounds.
- Claps the syllables of a name and recognises words that rhyme with support.
- Hears and says the first sound of a few familiar words (s, a, t, p) in games.
- Handles a book correctly, turns pages and points to and names pictures.
- Makes large and small marks, patterns and lines with crayons, chalk, fingers and sand.
- Recognises own name card and the first letter of own name with support.

## Developmental expectations
- Spoken language develops at different speeds; understanding comes before speaking.
- Children learn through repetition, so favourite songs and stories are repeated many times.
- Listening for sounds is a skill that must be taught and practised in short, playful bursts.
- Pre-writing starts with the whole arm, then the hand, then the fingers. Pencil grip is not expected.
- Home languages are a strength. Teachers welcome them and link them to English words.

## Key competencies
- Attentive listening and following simple instructions.
- Expressive speech: words, phrases and short sentences.
- Joining in with songs, rhymes and stories.
- Sound awareness: rhythm, rhyme, syllables and first sounds.
- Early book and print awareness and large mark-making.

## Curriculum strands
- **Listening and sound awareness** (LS): Environmental sounds, voice sounds, loud and quiet, rhythm and body percussion.
- **Speaking and vocabulary** (SP): Names, greetings, naming objects, simple sentences, questions and answers.
- **Rhymes, songs and stories** (RS): Action rhymes, nursery rhymes, retelling stories, sequencing pictures.
- **Phonological awareness** (PA): Rhyme, syllables and first sounds (s, a, t, p) heard and said.
- **Books and print** (BP): Handling books, naming pictures, noticing print, name cards and first letters.
- **Mark-making and pre-writing** (WM): Finger play, big marks, patterns and lines, and making a first book.

## Prior knowledge
None assumed. Children may know songs from home. Observe in the first two weeks how each child listens, speaks and handles books, and record it as the baseline.

## Progression across the year
- **Term 1:** Term 1 builds listening, voice and vocabulary: sounds around us, loud and quiet, animal and instrument sounds, greetings and names, action rhymes, picture books and naming things.
- **Term 2:** Term 2 builds sound play and early print: rhyme, syllables, finger strength, big mark-making, vocabulary themes, songs, retelling and questions, and noticing print.
- **Term 3:** Term 3 introduces the first sounds s, a, t and p as listening and speaking games, develops pre-writing patterns, two-step instructions, story sequencing, own name and a first handmade book.

## Assessment principles
- Assessment is by watching, listening and recording what children say and do during play and stories. There are no written tests.
- Levels Emerging, Developing, Secure and Exceeding follow the lesson success criteria (see ASSESSMENT_FRAMEWORK.md).
- Record exact words children use now and then, to show growth in language over the year.
- Share with parents in plain language, always with a strength, a next step and a home idea.

## Inclusion and differentiation
- Every lesson has a support and an extension version.
- Use gesture, pictures, objects and songs together so children with different strengths can take part.
- Accept home-language words and repeat them in English in a natural way.
- For children who speak little, value pointing, sounds and actions and give extra time.

## Cross-curricular connections
- Numeracy: counting rhymes, patterns and sorting books by size.
- Science: listening walks, animals, my body and the senses.
- Social Habits: greetings, turn-taking, listening manners and sharing books.
- EYFS mapping: Communication and Language and Literacy, with Expressive Arts and Design and Physical Development (see STANDARDS_ALIGNMENT.md).

## Parent connection
- Every lesson ends with a short home idea using talk, songs, books or objects at home.
- Parents are encouraged to talk, sing and read together every day, in any language they use at home.
- Families are invited to lend a favourite song, story or rhyme from their own language and culture.
