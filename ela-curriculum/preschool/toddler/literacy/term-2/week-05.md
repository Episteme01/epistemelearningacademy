# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 5: Vocabulary

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W05 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Fruits and vegetables; Market people and things; Asking for things politely

## Learning objectives
- Children will name fruits, vegetables and market things.
- Children will ask for something using a short phrase.

**Key vocabulary:** orange, mango, tomato, yam, market, seller

**Content:** Naming fruits and vegetables, market people and things, and asking for things politely.

**Teaching activities:** Use real or toy foods from the local market; model "Good morning ma, I want two oranges, please"; give children time to say the word and accept gestures; build the market corner over the week.

**Learner activities:** Name fruits, vegetables and market items and ask for something with a short phrase.

**Resources:** Real or toy oranges, mangoes, tomatoes, yams and onions; Baskets; A market stall table; Toy coins; An apron

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names six fruits or vegetables and asks for an item with a short phrase.

## Expected outcomes
- Names at least six fruits or vegetables.
- Uses a short phrase to ask for an item.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: community and market; Mathematics: counting and money; Social Habits: polite words

## Day 1 (Monday): Fruits we like

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Fruits and vegetables

### Learning objectives
- Children will name three fruits and say which one they like.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to an orange, a mango and a banana
- Says which fruit they like

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar foods and objects and use two words to ask for something.

**Key vocabulary:** orange, mango, tomato, yam, market, seller

**Instructional materials:** Real or toy orange, mango and banana; A basket; A tray

### Starter
Show a ripe mango and say "This is a mango. Mmm." Ask "What do you like?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold up each fruit and say its name. Pass it round to touch. Children: Children touch and say the name.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Where is the orange?" and wait. Children: Child points to the orange.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which fruit do you like?" Children: Child points to a fruit or says its name.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a fruit and say its name. Children: Child holds up the fruit and names it.

### Practical activity: Fruit basket
1. Put three fruits in a basket.
2. Each child takes one and names it.
3. Say each name together.

**Safety:** If real fruit is used, wash it, check for allergies and keep it for looking and touching unless eating is planned. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two fruits and say the name with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend what colour the fruit is.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this fruit?
  **Expected response:** Orange, mango or banana.
- **Ask:** What do you like?
  **Expected response:** A fruit name.

### Expected outcomes
- Names three fruits
- Says which fruit they like

### Conclusion
Put the fruits back and say "Thank you, fruits."

### Home connection
Name the fruits at home and let your child choose one.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name three fruits and say which one they like" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Vegetables and yams

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Fruits and vegetables

### Learning objectives
- Children will name three vegetables.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to a tomato, an onion and a yam
- Says a word for a vegetable

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar foods and objects and use two words to ask for something.

**Key vocabulary:** orange, mango, tomato, yam, market, seller

**Instructional materials:** Real or toy tomatoes, onions and yams; A basket; A cloth

### Starter
Hold up a tomato and say "Red tomato." Ask "What colour is it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each vegetable, name it and let children touch it. Children: Children touch and name.

Step 2. Teacher: Hide one under the cloth and ask "What is it?" Children: Child feels or guesses the name.

Step 3. Teacher: Sort the fruits and vegetables into two baskets. Children: Child puts each in a basket.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What do we cook with?" and show the pictures. Children: Child points to a vegetable.

### Practical activity: Fruit and vegetable sort
1. Put out two baskets.
2. Each child picks an item and names it.
3. Put it in the fruit or vegetable basket.

**Safety:** Wash real vegetables first and supervise. Keep onions away from eyes and wash hands after touching.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two items and say the names for the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name a vegetable they eat at home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Tomato, onion or yam.
- **Ask:** What colour is the tomato?
  **Expected response:** Red.

### Expected outcomes
- Names three vegetables
- Sorts fruit and vegetables with support

### Conclusion
Put away the vegetables and wash hands.

### Home connection
Name the vegetables when you cook or shop together.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name three vegetables" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): At the market

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Market people and things

### Learning objectives
- Children will name market people and things.

### Success criteria
- Names a seller and a buyer
- Names two market items

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar foods and objects and use two words to ask for something.

**Key vocabulary:** orange, mango, tomato, yam, market, seller

**Instructional materials:** A market stall table; Baskets of toy foods; An apron; Toy coins

### Starter
Put on the apron and say "I am the seller. Come to my market!" Hold up a basket.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the stall and name each thing: seller, buyer, basket, coins. Children: Children point and say each word.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Good morning, ma" and show a greeting to the seller. Children: Children say good morning.

Step 3. Teacher: Show how a buyer points to what they want. Children: Child points to an item.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child be the buyer and take an item. Children: Child names the item.

### Practical activity: Market corner
1. Set up the stall with baskets of toy foods.
2. Children take turns as the seller and buyer.
3. Say the names of items together.

**Safety:** Use toy foods only and keep coins large. Supervise turn-taking and keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Be the seller and help the child to point and name.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the seller and greet a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who sells?
  **Expected response:** The seller.
- **Ask:** What do we buy?
  **Expected response:** Food, such as oranges.

### Expected outcomes
- Names market people and things
- Greets the seller

### Conclusion
Close the market and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Take your child to the market and name the things and the people you see.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name market people and things" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Asking politely

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Asking for things politely

### Learning objectives
- Children will ask for an item using a short phrase.

### Success criteria
- Says I want and the item name with please
- Says thank you

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar foods and objects and use two words to ask for something.

**Key vocabulary:** orange, mango, tomato, yam, market, seller

**Instructional materials:** Market stall; Toy foods; Toy coins; An apron

### Starter
Say "Good morning, ma. Two oranges, please." Hand over a toy coin and say "Thank you."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Model the phrases "I want an orange, please" and "Thank you". Children: Children repeat.

Step 2. Teacher: Play the buyer and let a child play the seller. Children: Child serves and says "here you are".

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child be the buyer and ask for an item. Children: Child asks with a short phrase.

Step 4. Teacher: Change roles and play again. Children: Child asks or serves.

### Practical activity: Market role play
1. Set up the stall with toy foods.
2. Take turns as buyer and seller.
3. The buyer asks politely and says thank you.

**Safety:** Use toy foods and large coins only. Supervise each pair and keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say the phrase with the child and let them choose the item.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to ask for two different items.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you say when you ask?
  **Expected response:** Please.
- **Ask:** What do you say after you get it?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Asks for an item with a short phrase
- Uses thank you

### Conclusion
Close the market and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Let your child ask for what they want in a shop or at the market using please.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "ask for an item using a short phrase" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 market check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Asking for things politely

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names market items and asks politely.

### Success criteria
- Names at least six fruits or vegetables
- Asks for an item with a short phrase

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar foods and objects and use two words to ask for something.

**Key vocabulary:** orange, mango, tomato, yam, market, seller

**Instructional materials:** Market stall; Toy foods; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing "Market, market, come and see" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: fruit, vegetables and market role play. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is this?" and "What do you want?" Children: Child names the item and asks.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Market stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use toy foods and large coins. Supervise each station and keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and accept gestures.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to run the stall.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names fruits and vegetables; names market people; asks politely with a short phrase.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** The name of the item.
- **Ask:** What do you want?
  **Expected response:** A short phrase with please.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Names market items and asks politely

### Conclusion
Close the market and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Practise asking politely in a shop or at home.
