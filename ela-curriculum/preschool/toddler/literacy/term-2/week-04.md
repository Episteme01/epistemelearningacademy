# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 4: Mark-making

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W04 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Marks in sand and flour; Chalk and water on the ground; Crayons on big paper

## Learning objectives
- Children will make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons.
- Children will say what their marks show.

**Key vocabulary:** mark, draw, chalk, crayon, sand, paper

**Content:** Making marks in sand and flour, with chalk and water on the ground, and with crayons on big paper.

**Teaching activities:** Let children move freely with big arm movements; never correct how they hold a tool; ask "Tell me about your marks" and write down what they say; use large surfaces.

**Learner activities:** Make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons and say what their marks show.

**Resources:** A tray of sand or flour; Chalk; A bucket of water and a brush; Large paper; Fat crayons; An apron or old shirt

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child makes marks with a tool and says a word about their marks.

## Expected outcomes
- Makes marks with a tool.
- Says a word about their marks.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Creative Development: art; Physical Development: arm movement; Understanding the World: materials

## Day 1 (Monday): Marks in sand

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Marks in sand and flour

### Learning objectives
- Children will make marks in sand or flour with their fingers.

### Success criteria
- Draws a line or a dot with a finger
- Says a word about the mark

**Previous knowledge:** Children can squeeze, roll and thread and enjoy exploring materials with their hands.

**Key vocabulary:** mark, draw, chalk, crayon, sand, paper

**Instructional materials:** A tray of dry sand or flour; A smoothing stick; A towel

### Starter
Draw a long line in the sand with your finger and say "I made a line. Can you make a mark?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Smooth the sand flat and draw a line, a dot and a circle with your finger. Children: Children watch and look at the marks.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Now you make a mark." Give each child a turn. Children: Child makes a mark with a finger.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What is your mark?" and wait. Children: Child says a word or shows a gesture.

Step 4. Teacher: Smooth the sand again and let the children make more marks. Children: Child makes a new mark.

### Practical activity: Sand marks
1. Put a tray of sand on a table or mat.
2. Each child makes marks with a finger.
3. Smooth the sand and start again.

**Safety:** Keep sand away from eyes and mouths and wash hands after play. Use fine, clean sand and sweep up spills.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move the child’s finger along the sand together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a line and a circle and tell a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What mark did you make?
  **Expected response:** Line, dot or circle.
- **Ask:** Can you make a long line?
  **Expected response:** Child draws a long line.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes marks in sand with a finger
- Says a word about the mark

### Conclusion
Smooth the sand flat and wash hands.

### Home connection
Draw lines in sand or flour with your child at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make marks in sand or flour with their fingers" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Chalk and water

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Chalk and water on the ground

### Learning objectives
- Children will make big marks with chalk and water on the ground.

### Success criteria
- Uses chalk or a wet brush to make marks
- Uses big arm movements

**Previous knowledge:** Children can squeeze, roll and thread and enjoy exploring materials with their hands.

**Key vocabulary:** mark, draw, chalk, crayon, sand, paper

**Instructional materials:** Chalk; A bucket of water and a large brush; A paved outdoor area

### Starter
Dip the brush in water and paint a line on the ground. Say "Look, a wet line. It will dry in the sun."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Make a big line with the brush. Draw a big circle with chalk. Children: Children watch and look at the marks.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a brush and let them paint on the ground. Children: Child paints a line or a shape.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each child chalk and let them draw big marks. Children: Child draws marks with chalk.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What did you make?" Children: Child says a word or points.

### Practical activity: Ground painting
1. Go to a safe paved area outside.
2. Paint with water and draw with chalk.
3. Watch the water marks dry in the sun.

**Safety:** Stay away from the road and check the ground for sharp objects. Use hats in strong sun and wash hands after chalk.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the brush together and move across the ground.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to draw a long line from one end to the other.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What are you making?
  **Expected response:** A line, a circle or a word.
- **Ask:** Where did the water go?
  **Expected response:** It dried in the sun.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes big marks with chalk or water
- Says a word about their marks

### Conclusion
Look at the marks together and wave goodbye to them.

### Home connection
Draw on a path with chalk or paint with water at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make big marks with chalk and water on the ground" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- CHALK
- WATER

**Picture suggestions:**
- Chalk marks
- A wet line

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Crayons on big paper

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Crayons on big paper

### Learning objectives
- Children will make marks with fat crayons on big paper.

### Success criteria
- Holds a crayon
- Makes marks on paper

**Previous knowledge:** Children can squeeze, roll and thread and enjoy exploring materials with their hands.

**Key vocabulary:** mark, draw, chalk, crayon, sand, paper

**Instructional materials:** Fat crayons; A long sheet of paper taped to the floor; Tape

### Starter
Tape a long sheet of paper on the floor and make a big scribble with a crayon. Say "I made marks. You try."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to hold a fat crayon in your fist. Make marks on the paper. Children: Children watch and hold their crayons.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child kneel at the paper and make marks. Children: Child makes marks with arm movements.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Tell me about your marks." Children: Child says a word or shows a gesture.

Step 4. Teacher: Write the child’s word beside their marks. Children: Child watches and says the word.

### Practical activity: Big paper marks
1. Tape a long sheet of paper to the floor.
2. Children kneel and make marks with fat crayons.
3. Talk about the marks together.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic crayons and keep crayons out of mouths. Tape the paper down to prevent slipping.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the crayon together and move it across the paper.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to draw a line and a circle on the paper.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you draw?
  **Expected response:** A line, a circle or a name for the picture.
- **Ask:** What colour is your crayon?
  **Expected response:** Child names or points to the colour.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes marks with a crayon
- Says what the marks show

### Conclusion
Put the crayons back in the tin and look at the paper.

### Home connection
Give your child a big sheet of paper and a fat crayon at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make marks with fat crayons on big paper" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- CRAYON
- PAPER

**Picture suggestions:**
- A crayon
- A big paper with marks

## Day 4 (Thursday): Tell me about my marks

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Crayons on big paper

### Learning objectives
- Children will talk about their marks and say what they show.

### Success criteria
- Says a word about their marks
- Points to a part of the picture

**Previous knowledge:** Children can squeeze, roll and thread and enjoy exploring materials with their hands.

**Key vocabulary:** mark, draw, chalk, crayon, sand, paper

**Instructional materials:** Marks made earlier in the week; Paper and crayons; A basket

### Starter
Show a child’s paper from earlier and say "Look at these marks. Tell me about them."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sit with a child and look at their marks. Ask "What did you make?" Children: Child says a word or points.

Step 2. Teacher: Repeat the child’s words and write them beside the marks. Children: Child watches and nods.

Step 3. Teacher: Let the child make another picture and tell you about it. Children: Child draws and tells you.

Step 4. Teacher: Share a picture with the group. Children: Child shows and says a word.

### Practical activity: Mark talk
1. Give each child paper and a crayon.
2. Make a picture and tell a friend about it.
3. Write a word beside each picture.

**Safety:** Keep crayons out of mouths and supervise sharing. Use non-toxic materials only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the marks and offer words for the child to choose.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell you a short sentence about the picture.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** The child’s name for the picture.
- **Ask:** What colour is it?
  **Expected response:** A colour name.

### Expected outcomes
- Says what marks show
- Shows a picture to a friend

### Conclusion
Put the pictures on the wall and say "Well done."

### Home connection
Ask your child to tell you about their drawings and write what they say.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "talk about their marks and say what they show" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 4 marks check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Marks in sand and flour

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child makes marks and talks about them.

### Success criteria
- Makes marks with a tool
- Says a word about their marks

**Previous knowledge:** Children can squeeze, roll and thread and enjoy exploring materials with their hands.

**Key vocabulary:** mark, draw, chalk, crayon, sand, paper

**Instructional materials:** Sand tray; Chalk and water; Crayons and paper; Class record sheet

### Starter
Show three mark-making stations and say "Today we make marks."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: sand, chalk and crayons. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: Watch each child make marks and ask "What did you make?" Children: Child says a word.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Mark stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise sand and water carefully. Use non-toxic chalk and crayons and wash hands.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and offer two tools.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to draw a pattern.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: makes marks with a tool; uses big arm movements; says a word about their marks.

- **Ask:** What did you make?
  **Expected response:** A word or gesture.
- **Ask:** Can you make a line?
  **Expected response:** Child makes a line.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Makes marks with different tools

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Make marks in sand, flour or on paper at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child makes marks and talks about them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
