# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 8: Speaking

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Who is it?; What is it?; Where is it?

## Learning objectives
- Children will answer simple who, what and where questions.
- Children will ask a simple question such as "What is that?"

**Key vocabulary:** who, what, where, ask, answer, yes, no

**Content:** Answering who, what and where questions and asking a simple question such as "What is that?"

**Teaching activities:** Ask one question at a time and wait at least five seconds; use real objects and people; accept a word, a point or a gesture; model the question "What is that?" often.

**Learner activities:** Answer who, what and where questions with a word or phrase and ask one simple question.

**Resources:** Family and classroom photographs; A box with toys inside; A teddy bear; A hiding place such as a box or a cloth

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child answers who, what and where questions and asks at least one question.

## Expected outcomes
- Answers who, what and where questions with a word or phrase.
- Asks one question.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: people and places; Social Habits: listening and answering; Mathematics: position words

## Day 1 (Monday): Who is it?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Who is it?

### Learning objectives
- Children will answer the question "Who is it?" about a person or a toy.

### Success criteria
- Points to or names a person when asked who
- Listens to the question

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name people, objects and animals and use short phrases to ask for things.

**Key vocabulary:** who, what, where, ask, answer, yes, no

**Instructional materials:** Photographs of teachers and children; A teddy bear; A mirror

### Starter
Hold up a photograph of the teacher and ask "Who is it?" Say "It is Teacher."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a photograph and ask "Who is it?" and answer yourself the first time. Children: Children look and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Show a second photograph and wait for the child to answer. Children: Child says the name or points.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Who is that?" about a friend in the circle. Children: Child names or points to the friend.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children ask "Who is it?" about a photograph. Children: Child asks and the group answers.

### Practical activity: Who is it?
1. Show a set of photographs.
2. Ask "Who is it?" and let children answer.
3. Let each child hold a photograph and ask a friend.

**Safety:** Handle photographs gently and return them. Be sensitive and do not use photographs of children whose families have not agreed.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two photographs and say the answer with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a friend and ask "Who is it?"

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is it?
  **Expected response:** The name of the person in the photograph.
- **Ask:** Who is your friend?
  **Expected response:** A friend’s name.

### Expected outcomes
- Answers who questions with a word or point
- Asks "Who is it?" with support

### Conclusion
Put the photographs away and say goodbye to each person.

### Home connection
Look at family photographs and ask "Who is it?" for each person.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "answer the question "Who is it?" about a person or a toy" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- WHO?

**Picture suggestions:**
- A photograph of a teacher
- A photograph of a friend

## Day 2 (Tuesday): What is it?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** What is it?

### Learning objectives
- Children will answer "What is it?" and ask "What is that?"

### Success criteria
- Names an object when asked what
- Asks "What is that?" with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name people, objects and animals and use short phrases to ask for things.

**Key vocabulary:** who, what, where, ask, answer, yes, no

**Instructional materials:** A mystery box with a ball, a cup, a spoon and a toy car; A cloth

### Starter
Shake the mystery box and say "What is in the box? What is it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Take out one object and ask "What is it?" Wait for the answer. Children: Child names the object.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the child how to ask "What is that?" by pointing to the box. Children: Child asks or points.

Step 3. Teacher: Cover an object with the cloth and ask "What is it?" Children: Child guesses or names it.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child take an object from the box and ask the group. Children: Child asks "What is it?"

### Practical activity: Mystery box
1. Put four objects in a box.
2. Each child takes one out and says what it is.
3. Ask a friend "What is that?"

**Safety:** Use large safe objects only. Keep the objects clean and supervise handling.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects and say the word with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put an object in the box and ask a friend to guess.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is it?
  **Expected response:** The name of the object.
- **Ask:** What is that?
  **Expected response:** The name of what is pointed to.

### Expected outcomes
- Answers what questions
- Asks "What is that?" with support

### Conclusion
Put all objects back in the box and say "Goodbye."

### Home connection
Ask your child "What is that?" when you walk together and wait for an answer.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "answer "What is it?" and ask "What is that?"" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- WHAT?

**Picture suggestions:**
- A mystery box
- A ball and a cup

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Where is it?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Where is it?

### Learning objectives
- Children will answer "Where is it?" by pointing or using a word such as in or on.

### Success criteria
- Points to where an object is
- Says in, on or here with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name people, objects and animals and use short phrases to ask for things.

**Key vocabulary:** who, what, where, ask, answer, yes, no

**Instructional materials:** A teddy bear; A box; A chair; A cloth

### Starter
Hide the teddy bear in the box and ask "Where is teddy? Where is he?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Ask "Where is teddy?" and wait for children to look and point. Children: Children look and point to the box.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Teddy is in the box" and show the word in. Children: Children say "in".

Step 3. Teacher: Put teddy on the chair and ask "Where is teddy now?" Children: Child points or says "on".

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child hide teddy and ask "Where is teddy?" Children: Child hides and friends answer.

### Practical activity: Hide teddy
1. Hide teddy in the room.
2. Ask "Where is teddy?"
3. Children look and point to where he is.

**Safety:** Hide teddy in easy places away from hazards. Keep children together and supervise looking.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point and say "teddy is in the box" with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to hide teddy and tell where he is.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is teddy?
  **Expected response:** In the box or on the chair.
- **Ask:** Is he in the box?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no.

### Expected outcomes
- Answers where questions
- Uses in or on with support

### Conclusion
Put teddy to bed and say goodnight.

### Home connection
Hide a toy at home and ask "Where is it?"

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "answer "Where is it?" by pointing or using a word such as in or on" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- WHERE?
- IN the box
- ON the chair

**Picture suggestions:**
- A teddy in a box
- A teddy on a chair

## Day 4 (Thursday): Who, what, where game

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Who is it?

### Learning objectives
- Children will answer who, what and where questions in a game.

### Success criteria
- Answers a who, what or where question
- Listens to a friend

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name people, objects and animals and use short phrases to ask for things.

**Key vocabulary:** who, what, where, ask, answer, yes, no

**Instructional materials:** Three question cards: who, what, where; Photographs and toys; A basket

### Starter
Show the three question cards and say "Who? What? Where?" Ask the children to repeat.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pick a card and ask the matching question with a photograph or toy. Children: Children answer with a word or gesture.

Step 2. Teacher: Pick another card and ask again. Children: Children answer.

Step 3. Teacher: Let a child pick a card and ask a friend. Children: Child asks and the friend answers.

Step 4. Teacher: Play the game in pairs. Children: Children ask and answer.

### Practical activity: Question game
1. Put three cards in a basket.
2. A child picks a card and asks the question.
3. The friend answers with a point or word.

**Safety:** Supervise pairs and keep voices gentle. Help children who are shy by accepting gestures.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Ask the question with the child and give a picture clue.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to ask a friend two different questions.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is that?
  **Expected response:** A person’s name.
- **Ask:** Where is the ball?
  **Expected response:** Child points or says where.

### Expected outcomes
- Answers who, what and where questions
- Asks a question with support

### Conclusion
Put the cards back and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Ask your child who, what and where questions at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "answer who, what and where questions in a game" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- WHO
- WHAT
- WHERE

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three question cards

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 questions check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** What is it?

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child answers and asks questions.

### Success criteria
- Answers who, what and where questions
- Asks one question

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name people, objects and animals and use short phrases to ask for things.

**Key vocabulary:** who, what, where, ask, answer, yes, no

**Instructional materials:** Photographs; Mystery box; Teddy bear; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "Who? What? Where?" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: who, what and where. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask the matching question and wait. Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Question stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use only safe objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use real objects.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to ask a friend a question.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: answers who questions; answers what questions; answers where questions; asks one question.

- **Ask:** Who is this?
  **Expected response:** A person’s name.
- **Ask:** Where is teddy?
  **Expected response:** In or on something.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Answers simple questions

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to teddy and the box.

### Home connection
Ask questions at home and wait for your child to answer.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child answers and asks questions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Questions

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
