# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 2: Syllables

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Clapping my name; Clapping food and animal names; Syllable sorting

## Learning objectives
- Children will clap the beats in their name.
- Children will clap the beats in simple words.

**Key vocabulary:** clap, beat, name, one beat, two beats, three beats

**Content:** Clapping the beats in names, in food and animal names, and sorting words by one, two or three beats.

**Teaching activities:** Say each word in a steady rhythm and clap once for each beat; start with children’s own names; use two hands or a drum; never ask children to count beats yet, only to clap them.

**Learner activities:** Clap the beats in their name and in simple words and match a word to one, two or three claps with support.

**Resources:** Name cards; A drum; Picture cards of a cat, a banana, an elephant and a mango; Three baskets

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child claps the beats in their own name and matches a word to a number of claps with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Claps the beats in their own name.
- Matches a word to the number of claps with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Music: rhythm and beat; Mathematics: counting claps; Social Habits: listening to names

## Day 1 (Monday): Clap my name

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Clapping my name

### Learning objectives
- Children will clap the beats in their own name.

### Success criteria
- Claps once for each beat of their name
- Listens as friends clap names

**Previous knowledge:** Children can copy a short beat and notice when words sound the same at the end.

**Key vocabulary:** clap, beat, name, one beat, two beats, three beats

**Instructional materials:** Name cards; A drum; A mat

### Starter
Say a child’s name slowly and clap each beat, for example "A-de-sua" with three claps. Ask "Can we clap your name?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say your own name and clap each beat. Say "Clap with me". Children: Children clap with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Say one child’s name slowly and clap each beat with the group. Children: Children clap the beats of the name together.

Step 3. Teacher: Go round the circle and clap each child’s name. Children: Child claps their own name with support.

Step 4. Teacher: Play the drum for each name instead of clapping. Children: Child taps the drum for the beats.

### Practical activity: Name claps
1. Sit in a circle.
2. Say a name and clap its beats together.
3. Go round until every child has a turn.

**Safety:** Keep clapping gentle so it does not hurt hands. Include every child’s name and say each correctly.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Clap the child’s hands for them and say each beat of the name.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to clap a friend’s name.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you clap your name?
  **Expected response:** Child claps the beats.
- **Ask:** How many claps?
  **Expected response:** Child shows claps; counting is not required.

### Expected outcomes
- Claps the beats in their own name with support
- Listens as friends clap names

### Conclusion
Clap everyone’s name together in one big clap.

### Home connection
Clap the names of family members at home and say the name as you clap.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "clap the beats in their own name" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MY NAME
- Clap, clap, clap

**Picture suggestions:**
- Hands clapping
- Name cards

**Song or rhyme:** Clap your name, clap your name, clap, clap, clap.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Clap food and animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Clapping food and animal names

### Learning objectives
- Children will clap the beats in the names of foods and animals.

### Success criteria
- Claps the beats of a food or animal name
- Says the word as they clap

**Previous knowledge:** Children can copy a short beat and notice when words sound the same at the end.

**Key vocabulary:** clap, beat, name, one beat, two beats, three beats

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards: cat, banana, mango, elephant; A drum

### Starter
Show the cat card, say "cat" and clap once. Show the banana card, say "ba-na-na" and clap three times.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a picture, say the word slowly and clap each beat. Children: Children say and clap each beat.

Step 2. Teacher: Show another picture and wait for children to clap before you do. Children: Children clap the beats on their own.

Step 3. Teacher: Play the drum for each word. Children: Children tap or clap along.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a card and lead the clapping. Children: Child chooses and the group claps.

### Practical activity: Word claps
1. Place four picture cards face up.
2. Each child picks one and says the word.
3. The group claps the beats and says the word again.

**Safety:** Use large cards and keep clapping gentle. Make sure every child has a turn.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Clap the child’s hands for each beat.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a word with three claps.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you clap banana?
  **Expected response:** Child claps three times.
- **Ask:** Can you clap cat?
  **Expected response:** Child claps once.

### Expected outcomes
- Claps beats in simple words
- Says the word as they clap

### Conclusion
Clap a favourite word together and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Clap the names of foods at mealtimes, such as "rice" and "ba-na-na".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "clap the beats in the names of foods and animals" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- CAT (1)
- MANGO (2)
- BANANA (3)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A cat

- A banana

## Day 3 (Wednesday): One, two or three claps

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Syllable sorting

### Learning objectives
- Children will match a word to one, two or three claps.

### Success criteria
- Points to the right clap card
- Claps the word before choosing

**Previous knowledge:** Children can copy a short beat and notice when words sound the same at the end.

**Key vocabulary:** clap, beat, name, one beat, two beats, three beats

**Instructional materials:** Three baskets labelled one, two and three claps; Picture cards; A drum

### Starter
Show three baskets with one, two and three hands on them. Say "Some words have one clap, some have two, some have three."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the cat card, clap once and put it in the one-clap basket. Children: Children clap once and watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the mango card, clap twice and put it in the two-clap basket. Children: Children clap twice.

Step 3. Teacher: Show the banana card, clap three times and put it in the three-clap basket. Children: Children clap three times.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick a card, clap and choose a basket. Children: Child claps and places the card.

### Practical activity: Clap sort
1. Put out three baskets.
2. Each child picks a card and claps the word.
3. Place the card in the matching basket.

**Safety:** Use large cards and keep baskets stable. Supervise turn-taking and help any child who is unsure.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Clap together and ask the child to point to the basket.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose a new word and sort it.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many claps in mango?
  **Expected response:** Two.
- **Ask:** Which basket does banana go in?
  **Expected response:** The three-clap basket.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts words by number of claps with support
- Claps before sorting

### Conclusion
Put the cards back in order and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Clap the beats in names of things at home and say "one", "two" or "three".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match a word to one, two or three claps" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 clap
- 2 claps
- 3 claps

**Picture suggestions:**
- One hand
- Two hands
- Three hands

## Day 4 (Thursday): Clap and move

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Clapping my name

### Learning objectives
- Children will clap and step the beats of names and words.

### Success criteria
- Steps or taps once for each beat
- Stays with the group beat

**Previous knowledge:** Children can copy a short beat and notice when words sound the same at the end.

**Key vocabulary:** clap, beat, name, one beat, two beats, three beats

**Instructional materials:** A drum; Name cards; A space for walking

### Starter
Say "Walk, walk" and step on each word. Say a name and step on each beat.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say a name and step one step for each beat. Children: Children step with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Play the drum and let children step on each beat. Children: Children walk on the beat.

Step 3. Teacher: Say a food name and let the children step its beats. Children: Child steps the right number of beats.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child choose a word to step. Children: Child steps and the group joins.

### Practical activity: Step the beats
1. Make a line on the floor.
2. Say a word and step each beat along the line.
3. Say the word again at the end.

**Safety:** Clear the floor and keep children well apart. Watch that no one runs.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand and step each beat together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead a friend along the line.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many steps in your name?
  **Expected response:** Child steps the beats.
- **Ask:** Can you step mango?
  **Expected response:** Two steps.

### Expected outcomes
- Steps the beats of words with support
- Stays with the beat

### Conclusion
Take a big step and say "All done."

### Home connection
Walk and clap the names of family members.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "clap and step the beats of names and words" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- STEP
- CLAP

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 beats check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Syllable sorting

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child claps beats in names and words.

### Success criteria
- Claps the beats in their name
- Matches a word to a number of claps

**Previous knowledge:** Children can copy a short beat and notice when words sound the same at the end.

**Key vocabulary:** clap, beat, name, one beat, two beats, three beats

**Instructional materials:** Name cards; Picture cards; A drum; Class record sheet

### Starter
Clap names together and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: clap names, clap pictures and clap sort. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask the child to clap a word. Children: Child claps the word.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Beat stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use soft toys and large cards. Supervise each station and keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and clap together.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to clap a friend’s name.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: claps beats in their own name; claps beats in simple words; sorts words by claps with support.

- **Ask:** Can you clap your name?
  **Expected response:** Child claps the beats.
- **Ask:** How many claps in mango?
  **Expected response:** Two.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Claps the beats in their own name

### Conclusion
Clap goodbye together.

### Home connection
Clap names at home and say them slowly.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child claps beats in names and words" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
