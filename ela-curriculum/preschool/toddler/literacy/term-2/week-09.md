# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 9: Books and print

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Words on signs and labels; My name card; Looking at print in books

## Learning objectives
- Children will notice print on signs, labels and books.
- Children will recognise their own name card with support.

**Key vocabulary:** sign, label, letter, word, name card, read

**Content:** Noticing print on signs, labels and books, recognising their own name card, and looking at print in books.

**Teaching activities:** Point to print as you talk about it; walk around the school to find signs; always say what a sign says; make children’s name cards with a photo and write names in clear lower-case letters with a capital first letter.

**Learner activities:** Notice print around them, say what it is for and find their own name card among three.

**Resources:** Labels from food packets; A "STOP" sign, an "EXIT" sign and a toilet sign; Name cards with photos; Books with large print

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child points to print and finds their own name card among three.

## Expected outcomes
- Points to print and says what it is for.
- Finds their own name card among three.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: signs in the community; Social Habits: following signs; Literacy: early reading

## Day 1 (Monday): Words on signs

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Words on signs and labels

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice print on signs and say what a sign is for.

### Success criteria
- Points to a sign
- Says what the sign tells us

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, name pictures and recognise their own name card with a photo.

**Key vocabulary:** sign, label, letter, word, name card, read

**Instructional materials:** A STOP sign, an EXIT sign and a toilet sign; A photograph walk path

### Starter
Show a STOP sign and say "This sign says STOP. It tells us to stop." Hold up a hand.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each sign and say what it says: "This says STOP. This says EXIT." Children: Children look and point to the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Walk around the school to find signs. Children: Children walk and point to signs.

Step 3. Teacher: Stop at each sign and ask "What does it say?" Children: Child points or says the word.

Step 4. Teacher: Return to class and talk about the signs. Children: Child names a sign.

### Practical activity: Sign walk
1. Walk around the school in a line.
2. Stop at each sign and talk about it.
3. Return and draw one sign.

**Safety:** Walk in a line with an adult at the front and the back, away from traffic. Hold hands at doors and steps.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold hands and point to the sign together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a sign at home and tell a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does this sign say?
  **Expected response:** STOP or EXIT.
- **Ask:** What is the sign for?
  **Expected response:** It tells us what to do.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to print on signs
- Says what a sign is for

### Conclusion
Say "Stop" with a raised hand and wave.

### Home connection
Point out signs on the road or in the market and say what they say.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice print on signs and say what a sign is for" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Labels and packets

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Words on signs and labels

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice print on food and toy packets.

### Success criteria
- Points to print on a packet
- Names the item from the label

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, name pictures and recognise their own name card with a photo.

**Key vocabulary:** sign, label, letter, word, name card, read

**Instructional materials:** Empty clean food packets and tins; A basket; A tray

### Starter
Hold up an empty milk tin and say "Look, there are words on this tin. It says milk."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each packet and point to the words. Say what it says. Children: Children point to the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Where are the words?" and wait. Children: Child points to the print.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What is it?" and let children name the item. Children: Child names the item.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick a packet and tell a friend. Children: Child shows and says the name.

### Practical activity: Label shop
1. Set up a pretend shop with clean empty packets.
2. Children point to the print on each packet.
3. Say what each one is.

**Safety:** Use only clean, empty, undamaged packets with no sharp edges. Wash hands after handling and do not use real food.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the words together and say the item name.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a packet with a picture and words.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where are the words?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the print.
- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Milk, rice or the item named.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to print on a label
- Names items from packets

### Conclusion
Put the packets back and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Show your child words on packets at home and read them aloud.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice print on food and toy packets" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MILK
- RICE

**Picture suggestions:**
- A milk tin
- A rice packet

## Day 3 (Wednesday): My name card

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** My name card

### Learning objectives
- Children will find their own name card among three.

### Success criteria
- Points to their own name card
- Says their name

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, name pictures and recognise their own name card with a photo.

**Key vocabulary:** sign, label, letter, word, name card, read

**Instructional materials:** Name cards with photos; A tray; A basket

### Starter
Hold up a name card and say "This says Tola. This is Tola’s card." Ask "Whose card is this?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each card and say the name. Point to the photo and the word. Children: Children look and say the name.

Step 2. Teacher: Put three cards on the floor and ask each child to find their own. Children: Child points to their card.

Step 3. Teacher: Cover the photo with your thumb and ask again. Children: Child recognises the card by the word or first letter.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child hold their card and show it to a friend. Children: Child holds up the card and says their name.

### Practical activity: Find my name
1. Put three name cards on the floor.
2. Each child finds their own card.
3. Hold it up and say the name.

**Safety:** Make the cards smooth and laminated if possible. Return cards to their basket at the end.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Keep the photo visible and point to the word with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a friend’s name card.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which one is your name?
  **Expected response:** Child points to their card.
- **Ask:** Whose card is this?
  **Expected response:** A friend’s name.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds their own name card with support
- Says their name

### Conclusion
Put the cards in the basket and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Write your child’s name on paper and look at it together.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find their own name card among three" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MY NAME

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Print in books

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Looking at print in books

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice print in books and point to words.

### Success criteria
- Points to words in a book
- Says that the words tell the story

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, name pictures and recognise their own name card with a photo.

**Key vocabulary:** sign, label, letter, word, name card, read

**Instructional materials:** A big book or a board book with large print; A pointer; A cushion

### Starter
Open a book and point to the words. Say "These are words. They tell us the story."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Read a page and run your finger under the words. Children: Children look at the words and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Where are the words?" and wait. Children: Child points to the words.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Where is the picture?" Children: Child points to the picture.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child point to the words on a page. Children: Child points and friends watch.

### Practical activity: Words and pictures
1. Open a big book on the floor.
2. Each child points to a picture and to the words.
3. Say "picture" and "words".

**Safety:** Keep the book low so that everyone can see. Handle the book gently and sit with space.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point together and say "words" or "picture".
- **Extension:** Ask the child to point to a long word and a short word.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where are the words?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the print.
- **Ask:** Where is the picture?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the picture.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to words in a book
- Tells words from pictures with support

### Conclusion
Close the book and say "The end."

### Home connection
Run your finger under the words as you read to your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice print in books and point to words" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- WORDS
- PICTURE

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 9 print check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** My name card

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child notices print and finds their name card.

### Success criteria
- Points to print
- Finds their own name card

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, name pictures and recognise their own name card with a photo.

**Key vocabulary:** sign, label, letter, word, name card, read

**Instructional materials:** Signs; Packets; Name cards; Books; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "Where are the words?" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: signs, packets and name cards. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Where are the words?" Children: Child points to the print.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Print stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use clean, undamaged packets and signs.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use photo name cards.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to find a friend’s name card.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: points to print; says what a sign is for; finds their own name card among three.

- **Ask:** Where are the words?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the print.
- **Ask:** Which is your name?
  **Expected response:** Child points to their card.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Notices print around them

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say "We saw words everywhere."

### Home connection
Point out print around your home and read it aloud.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child notices print and finds their name card" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
