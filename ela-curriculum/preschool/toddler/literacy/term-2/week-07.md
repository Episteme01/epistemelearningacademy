# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 7: Stories

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** A story with props; Retelling in my own words; Puppets and the story

## Learning objectives
- Children will retell a short story using props or puppets.
- Children will use words such as first, then and the end.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, the end, puppet, story, tell

**Content:** Telling a story with props, retelling it in their own words, and using puppets with the story.

**Teaching activities:** Use the same story all week; place props in a story box; use first, then and the end naturally; give children props and let them tell the story their way; accept words, gestures and actions.

**Learner activities:** Retell a short story using props or puppets and use story words such as first, then and the end.

**Resources:** A story box with a toy hen, a toy egg, a toy chick and a toy cat; Puppets; Picture cards; A scarf

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child retells part of a story with props and uses a story word.

## Expected outcomes
- Retells part of a story with props.
- Uses a story word.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: animals; Creative Development: puppets; Social Habits: sharing props

## Day 1 (Monday): The little hen story

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** A story with props

### Learning objectives
- Children will listen to a short story told with props.

### Success criteria
- Listens to the story
- Points to a prop when named

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen to a short story and join in a repeated line; many can name pictures in a book.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, the end, puppet, story, tell

**Instructional materials:** A story box; A toy hen, egg, chick and cat; A cloth nest

### Starter
Take the hen from the story box and say "This is Hen. Hen has a secret in her nest."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the story: "First, Hen sat on her nest. Then she laid an egg. Then crack! Out came a chick. The end." Use each prop as you tell it. Children: Children listen and watch the props.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Who sat on the nest?" and "What came out?" Children: Children point to the hen and the chick.

Step 3. Teacher: Tell the story again and let children help move the props. Children: Child moves a prop at the right time.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "The end" together. Children: Children say "the end".

### Practical activity: Story box
1. Put the props in a story box.
2. Take them out one at a time as you tell the story.
3. Pass the props round so each child holds one.

**Safety:** Use large soft props that cannot be swallowed. Supervise handling and do not allow children to put props in mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the prop together and move it in time with the story.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell what happened first.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who sat on the nest?
  **Expected response:** Hen.
- **Ask:** What came out of the egg?
  **Expected response:** A chick.

### Expected outcomes
- Listens to a story with props
- Names or points to the props

### Conclusion
Put the props back in the box and say "The end."

### Home connection
Tell a short story at home with two toys and let your child move them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "listen to a short story told with props" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HEN
- EGG
- CHICK

**Picture suggestions:**
- A hen on a nest
- An egg
- A chick

**Short story:** First, Hen sat on her nest. Then she laid an egg. Then crack! Out came a chick. The end.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): First, then, the end

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Retelling in my own words

### Learning objectives
- Children will use the words first, then and the end to retell the story.

### Success criteria
- Says or signs first, then or the end
- Points to the props in order

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen to a short story and join in a repeated line; many can name pictures in a book.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, the end, puppet, story, tell

**Instructional materials:** The story box; Three picture cards in order; A cloth

### Starter
Hold up the three cards and say "First, then, the end. This is the order of our story."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the three cards on the floor in order and point as you say first, then, the end. Children: Children look and say the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell the story again with the cards and ask "What came first?" Children: Child points to the first card.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What happened then?" and "What happened at the end?" Children: Child points to the cards.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick up a card and say its story word. Children: Child holds up a card and says first, then or end.

### Practical activity: Story steps
1. Put three cards on the floor in a row.
2. Children stand on each card as the story moves along.
3. Say first, then, the end together.

**Safety:** Use big soft cards on a clear floor. Keep children well apart and supervise movement.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to each card and say the word for the child to copy.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put the cards in order alone.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What came first?
  **Expected response:** Hen sat on the nest.
- **Ask:** What happened at the end?
  **Expected response:** A chick came out.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses story words with support
- Puts cards in order with support

### Conclusion
Say "The end" and clap.

### Home connection
Use the words first, then and the end when you tell stories at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use the words first, then and the end to retell the story" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FIRST
- THEN
- THE END

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three story cards in order

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Puppets tell the story

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Puppets and the story

### Learning objectives
- Children will use puppets to tell part of the story.

### Success criteria
- Moves a puppet for the story
- Says a word as the puppet speaks

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen to a short story and join in a repeated line; many can name pictures in a book.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, the end, puppet, story, tell

**Instructional materials:** Hen and chick puppets; A cloth nest; A small table or stage

### Starter
Make the hen puppet say "Cluck, cluck. I am Hen." Ask "Who is this?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Use the hen puppet to tell the first part of the story. Children: Children watch and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Give the chick puppet to a child and ask them to make it say "cheep". Children: Child makes the chick say cheep.

Step 3. Teacher: Retell the story together with two puppets. Children: Children move puppets and say words.

Step 4. Teacher: Let pairs of children tell the story to each other. Children: Child speaks for a puppet.

### Practical activity: Puppet show
1. Set up a small stage with a cloth nest.
2. Give each child a puppet.
3. Tell the story with the puppets together.

**Safety:** Check that puppets are clean and have no loose parts. Supervise sharing and keep puppets from mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move the puppet together and say the words with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make up a new line for the puppet.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is this?
  **Expected response:** Hen or chick.
- **Ask:** What does the chick say?
  **Expected response:** Cheep.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses puppets to retell part of a story
- Speaks for a puppet with support

### Conclusion
Put the puppets to bed and say goodnight.

### Home connection
Make a puppet from a sock at home and let your child tell a story with it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use puppets to tell part of the story" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- PUPPET
- STORY

**Picture suggestions:**
- A hen puppet
- A chick puppet

## Day 4 (Thursday): Tell it in my own way

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Retelling in my own words

### Learning objectives
- Children will retell the story in their own words or actions.

### Success criteria
- Retells at least one part of the story
- Uses a prop or an action

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen to a short story and join in a repeated line; many can name pictures in a book.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, the end, puppet, story, tell

**Instructional materials:** The story box; Puppets; A cushion for the storyteller

### Starter
Sit on the storyteller’s cushion and say "Who can tell our hen story?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Invite a child to sit on the storyteller’s cushion and choose a prop. Children: Child chooses a prop.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "What happened first?" and wait. Children: Child says a word or shows an action.

Step 3. Teacher: Add the child’s words to the story and tell it back to them. Children: Child listens and nods.

Step 4. Teacher: Let two or three children each tell a part. Children: Child tells a part and the group claps.

### Practical activity: Storyteller’s cushion
1. Place a cushion in the circle.
2. A child sits and tells part of the story with a prop.
3. The group claps and the next child sits.

**Safety:** Never make a child perform if they do not want to. Accept a gesture or a single word.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the prop together and offer the first word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell the whole story with first, then and the end.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What happened in the story?
  **Expected response:** Any part of the story in words or actions.
- **Ask:** Who is your favourite?
  **Expected response:** A character name.

### Expected outcomes
- Retells part of a story
- Uses props or actions

### Conclusion
Clap for all the storytellers and put the props away.

### Home connection
Ask your child to tell you a story and listen.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "retell the story in their own words or actions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- TELL THE STORY

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 retelling check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** A story with props

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child retells a story with props.

### Success criteria
- Retells part of a story
- Uses a story word

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen to a short story and join in a repeated line; many can name pictures in a book.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, the end, puppet, story, tell

**Instructional materials:** The story box; Puppets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Show the story box and three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: props, picture cards and puppets. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What happened?" Children: Child retells with a prop or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Story stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise props closely and keep floors clear. Use large soft props only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and offer two props.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell the story to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: retells part of the story; uses first, then or the end; speaks for a puppet.

- **Ask:** What happened first?
  **Expected response:** Hen sat on the nest.
- **Ask:** What happened at the end?
  **Expected response:** A chick came out.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Retells part of a story with props

### Conclusion
Say "The end" and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Tell stories with toys at home and use the words first, then and the end.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
