# Toddler Literacy — Term 2 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Rhyme**: Hearing words that sound the same at the end; Finishing a rhyme; Rhyming pairs with pictures | Children will notice when two words rhyme. Children will finish a familiar rhyme with a rhyming word. | Hearing when words sound the same at the end, finishing a rhyme with a rhyming word, and matching rhyming pairs with pictures. | Say rhyming pairs slowly and stretch the ending; use real objects so children can hold the pair; always pause before the rhyming word; never ask children to explain why words rhyme. | Notice rhyming words, finish a familiar rhyme and match two rhyming pictures with support. | Toy cat and hat, dog and log, pan and fan; Rhyme picture cards; A basket; A hand puppet | Observe whether each child says the rhyming word in a familiar rhyme and matches two pictures that rhyme with support. | Says the rhyming word in a familiar rhyme. Matches two pictures that rhyme with support. | Music: songs and chants; Understanding the World: everyday objects; Social Habits: listening to a friend |
| 2 | **Syllables**: Clapping my name; Clapping food and animal names; Syllable sorting | Children will clap the beats in their name. Children will clap the beats in simple words. | Clapping the beats in names, in food and animal names, and sorting words by one, two or three beats. | Say each word in a steady rhythm and clap once for each beat; start with children’s own names; use two hands or a drum; never ask children to count beats yet, only to clap them. | Clap the beats in their name and in simple words and match a word to one, two or three claps with support. | Name cards; A drum; Picture cards of a cat, a banana, an elephant and a mango; Three baskets | Observe whether each child claps the beats in their own name and matches a word to a number of claps with support. | Claps the beats in their own name. Matches a word to the number of claps with support. | Music: rhythm and beat; Mathematics: counting claps; Social Habits: listening to names |
| 3 | **Fine motor**: Squeezing and pinching; Threading large beads; Finger games with playdough | Children will squeeze, pinch and poke playdough and sponges. Children will thread large objects with support. | Squeezing and pinching playdough and sponges, threading large beads, and finger games. | Show each movement slowly and let children feel it; work at a table with the children sitting comfortably; use big, soft materials; celebrate effort over neatness. | Squeeze, pinch, poke and roll playdough and sponges, and thread large beads with support. | Playdough; Sponges; Large wooden or plastic beads; Thick string with a stiff end; Trays; Wipes or a damp cloth | Observe whether each child squeezes and rolls playdough and threads at least one large bead. | Squeezes and rolls playdough. Threads at least one large bead. | Physical Development: fine motor; Science: soft and hard; Social Habits: sharing materials |
| 4 | **Mark-making**: Marks in sand and flour; Chalk and water on the ground; Crayons on big paper | Children will make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons. Children will say what their marks show. | Making marks in sand and flour, with chalk and water on the ground, and with crayons on big paper. | Let children move freely with big arm movements; never correct how they hold a tool; ask "Tell me about your marks" and write down what they say; use large surfaces. | Make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons and say what their marks show. | A tray of sand or flour; Chalk; A bucket of water and a brush; Large paper; Fat crayons; An apron or old shirt | Observe whether each child makes marks with a tool and says a word about their marks. | Makes marks with a tool. Says a word about their marks. | Creative Development: art; Physical Development: arm movement; Understanding the World: materials |
| 5 | **Vocabulary**: Fruits and vegetables; Market people and things; Asking for things politely | Children will name fruits, vegetables and market things. Children will ask for something using a short phrase. | Naming fruits and vegetables, market people and things, and asking for things politely. | Use real or toy foods from the local market; model "Good morning ma, I want two oranges, please"; give children time to say the word and accept gestures; build the market corner over the week. | Name fruits, vegetables and market items and ask for something with a short phrase. | Real or toy oranges, mangoes, tomatoes, yams and onions; Baskets; A market stall table; Toy coins; An apron | Observe whether each child names six fruits or vegetables and asks for an item with a short phrase. | Names at least six fruits or vegetables. Uses a short phrase to ask for an item. | Understanding the World: community and market; Mathematics: counting and money; Social Habits: polite words |
| 6 | **Rhymes and songs**: Head, shoulders and body songs; Songs about animals; Songs with counting | Children will sing and do actions to a song. Children will remember words of a favourite song. | Body songs, animal songs and counting songs with actions, and remembering the words of a favourite song. | Teach one song at a time with the same actions each day; sing slowly first and then at a normal speed; let children choose the next song; sing in a comfortable voice and never test children on words. | Sing songs with actions, do the right action at the right word and remember a favourite song. | A hand puppet; A toy frog and a toy duck; Five paper stars; A scarf; Picture cards for each song | Observe whether each child sings along with several songs and does the right action at the right word. | Sings along with several songs. Does the right action at the right word. | Music: singing; Physical Development: body awareness; Mathematics: counting in songs |
| 7 | **Stories**: A story with props; Retelling in my own words; Puppets and the story | Children will retell a short story using props or puppets. Children will use words such as first, then and the end. | Telling a story with props, retelling it in their own words, and using puppets with the story. | Use the same story all week; place props in a story box; use first, then and the end naturally; give children props and let them tell the story their way; accept words, gestures and actions. | Retell a short story using props or puppets and use story words such as first, then and the end. | A story box with a toy hen, a toy egg, a toy chick and a toy cat; Puppets; Picture cards; A scarf | Observe whether each child retells part of a story with props and uses a story word. | Retells part of a story with props. Uses a story word. | Understanding the World: animals; Creative Development: puppets; Social Habits: sharing props |
| 8 | **Speaking**: Who is it?; What is it?; Where is it? | Children will answer simple who, what and where questions. Children will ask a simple question such as "What is that?" | Answering who, what and where questions and asking a simple question such as "What is that?" | Ask one question at a time and wait at least five seconds; use real objects and people; accept a word, a point or a gesture; model the question "What is that?" often. | Answer who, what and where questions with a word or phrase and ask one simple question. | Family and classroom photographs; A box with toys inside; A teddy bear; A hiding place such as a box or a cloth | Observe whether each child answers who, what and where questions and asks at least one question. | Answers who, what and where questions with a word or phrase. Asks one question. | Understanding the World: people and places; Social Habits: listening and answering; Mathematics: position words |
| 9 | **Books and print**: Words on signs and labels; My name card; Looking at print in books | Children will notice print on signs, labels and books. Children will recognise their own name card with support. | Noticing print on signs, labels and books, recognising their own name card, and looking at print in books. | Point to print as you talk about it; walk around the school to find signs; always say what a sign says; make children’s name cards with a photo and write names in clear lower-case letters with a capital first letter. | Notice print around them, say what it is for and find their own name card among three. | Labels from food packets; A "STOP" sign, an "EXIT" sign and a toilet sign; Name cards with photos; Books with large print | Observe whether each child points to print and finds their own name card among three. | Points to print and says what it is for. Finds their own name card among three. | Understanding the World: signs in the community; Social Habits: following signs; Literacy: early reading |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising rhyme and syllables; Revising marks and fingers; Revising vocabulary and stories | Children will show Term 2 learning through play stations. Teachers will gather evidence for every child’s Term 2 record. | Revising rhyme and syllables, marks and fingers, and vocabulary and stories through play stations and gathering Term 2 evidence. | Keep every activity familiar and playful; observe quietly and record without interrupting; offer help only when a child is stuck; celebrate what each child can do. | Show what they know about rhymes, beats, marks, words and stories through play. | Rhyme picture cards; A drum; Playdough and crayons; The story box; Market stall; Name cards; Class record sheets | Observe and record each child’s Term 2 literacy learning using the four-level scale. | Completes familiar tasks with growing independence. Each child has an observation record for Term 2. | Music: rhythm and singing; Physical Development: fine motor; Understanding the World: market and print |
