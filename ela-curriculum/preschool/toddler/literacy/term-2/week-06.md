# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 6: Rhymes and songs

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Head, shoulders and body songs; Songs about animals; Songs with counting

## Learning objectives
- Children will sing and do actions to a song.
- Children will remember words of a favourite song.

**Key vocabulary:** sing, song, head, hands, feet, jump

**Content:** Body songs, animal songs and counting songs with actions, and remembering the words of a favourite song.

**Teaching activities:** Teach one song at a time with the same actions each day; sing slowly first and then at a normal speed; let children choose the next song; sing in a comfortable voice and never test children on words.

**Learner activities:** Sing songs with actions, do the right action at the right word and remember a favourite song.

**Resources:** A hand puppet; A toy frog and a toy duck; Five paper stars; A scarf; Picture cards for each song

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child sings along with several songs and does the right action at the right word.

## Expected outcomes
- Sings along with several songs.
- Does the right action at the right word.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Music: singing; Physical Development: body awareness; Mathematics: counting in songs

## Day 1 (Monday): Head, shoulders, knees and toes

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Head, shoulders and body songs

### Learning objectives
- Children will touch the right body part as they sing a body song.

### Success criteria
- Touches head, shoulders, knees and toes
- Sings or hums along

**Previous knowledge:** Children know rhymes from Term 1 and can hear rhyming pairs; many can sing along with actions.

**Key vocabulary:** sing, song, head, hands, feet, jump

**Instructional materials:** A mat for standing; A hand puppet; A body picture card

### Starter
Touch your head and say "This is my head." Touch your knees and say "These are my knees." Ask "Where is your head?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say each body part and touch it slowly: head, shoulders, knees, toes. Children: Children touch each part as you say it.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing the song slowly: "Head, shoulders, knees and toes, knees and toes" and do the actions. Children: Children touch the parts and sing or hum.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing it a little faster and wait for the children to lead. Children: Children do the actions at the right word.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child lead the song with the puppet. Children: Child leads and friends follow.

### Practical activity: Body song
1. Stand in a circle with space.
2. Sing the song and touch each part.
3. Sing it faster each time.

**Safety:** Leave space around each child so no one bumps. Stop when children look tired and sit down to rest.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Touch the child’s head, shoulders, knees and toes gently with the words.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sing it quietly and then loudly.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is your head?
  **Expected response:** Child touches their head.
- **Ask:** Can you touch your toes?
  **Expected response:** Child touches their toes.

### Expected outcomes
- Touches body parts as named
- Sings or hums along

### Conclusion
Sing the song very slowly and sit down.

### Home connection
Sing the song at bath time and touch each body part.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "touch the right body part as they sing a body song" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HEAD
- SHOULDERS
- KNEES
- TOES

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child touching their head
- A child touching their toes

**Song or rhyme:** Head, shoulders, knees and toes, knees and toes.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Five little ducks

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Songs with counting

### Learning objectives
- Children will join in the actions and words of a counting song about ducks.

### Success criteria
- Holds up fingers or says a number
- Says quack in the song

**Previous knowledge:** Children know rhymes from Term 1 and can hear rhyming pairs; many can sing along with actions.

**Key vocabulary:** sing, song, head, hands, feet, jump

**Instructional materials:** A toy duck; Five paper ducks; A blue cloth

### Starter
Hold up the toy duck and say "Quack, quack." Ask "Can you quack?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing "Five little ducks went swimming one day. Over the hill and far away." Show five ducks on the cloth. Children: Children watch the ducks and say "quack".

Step 2. Teacher: Sing again and take one duck away each time. Children: Children hold up fingers or point to the missing ducks.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing again and wait for the children to say "quack". Children: Children say "quack, quack".

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child move a duck across the blue cloth. Children: Child moves a duck and says quack.

### Practical activity: Ducks on the water
1. Spread a blue cloth on the floor.
2. Each child holds a paper duck.
3. Sing the song and move the ducks across the cloth.

**Safety:** Use paper or cloth ducks and keep the cloth flat to avoid tripping. Supervise movement.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold up the child’s fingers as you sing the numbers.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count the ducks that are left.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do the ducks say?
  **Expected response:** Quack.
- **Ask:** How many ducks are left?
  **Expected response:** The number left, by fingers or word.

### Expected outcomes
- Joins in a counting song
- Says quack at the right time

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the ducks as they swim away.

### Home connection
Sing "Five little ducks" at home and use fingers to count.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "join in the actions and words of a counting song about ducks" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FIVE DUCKS
- QUACK

**Picture suggestions:**
- Five ducks
- A duck swimming

**Song or rhyme:** Five little ducks went swimming one day. Over the hill and far away.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Frog on a log

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Songs about animals

### Learning objectives
- Children will do the actions of a song about a frog and a log.

### Success criteria
- Jumps like a frog on request
- Joins in the words "ribbit"

**Previous knowledge:** Children know rhymes from Term 1 and can hear rhyming pairs; many can sing along with actions.

**Key vocabulary:** sing, song, head, hands, feet, jump

**Instructional materials:** A toy frog; A log or a bench; A green cloth

### Starter
Hop the toy frog along the mat and say "Ribbit, ribbit. Frog is hopping."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing "Little frog, little frog, hop, hop, hop. Sit on the log and stop, stop, stop." Children: Children listen and hop with their fingers.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing again and let children hop on the spot. Children: Children hop on the spot on "hop" and sit on "stop".

Step 3. Teacher: Wait for children to say "hop" and "stop". Children: Children say the words.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child be the frog and sit on a log. Children: Child hops to the log and sits.

### Practical activity: Hop to the log
1. Place a soft log or cushion on the floor.
2. Children hop towards it and sit when the song says stop.
3. Repeat with each child as frog.

**Safety:** Keep the area clear and hop on a soft mat. Limit the hopping to short bursts to avoid tiredness.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand and hop together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to hop three times and then sit.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the frog do?
  **Expected response:** Hops.
- **Ask:** Where does it sit?
  **Expected response:** On the log.

### Expected outcomes
- Hops and stops on request
- Joins in key words

### Conclusion
Settle like a sleeping frog on the log.

### Home connection
Hop like a frog at home and sing the song with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "do the actions of a song about a frog and a log" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FROG
- LOG
- HOP

**Picture suggestions:**
- A frog
- A log

**Song or rhyme:** Little frog, little frog, hop, hop, hop. Sit on the log and stop, stop, stop.

## Day 4 (Thursday): Choose a song

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Head, shoulders and body songs

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose and sing a favourite song with the group.

### Success criteria
- Chooses a song
- Sings or does actions along

**Previous knowledge:** Children know rhymes from Term 1 and can hear rhyming pairs; many can sing along with actions.

**Key vocabulary:** sing, song, head, hands, feet, jump

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards for the body song, ducks and frog; A basket; A hand puppet

### Starter
Show the three picture cards and say "Which song shall we sing?" Let a child pick one.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the three cards and let a child pick one from the basket. Children: Child chooses and the group sings.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing the chosen song with the actions. Children: Children sing and do the actions.

Step 3. Teacher: Pick another card and sing again. Children: Children join in.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child hold the puppet as the singer. Children: Child sings with the puppet.

### Practical activity: Song basket
1. Put three song cards in a basket.
2. Each child picks one and the group sings it.
3. Repeat until every child has chosen.

**Safety:** Keep voices gentle and give water if needed. Supervise passing the basket.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sing softly in the child’s ear and do the actions together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sing a verse alone or with a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which song do you like?
  **Expected response:** Child points to a card.
- **Ask:** Can you do the actions?
  **Expected response:** Child does them.

### Expected outcomes
- Chooses a favourite song
- Does the right action at the right word

### Conclusion
Sing the favourite song once more and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Let your child choose a song at home and sing it with you.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose and sing a favourite song with the group" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SONGS

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three song cards

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 songs check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Songs with counting

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child sings and does actions.

### Success criteria
- Sings or hums along
- Does the right action at the right word

**Previous knowledge:** Children know rhymes from Term 1 and can hear rhyming pairs; many can sing along with actions.

**Key vocabulary:** sing, song, head, hands, feet, jump

**Instructional materials:** Song cards; Toy duck and frog; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the body song and show three song stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: body song, ducks and frog. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station sing a song and watch the child’s actions. Children: Child sings and does actions.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Song stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep the floor clear. Use soft toys and paper props.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and sing the same song.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to lead a song.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: sings along; does the right action at the right word; remembers the words of a favourite song.

- **Ask:** Can you sing with me?
  **Expected response:** Child sings or hums.
- **Ask:** Can you do the action?
  **Expected response:** Child does the action.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Sings along with several songs

### Conclusion
Sing a quiet goodbye song and wave.

### Home connection
Sing three songs at home each day with actions.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child sings and does actions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
