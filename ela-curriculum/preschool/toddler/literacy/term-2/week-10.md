# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 10: Revision and assessment

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W10 · **Week type:** revision-assessment · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Revising rhyme and syllables; Revising marks and fingers; Revising vocabulary and stories

## Learning objectives
- Children will show Term 2 learning through play stations.
- Teachers will gather evidence for every child’s Term 2 record.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, clap, mark, story, name, book

**Content:** Revising rhyme and syllables, marks and fingers, and vocabulary and stories through play stations and gathering Term 2 evidence.

**Teaching activities:** Keep every activity familiar and playful; observe quietly and record without interrupting; offer help only when a child is stuck; celebrate what each child can do.

**Learner activities:** Show what they know about rhymes, beats, marks, words and stories through play.

**Resources:** Rhyme picture cards; A drum; Playdough and crayons; The story box; Market stall; Name cards; Class record sheets

**Assessment:** Observe and record each child’s Term 2 literacy learning using the four-level scale.

## Expected outcomes
- Completes familiar tasks with growing independence.
- Each child has an observation record for Term 2.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Music: rhythm and singing; Physical Development: fine motor; Understanding the World: market and print

## Day 1 (Monday): Revising rhyme and beats

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W10-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** revision · **Subtopic:** Revising rhyme and syllables

### Learning objectives
- Children will revisit rhyming pairs and clapping the beats in names and words.

### Success criteria
- Finishes a familiar rhyme
- Claps the beats in their name

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on rhymes, beats, fingers, marks, vocabulary, songs, stories, questions and print this term.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, clap, mark, story, name, book

**Instructional materials:** Rhyme cards; A drum; Name cards

### Starter
Say "cat, hat" and clap. Say "Today we play our favourite rhyme and clap games."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Play finish the rhyme with the cat and hat cards. Children: Children say the rhyming word.

Step 2. Teacher: Clap the beats in names around the circle. Children: Children clap their names.

Step 3. Teacher: Play rhyme lotto with the six cards. Children: Children match pairs.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a game. Children: Child chooses and plays.

### Practical activity: Rhyme and clap games
1. Set up two game spaces.
2. Children play rhyme lotto and name claps.
3. The teacher notes who finishes rhymes and claps beats.

**Safety:** Keep clapping gentle and supervise turn-taking. Clear the floor.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Play with the child and use two pairs only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach a friend a rhyme game.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What rhymes with cat?
  **Expected response:** Hat.
- **Ask:** Can you clap your name?
  **Expected response:** Child claps the beats.

### Expected outcomes
- Finishes familiar rhymes
- Claps the beats in names

### Conclusion
Clap goodbye and say "Cat, hat".

### Home connection
Say rhyming pairs and clap names at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "revisit rhyming pairs and clapping the beats in names and words" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- RHYME
- CLAP

**Picture suggestions:**
- A cat and a hat
- Hands clapping

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Revising marks and fingers

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W10-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** revision · **Subtopic:** Revising marks and fingers

### Learning objectives
- Children will revisit squeezing, threading and mark-making.

### Success criteria
- Squeezes and rolls dough
- Makes marks with a crayon

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on rhymes, beats, fingers, marks, vocabulary, songs, stories, questions and print this term.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, clap, mark, story, name, book

**Instructional materials:** Playdough; Beads and strings; Crayons and paper; A sand tray

### Starter
Wiggle your fingers and say "Today our fingers play and make marks."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up playdough and bead threading tables. Children: Children squeeze, roll and thread.

Step 2. Teacher: Set up crayons and sand for marks. Children: Children make marks and say a word.

Step 3. Teacher: Walk round and ask "What did you make?" Children: Child shows and names their work.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a favourite activity. Children: Child chooses and plays.

### Practical activity: Fingers and marks
1. Set up four tables with different materials.
2. Children move between tables.
3. The teacher notes how each child holds and uses tools.

**Safety:** Supervise beads and water closely. Use non-toxic dough and crayons and wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Work beside the child with one material.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a pattern with beads or marks.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you make?
  **Expected response:** A name for their creation.
- **Ask:** Can you thread a bead?
  **Expected response:** Child threads a bead.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses fingers with growing control
- Makes marks and says what they show

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say goodbye to the materials.

### Home connection
Let your child play with dough and crayons at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "revisit squeezing, threading and mark-making" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SQUEEZE
- MARKS

**Picture suggestions:**
- Playdough
- Crayon marks

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Revising words and stories

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W10-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** revision · **Subtopic:** Revising vocabulary and stories

### Learning objectives
- Children will revisit market words, songs, stories and questions.

### Success criteria
- Names market items
- Retells part of a story

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on rhymes, beats, fingers, marks, vocabulary, songs, stories, questions and print this term.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, clap, mark, story, name, book

**Instructional materials:** The market stall; The story box; Name cards; A hand puppet

### Starter
Say "Good morning, ma" and say "Today we visit the market and hear a story."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Play at the market stall with polite words. Children: Children ask for items with please.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell the little hen story with the story box. Children: Children join in and retell.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask who, what and where questions about the story. Children: Children answer with words or gestures.

Step 4. Teacher: Find name cards and say whose they are. Children: Child finds their card.

### Practical activity: Market and story
1. Set up the market stall and the story box.
2. Children visit each in turn.
3. The teacher notes words and story retelling.

**Safety:** Supervise each space and keep floors clear. Use toy foods and large props.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two choices and say the words with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the seller or the storyteller.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you want?
  **Expected response:** A short phrase with please.
- **Ask:** What happened first?
  **Expected response:** Hen sat on the nest.

### Expected outcomes
- Names market items
- Retells part of a story

### Conclusion
Close the market and say "The end."

### Home connection
Tell stories and play shop at home with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "revisit market words, songs, stories and questions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MARKET
- STORY

**Picture suggestions:**
- A market stall
- A toy hen

## Day 4 (Thursday): Play stations again

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W10-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Revising rhyme and syllables

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will gather evidence of each child’s Term 2 literacy learning at play stations.

### Success criteria
- Completes familiar tasks with growing independence
- Shows rhyme, speaking, print and fine motor skills

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on rhymes, beats, fingers, marks, vocabulary, songs, stories, questions and print this term.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, clap, mark, story, name, book

**Instructional materials:** Six station sets; Class record sheets; A camera or notebook

### Starter
Sing a favourite song and show six stations to the class.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up six stations: rhyme, beats, fingers, marks, story and print. Children: Children rotate every three minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: Observe each child at two stations and record what they can do. Children: Children play at stations.

Step 3. Teacher: Take photographs and make notes. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Six stations
1. Set up six stations with familiar materials.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station with an adult or helper. Keep floors clear and materials large and soft.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and stay at familiar stations.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to show friends how a station works.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: finishes rhymes; claps beats; uses fingers; makes marks; retells a story; finds their name card.

- **Ask:** What do you like?
  **Expected response:** Child points or names.
- **Ask:** Show me your favourite game.
  **Expected response:** Child chooses a station.

### Expected outcomes
- Has a Term 2 observation record
- Shows familiar skills at stations

### Conclusion
Say well done and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Play a favourite game from this term at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will gather evidence of each child’s Term 2 literacy learning at play stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Term 2 celebration

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W10-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Revising vocabulary and stories

### Learning objectives
- Children will celebrate Term 2 with songs, stories and games, and teachers will complete their records.

### Success criteria
- Joins in songs and rhymes
- Shows what they enjoy

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on rhymes, beats, fingers, marks, vocabulary, songs, stories, questions and print this term.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, clap, mark, story, name, book

**Instructional materials:** Favourite songs and rhymes; The story box; Stickers or a certificate; Class record sheets

### Starter
Say "Today we celebrate. Let us sing our favourite songs and tell our favourite story."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing favourite songs with actions. Children: Children join in and choose a song.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell the hen story with the props. Children: Children retell with props.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each child a sticker or praise and say their name. Children: Child waves and says thank you.

### Practical activity: Celebration circle
1. Sit in a circle.
2. Sing, tell the story and say each name.
3. Hand out stickers or certificates.

**Safety:** Check for allergies before any snack and use non-toxic stickers. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand and sing along.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose the last song.

### Assessment
Complete each child’s Term 2 literacy record using today’s observations and the week’s notes. Record a next step for Term 3.

- **Ask:** What is your favourite song?
  **Expected response:** Child sings or points.
- **Ask:** Did you like the story?
  **Expected response:** Yes with a smile or word.

### Expected outcomes
- Has a completed Term 2 record
- Enjoys songs, rhymes and stories

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say "See you in Term 3."

### Home connection
Keep singing, telling stories and playing with sounds during the holiday.
