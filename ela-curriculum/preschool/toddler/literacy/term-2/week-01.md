# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 1: Rhyme

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Hearing words that sound the same at the end; Finishing a rhyme; Rhyming pairs with pictures

## Learning objectives
- Children will notice when two words rhyme.
- Children will finish a familiar rhyme with a rhyming word.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sounds the same, cat, hat, dog, log

**Content:** Hearing when words sound the same at the end, finishing a rhyme with a rhyming word, and matching rhyming pairs with pictures.

**Teaching activities:** Say rhyming pairs slowly and stretch the ending; use real objects so children can hold the pair; always pause before the rhyming word; never ask children to explain why words rhyme.

**Learner activities:** Notice rhyming words, finish a familiar rhyme and match two rhyming pictures with support.

**Resources:** Toy cat and hat, dog and log, pan and fan; Rhyme picture cards; A basket; A hand puppet

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says the rhyming word in a familiar rhyme and matches two pictures that rhyme with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Says the rhyming word in a familiar rhyme.
- Matches two pictures that rhyme with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Music: songs and chants; Understanding the World: everyday objects; Social Habits: listening to a friend

## Day 1 (Monday): Words that sound the same

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Hearing words that sound the same at the end

### Learning objectives
- Children will hear two words that rhyme and repeat them.

### Success criteria
- Repeats both words of a rhyming pair
- Smiles or claps when words rhyme

**Previous knowledge:** Children know several rhymes and songs from Term 1 and can say the last word of a line when the teacher pauses.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sounds the same, cat, hat, dog, log

**Instructional materials:** A toy cat and a toy hat; A basket; A hand puppet

### Starter
Hold up the toy cat and the toy hat and say "Cat, hat. They sound the same at the end. Cat, hat."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "cat, hat" slowly, stretching the ending "-at". Hold up each toy as you say it. Children: Children look and repeat "cat, hat".

Step 2. Teacher: Say "cat, hat" again and clap when they rhyme. Children: Children clap with you.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "cat, dog" and shake your head. Say "cat, hat" and nod. Children: Children copy the nod or shake of the head.

Step 4. Teacher: Let the puppet say pairs and ask "Do they sound the same?" Children: Children nod for a rhyme and shake for no rhyme.

### Practical activity: Cat and hat
1. Put a toy cat and a toy hat in the basket.
2. Each child takes one out and says its name.
3. The group says "cat, hat" together and claps.

**Safety:** Use clean, large toys that cannot be swallowed. Keep the basket within reach and supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the toys and say the two words slowly with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say another word that sounds like cat.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Do cat and hat sound the same?
  **Expected response:** Yes, with a nod or word.
- **Ask:** Do cat and dog sound the same?
  **Expected response:** No, with a shake of the head or word.

### Expected outcomes
- Repeats a rhyming pair
- Shows when two words rhyme with an action

### Conclusion
Say "cat, hat" once more and put the toys to bed.

### Home connection
Say a rhyming pair such as "cat, hat" at home and clap together.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hear two words that rhyme and repeat them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- CAT
- HAT

**Picture suggestions:**
- A cat
- A hat

**Song or rhyme:** Cat and hat, cat and hat, they sound the same. Cat and hat.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Dog and log

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Hearing words that sound the same at the end

### Learning objectives
- Children will listen to more rhyming pairs and repeat them.

### Success criteria
- Repeats dog and log
- Joins in with pan and fan

**Previous knowledge:** Children know several rhymes and songs from Term 1 and can say the last word of a line when the teacher pauses.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sounds the same, cat, hat, dog, log

**Instructional materials:** A toy dog and a small log or stick; A pan and a hand fan; A basket

### Starter
Hold up the dog and the log and say "Dog, log. They sound the same." Wag the dog.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "dog, log" slowly and show each object. Children: Children repeat the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the pan and the fan and say "pan, fan". Children: Children repeat the pair.

Step 3. Teacher: Mix the pairs: say "dog, fan" and ask "Same?" Children: Children shake their heads for no.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "pan, fan" and ask "Same?" Children: Children nod and clap.

### Practical activity: Rhyme sort
1. Put the four objects on the mat.
2. Ask each child to take two that sound the same.
3. Say the pair together.

**Safety:** Use a smooth, short stick that is not sharp. Keep objects large and away from mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two pairs only and say the pair together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a rhyming pair in the classroom.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Do pan and fan sound the same?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** What goes with dog?
  **Expected response:** Log.

### Expected outcomes
- Repeats rhyming pairs
- Picks two objects that rhyme with support

### Conclusion
Put each pair in the basket and say the pair.

### Home connection
Play with a pan and a fan at home and say "pan, fan".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "listen to more rhyming pairs and repeat them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- DOG
- LOG
- PAN
- FAN

**Picture suggestions:**
- A dog
- A log
- A pan
- A fan

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Finish the rhyme

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Finishing a rhyme

### Learning objectives
- Children will finish a familiar rhyme with a rhyming word.

### Success criteria
- Says the last word of a rhyme
- Joins in the actions

**Previous knowledge:** Children know several rhymes and songs from Term 1 and can say the last word of a line when the teacher pauses.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sounds the same, cat, hat, dog, log

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards of a cat, a hat, a dog and a log; A hand puppet

### Starter
Say "Cat in a hat. Cat in a" and pause. Point to the hat card.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say the rhyme "The cat sat on a hat" and "The dog sat on a log". Show the cards. Children: Children listen and look at the pictures.

Step 2. Teacher: Say the rhyme again and stop before the last word. Children: Children say "hat" or "log".

Step 3. Teacher: Say it again and use the picture card as a clue if needed. Children: Child says the word or points to the card.

Step 4. Teacher: Let the puppet say a line and wait for a child to finish it. Children: Child finishes the rhyme.

### Practical activity: Puppet rhymes
1. Sit in a circle with the puppet.
2. The puppet says a rhyme and pauses.
3. The child who is chosen finishes the rhyme.

**Safety:** Keep voices gentle and give each child time to answer. Do not hurry a shy child.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Show the picture clue before the pause.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make up a line about a cat or a dog.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** The cat sat on a what?
  **Expected response:** Hat.
- **Ask:** The dog sat on a what?
  **Expected response:** Log.

### Expected outcomes
- Finishes a rhyme with the right word
- Uses a picture clue

### Conclusion
Say the two rhymes together and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Say a simple rhyme at home and pause for your child to say the last word.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "finish a familiar rhyme with a rhyming word" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- The cat sat on a hat
- The dog sat on a log

**Picture suggestions:**
- A cat on a hat
- A dog on a log

**Song or rhyme:** The cat sat on a hat. The dog sat on a log.

## Day 4 (Thursday): Rhyming pairs

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Rhyming pairs with pictures

### Learning objectives
- Children will match two pictures that rhyme with support.

### Success criteria
- Picks two pictures that sound the same
- Names the pictures

**Previous knowledge:** Children know several rhymes and songs from Term 1 and can say the last word of a line when the teacher pauses.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sounds the same, cat, hat, dog, log

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards: cat, hat, dog, log, pan, fan; A basket; A mat

### Starter
Show the cat and hat cards and say "Cat and hat. They make a pair."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Lay out the six cards face up. Pick up the cat and ask "What goes with cat?" Children: Children point to the hat.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "dog" and ask "What goes with dog?" Children: Children point to the log.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pick a card and find its pair. Children: Child finds the pair and says the words.

Step 4. Teacher: Say each pair together at the end. Children: Children say the pairs.

### Practical activity: Rhyme lotto
1. Spread six cards on the mat.
2. Each child takes one card and finds the one that rhymes.
3. Say both words and clap.

**Safety:** Use large, soft cards without sharp corners. Supervise turn-taking.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use only four cards and say the pair for the child to copy.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find all three pairs without help.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What goes with fan?
  **Expected response:** Pan.
- **Ask:** Do dog and log rhyme?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches rhyming pictures with support
- Names the pictures

### Conclusion
Put each pair in the basket and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Look for two things that sound the same, such as a pan and a fan, and say them together.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match two pictures that rhyme with support" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Pairs
- Cat–hat
- Dog–log
- Pan–fan

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three pairs of pictures

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 1 rhyme check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Rhyming pairs with pictures

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child notices and finishes rhymes.

### Success criteria
- Finishes a familiar rhyme
- Matches two pictures that rhyme

**Previous knowledge:** Children know several rhymes and songs from Term 1 and can say the last word of a line when the teacher pauses.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sounds the same, cat, hat, dog, log

**Instructional materials:** Rhyme cards; A hand puppet; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the cat and hat song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: finish the rhyme, rhyme lotto and puppet pairs. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station say a rhyme and pause, then ask "Which goes with it?" Children: Child says the word or points.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Rhyme stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use soft cards and toys. Supervise each station and keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two pairs.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to say a new rhyming pair.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says the rhyming word in a familiar rhyme; matches rhyming pictures; repeats a rhyming pair.

- **Ask:** What rhymes with cat?
  **Expected response:** Hat.
- **Ask:** What rhymes with dog?
  **Expected response:** Log.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Finishes a familiar rhyme

### Conclusion
Sing the rhyme song and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Say rhyming pairs at home and let your child finish the rhyme.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child notices and finishes rhymes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Rhyme

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
