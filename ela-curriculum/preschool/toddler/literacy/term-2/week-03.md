# Toddler Literacy — Term 2, Week 3: Fine motor

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Squeezing and pinching; Threading large beads; Finger games with playdough

## Learning objectives
- Children will squeeze, pinch and poke playdough and sponges.
- Children will thread large objects with support.

**Key vocabulary:** squeeze, pinch, poke, roll, thread, fingers

**Content:** Squeezing and pinching playdough and sponges, threading large beads, and finger games.

**Teaching activities:** Show each movement slowly and let children feel it; work at a table with the children sitting comfortably; use big, soft materials; celebrate effort over neatness.

**Learner activities:** Squeeze, pinch, poke and roll playdough and sponges, and thread large beads with support.

**Resources:** Playdough; Sponges; Large wooden or plastic beads; Thick string with a stiff end; Trays; Wipes or a damp cloth

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child squeezes and rolls playdough and threads at least one large bead.

## Expected outcomes
- Squeezes and rolls playdough.
- Threads at least one large bead.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Physical Development: fine motor; Science: soft and hard; Social Habits: sharing materials

## Day 1 (Monday): Squeeze and pinch

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Squeezing and pinching

### Learning objectives
- Children will squeeze and pinch playdough with their fingers.

### Success criteria
- Squeezes playdough with the whole hand
- Pinches playdough with fingers

**Previous knowledge:** Children can clap beats, do finger rhymes and hold a book; many are still developing hand strength.

**Key vocabulary:** squeeze, pinch, poke, roll, thread, fingers

**Instructional materials:** Playdough; Trays; A damp cloth

### Starter
Hold a ball of playdough and say "Squeeze, squeeze, squeeze." Show a pinch and say "Pinch, pinch."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Squeeze the playdough in your fist and say "Squeeze". Show it to the children. Children: Children squeeze their own piece.

Step 2. Teacher: Pinch a small piece with your thumb and finger and say "Pinch". Children: Children pinch with their fingers.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Squeeze" and "Pinch" in turn and wait. Children: Children change their action.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child make a squashed shape and show a friend. Children: Child shows their shape.

### Practical activity: Playdough time
1. Give each child a tray and a ball of playdough.
2. Squeeze and pinch it together.
3. Make a shape and show a friend.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic playdough and check for allergies. Do not let children eat it. Wash hands before and after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Squeeze the dough together with a hand over the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to pinch five small pieces.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you squeeze?
  **Expected response:** Child squeezes the dough.
- **Ask:** Can you pinch?
  **Expected response:** Child pinches with fingers.

### Expected outcomes
- Squeezes and pinches playdough
- Names the actions squeeze and pinch

### Conclusion
Put the playdough away and wash hands.

### Home connection
Let your child squeeze and pinch bread dough or soft clay with you.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "squeeze and pinch playdough with their fingers" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SQUEEZE
- PINCH

**Picture suggestions:**
- A hand squeezing
- Fingers pinching

**Song or rhyme:** Squeeze, squeeze, squeeze it. Pinch, pinch, pinch it.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Roll and poke

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Squeezing and pinching

### Learning objectives
- Children will roll and poke playdough to make shapes.

### Success criteria
- Rolls dough into a ball or a snake
- Pokes holes with a finger

**Previous knowledge:** Children can clap beats, do finger rhymes and hold a book; many are still developing hand strength.

**Key vocabulary:** squeeze, pinch, poke, roll, thread, fingers

**Instructional materials:** Playdough; Trays; Plastic lollipop sticks or fingers

### Starter
Roll a ball of playdough in your palms and say "Roll, roll, roll." Poke a hole and say "Poke."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Roll a ball between your hands. Then roll a long snake on the tray. Children: Children copy rolling a ball and a snake.

Step 2. Teacher: Poke the ball with one finger to make a hole. Children: Children poke their balls.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Roll", "Poke", "Squeeze" in turn and wait. Children: Children do each action.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child make something and name it. Children: Child shows their shape and names it.

### Practical activity: Dough shapes
1. Give each child a tray and dough.
2. Roll a ball and a snake.
3. Poke holes and make a shape.

**Safety:** Use only non-toxic dough and keep it off the face. Wash hands after play and wipe the trays.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Roll the dough together and poke with the child’s finger.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a snake and curl it into a circle.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you roll the dough?
  **Expected response:** Child rolls the dough.
- **Ask:** What did you make?
  **Expected response:** Child names the shape.

### Expected outcomes
- Rolls playdough into a ball or snake
- Pokes with one finger

### Conclusion
Pack the dough into a box and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Roll and poke dough or mud at home with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "roll and poke playdough to make shapes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Sponge squeezing

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Squeezing and pinching

### Learning objectives
- Children will squeeze a sponge to move water from one bowl to another.

### Success criteria
- Squeezes a sponge with the whole hand
- Moves water between bowls

**Previous knowledge:** Children can clap beats, do finger rhymes and hold a book; many are still developing hand strength.

**Key vocabulary:** squeeze, pinch, poke, roll, thread, fingers

**Instructional materials:** Two bowls; Sponges; A towel; A tray

### Starter
Dip a sponge in water and squeeze it over the bowl. Say "Squeeze, squeeze. Drip, drip."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to squeeze the sponge so water drips into the bowl. Children: Children watch and say "drip".

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child dip a sponge and squeeze it into the empty bowl. Children: Child squeezes and watches the water.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Squeeze hard" and "Squeeze softly". Children: Children change how they squeeze.

Step 4. Teacher: Count how many squeezes it takes to fill a small bowl. Children: Children squeeze and count with you.

### Practical activity: Water squeeze
1. Set up two bowls on a tray with sponges.
2. Squeeze water from one bowl to the other.
3. Wipe the tray when done.

**Safety:** Keep water shallow and supervise at all times. Wipe spills quickly and use a towel under the tray.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a small sponge and squeeze together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to move all the water to the other bowl.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do with the sponge?
  **Expected response:** Squeeze.
- **Ask:** Where does the water go?
  **Expected response:** Into the bowl.

### Expected outcomes
- Squeezes a sponge
- Moves water from one bowl to another

### Conclusion
Wring the sponges and put them on the tray to dry.

### Home connection
Let your child squeeze a sponge in the bath or at the sink.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "squeeze a sponge to move water from one bowl to another" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Threading beads

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Threading large beads

### Learning objectives
- Children will thread a large bead onto a string with support.

### Success criteria
- Holds a string and a bead
- Pushes a string through a bead

**Previous knowledge:** Children can clap beats, do finger rhymes and hold a book; many are still developing hand strength.

**Key vocabulary:** squeeze, pinch, poke, roll, thread, fingers

**Instructional materials:** Large beads with big holes; Thick strings with a stiff end; A tray

### Starter
Hold up a bead and a string and say "We thread the bead on the string. Push it through."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to hold the bead in one hand and push the string through with the other. Children: Children watch and hold a bead.

Step 2. Teacher: Help each child thread one bead and pull it down the string. Children: Child threads a bead with support.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask the child to thread a second bead. Children: Child threads two beads.

Step 4. Teacher: Show the beads to a friend. Children: Child shows and says "bead".

### Practical activity: Bead threading
1. Give each child a tray with large beads and a string.
2. Thread beads onto the string.
3. Count the beads together at the end.

**Safety:** Use only beads larger than a child’s fist-sized opening so they cannot be swallowed. Supervise closely and put a knot at the end of each string.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the bead and let the child push the string through.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to thread five beads in a pattern.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we thread?
  **Expected response:** Beads on a string.
- **Ask:** Can you push the string?
  **Expected response:** Child pushes the string through.

### Expected outcomes
- Threads at least one large bead with support
- Shows beads to a friend

### Conclusion
Count the beads and put them in a bowl.

### Home connection
Thread large pasta tubes onto string with your child under supervision.

- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 fingers check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T2-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Finger games with playdough

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child uses their fingers to squeeze, roll and thread.

### Success criteria
- Squeezes and rolls playdough
- Threads a bead with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can clap beats, do finger rhymes and hold a book; many are still developing hand strength.

**Key vocabulary:** squeeze, pinch, poke, roll, thread, fingers

**Instructional materials:** Playdough; Sponges and bowls; Beads and strings; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing "Squeeze, squeeze, squeeze it" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: playdough, sponge squeezing and bead threading. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: Watch each child and note how they squeeze, roll and thread. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Finger stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise beads and water closely. Use non-toxic dough and wash hands after each station.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and offer one station to start.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to teach a friend how to thread.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: squeezes playdough; rolls dough; threads at least one large bead.

- **Ask:** Can you squeeze the dough?
  **Expected response:** Child squeezes.
- **Ask:** Can you thread a bead?
  **Expected response:** Child threads a bead.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Squeezes, rolls and threads

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Play with dough or clay at home and let your child squeeze and roll.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
