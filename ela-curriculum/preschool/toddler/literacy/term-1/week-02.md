# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 2: Listening

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Loud and quiet; Using my voice; High and low voices

## Learning objectives
- Children will make a loud and a quiet sound on request.
- Children will use their voices in play, such as a whisper and a shout.

**Key vocabulary:** loud, quiet, voice, whisper, shout

**Content:** Making loud and quiet sounds, using the voice for whispering, shouting and humming, and high and low voices.

**Teaching activities:** Show loud with big arms and quiet with a finger to the lips; model each voice before children try; praise every attempt and keep shouting short.

**Learner activities:** Make loud and quiet sounds on request, whisper and shout, and copy high and low voices.

**Resources:** A drum; A shaker; A puppet; Picture cards of a lion and a mouse; A toy telephone

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child changes the loudness of a sound on request and joins in voice play.

## Expected outcomes
- Changes the loudness of their voice on request.
- Uses a voice sound to join a game.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Music: dynamics; Social Habits: indoor and outdoor voices; Science: how sounds are made

## Day 1 (Monday): Loud and quiet drum

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Loud and quiet

### Learning objectives
- Children will make a loud and a quiet sound with a drum on request.

### Success criteria
- Plays the drum loudly when asked
- Plays the drum quietly when asked

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stop and listen for a few seconds and point to the source of a familiar sound.

**Key vocabulary:** loud, quiet, voice, whisper, shout

**Instructional materials:** A drum; A drum stick or a soft mallet; Picture cards of a lion and a mouse

### Starter
Beat the drum loudly and say "Loud! Boom, boom." Then tap it softly and whisper "Quiet. Tap, tap."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the lion card and play a loud beat. Show the mouse card and play a quiet beat. Say the words loud and quiet. Children: Children listen and open their arms wide for loud and put a finger to the lips for quiet.

Step 2. Teacher: Play loud or quiet and ask "Is it loud or quiet?" Children: Children answer with actions or words.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child play the drum when you hold up the lion or mouse card. Children: Child plays loud for the lion and quiet for the mouse.

Step 4. Teacher: Play a short pattern: loud, quiet, loud, quiet. Children: Children copy the actions.

### Practical activity: Lion and mouse drum
1. Sit in a circle with the drum in the middle.
2. Hold up the lion or mouse card.
3. Each child takes a turn to play loud or quiet to match the card.

**Safety:** Use a soft mallet and keep the drum away from the children’s ears. Stop if a child covers their ears.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand to play and say "loud" or "quiet" with them.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose a card and play for the group to guess.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the lion loud or quiet?
  **Expected response:** Loud.
- **Ask:** Can you make a quiet sound?
  **Expected response:** Child taps softly or whispers.

### Expected outcomes
- Plays a loud and a quiet sound on request
- Shows loud or quiet with an action

### Conclusion
Play a very quiet tap and a loud boom and say "Loud and quiet."

### Home connection
At home, bang a pot loudly and tap it quietly and say loud and quiet.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make a loud and a quiet sound with a drum on request" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- LOUD (a lion)
- QUIET (a mouse)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A lion
- A mouse
- A drum

**Song or rhyme:** Loud like a lion, boom, boom, boom. Quiet like a mouse, tap, tap, tap.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Whisper and shout

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Using my voice

### Learning objectives
- Children will whisper and say a word loudly on request.

### Success criteria
- Whispers a word when asked
- Says a word loudly when asked

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stop and listen for a few seconds and point to the source of a familiar sound.

**Key vocabulary:** loud, quiet, voice, whisper, shout

**Instructional materials:** A puppet; A toy telephone; A soft toy

### Starter
Hold up the puppet and whisper "Hello" in its ear. Then say "Hello!" in a big voice.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Make the puppet whisper "Hello" and then say "HELLO!" Say "Whisper" and "big voice". Children: Children watch and copy the puppet’s voices.

Step 2. Teacher: Whisper a word such as "baby" and ask the children to whisper it back. Children: Children whisper the word.

Step 3. Teacher: Say a word in a big voice and ask the children to say it loudly, once only. Children: Children say the word in a big voice.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child talk to the puppet in a whisper or a big voice. Children: Child chooses a voice and says hello.

### Practical activity: Telephone talk
1. Give a toy telephone to a pair of children.
2. One whispers hello and the other whispers back.
3. Change voice to a big voice and swap pairs.

**Safety:** Keep big voices short so no child strains their voice. Use a clean toy telephone and wipe it between turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Whisper with the child and use a gesture for the big voice.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose a word to whisper and then shout to the puppet.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you whisper?
  **Expected response:** Child whispers a sound or word.
- **Ask:** Show me your big voice.
  **Expected response:** Child says a word loudly.

### Expected outcomes
- Whispers on request
- Uses a big voice briefly on request

### Conclusion
Whisper "goodbye" to the puppet together.

### Home connection
At home, whisper a word to your child and ask them to whisper it back, then try a big voice.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "whisper and say a word loudly on request" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Whisper
- Big voice

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child whispering
- A child shouting hooray

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Voices in play

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Using my voice

### Learning objectives
- Children will use their voices to join in a play game.

### Success criteria
- Makes a voice sound in a game
- Waits for a turn

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stop and listen for a few seconds and point to the source of a familiar sound.

**Key vocabulary:** loud, quiet, voice, whisper, shout

**Instructional materials:** A toy car; A toy cat; A toy aeroplane; A play tunnel or table

### Starter
Push the toy car and say "Brum, brum." Ask the children "What does the car say?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Drive the car along the mat saying "Brum, brum". Then show the aeroplane and say "Nee-ow." Children: Children copy the voice sounds.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the cat and say "Meow." Ask "What does the cat say?" Children: Children say "meow".

Step 3. Teacher: Move each toy and wait for the children to make its sound before it moves. Children: Children make the sound to make the toy go.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a toy and make its sound. Children: Child moves the toy and makes the sound.

### Practical activity: Voice toys
1. Place three toys on a mat.
2. Each child picks a toy and moves it while making its sound.
3. Friends copy the sound.

**Safety:** Keep the toys large with no loose parts. Supervise closely and keep the floor clear of small items.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move the toy together and make the sound with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a sound for a toy and ask a friend to guess.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the car say?
  **Expected response:** Brum, brum.
- **Ask:** Can you make the cat sound?
  **Expected response:** Meow.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a voice sound in play
- Waits for the toy to move

### Conclusion
Park the toys in a box and say "Night, night car. Night, night cat."

### Home connection
Play with a toy car or a cup at home and make a sound for each move.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use their voices to join in a play game" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Car: brum
- Cat: meow

**Picture suggestions:**
- A car
- A cat
- An aeroplane

## Day 4 (Thursday): High and low voices

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** High and low voices

### Learning objectives
- Children will copy a high voice and a low voice.

### Success criteria
- Makes a high voice sound
- Makes a low voice sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stop and listen for a few seconds and point to the source of a familiar sound.

**Key vocabulary:** loud, quiet, voice, whisper, shout

**Instructional materials:** A mouse puppet; A bear puppet; A scarf

### Starter
Make the mouse puppet squeak in a high voice and the bear puppet growl in a low voice. Ask "Who is it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the mouse and squeak up high with a hand raised. Show the bear and growl low with a hand lowered. Children: Children copy the voice and hand position.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "High or low?" and make a sound. Children: Children raise or lower their hands to answer.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing "ooh" going up and down with the scarf. Children: Children follow the scarf with their voices and hands.

Step 4. Teacher: Give each child a turn to be the mouse or the bear. Children: Child makes a high or a low sound.

### Practical activity: Mouse and bear
1. Sit in a circle with the two puppets.
2. Hold up one puppet and sing "ooh".
3. Children copy the high or low sound together.

**Safety:** Keep voices gentle and stop if a child strains. Give sips of water if voices tire.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use one puppet at a time and show with your hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose a puppet and make the sound for others to guess.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the mouse high or low?
  **Expected response:** High.
- **Ask:** Show me the bear voice.
  **Expected response:** Child makes a low sound.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a high and a low sound
- Uses hands to show high or low

### Conclusion
Say goodbye to the mouse in a high voice and the bear in a low voice.

### Home connection
At home, sing "ooh" up and down and ask your child to copy.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "copy a high voice and a low voice" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HIGH (mouse)
- LOW (bear)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A mouse
- A bear

**Song or rhyme:** Mouse is high, squeak, squeak, squeak. Bear is low, growl, growl, growl.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 voice check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Loud and quiet

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child uses loud, quiet, high and low sounds.

### Success criteria
- Changes loudness on request
- Joins in a voice game

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stop and listen for a few seconds and point to the source of a familiar sound.

**Key vocabulary:** loud, quiet, voice, whisper, shout

**Instructional materials:** A drum; Mouse and bear puppets; A toy telephone; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the lion and mouse song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes: drum station, puppet station, telephone station. Children: Children play at each station and change their sounds on request.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Can you play loud?", "Can you whisper?" and "Can you do a high voice?" Children: Child shows each sound with a voice or an instrument.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Voice stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher listens and records.

**Safety:** Supervise instruments and keep volume moderate. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and offer two choices.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to lead a loud and quiet game.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: changes loud and quiet on request; whispers; joins in a voice game.

- **Ask:** Can you be quiet?
  **Expected response:** Child makes a quiet sound.
- **Ask:** Can you be loud?
  **Expected response:** Child makes a loud sound.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Changes loudness on request

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye in a quiet voice and a big voice.

### Home connection
Play a loud and quiet game with a cup and spoon at home.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Loud
- Quiet

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
