# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 8: Vocabulary

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Family and people; Food we eat; Things in our classroom

## Learning objectives
- Children will name familiar people, foods and objects.
- Children will say a word or two to ask for something.

**Key vocabulary:** mummy, daddy, baby, rice, water, chair

**Content:** Naming family, people, food and classroom objects, and using two words to ask for something.

**Teaching activities:** Use real objects first and pictures second; name each item before asking for it; extend single words into two words, such as "more rice"; celebrate every word.

**Learner activities:** Name familiar people, foods and objects, and use two words together to ask for something.

**Resources:** Family photographs; Real foods or toy foods: rice, bread, banana, water; Classroom objects: chair, table, cup, ball; A basket

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names at least ten things and uses two words together to ask.

## Expected outcomes
- Names at least ten familiar things.
- Uses two words together to ask for something.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: family and community; Social Habits: asking politely; Mathematics: sorting

## Day 1 (Monday): Family and people

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Family and people

### Learning objectives
- Children will name familiar people in a family picture.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to mummy, daddy and baby
- Says a person’s name or a word for them

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals and pictures in books and say a few words with gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** mummy, daddy, baby, rice, water, chair

**Instructional materials:** A family picture with mummy, daddy, baby and grandma; Family photographs from home if available; A basket

### Starter
Show the family picture and say "This is mummy. This is daddy. This is baby." Point to each.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Point to each person and say the name. Ask "Who is this?" Children: Children point and say the name.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Where is baby?" and wait. Children: Child points to the baby.

Step 3. Teacher: Show a photograph and ask "Who is this?" Children: Child says "mummy" or "grandma".

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child hold a picture and show it to a friend. Children: Child shows the picture and says the word.

### Practical activity: My family
1. Put family pictures on the mat.
2. Each child points to mummy, daddy and baby.
3. The group says the names together.

**Safety:** Handle photographs gently and return them at the end of the day. Be sensitive to children who live with relatives or one parent.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point together and say the word before the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name more people, such as uncle and aunty.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is this?
  **Expected response:** Mummy, daddy, baby or grandma.
- **Ask:** Where is baby?
  **Expected response:** Child points to baby.

### Expected outcomes
- Names familiar people
- Points to a named person

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the family pictures.

### Home connection
Look at family photographs together and name each person.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name familiar people in a family picture" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MUMMY
- DADDY
- BABY

**Picture suggestions:**
- A mummy
- A daddy
- A baby

**Song or rhyme:** Mummy, daddy, baby, me. We are a family.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Food we eat

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Food we eat

### Learning objectives
- Children will name foods they eat and say a word to ask for them.

### Success criteria
- Names at least three foods
- Asks for a food with a word or phrase

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals and pictures in books and say a few words with gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** mummy, daddy, baby, rice, water, chair

**Instructional materials:** Toy foods or real washed fruit: rice, bread, banana, water; A tray; Napkins

### Starter
Put the foods on a tray and say "Look! Rice, bread, banana." Ask "What do you like?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold up each food and say its name clearly. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "I want rice, please." Pretend to eat it. Children: Children copy "rice, please".

Step 3. Teacher: Hold two foods and ask "Rice or bread?" Children: Child points or says the food name.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick a food from the tray. Children: Child names the food and says please.

### Practical activity: Pretend snack
1. Set up a pretend snack table with toy foods.
2. Each child asks for a food by name.
3. Say thank you together.

**Safety:** Use toy foods or check allergies before any real food is shared. Wash hands first and keep real food for supervised snack time only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two foods and say the name with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name three foods they eat at home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Rice, bread, banana or water.
- **Ask:** What do you want?
  **Expected response:** A food name with or without please.

### Expected outcomes
- Names at least three foods
- Asks for a food

### Conclusion
Put the foods away and say "Thank you for the food."

### Home connection
Name the foods at mealtime and ask your child "What do you want?"

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name foods they eat and say a word to ask for them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- RICE
- BREAD
- BANANA
- WATER

**Picture suggestions:**
- A plate of rice
- Bread
- A banana
- A cup of water

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Things in our classroom

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Things in our classroom

### Learning objectives
- Children will name objects in the classroom.

### Success criteria
- Points to a chair, a table, a cup and a ball
- Names an object

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals and pictures in books and say a few words with gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** mummy, daddy, baby, rice, water, chair

**Instructional materials:** A chair, a table, a cup and a ball; A basket; A cloth

### Starter
Walk around the classroom and touch a chair. Say "This is a chair." Ask "What is this?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Walk around and name each object and let children touch it. Children: Children touch and say the name.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Bring me the ball" and wait. Children: Child brings the ball.

Step 3. Teacher: Hide an object under the cloth and ask "What is it?" Children: Child feels or guesses and names it.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child name an object and show a friend. Children: Child shows the object and says the word.

### Practical activity: Classroom treasure hunt
1. Choose four classroom objects.
2. Ask each child to find one when you name it.
3. Bring them to the mat and name them together.

**Safety:** Make sure the objects are light and safe to carry. Keep the floor clear and supervise movement.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk with the child and point to the object.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find two objects when you name them.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Chair, table, cup or ball.
- **Ask:** Can you bring me the cup?
  **Expected response:** Child brings a cup.

### Expected outcomes
- Names classroom objects
- Follows a one-step request

### Conclusion
Return each object to its place and say its name.

### Home connection
Name things at home and ask your child to fetch one by name.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name objects in the classroom" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- CHAIR
- TABLE
- CUP
- BALL

**Picture suggestions:**
- A chair
- A table
- A cup
- A ball

## Day 4 (Thursday): Two words together

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Food we eat

### Learning objectives
- Children will use two words together to ask for something.

### Success criteria
- Says "more rice" or a similar phrase
- Uses please with a request

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals and pictures in books and say a few words with gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** mummy, daddy, baby, rice, water, chair

**Instructional materials:** Toy foods; A toy shop counter; Cups and plates

### Starter
Hold a plate and say "More rice, please." Ask the children "What do you want?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Model the phrases "more rice", "my cup" and "big ball". Children: Children repeat the phrases.

Step 2. Teacher: Hold out the plate and wait for the child to ask. Children: Child says "more rice" or "rice, please".

Step 3. Teacher: Give the item and say "Here you are." Wait for thank you. Children: Child says thank you.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child be the shopkeeper and serve a friend. Children: Child serves and the friend asks.

### Practical activity: Pretend shop
1. Set up a pretend shop with toy foods.
2. The customer asks with two words.
3. The shopkeeper serves and the customer says thank you.

**Safety:** Use toy foods and cups only. Keep the area clear and supervise turn-taking.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say the first word and wait for the child to add the second.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to ask for two things in a row.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you want?
  **Expected response:** A two-word request such as "more rice".
- **Ask:** What do you say after you get it?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses two words together
- Uses please and thank you

### Conclusion
Close the shop and say goodbye to the customers.

### Home connection
At mealtimes, wait for your child to ask with two words such as "more water".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use two words together to ask for something" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MORE RICE
- MY CUP
- BIG BALL

**Picture suggestions:**
- A plate of rice
- A cup
- A big ball

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 words check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Things in our classroom

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how many words each child names and how they ask for things.

### Success criteria
- Names at least ten things
- Uses two words to ask

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals and pictures in books and say a few words with gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** mummy, daddy, baby, rice, water, chair

**Instructional materials:** Family pictures; Toy foods; Classroom objects; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the "What is this?" song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: people, food and objects. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is this?" and "What do you want?" Children: Child names the item and asks.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Words stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use toy foods and large pictures only. Supervise each station and keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use real objects.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to name things for a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names familiar things; uses two words together; asks with please.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Child names the object.
- **Ask:** What do you want?
  **Expected response:** Child says a word or phrase.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Names familiar things

### Conclusion
Say goodbye to each object.

### Home connection
Name things at home and ask your child to ask for them with two words.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how many words each child names and how they ask for things" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)

- Words

- Photos of stations
