# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 1: Listening

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Listening walk; Inside and outside sounds; Guess the sound

## Learning objectives
- Children will stop, listen and say or show what they hear.
- Children will match a sound to the thing that makes it with support.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, hear, sound, quiet, noise

**Content:** Stopping to listen, finding the sources of everyday sounds inside and outside, and guessing hidden sounds.

**Teaching activities:** Model listening with a finger to your ear and a calm face; whisper the instruction "Shh, listen"; name each sound clearly and give children time to point.

**Learner activities:** Stop, listen, point to or name what they hear, and join a guess-the-sound game.

**Resources:** A bell, a spoon and a cup, a rattle; A cloth bag; Pictures of a door, a bird, a car and a tap; A small drum

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child stops to listen, turns towards a sound and points to its source.

## Expected outcomes
- Stays still to listen for a short time.
- Names or points to the source of a familiar sound.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: sounds in the environment; Social Habits: being quiet together; Physical Development: walking in a line

## Day 1 (Monday): Shh, listen!

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Listening walk

### Learning objectives
- Children will stop and stay still to listen for a few seconds.

### Success criteria
- Stops moving when the teacher says "Shh, listen"
- Turns towards a sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they may respond to their name, turn towards sounds and enjoy simple songs.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, hear, sound, quiet, noise

**Instructional materials:** A small bell; A cloth to cover the bell; A carpet or mat for sitting

### Starter
Sit the children in a circle, put a finger to your ear and whisper "Shh, listen." Ring the bell once and let the sound fade.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Whisper "Shh, listen" and ring the bell once. Hold still until the sound fades, then say "I heard a bell." Children: Children stop, look at you and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Ring the bell behind your back. Ask "What can you hear?" and wait a few seconds. Children: Children turn towards the sound or say or point to the bell.

Step 3. Teacher: Teach the listening action: finger to the ear, then say "listen" together. Children: Children copy the action and say "listen".

Step 4. Teacher: Ring the bell again and let each child ring it once while the others listen. Children: Each child rings gently and the others stay still to listen.

### Practical activity: Listening statues
1. Ask children to walk gently round the mat while you hum.
2. Ring the bell and say "Shh, listen". Everyone stands still like a statue.
3. When the sound fades, children wave and walk again. Repeat four times.

**Safety:** Clear the mat of toys and chairs so children can move safely. Keep the bell sound gentle so it does not startle anyone.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sit the child on your lap and put a hand on theirs to show the listening action.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to ring the bell for the group and tell friends to listen.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you listen?
  **Expected response:** Child stops and looks or points to the ear.
- **Ask:** What did you hear?
  **Expected response:** Bell, or a pointing gesture towards it.

### Expected outcomes
- Stops to listen for a few seconds
- Copies the listening action

### Conclusion
Whisper "Shh, listen" one last time and finish with a quiet clap. Say "Good listening."

### Home connection
At home, say "Shh, listen" and ask your child to point to the sound they hear, such as a fan or a car.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "stop and stay still to listen for a few seconds" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- LISTEN (finger to ear)
- A bell

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child with a finger to the ear
- A bell

**Song or rhyme:** Listen, listen, shh, shh, shh. What can you hear? What can you hear?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Inside sounds

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Inside and outside sounds

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to the objects that make sounds in the classroom.

### Success criteria
- Points to an object that makes a sound
- Copies a sound with the voice

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they may respond to their name, turn towards sounds and enjoy simple songs.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, hear, sound, quiet, noise

**Instructional materials:** A spoon and a cup; A rattle; A door that can be closed; A tap or water jug

### Starter
Tap a spoon on a cup and say "Tap, tap, tap. What is making the sound?" Let children point to the spoon.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Make three sounds in turn: tap the cup, shake the rattle, close the door. Name each one. Children: Children watch and point to each thing that made the sound.

Step 2. Teacher: Make a sound with your back turned and ask "What was that?" Children: Children point to the object or say the word.

Step 3. Teacher: Pour water from the jug into a basin. Say "Splash, splash." Children: Children listen and say "splash".

Step 4. Teacher: Give each child one object to make a sound while the others listen. Children: Child makes a sound; friends point to what they heard.

### Practical activity: Sound basket
1. Put four safe sound-makers in a basket.
2. Each child takes one out and makes its sound.
3. Everyone says the name of the object together.

**Safety:** Use only large, smooth objects that cannot be swallowed. Keep the rattle sound soft and watch that children do not put objects in their mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects only and let the child choose which to shake.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose an object and make a sound for friends to name.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What makes this sound?
  **Expected response:** Child names or points to the object.
- **Ask:** Can you tap like me?
  **Expected response:** Child taps with a spoon.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to the source of a classroom sound
- Makes a sound with an object

### Conclusion
Put the objects back in the basket one at a time, saying each name together.

### Home connection
Ask your child to find something at home that makes a sound, such as a spoon on a pot.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to the objects that make sounds in the classroom" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Cup and spoon
- Rattle
- Door

**Picture suggestions:**
- A cup and spoon
- A rattle
- A door

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Outside sounds

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Inside and outside sounds

### Learning objectives
- Children will listen outside and say or point to what they hear.

### Success criteria
- Listens quietly outside
- Points to or names a sound heard outside

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they may respond to their name, turn towards sounds and enjoy simple songs.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, hear, sound, quiet, noise

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a bird, a car, a hen and the wind; A safe outdoor area; A hat for each child where needed

### Starter
Show the picture of a bird and say "Tweet, tweet. We will listen for a bird outside." Hold a finger to your ear.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Walk the children in a line to the safe outdoor area, holding the rope or hands. Children: Children walk gently and hold their place in the line.

Step 2. Teacher: Stop, say "Shh, listen" and wait ten seconds. Say what you hear, such as a bird. Children: Children stop and listen, and some point or name a sound.

Step 3. Teacher: Show the four pictures. Ask "Did we hear a bird? A car?" Children: Children point to the picture of the sound they heard.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk to a second spot and listen again. Children: Children listen and say "car", "bird" or make the sound.

### Practical activity: Listening spots
1. Choose two spots outside, such as the gate and the tree.
2. At each spot, listen for ten seconds.
3. Point to the matching picture card.

**Safety:** Keep children in a line with an adult at the front and back, away from the road and the gate. Check the ground for sharp objects and use sun hats in strong sun.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand and point to the thing that makes the sound.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend one sound they heard.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What can you hear?
  **Expected response:** Bird, car, wind or a sound made with the voice.
- **Ask:** Is it loud or quiet?
  **Expected response:** Child answers loud or quiet by word or gesture.

### Expected outcomes
- Listens outside for ten seconds
- Names or imitates one outside sound

### Conclusion
Walk back in a line and say "We listened well outside."

### Home connection
On the way home or at the compound, ask "What can you hear?" and wait for your child to point.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "listen outside and say or point to what they hear" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Bird
- Car
- Wind

**Picture suggestions:**
- A bird in a tree
- A car
- Leaves in the wind

**Song or rhyme:** Tweet, tweet, tweet, I hear a bird. Brum, brum, brum, I hear a car.

## Day 4 (Thursday): Guess the sound

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Guess the sound

### Learning objectives
- Children will match a hidden sound to a picture of its source.

### Success criteria
- Points to the picture that matches a sound
- Waits quietly while the sound is made

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they may respond to their name, turn towards sounds and enjoy simple songs.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, hear, sound, quiet, noise

**Instructional materials:** A cloth bag; A bell, a rattle and a spoon with a cup; Three picture cards of the same objects

### Starter
Show the cloth bag and say "I have a secret sound in my bag. Shh, listen." Shake it softly.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the bell in the bag, shake it and ask "What is it?" Take it out to show. Children: Children listen, guess by pointing or naming, and look at the reveal.

Step 2. Teacher: Lay the three picture cards on the mat. Make a sound behind a screen. Children: Children point to the matching picture.

Step 3. Teacher: Repeat with the rattle and the spoon and cup. Children: Children point and say the object name.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child choose a card and ask you to make that sound. Children: Child points to a card and listens to the sound.

### Practical activity: Sound lotto
1. Place three picture cards on the mat.
2. Hide behind a screen and make one sound.
3. Children point to the matching card, and the teacher shows the object.

**Safety:** Use soft sounds only so no child is frightened. Keep objects large and out of mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two cards instead of three and make the sound twice.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to hide behind the screen and make a sound for the group.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is the sound?
  **Expected response:** Child names or points to the matching picture.
- **Ask:** Is it the bell?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no with a nod or word.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches a sound to its picture with support
- Waits quietly to listen

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say "Shh, listen" for a last quiet moment.

### Home connection
Play guess the sound at home with a spoon and a pot and ask your child to point to what made it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match a hidden sound to a picture of its source" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Bell
- Rattle
- Spoon and cup

**Picture suggestions:**
- A bell
- A rattle
- A spoon and cup

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 1 listening check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Listening walk

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child listens and responds to sounds.

### Success criteria
- Stops to listen when asked
- Points to or names the source of one sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they may respond to their name, turn towards sounds and enjoy simple songs.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, hear, sound, quiet, noise

**Instructional materials:** A bell, a rattle, a spoon and cup; Three picture cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing "Listen, listen, shh, shh, shh" and show the three listening stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations and rotate groups every four minutes. Station 1: bell and rattle. Station 2: picture cards. Station 3: listening statues. Children: Children play at each station and stop to listen on the signal.

Step 2. Teacher: Make one sound at each station and ask "What can you hear?" Children: Child points to or names the source.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take a photograph for the portfolio. Children: Children play on and tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Listening stations
1. Set up three stations with soft sound-makers.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use soft, large objects only and supervise every station. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one familiar sound.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to ring the bell for friends to listen.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: stops to listen; turns towards a sound; points to or names a sound source.

- **Ask:** Can you listen?
  **Expected response:** Child stops and listens.
- **Ask:** What made that sound?
  **Expected response:** Child names or points to the object.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Listens to a sound for a few seconds

### Conclusion
Whisper "Good listening" and wave goodbye to the sound-makers.

### Home connection
Say "Shh, listen" at home and ask your child to point to the sound.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child listens and responds to sounds" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Listen

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
