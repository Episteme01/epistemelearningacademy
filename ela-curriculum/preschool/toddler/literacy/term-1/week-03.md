# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 3: Sounds in nature

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Farm animals and their sounds; Birds and insects; Animal sound games

## Learning objectives
- Children will match animal pictures to sounds.
- Children will make animal sounds and say the animal name.

**Key vocabulary:** goat, hen, dog, cat, cow, bird

**Content:** Farm animals and their sounds, birds and insects, and games that match animal pictures to sounds.

**Teaching activities:** Use real familiar Nigerian animals such as goat, hen, dog, cat and cow; say the name first, then make the sound; let children make the sound before you show the picture.

**Learner activities:** Name animals, make animal sounds, match pictures to sounds and join animal sound games.

**Resources:** Large pictures of a goat, hen, dog, cat, cow and bird; Soft toy animals; A basket; A recording of animal sounds if available

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names at least three animals and makes at least one animal sound.

## Expected outcomes
- Names at least three animals.
- Makes the sound of a familiar animal.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: animals and their homes; Social Habits: kindness to animals; Music: copying sounds

## Day 1 (Monday): Farm friends

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Farm animals and their sounds

### Learning objectives
- Children will name three farm animals and make their sounds.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to a goat, a hen and a dog
- Makes the sound of at least one animal

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make loud and quiet sounds and use their voices in play, such as brum for a car.

**Key vocabulary:** goat, hen, dog, cat, cow, bird

**Instructional materials:** Large pictures of a goat, hen and dog; Soft toy animals if available; A basket

### Starter
Take the goat picture from the basket and say "Meh, meh. This is a goat." Ask "What does the goat say?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the goat picture, say its name and make its sound. Repeat with the hen ("cluck") and the dog ("woof"). Children: Children look, point and copy each sound.

Step 2. Teacher: Say a sound and ask "Which animal is it?" Children: Children point to the matching picture.

Step 3. Teacher: Say an animal name and ask the children to make its sound. Children: Children make the sound.

Step 4. Teacher: Give each child a picture to hold and call out the sound. Children: Child holds up the animal when they hear the sound.

### Practical activity: Animal baskets
1. Place three animal toys or pictures in a basket.
2. Each child picks one and says or shows its name.
3. The group makes the animal’s sound together.

**Safety:** Use clean toys and pictures. Remind children to be gentle with animals and to wash hands after real animals.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two animals and make the sound with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose an animal and say what it eats.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the goat say?
  **Expected response:** Meh meh.
- **Ask:** Which animal says woof?
  **Expected response:** The dog.

### Expected outcomes
- Names or points to three animals
- Makes an animal sound

### Conclusion
Put each picture back in the basket and say its name and sound.

### Home connection
Ask your child to make the sound of a goat, a hen or a dog at home or outside.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name three farm animals and make their sounds" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- GOAT (meh)
- HEN (cluck)
- DOG (woof)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A goat
- A hen
- A dog

**Song or rhyme:** The goat says meh, meh, meh. The hen says cluck, cluck, cluck.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Cat, cow and bird

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Birds and insects

### Learning objectives
- Children will name a cat, a cow and a bird and make their sounds.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to a cat, a cow and a bird
- Makes a sound for each

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make loud and quiet sounds and use their voices in play, such as brum for a car.

**Key vocabulary:** goat, hen, dog, cat, cow, bird

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a cat, a cow and a bird; A basket; A soft toy cat

### Starter
Hold up the soft toy cat and stroke it gently. Say "Meow, meow. What does the cat say?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the cat and say "Meow". Show the cow and say "Moo". Show the bird and say "Tweet". Children: Children point and make each sound.

Step 2. Teacher: Hide a picture behind your back and make its sound. Ask "Which animal?" Children: Children point to the picture they think it is.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Fly like a bird" and flap your arms. Say "Walk like a cow." Children: Children move like each animal.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick a card and make the animal sound. Children: Child shows the card and makes the sound.

### Practical activity: Move like an animal
1. Clear a space on the mat.
2. Call "bird", "cat" or "cow" and everyone moves like that animal.
3. Say "freeze" and listen for one sound.

**Safety:** Clear the floor of toys and keep children well apart. Stop the game if anyone looks tired.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two pictures and move beside the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead the group in an animal move and sound.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the cow say?
  **Expected response:** Moo.
- **Ask:** How does a bird move?
  **Expected response:** It flies or flaps arms.

### Expected outcomes
- Names three more animals
- Moves and sounds like an animal

### Conclusion
Fold your wings and settle on the mat like a bird going to sleep.

### Home connection
Look at pictures of animals in a book or outside and say each sound with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name a cat, a cow and a bird and make their sounds" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- CAT (meow)
- COW (moo)
- BIRD (tweet)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A cat
- A cow
- A bird

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Bees, ants and a story

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Birds and insects

### Learning objectives
- Children will listen for small animal sounds in a short story.

### Success criteria
- Joins in the sound "buzz"
- Points to the bee or the ant in a picture

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make loud and quiet sounds and use their voices in play, such as brum for a car.

**Key vocabulary:** goat, hen, dog, cat, cow, bird

**Instructional materials:** A picture of a bee and an ant; A picture book with insects; A yellow scarf

### Starter
Wave the yellow scarf and say "Bzzz. A bee is flying." Ask the children "Can you buzz?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the bee picture and say "Bzzz, bee". Show the ant and say "Tiny ant." Children: Children buzz and hold up a finger for tiny.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell a short story: "A bee flew to a flower. Bzzz. The bee went home." Use the scarf. Children: Children buzz and follow the scarf with their eyes.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Where is the bee?" and "Where is the ant?" Children: Children point to the pictures.

Step 4. Teacher: Give each child a turn to fly the scarf bee while others buzz. Children: Child flies the scarf and friends buzz.

### Practical activity: Buzzing bees
1. Give each child a scarf or a paper bee.
2. Walk slowly around the mat buzzing like a bee.
3. When you say "flower", everyone sits and stops buzzing.

**Safety:** Keep an adult watching for falls and remove sharp items. Tell children that real bees and ants are looked at, not touched.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s scarf and buzz together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend what the bee did in the story.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the bee say?
  **Expected response:** Buzz.
- **Ask:** Is the ant big or tiny?
  **Expected response:** Tiny.

### Expected outcomes
- Joins in animal sounds in a story
- Points to a bee or an ant

### Conclusion
Let the bee fly home and say "Goodnight bee."

### Home connection
Look for an ant or a bird outside and say its name and sound with your child, without touching.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "listen for small animal sounds in a short story" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BEE (buzz)
- ANT

**Picture suggestions:**
- A bee
- An ant
- A flower

## Day 4 (Thursday): Animal sound games

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Animal sound games

### Learning objectives
- Children will match six animal pictures to their sounds in a game.

### Success criteria
- Matches a sound to an animal picture
- Names at least three animals

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make loud and quiet sounds and use their voices in play, such as brum for a car.

**Key vocabulary:** goat, hen, dog, cat, cow, bird

**Instructional materials:** Six animal pictures: goat, hen, dog, cat, cow, bird; A basket; A cloth bag

### Starter
Say "I am thinking of an animal. Meh, meh, meh. Who am I?" Let the children point to the goat.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Lay out the six pictures. Make one sound and ask the children to point to the animal. Children: Children point to the matching picture.

Step 2. Teacher: Say a name and ask the children to make the sound. Children: Children make the sound.

Step 3. Teacher: Play "Which animal is missing?" by hiding one picture. Children: Children look and point to the empty space or name the animal.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child hide a picture and play with a friend. Children: Child hides, friend guesses.

### Practical activity: Animal lotto
1. Place six pictures on the mat.
2. The teacher makes a sound.
3. Children take turns to touch the animal that makes the sound.

**Safety:** Use large pictures and keep small pieces away from mouths. Supervise sharing and turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use three pictures and make the sound slowly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a sound for a friend to guess.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which animal says moo?
  **Expected response:** The cow.
- **Ask:** What does a hen say?
  **Expected response:** Cluck.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches animal sounds to pictures
- Names at least three animals

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to each animal and say its name.

### Home connection
Make an animal sound at home and ask your child to say which animal it is.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match six animal pictures to their sounds in a game" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Goat
- Hen
- Dog
- Cat
- Cow
- Bird

**Picture suggestions:**
- Six animal pictures

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 animal check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Animal sound games

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe which animals each child can name and which sounds they can make.

### Success criteria
- Names at least three animals
- Makes at least one animal sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make loud and quiet sounds and use their voices in play, such as brum for a car.

**Key vocabulary:** goat, hen, dog, cat, cow, bird

**Instructional materials:** Six animal pictures; Soft toy animals; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the animal song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: picture matching, animal toys and animal moves. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is this?" and "What does it say?" Children: Child names the animal or makes the sound.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Animal stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use soft toys and large pictures only. Keep floors clear and supervise movement.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and offer two pictures.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to choose an animal and teach a friend its sound.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names animals; makes animal sounds; matches a sound to a picture.

- **Ask:** What is this animal?
  **Expected response:** Goat, hen, dog, cat, cow or bird.
- **Ask:** What does it say?
  **Expected response:** Its sound.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Names and makes sounds of familiar animals

### Conclusion
Sing "The goat says meh" and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Ask your child to name the animals they see at home or on the street.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe which animals each child can name and which sounds they can make" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Animals

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
