# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 6: Rhymes and songs

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W06 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Finger rhymes; Action songs; Rhymes with a story

## Learning objectives
- Children will join in actions and words of a short rhyme.
- Children will fill in a missing word with support.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sing, clap, wave, finger, again

**Content:** Finger rhymes, action songs and a rhyme with a small story, with children filling in missing words.

**Teaching activities:** Say or sing each rhyme slowly with big actions; repeat it several times; pause before the last word of a line so children can fill it in; use the same rhymes all week.

**Learner activities:** Join in the actions and words of short rhymes and fill in a missing word with support.

**Resources:** A hand puppet; A basket of toy fish, a toy spider and a toy bus; Picture cards for each rhyme; A scarf

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child joins in at least two rhymes with actions and says the last word when you pause.

## Expected outcomes
- Joins in at least two rhymes with actions.
- Says the last word of a line when the teacher pauses.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Music: singing; Physical Development: finger control; Social Habits: taking turns

## Day 1 (Monday): Two little hands

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Finger rhymes

### Learning objectives
- Children will copy the finger actions of a short rhyme.

### Success criteria
- Copies at least one finger action
- Joins in with a word or sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, copy sounds and beats, greet others and respond to their names.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sing, clap, wave, finger, again

**Instructional materials:** A hand puppet; A mat for circle time

### Starter
Hold up both hands and wiggle your fingers. Say "Wiggle, wiggle, wiggle. Can you wiggle too?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say the rhyme "Ten little fingers, wiggle, wiggle, wiggle. Hide them in your lap, giggle, giggle, giggle." Show the actions slowly. Children: Children watch and wiggle their fingers.

Step 2. Teacher: Say the rhyme again and do the actions with the puppet. Children: Children copy the wiggle and the hiding action.

Step 3. Teacher: Say the rhyme again and pause before the last word of each line. Children: Children say "wiggle" or "giggle" or make the sound.

Step 4. Teacher: Let one child lead with the puppet while the others do the actions. Children: Child leads and friends copy.

### Practical activity: Fingers play
1. Sit in a circle with space to move hands.
2. Say the rhyme and do the actions together.
3. Repeat three times, faster each time.

**Safety:** Keep hands away from faces and eyes. Stop the game if any child becomes upset or tired.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move the child’s fingers gently for the actions and say the words with them.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach the rhyme actions to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you wiggle your fingers?
  **Expected response:** Child wiggles fingers.
- **Ask:** What do we do at the end?
  **Expected response:** Hide hands and giggle.

### Expected outcomes
- Copies finger actions
- Joins in with words or sounds

### Conclusion
Sing the rhyme once more very quietly and hide the hands.

### Home connection
Sing "Ten little fingers" at bath time and do the actions with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "copy the finger actions of a short rhyme" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Ten little fingers
- Wiggle, wiggle

**Picture suggestions:**
- Ten fingers
- A child giggling

**Song or rhyme:** Ten little fingers, wiggle, wiggle, wiggle. Hide them in your lap, giggle, giggle, giggle.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Five little fish

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Finger rhymes

### Learning objectives
- Children will join in the actions of a counting rhyme with fish.

### Success criteria
- Makes a swimming action
- Says a word or the number in the rhyme

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, copy sounds and beats, greet others and respond to their names.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sing, clap, wave, finger, again

**Instructional materials:** Five toy fish or paper fish; A blue cloth for the water; A basket

### Starter
Spread the blue cloth and put five fish on it. Say "Splash! The fish are swimming."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say the rhyme "Five little fish swimming in the sea. Splash, splash, splash, come to me." Show the swimming action with a hand. Children: Children watch and copy the hand swimming.

Step 2. Teacher: Say the rhyme again and move one fish at a time to you on the word "me". Children: Children say "splash" and watch the fish.

Step 3. Teacher: Pause before "splash" and wait. Children: Children say "splash" or clap.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick a fish and swim it across the cloth. Children: Child swims the fish and says "splash".

### Practical activity: Fish in the sea
1. Spread a blue cloth on the floor.
2. Each child holds a paper fish and swims it across the cloth.
3. Everyone says "splash" together.

**Safety:** Use soft paper or cloth fish with no small parts. Keep the cloth flat so no one trips.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the fish together and swim it with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count the fish as they swim to the teacher.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where are the fish swimming?
  **Expected response:** In the sea.
- **Ask:** What do the fish say?
  **Expected response:** Splash.

### Expected outcomes
- Copies the swimming action
- Says splash with the group

### Conclusion
Put the fish to sleep under the cloth and whisper goodnight.

### Home connection
Sing the fish rhyme at bath time and swim a toy in the water.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "join in the actions of a counting rhyme with fish" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Five little fish
- Splash!

**Picture suggestions:**
- Five fish
- The sea

**Song or rhyme:** Five little fish swimming in the sea. Splash, splash, splash, come to me.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Spider and bus

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Action songs

### Learning objectives
- Children will join in the actions of two action songs.

### Success criteria
- Does at least two actions in a song
- Sings or hums along

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, copy sounds and beats, greet others and respond to their names.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sing, clap, wave, finger, again

**Instructional materials:** A toy spider; A toy bus or a bus picture; A scarf

### Starter
Walk your fingers like a spider up your arm and say "The spider is climbing up."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing "The little spider climbs up, up, up. Down comes the rain, splash, splash. The spider climbs up again." Show climbing and rain actions. Children: Children copy the climbing and the falling rain.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing the song again and wait for the children to do the actions without help. Children: Children do the actions on their own.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing "The wheels on the bus go round and round" with the wheel action. Children: Children make circles with their arms.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose spider or bus and lead the song. Children: Child chooses and sings with the group.

### Practical activity: Song choice
1. Show the spider and bus pictures.
2. A child picks one and the group sings it.
3. Repeat so each child has a turn.

**Safety:** Leave enough space for arm movements and keep children from bumping. Keep voices gentle.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Do the actions with the child and sing in their ear.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make up a new action for a line of the song.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What goes up?
  **Expected response:** The spider.
- **Ask:** What goes round and round?
  **Expected response:** The wheels.

### Expected outcomes
- Joins in action songs
- Does the correct action at the right time

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the spider and the bus.

### Home connection
Sing "The wheels on the bus" at home and do the actions with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "join in the actions of two action songs" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SPIDER
- BUS

**Picture suggestions:**
- A spider
- A bus

**Song or rhyme:** The wheels on the bus go round and round, round and round, round and round.

## Day 4 (Thursday): A rhyme with a story

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Rhymes with a story

### Learning objectives
- Children will say the last word of a line in a story rhyme when the teacher pauses.

### Success criteria
- Fills in a missing word with support
- Does the actions of the rhyme

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, copy sounds and beats, greet others and respond to their names.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sing, clap, wave, finger, again

**Instructional materials:** A hen puppet; A basket; Picture cards of a hen, an egg and a chick

### Starter
Show the hen puppet and say "Cluck, cluck. The hen has something in the basket."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say the rhyme "Little hen, little hen, what do you lay? An egg, an egg, every day." Show the picture cards. Children: Children look and point to the egg.

Step 2. Teacher: Say it again and pause before "egg". Children: Children say "egg".

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Little hen, little hen, what comes out? A chick, a chick, cheep, cheep, shout!" and show the chick. Children: Children say "chick" and "cheep".

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child hold the hen puppet and lead the lines. Children: Child leads and friends fill in the words.

### Practical activity: Hen and chick
1. Hide an egg picture in the basket.
2. Say the rhyme and ask the children what the hen lays.
3. Show the egg, then the chick.

**Safety:** Keep pictures flat and clean and supervise sharing. Do not use real eggs.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the egg picture before you say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say the whole line "An egg, an egg" alone.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the hen lay?
  **Expected response:** An egg.
- **Ask:** What comes out of the egg?
  **Expected response:** A chick.

### Expected outcomes
- Fills in the last word with support
- Does the hen and chick actions

### Conclusion
Put the hen to sleep and whisper "Night, night, hen."

### Home connection
Say the hen rhyme at home and pause for your child to say "egg".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say the last word of a line in a story rhyme when the teacher pauses" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HEN
- EGG
- CHICK

**Picture suggestions:**
- A hen
- An egg
- A chick

**Song or rhyme:** Little hen, little hen, what do you lay? An egg, an egg, every day.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 rhyme check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Action songs

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child joins in rhymes and fills in missing words.

### Success criteria
- Joins in actions of a rhyme
- Says a word at the pause

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, copy sounds and beats, greet others and respond to their names.

**Key vocabulary:** rhyme, sing, clap, wave, finger, again

**Instructional materials:** A hand puppet; Rhyme picture cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the finger rhyme and show three rhyme stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: finger rhymes, action songs and a rhyme with a story. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station say a rhyme and pause for the last word. Children: Child does the actions and says the word.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Rhyme stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use soft toys and pictures. Supervise each station and keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and repeat the rhyme twice.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to lead a rhyme.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: joins in at least two rhymes with actions; fills in a missing word; sings or hums along.

- **Ask:** Can you do the actions?
  **Expected response:** Child does the actions.
- **Ask:** What is the last word?
  **Expected response:** Child says the missing word.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Joins in rhymes with actions

### Conclusion
Sing the goodbye song and wave.

### Home connection
Sing two rhymes at home and pause for your child to fill in a word.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child joins in rhymes and fills in missing words" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Rhymes

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
