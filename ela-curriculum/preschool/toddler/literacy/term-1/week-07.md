# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 7: Books

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Holding a book and turning pages; Pointing to and naming pictures; A favourite book

## Learning objectives
- Children will hold a book the right way up and turn pages.
- Children will point to and name pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, picture, turn, look, read

**Content:** Holding a book the right way up, turning pages, pointing to and naming pictures, and sharing a favourite book.

**Teaching activities:** Hold the book so every child can see; run your finger under the pictures; name each picture and wait for the child to say it; let children turn pages with you.

**Learner activities:** Hold a book the right way up, turn pages one at a time, point to and name pictures.

**Resources:** Sturdy board books with large pictures; A book corner with cushions; A basket of books; A big book if available

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child holds a book correctly, turns pages and names three pictures.

## Expected outcomes
- Turns pages one at a time with support.
- Names three pictures in a book.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: looking at pictures; Social Habits: caring for books; Physical Development: fine motor

## Day 1 (Monday): This is a book

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Holding a book and turning pages

### Learning objectives
- Children will hold a book the right way up and say or show that it is a book.

### Success criteria
- Holds a book the right way up
- Says or signs book

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join in rhymes, name animals and greet others, and many point to pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, picture, turn, look, read

**Instructional materials:** Board books; A cushion; A basket

### Starter
Hold up a book and say "This is a book. Look at the front." Turn it upside down and say "Oops!" Turn it the right way and say "Now we can see."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the front, the back and the spine. Say "This is the front." Children: Children look and point to the front.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a book and say "Hold it the right way up." Children: Child holds the book the right way up with support.

Step 3. Teacher: Show how to turn a page gently and say "Turn the page". Children: Children copy turning a page.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk round and say each child’s name and "Well done holding your book". Children: Child smiles and shows their book.

### Practical activity: Book time
1. Give each child a board book and sit comfortably.
2. Children hold the book and turn pages.
3. Say "page" each time a page is turned.

**Safety:** Use sturdy board books with rounded corners. Keep books off the floor and do not allow chewing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the book together and turn each page with a hand over the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find the front of the book and show a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** A book.
- **Ask:** Show me the front.
  **Expected response:** Child points to the cover.

### Expected outcomes
- Holds a book the right way up with support
- Says or signs book

### Conclusion
Close the books and put them back in the basket together.

### Home connection
Share a book at home every day and let your child turn the pages.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hold a book the right way up and say or show that it is a book" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BOOK
- FRONT

**Picture suggestions:**
- A book
- A child holding a book

**Song or rhyme:** Open the book, turn the page. Look and see, look and see.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Turning the pages

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Holding a book and turning pages

### Learning objectives
- Children will turn the pages of a book one at a time.

### Success criteria
- Turns one page at a time with support
- Says "page" or "more"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join in rhymes, name animals and greet others, and many point to pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, picture, turn, look, read

**Instructional materials:** A large picture board book; A cushion; A book corner

### Starter
Open the book slowly and say "Open, open, what can we see?" Turn a page and say "Another page."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a page and say "Turn the page" while turning it. Children: Children watch and say "page".

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child come forward to turn a page of the big book. Children: Child turns the page with support.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What is on this page?" and wait. Children: Child points to or names the picture.

Step 4. Teacher: Let the children turn the pages of their own books. Children: Child turns pages one at a time.

### Practical activity: Book corner
1. Set up cushions and board books.
2. Each child chooses a book and turns pages.
3. Name a picture on each page with a friend.

**Safety:** Keep the book corner uncluttered. Show children to sit with space between them and to handle books gently.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a book with thick pages and turn the pages together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to turn the pages for a friend and name the pictures.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you turn the page?
  **Expected response:** Child turns a page.
- **Ask:** What is on this page?
  **Expected response:** Child names a picture.

### Expected outcomes
- Turns pages one at a time with support
- Names a picture

### Conclusion
Close the book and say "The end."

### Home connection
Let your child turn the pages while you point to the pictures.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "turn the pages of a book one at a time" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- TURN THE PAGE

**Picture suggestions:**
- A hand turning a page

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Pointing to pictures

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Pointing to and naming pictures

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to and name pictures in a book.

### Success criteria
- Points to a named picture
- Names a picture

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join in rhymes, name animals and greet others, and many point to pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, picture, turn, look, read

**Instructional materials:** A picture book with clear pictures of a dog, a ball, a cup and a baby; A cushion

### Starter
Open the book and say "Where is the dog? Point to the dog." Point yourself first.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Point to each picture and say its name clearly. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say a name and ask "Where is the ball?" Children: Children point to the ball.

Step 3. Teacher: Point to a picture and ask "What is this?" and wait. Children: Child names the picture or says a sound.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child point to a picture and ask "What is this?" Children: Child points and the group names it.

### Practical activity: I spy a picture
1. Open a book to a page with four pictures.
2. Say "I spy a dog."
3. Children point to the dog. Repeat for the other pictures.

**Safety:** Keep the book low so children can see. Remind children to touch pictures gently and not tear pages.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point together and say the name before the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name three pictures in a row.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is the cup?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the cup.
- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Child names the picture.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to named pictures
- Names at least three pictures

### Conclusion
Close the book and say "Goodbye, dog, ball, cup, baby."

### Home connection
Point to pictures in a book at home and ask "Where is the ball?"

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to and name pictures in a book" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- DOG
- BALL
- CUP
- BABY

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): My favourite book

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** A favourite book

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose a favourite book and share it with a friend.

### Success criteria
- Chooses a book
- Shows or tells a friend about a picture

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join in rhymes, name animals and greet others, and many point to pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, picture, turn, look, read

**Instructional materials:** Four different board books; Cushions; A basket

### Starter
Put four books on the mat and say "Which book do you like? Choose one."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each book front cover and say its name or topic. Children: Children look and point to the one they like.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child pick a book and sit with a friend. Children: Child chooses and sits with a friend.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask each pair to look at the book together. Children: Children turn pages and point to pictures.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk round and ask "What do you like?" Children: Child shows a picture or says a word.

### Practical activity: Share with a friend
1. Set up cushions in pairs.
2. Each pair has one book.
3. Children take turns to turn pages and point.

**Safety:** Check that books have no loose parts. Sit friends with space so they do not squash the book.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sit with the child and point to the pictures.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell the friend about one page.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which book do you like?
  **Expected response:** Child points to a book.
- **Ask:** What do you see?
  **Expected response:** Child names a picture.

### Expected outcomes
- Chooses a favourite book
- Shares a picture with a friend

### Conclusion
Put the books in the basket and say "Thank you, books."

### Home connection
Let your child choose a book each night and share it with you.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose a favourite book and share it with a friend" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MY BOOK

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children sharing a book

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 book check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Pointing to and naming pictures

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child handles a book and names pictures.

### Success criteria
- Holds a book the right way up
- Names pictures in a book

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join in rhymes, name animals and greet others, and many point to pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, picture, turn, look, read

**Instructional materials:** Board books; Picture cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the book song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: book corner, picture cards and big book. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is this?" and "Can you turn the page?" Children: Child names the picture and turns a page.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Book stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use sturdy books and large cards. Supervise each station and keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two pictures.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell a friend a story from the pictures.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: holds a book the right way up; turns pages; names three pictures.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Child names the picture.
- **Ask:** Can you turn the page?
  **Expected response:** Child turns a page.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Handles a book correctly

### Conclusion
Close the books and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Share a book every day and name the pictures together.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child handles a book and names pictures" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
