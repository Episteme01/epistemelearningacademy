# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 5: Speaking

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W05 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Good morning and goodbye; My name and my friends’ names; Please and thank you

## Learning objectives
- Children will say or sign a greeting to an adult or friend.
- Children will say or point to their own name and a friend’s name.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, goodbye, name, please, thank you

**Content:** Greeting adults and friends, learning names, and saying please and thank you.

**Teaching activities:** Greet every child by name each morning; use a wave and a word together; model "Good morning ma" and "Good morning sir" as the school culture requires; praise every attempt.

**Learner activities:** Say or sign hello and goodbye, say or point to their own name and a friend’s, and use please and thank you.

**Resources:** A hello puppet; Name cards with photos; A basket of toys; A mirror

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child greets someone, responds to their name and uses please or thank you with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Greets someone with a word or wave.
- Responds to their own name.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Social Habits: greetings and manners; Personal, Social and Emotional Development: friendship; Understanding the World: me and my family

## Day 1 (Monday): Hello and goodbye

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Good morning and goodbye

### Learning objectives
- Children will say or wave hello and goodbye.

### Success criteria
- Waves or says hello
- Waves or says goodbye

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, make sounds and copy rhythms; many use single words or gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, goodbye, name, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** A hello puppet; A mat for circle time

### Starter
Wave the puppet and say "Hello, hello!" Wave and say "Goodbye, goodbye!" Ask the children to wave.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Have the puppet greet each child by name with a wave. Children: Child waves or says hello back.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Good morning" and wave. Say "Goodbye" and wave. Children: Children copy the words and wave.

Step 3. Teacher: Greet the children as a group and ask "Can you say hello?" Children: Children say hello or wave.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child take the puppet and say hello or goodbye to a friend. Children: Child greets a friend with a wave or a word.

### Practical activity: Greeting circle
1. Sit in a circle and pass the puppet.
2. The child holding the puppet greets the next child.
3. The next child greets back and passes it on.

**Safety:** Keep the circle wide enough for safe passing. Do not force a shy child to speak; accept a wave or a smile.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Wave with the child and say hello together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to greet the head teacher or visitor with a word.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say when we meet someone?
  **Expected response:** Hello or good morning.
- **Ask:** What do we say when we go?
  **Expected response:** Goodbye.

### Expected outcomes
- Greets someone with a wave or word
- Says goodbye with a wave or word

### Conclusion
Say goodbye to the puppet and wave to each other.

### Home connection
Greet your child with "Good morning" each day and wait for a wave or word.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say or wave hello and goodbye" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HELLO
- GOODBYE

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child waving hello
- A child waving goodbye

**Song or rhyme:** Hello, hello, how are you? Hello, hello, I am fine.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): My name

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** My name and my friends’ names

### Learning objectives
- Children will respond to their name and point to their own name card.

### Success criteria
- Looks or answers when their name is called
- Points to their own name card with a photo

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, make sounds and copy rhythms; many use single words or gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, goodbye, name, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** Name cards with photos; A basket; A mirror

### Starter
Say "Hello, [child’s name]" and wave. Look at each child and say their name with a smile.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold up each name card and say the name. Ask "Who is this?" Children: Children look and say or point to themselves.

Step 2. Teacher: Look in the mirror together and say "I am [name]." Children: Child looks and says or points to their name.

Step 3. Teacher: Put three name cards on the mat and ask the child to find theirs. Children: Child points to their card.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Hello" to each child by name and wait for a response. Children: Child waves, smiles or says their name.

### Practical activity: Name cards
1. Place three name cards on the mat.
2. Each child finds their own card and holds it up.
3. The group says the name together.

**Safety:** Keep cards smooth and large so they can be held safely. Handle cards gently and do not tear them.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a photo card and point to the child’s picture.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a friend’s name card and say the name.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is your name?
  **Expected response:** Child says or points to their name.
- **Ask:** Where is your card?
  **Expected response:** Child points to their card.

### Expected outcomes
- Responds to their own name
- Finds their own name card with support

### Conclusion
Put the cards in the basket and wave goodbye to each name.

### Home connection
Say your child’s name often and ask them to say it back to you.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "respond to their name and point to their own name card" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- My name card (with photo)

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of children

## Day 3 (Wednesday): My friends

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** My name and my friends’ names

### Learning objectives
- Children will say or point to a friend’s name.

### Success criteria
- Points to a friend when the name is said
- Says a friend’s name with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, make sounds and copy rhythms; many use single words or gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, goodbye, name, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** Name cards with photos; A ball for rolling

### Starter
Roll the ball to a child and say "Hello [name]." Ask the child to roll it back.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sit in a circle and roll the ball to a child, naming them. The child rolls it back. Children: Child receives the ball and rolls it on.

Step 2. Teacher: Say a friend’s name and ask "Where is [name]?" Children: Children point to the friend.

Step 3. Teacher: Show a photo card of a friend and ask "Who is this?" Children: Child names or points to the friend.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child roll the ball to a friend and say their name. Children: Child names a friend and rolls the ball.

### Practical activity: Ball and names
1. Sit in a circle with space between children.
2. Roll the ball to a friend and say their name.
3. The friend says hello and rolls it on.

**Safety:** Roll the ball slowly along the floor and keep fingers clear. Sit children with legs apart so they are safe.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Roll the ball together and say the name with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say a friend’s name and one thing the friend likes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is this friend?
  **Expected response:** Child names or points to the friend.
- **Ask:** Can you say hello to a friend?
  **Expected response:** Child says hello.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to a friend by name
- Says a friend’s name with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to each friend by name.

### Home connection
Talk about your child’s friends by name and ask them to say hello.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say or point to a friend’s name" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Friends

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of friends

## Day 4 (Thursday): Please and thank you

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Please and thank you

### Learning objectives
- Children will use please and thank you when asking for or receiving something.

### Success criteria
- Says or signs please
- Says or signs thank you

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, make sounds and copy rhythms; many use single words or gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, goodbye, name, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** A basket of toys; A hello puppet; Snack pieces of fruit

### Starter
Hold a toy and say "Please." Give it to the puppet and say "Thank you." Ask "What did I say?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show that when you want a toy you say "Please" and when you get it you say "Thank you". Children: Children watch and repeat the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Hold out a toy and wait for the child to say or sign please. Children: Child says please or makes the sign.

Step 3. Teacher: Give the toy and say "Thank you" and wait. Children: Child says thank you or signs.

Step 4. Teacher: Pass fruit slices and encourage please and thank you. Children: Child says please and thank you.

### Practical activity: Toy shop
1. Set up a toy shop with a basket of toys.
2. Each child asks for a toy with please.
3. They say thank you when they get it.

**Safety:** Check for allergies before giving any food and use only washed fruit. Supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a sign for please and say it with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the shopkeeper and give toys to friends.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say when we ask?
  **Expected response:** Please.
- **Ask:** What do we say when we get something?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses please with support
- Uses thank you with support

### Conclusion
Say "Thank you" together and wave.

### Home connection
Model please and thank you at mealtimes and wait for your child to say them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use please and thank you when asking for or receiving something" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- PLEASE
- THANK YOU

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child asking politely
- A child saying thank you

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 greetings check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Good morning and goodbye

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child greets others and responds to their name.

### Success criteria
- Greets someone with a word or wave
- Responds to their own name

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, make sounds and copy rhythms; many use single words or gestures.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, goodbye, name, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** A hello puppet; Name cards with photos; Toys; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the hello song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: hello puppet, name cards and toy shop. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station greet the child and wait for a response. Ask "What is your name?" Children: Child greets, responds or points.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Greeting stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use soft toys and large cards. Supervise each station and keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and accept gestures.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to greet a visitor.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: greets with a word or wave; responds to their own name; says or signs please and thank you.

- **Ask:** What do you say when you meet someone?
  **Expected response:** Hello or good morning.
- **Ask:** Where is your name card?
  **Expected response:** Child points to it.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Greets with a word or wave

### Conclusion
Sing the goodbye song and wave to everyone.

### Home connection
Practise hello, goodbye, please and thank you at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child greets others and responds to their name" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Hello

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
