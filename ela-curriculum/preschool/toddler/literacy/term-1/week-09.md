# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 9: Stories

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Listening to a short story; Joining in with a repeated line; Acting the story

## Learning objectives
- Children will listen to a story with pictures and objects.
- Children will join in with a repeated line and actions.

**Key vocabulary:** story, once, then, the end, listen, act

**Content:** Listening to a short story with pictures and objects, joining in with a repeated line, and acting the story.

**Teaching activities:** Tell the same story all week using objects and a simple repeated line; use a different voice for each character; pause before the repeated line; let children act each part.

**Learner activities:** Listen to a short story, join in the repeated line and act the story with actions.

**Resources:** A story with a repeated line: "The Hungry Goat" written by the teacher; A toy goat, a basket, a leaf; Picture cards of the story; A scarf

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child listens to the story and joins in the repeated line.

## Expected outcomes
- Listens to a short story.
- Joins in with a repeated line.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: animals; Physical Development: acting; Social Habits: listening to others

## Day 1 (Monday): The hungry goat

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Listening to a short story

### Learning objectives
- Children will listen to a short story with pictures and objects.

### Success criteria
- Listens to the story for a short time
- Points to a picture in the story

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name pictures and objects, join in rhymes and handle a book.

**Key vocabulary:** story, once, then, the end, listen, act

**Instructional materials:** A toy goat; A basket of leaves; Three picture cards

### Starter
Hold up the toy goat and say "This is Goat. Goat is hungry. Meh, meh, meh." Ask "What does Goat want?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the story: "Goat was hungry. He looked for food. He found a leaf. Munch, munch. Goat was happy." Show a picture for each part. Children: Children listen and look at the pictures.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Who is hungry?" and wait. Children: Child points to Goat or says goat.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What did Goat find?" Children: Child points to the leaf.

Step 4. Teacher: Tell the story again with the toy goat and let a child feed the goat a leaf. Children: Child feeds the goat and says "munch".

### Practical activity: Feed the goat
1. Place the basket of leaves on the mat.
2. Each child feeds the toy goat a leaf.
3. Everyone says "munch, munch" together.

**Safety:** Use clean paper or washed leaves and make sure no child puts leaves in their mouth. Wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the goat together and point to each picture.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend what Goat found.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is in the story?
  **Expected response:** Goat.
- **Ask:** What did Goat eat?
  **Expected response:** A leaf.

### Expected outcomes
- Listens to a short story
- Points to the story pictures

### Conclusion
Put Goat to sleep and whisper "Goodnight, Goat."

### Home connection
Tell your child a short story at bedtime with a toy and point to the pictures.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "listen to a short story with pictures and objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- GOAT
- LEAF

**Picture suggestions:**
- A goat
- A leaf
- A happy goat

**Short story:** Goat was hungry. He looked for food. He found a leaf. Munch, munch. Goat was happy.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Munch, munch

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Joining in with a repeated line

### Learning objectives
- Children will join in the repeated line "Munch, munch, munch".

### Success criteria
- Says or mimes "munch, munch"
- Joins in at the right time

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name pictures and objects, join in rhymes and handle a book.

**Key vocabulary:** story, once, then, the end, listen, act

**Instructional materials:** A toy goat; Picture cards; A basket

### Starter
Hold up the goat and say "Munch, munch, munch." Ask "Can you munch like Goat?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the story again and say the repeated line "Munch, munch, munch" at every page. Children: Children listen for the line.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell the story and pause before the line. Children: Children say "munch, munch" or mime chewing.

Step 3. Teacher: Show each picture and wait for the line. Children: Children say the line at the right time.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child be Goat and say the line while the others join in. Children: Child leads and friends join in.

### Practical activity: Munch with Goat
1. Sit in a circle with a toy goat.
2. Say the story and wait for the repeated line.
3. Pass Goat round and everyone says "munch".

**Safety:** Keep toys clean and large. Supervise turn-taking and do not allow children to put toys in their mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say the line together and mime chewing.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say the line in a quiet voice and a loud voice.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does Goat say?
  **Expected response:** Munch, munch, munch.
- **Ask:** Can you munch?
  **Expected response:** Child mimes chewing.

### Expected outcomes
- Joins in the repeated line
- Mimes the action

### Conclusion
Say the repeated line one last time and wave.

### Home connection
Tell the story at home and ask your child to say "munch, munch".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "join in the repeated line "Munch, munch, munch"" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MUNCH, MUNCH

**Picture suggestions:**
- A goat eating

**Short story:** Goat was hungry. Munch, munch, munch. He found a leaf. Munch, munch, munch.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Acting the story

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Acting the story

### Learning objectives
- Children will act out parts of the story.

### Success criteria
- Acts as Goat
- Does an action for part of the story

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name pictures and objects, join in rhymes and handle a book.

**Key vocabulary:** story, once, then, the end, listen, act

**Instructional materials:** A toy goat; A scarf as a leaf; A clear space

### Starter
Say "Let us be Goat. Walk like Goat. Meh, meh." Walk on all fours or use a gentle walk.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the story and show actions for each part: look, find, munch, happy. Children: Children watch and copy the actions.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell the story again and let the children act at each part. Children: Children act as Goat.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask one child to find the scarf leaf. Children: Child finds the scarf and munches.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child take a turn as Goat. Children: Child acts as Goat and friends say "munch".

### Practical activity: Be Goat
1. Clear a space on the mat.
2. Children walk like Goat, look for a leaf and munch.
3. Everyone says "meh" and "munch".

**Safety:** Clear the floor and keep children apart so they do not bump. Use soft scarves only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Act beside the child and hold hands if needed.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the storyteller and say what Goat does.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does Goat do?
  **Expected response:** Looks, finds and munches.
- **Ask:** Can you walk like Goat?
  **Expected response:** Child walks like Goat.

### Expected outcomes
- Acts parts of a story
- Uses actions with the story words

### Conclusion
Settle Goat down and say "The end."

### Home connection
Act a short story at home with your child and use actions.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "act out parts of the story" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- ACT
- GOAT

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children acting as goats

## Day 4 (Thursday): Retell with pictures

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Joining in with a repeated line

### Learning objectives
- Children will look at pictures and say words about the story.

### Success criteria
- Points to a picture from the story
- Says a word for what they see

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name pictures and objects, join in rhymes and handle a book.

**Key vocabulary:** story, once, then, the end, listen, act

**Instructional materials:** Three picture cards: hungry goat, leaf, happy goat; A toy goat

### Starter
Show the three pictures and say "Look at Goat’s story. What is happening?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the pictures on the floor and point to each. Children: Children look and point.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Where is the hungry goat?" and wait. Children: Child points to the hungry goat.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "What did Goat find?" and point. Children: Child says "leaf" or points.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child tell a friend about a picture. Children: Child says a word and the friend listens.

### Practical activity: Picture talk
1. Lay three pictures on the mat.
2. Each child points to one and says a word.
3. The group says the repeated line together.

**Safety:** Use large pictures with no sharp corners. Supervise turn-taking.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two pictures and say the word with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put the pictures in order and say first, then.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this picture?
  **Expected response:** Goat or leaf.
- **Ask:** Is Goat happy?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Names the pictures in the story
- Says a word about the story

### Conclusion
Put the pictures in the basket and say "The end."

### Home connection
Ask your child to tell you what happened in the goat story.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "look at pictures and say words about the story" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HUNGRY
- HAPPY

**Picture suggestions:**
- A hungry goat
- A leaf
- A happy goat

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 9 story check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Listening to a short story

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child listens to a story and joins in.

### Success criteria
- Listens to a story
- Joins in the repeated line

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name pictures and objects, join in rhymes and handle a book.

**Key vocabulary:** story, once, then, the end, listen, act

**Instructional materials:** Toy goat; Picture cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "Munch, munch" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: story listening, acting and picture talk. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell a short story at each station and wait for the repeated line. Children: Child joins in and acts.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Story stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use soft toys and large pictures. Supervise each station and keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one picture.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell the story to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: listens to a story; joins in the repeated line; acts a part.

- **Ask:** What does Goat say?
  **Expected response:** Munch, munch.
- **Ask:** What happened?
  **Expected response:** Goat found a leaf.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Joins in a story

### Conclusion
Say "The end" and wave goodbye to Goat.

### Home connection
Tell a short story every night and pause for your child to join in.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child listens to a story and joins in" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

- Story
