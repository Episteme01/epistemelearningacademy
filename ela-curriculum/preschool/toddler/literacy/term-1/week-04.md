# Toddler Literacy — Term 1, Week 4: Rhythm

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W04 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Drums and shakers; Clapping and stamping; Fast and slow

## Learning objectives
- Children will copy a short beat with an instrument or their body.
- Children will say fast or slow with support.

**Key vocabulary:** drum, shaker, clap, stamp, fast, slow

**Content:** Playing drums and shakers gently, clapping and stamping beats, and fast and slow sounds.

**Teaching activities:** Play two-beat patterns and wait for children to copy; show fast with quick hands and slow with big gentle hands; model gentle playing and put instruments away together.

**Learner activities:** Play drums and shakers, clap and stamp beats, copy a short pattern and say fast or slow.

**Resources:** Two drums; Shakers made from bottles and rice; Rhythm sticks; A tortoise and a rabbit picture; A scarf

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child copies a two-beat pattern and plays an instrument gently.

## Expected outcomes
- Copies a two-beat pattern.
- Plays an instrument gently.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Music: beat and rhythm; Physical Development: coordination; Social Habits: sharing instruments

## Day 1 (Monday): Drums and shakers

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Drums and shakers

### Learning objectives
- Children will play a drum and a shaker gently.

### Success criteria
- Plays a drum with a soft hand
- Shakes a shaker gently

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals, make animal sounds and change the loudness of their voices.

**Key vocabulary:** drum, shaker, clap, stamp, fast, slow

**Instructional materials:** Two drums; Four shakers made from sealed bottles with rice; A basket

### Starter
Show a drum and say "This is a drum. Boom, boom." Show a shaker and say "This is a shaker. Shh, shh."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Play the drum with a soft hand and say "Gentle hands." Shake the shaker and name it. Children: Children watch and say "drum" and "shaker".

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a shaker and say "Shake, shake, shake" and "Stop". Children: Children shake and stop on the signal.

Step 3. Teacher: Pass the drum round the circle so each child plays once. Children: Child plays the drum gently and passes it on.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Put it back in the basket" and collect each instrument. Children: Child puts the instrument in the basket.

### Practical activity: Play and stop
1. Sit in a circle and give everyone a shaker.
2. Play music and shake along.
3. Stop the music and everyone holds still.

**Safety:** Check each shaker is sealed with tape and cannot be opened. Keep instruments out of mouths and the volume gentle.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the shaker with the child and shake together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the leader and tell friends when to shake and when to stop.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** A drum or a shaker.
- **Ask:** Can you play gently?
  **Expected response:** Child plays softly.

### Expected outcomes
- Names a drum and a shaker
- Plays an instrument gently

### Conclusion
Put the instruments in the basket and say "Drum, shaker, goodbye."

### Home connection
At home, make a shaker with a sealed bottle and a few beans and shake with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "play a drum and a shaker gently" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- DRUM
- SHAKER

**Picture suggestions:**
- A drum
- A shaker

**Song or rhyme:** Shake, shake, shake your shaker. Stop, stop, stop.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Clap and stamp

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Clapping and stamping

### Learning objectives
- Children will copy a clap and a stamp.

### Success criteria
- Claps on request
- Stamps on request

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals, make animal sounds and change the loudness of their voices.

**Key vocabulary:** drum, shaker, clap, stamp, fast, slow

**Instructional materials:** A drum; A clear space on the mat; A scarf

### Starter
Clap twice and say "Clap, clap." Stamp twice and say "Stamp, stamp." Ask the children to copy.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Clap once and wait for the children to clap once. Stamp once and wait for them to stamp. Children: Children copy each action.

Step 2. Teacher: Play a two-beat pattern: clap, clap. Then stamp, stamp. Children: Children copy the pattern.

Step 3. Teacher: Mix the actions: clap, stamp. Children: Children copy the mixed pattern with support.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child choose clap or stamp and lead the group. Children: Child leads and the group copies.

### Practical activity: Echo clapping
1. Stand in a circle with room to move.
2. The teacher plays a short pattern.
3. Children echo the pattern back.

**Safety:** Leave space so children do not bump each other. Stamp gently on the floor and stop if anyone is tired.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Clap with the child’s hands and stamp beside them.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a two-beat pattern for friends to copy.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you clap?
  **Expected response:** Child claps.
- **Ask:** Show me stamp, stamp.
  **Expected response:** Child stamps twice.

### Expected outcomes
- Copies a clap and a stamp
- Copies a two-beat pattern with support

### Conclusion
Finish with one big clap and a soft stamp.

### Home connection
Clap a pattern at home and ask your child to clap it back.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "copy a clap and a stamp" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- CLAP
- STAMP

**Picture suggestions:**
- Hands clapping
- Feet stamping

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Fast and slow

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Fast and slow

### Learning objectives
- Children will clap or play fast and slow.

### Success criteria
- Claps fast when asked
- Claps slow when asked

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals, make animal sounds and change the loudness of their voices.

**Key vocabulary:** drum, shaker, clap, stamp, fast, slow

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a rabbit and a tortoise; A drum; A scarf

### Starter
Show the rabbit and clap fast. Show the tortoise and clap slowly. Say "Fast, slow."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tap the drum fast for the rabbit and slow for the tortoise. Children: Children watch and listen, then copy with their hands.

Step 2. Teacher: Show a card and ask the children to clap fast or slow to match. Children: Children clap at the right speed.

Step 3. Teacher: Move the scarf fast or slowly and ask the children to move their hands the same way. Children: Children match the scarf speed.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a card and play the drum. Children: Child plays fast or slow.

### Practical activity: Rabbit and tortoise
1. Place the two pictures on the floor.
2. Walk fast like a rabbit or slow like a tortoise.
3. Stop when the drum stops.

**Safety:** Keep an adult at the front and back and watch that children do not run into each other. Clear the floor first.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use one card at a time and move with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say fast or slow and lead the movement.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the rabbit fast or slow?
  **Expected response:** Fast.
- **Ask:** Show me slow.
  **Expected response:** Child moves or claps slowly.

### Expected outcomes
- Claps fast and slow on request
- Says fast or slow with support

### Conclusion
Slow down your clapping until it stops, and whisper "slow".

### Home connection
Play fast and slow with a spoon on a pot at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "clap or play fast and slow" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FAST (rabbit)
- SLOW (tortoise)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A rabbit
- A tortoise

**Song or rhyme:** Rabbit goes fast, fast, fast. Tortoise goes slow, slow, slow.

## Day 4 (Thursday): Our little band

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Drums and shakers

### Learning objectives
- Children will play together in a small band and follow the leader.

### Success criteria
- Plays an instrument with the group
- Stops when the leader stops

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals, make animal sounds and change the loudness of their voices.

**Key vocabulary:** drum, shaker, clap, stamp, fast, slow

**Instructional materials:** Drums; Shakers; Rhythm sticks; A conductor’s scarf

### Starter
Wave the scarf and say "I am the band leader. When the scarf goes up, we play. When it goes down, we stop."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give each child an instrument. Wave the scarf up and children play, then down and they stop. Children: Children play and stop on the signal.

Step 2. Teacher: Show loud, then quiet with the scarf. Show fast, then slow. Children: Children change their playing to match.

Step 3. Teacher: Let two children take turns as the band leader. Children: Leaders hold the scarf and the band follows.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap instruments and play again. Children: Children play a different instrument.

### Practical activity: Little band
1. Give out the instruments.
2. The leader waves the scarf up and down.
3. Swap instruments and leaders after two rounds.

**Safety:** Check that instruments are safe and sealed. Keep the volume gentle and give quiet breaks.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sit beside the child and play the same instrument.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the band leader and show the group loud or quiet.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do when the scarf goes down?
  **Expected response:** Stop playing.
- **Ask:** Can you play with your friends?
  **Expected response:** Child plays with the group.

### Expected outcomes
- Plays with a group
- Follows the leader to start and stop

### Conclusion
Take a bow and put the instruments in the basket.

### Home connection
Make a band with pots and spoons at home and stop when you say "stop".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "play together in a small band and follow the leader" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BAND
- Play
- Stop

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children playing drums and shakers

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 4 rhythm check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T1-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Clapping and stamping

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child copies beats and plays an instrument.

### Success criteria
- Copies a two-beat pattern
- Plays an instrument gently

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals, make animal sounds and change the loudness of their voices.

**Key vocabulary:** drum, shaker, clap, stamp, fast, slow

**Instructional materials:** Drums and shakers; Fast and slow picture cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Play a short beat and say "Can you copy me?" Show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: echo clapping, instruments and fast and slow. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: Play a two-beat pattern at each station and ask the child to copy it. Children: Child copies the pattern.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Rhythm stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use sealed shakers and soft drums only. Supervise each station and keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one-beat patterns.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to make a pattern for friends to copy.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: copies a two-beat pattern; plays gently; says or shows fast and slow.

- **Ask:** Can you copy my clap?
  **Expected response:** Child claps the same pattern.
- **Ask:** Is this fast or slow?
  **Expected response:** Child says or shows the speed.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Copies a short rhythm

### Conclusion
Take a bow and say goodbye to the instruments.

### Home connection
Practise clap, clap and stamp, stamp at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child copies beats and plays an instrument" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Clap

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
