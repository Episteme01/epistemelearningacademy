# Toddler Literacy — Term 3, Week 2: First sounds

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Listening for /a/; Things that start with /a/; The /a/ song

## Learning objectives
- Children will hear and say the sound /a/ at the start of words.
- Children will find objects that start with /a/ with support.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, apple, axe, sound, aaa

**Content:** Listening for the sound /a/ at the start of words, finding things that start with /a/, and the /a/ song.

**Teaching activities:** Say /a/ as a short, clear sound as in "ant", with no extra sound; show an open mouth; link the sound to an ant that wiggles; use real objects; children hear and say the sound only.

**Learner activities:** Hear and say the sound /a/ at the start of words and find objects that start with /a/ with support.

**Resources:** A toy ant or a picture of an ant; A toy apple; A toy axe or a picture; A mirror; A tray

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says /a/ clearly and points to an object that starts with /a/.

## Expected outcomes
- Says /a/ clearly.
- Points to an object that starts with /a/.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: insects; Music: sound songs; Physical Development: mouth shapes

## Day 1 (Monday): The ant says a, a, a

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Listening for /a/

### Learning objectives
- Children will hear and say the sound /a/ as the ant sound.

### Success criteria
- Says the sound a when shown the ant
- Watches the teacher’s mouth

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say the sound /s/ and find objects that start with it.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, apple, axe, sound, aaa

**Instructional materials:** A toy ant or a picture of an ant; A mirror; A mat

### Starter
Hold up the ant and say "This is an ant. The ant says a, a, a. Tickle, tickle, tickle." Wiggle your fingers.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "a, a, a" in a short clear sound with your mouth open. Show how your mouth is open. Children: Children watch your mouth and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "a, a, a" together while the ant crawls on your arm. Children: Children say "a" with you and wiggle their fingers.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "a" softly and loudly. Children: Children copy a soft a and a loud a.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child look in the mirror and say a. Children: Child watches their mouth and says a.

### Practical activity: Ant sound
1. Give each child a pretend ant with their fingers.
2. Crawl the ant up your arm and say a, a, a.
3. Stop and listen quietly.

**Safety:** Tell children the ant is a toy. Keep the sound gentle and watch that no one touches a real ant.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Crawl the ant on the child’s arm and say a together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend what the ant says.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the ant say?
  **Expected response:** A, a, a.
- **Ask:** Can you make the ant sound?
  **Expected response:** Child says a.

### Expected outcomes
- Says the sound /a/ clearly
- Links /a/ to the ant

### Conclusion
Put the ant to bed with a quiet a.

### Home connection
Crawl fingers up your child’s arm and say a, a, a.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hear and say the sound /a/ as the ant sound" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- A (an ant)

**Picture suggestions:**
- An ant
- A child saying a

**Song or rhyme:** The ant says a, a, a. Tickle, tickle, tickle.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Ant, apple and axe

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Things that start with /a/

### Learning objectives
- Children will hear that ant, apple and axe start with the sound /a/.

### Success criteria
- Repeats the word and the first sound
- Points to an object that starts with /a/

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say the sound /s/ and find objects that start with it.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, apple, axe, sound, aaa

**Instructional materials:** A toy ant, a toy apple, a picture of an axe; A tray; A bag

### Starter
Hold up the apple and say "Apple. A-pple. It starts with a." Show the ant and say "Ant. A-nt."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say each word slowly and stress the first sound: a-nt, a-pple, a-xe. Children: Children repeat the word and the first sound.

Step 2. Teacher: Put the three things on a tray and say "These all start with a." Children: Children touch each and say a.

Step 3. Teacher: Add a ball and ask "Does ball start with a?" Children: Child shakes head or says no.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick an a-object and say the word. Children: Child picks and says the word and a.

### Practical activity: A tray
1. Put the ant, apple, axe picture and a ball on a tray.
2. Ask each child to take something that starts with a.
3. Say the word and the sound.

**Safety:** Use a toy apple and a picture for the axe. Never use a real axe. Keep objects clean and supervise handling.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two things, one that starts with a, and say the sound first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another thing that starts with a.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What sound does apple start with?
  **Expected response:** The sound a.
- **Ask:** Does ball start with a?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Hears /a/ at the start of words
- Picks objects that start with /a/ with support

### Conclusion
Put the objects in the bag and say a.

### Home connection
Look for things that start with a, such as an apple, and say the sound.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hear that ant, apple and axe start with the sound /a/" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- ANT
- APPLE
- AXE

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): The a song

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** The /a/ song

### Learning objectives
- Children will sing a song about the sound /a/ with actions.

### Success criteria
- Sings or hums the a song
- Does the ant action

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say the sound /s/ and find objects that start with it.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, apple, axe, sound, aaa

**Instructional materials:** A toy ant; Picture cards: ant, apple, axe; A basket

### Starter
Crawl the ant up your arm and sing "a, a, a". Ask "Who knows the ant song?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing the song slowly: "A, a, a, ant and apple and axe. A, a, a, what can you pack?" Show the cards. Children: Children listen and look at the cards.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing again and do the ant action. Children: Children do the ant action and say a.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing again and let children hold up a card at the right word. Children: Child holds up the ant, apple or axe card.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child lead the song with the ant. Children: Child leads and friends sing.

### Practical activity: Ant parade
1. Give each child a card from the song.
2. Sing the song and parade round the mat.
3. Hold up the card at the right word.

**Safety:** Clear the floor so children can walk freely. Keep the volume gentle and use soft cards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sing softly with the child and do the actions together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sing the song to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What sound do we sing?
  **Expected response:** The sound a.
- **Ask:** Which things start with a?
  **Expected response:** Ant, apple and axe.

### Expected outcomes
- Sings the a song with actions
- Names ant, apple and axe

### Conclusion
Sing a quiet last verse and put the ant to bed.

### Home connection
Sing the ant song at home with finger actions.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sing a song about the sound /a/ with actions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- ANT
- APPLE
- AXE

**Picture suggestions:**
- An ant
- An apple
- An axe picture

**Song or rhyme:** A, a, a, ant and apple and axe. A, a, a, what can you pack?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Find the a things

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Things that start with /a/

### Learning objectives
- Children will find objects that start with /a/ in a sound hunt.

### Success criteria
- Finds an a-object with support
- Says the word and the sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say the sound /s/ and find objects that start with it.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, apple, axe, sound, aaa

**Instructional materials:** Six objects, three starting with /a/ and three that do not; A basket; A bag

### Starter
Say "Let us hunt for a things. A, a, a." Show the empty basket.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hide the objects around the room in easy places. Children: Children watch where you put the objects.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Find an a thing" and let each child look. Children: Child finds an object and brings it back.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Does it start with a?" and say the word. Children: Child says yes or no and the word.

Step 4. Teacher: Put the a things in the basket and the others on the mat. Children: Child sorts the objects.

### Practical activity: A hunt
1. Hide six objects in easy places in the room.
2. Children find objects and bring them to the mat.
3. Sort the a objects into the basket.

**Safety:** Hide objects only in clear, low places away from hazards. Supervise the search and keep children together.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk with the child and point to the object after saying the sound.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to hide an a-object for a friend to find.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does apple start with a?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** Does cup start with a?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds objects starting with /a/ with support
- Says the word and sound

### Conclusion
Count the objects in the basket and say a.

### Home connection
Play a sound hunt at home for things that start with a.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find objects that start with /a/ in a sound hunt" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- A HUNT

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children searching
- A basket of objects

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 a check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Things that start with /a/

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child hears and says /a/.

### Success criteria
- Says /a/ clearly
- Points to an object that starts with /a/

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say the sound /s/ and find objects that start with it.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, apple, axe, sound, aaa

**Instructional materials:** A-objects; Other objects; A toy ant; Class record sheet

### Starter
Crawl the ant and say "a". Show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: ant sound, a-objects and the song. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What sound?" and "Which one starts with a?" Children: Child says a and points.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Sound stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use large, clean objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two objects.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to name two more a words.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says /a/ clearly; points to an object starting with /a/; joins in the song.

- **Ask:** What sound does the ant make?
  **Expected response:** The sound a.
- **Ask:** Show me something that starts with a.
  **Expected response:** Ant, apple or axe.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Says /a/ clearly

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the ant with a soft a.

### Home connection
Play sound games at home with the sound a.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
