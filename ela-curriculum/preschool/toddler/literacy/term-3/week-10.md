# Toddler Literacy — Term 3, Week 10: Revision and assessment

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W10 · **Week type:** revision-assessment · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Revising listening, rhymes and sounds; Revising stories and books; Celebration and end-of-year record

## Learning objectives
- Children will show Year learning through games and stations.
- Teachers will complete every child’s end-of-year record and transition note.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, rhyme, sound, story, book, name

**Content:** Revising listening, rhymes and sounds, stories and books, and celebrating the year with end-of-year records and a transition note.

**Teaching activities:** Keep every activity familiar and joyful; observe quietly; record what children can do; invite parents to the celebration; complete the transition note honestly for the next teacher.

**Learner activities:** Show what they know through games and stations and enjoy a literacy celebration.

**Resources:** Songs and rhymes from the year; The four sound objects; Books, name cards and story boxes; Children’s own books; Certificates or stickers; End-of-year record sheets

**Assessment:** Observe and record each child’s end-of-year literacy learning and complete the transition note.

## Expected outcomes
- Takes part confidently in familiar literacy games.
- Each child has an end-of-year observation record.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Music: favourite songs; Understanding the World: our school; Social Habits: celebration and thanks

## Day 1 (Monday): Revising listening, rhymes and sounds

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W10-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** revision · **Subtopic:** Revising listening, rhymes and sounds

### Learning objectives
- Children will revisit sound games, rhymes and the four sounds s, a, t and p.

### Success criteria
- Says at least two of the four sounds
- Joins in a favourite rhyme

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on listening, speaking, rhymes, stories, early sounds, mark-making, names and books across the year.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, rhyme, sound, story, book, name

**Instructional materials:** Sound objects; Rhyme cards; A drum; A bell

### Starter
Ring the bell and say "Shh, listen." Say "Today we play our favourite sound and rhyme games."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Play listening statues with the bell. Children: Children stop and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing a favourite rhyme and pause for the last word. Children: Children finish the rhyme.

Step 3. Teacher: Play the sound sort game with sss, a, t and p. Children: Children sort by first sound with support.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a favourite game. Children: Child chooses and plays.

### Practical activity: Sound and rhyme games
1. Set up three game spaces.
2. Children play each game for four minutes.
3. The teacher notes which sounds each child can say.

**Safety:** Keep sounds gentle and the floor clear. Supervise movement.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Play with the child and use two sounds.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead a game.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What sound does the snake make?
  **Expected response:** Sss.
- **Ask:** What rhymes with cat?
  **Expected response:** Hat.

### Expected outcomes
- Shows listening and sound skills
- Joins in rhymes

### Conclusion
Ring the bell and say "Good listening."

### Home connection
Play sound and rhyme games at home during the holiday.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "revisit sound games, rhymes and the four sounds s, a, t and p" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Revising stories and books

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W10-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** revision · **Subtopic:** Revising stories and books

### Learning objectives
- Children will revisit stories, books and their own books.

### Success criteria
- Retells part of a story
- Shares a book with a friend

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on listening, speaking, rhymes, stories, early sounds, mark-making, names and books across the year.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, rhyme, sound, story, book, name

**Instructional materials:** Story box; Books; Children’s own books; Picture cards

### Starter
Say "Let us visit our stories and our books today." Open the story box.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell a favourite story with props. Children: Children join in and retell.

Step 2. Teacher: Put three picture cards in order and tell the story. Children: Child orders and tells.

Step 3. Teacher: Share the books they made. Children: Child shares their book with a friend.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a favourite book to look at. Children: Child chooses and turns pages.

### Practical activity: Story and book time
1. Set up the story box and book corner.
2. Children visit each in turn.
3. The teacher notes retelling and book handling.

**Safety:** Supervise props and books. Keep floors clear and cushions tidy.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sit with the child and point to pictures.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a story to a younger friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What happened first?
  **Expected response:** The first event.
- **Ask:** What is your favourite book?
  **Expected response:** A book they point to.

### Expected outcomes
- Retells part of a story
- Shares a book

### Conclusion
Put the books away and say "The end."

### Home connection
Read together every day during the holiday.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "revisit stories, books and their own books" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- STORY
- BOOK

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Play stations for the year

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W10-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Revising listening, rhymes and sounds

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will gather evidence of each child’s literacy learning across the year at play stations.

### Success criteria
- Completes familiar tasks with confidence
- Shows listening, speaking, sound and mark-making skills

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on listening, speaking, rhymes, stories, early sounds, mark-making, names and books across the year.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, rhyme, sound, story, book, name

**Instructional materials:** Six station sets; End-of-year record sheets; A camera or notebook

### Starter
Sing the hello song and show six stations to the class.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up six stations: sounds, rhymes, stories, books, names and marks. Children: Children rotate every three minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: Observe each child at two stations and record what they can do. Children: Children play at stations.

Step 3. Teacher: Take photographs and make notes. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Six stations
1. Set up six stations with familiar materials.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station with an adult or helper. Keep floors clear and materials large and soft.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and stay at familiar stations.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to show friends how a station works.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: listens; says s, a, t and p; joins in rhymes; retells a story; finds their name; makes marks and lines.

- **Ask:** What do you like?
  **Expected response:** Child points or names.
- **Ask:** Show me your favourite game.
  **Expected response:** Child chooses a station.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an end-of-year observation record
- Shows familiar skills at stations

### Conclusion
Say well done to everyone and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Play a favourite game from this year at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will gather evidence of each child’s literacy learning across the year at play stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Literacy celebration

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W10-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Celebration and end-of-year record

### Learning objectives
- Children will celebrate the year with songs, rhymes, stories and their own books.

### Success criteria
- Joins in songs and rhymes
- Shares their book with an adult

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on listening, speaking, rhymes, stories, early sounds, mark-making, names and books across the year.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, rhyme, sound, story, book, name

**Instructional materials:** Favourite songs; The children’s own books; Certificates or stickers; Class record sheets

### Starter
Say "Today we celebrate everything we learned. Let us sing our favourite songs."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing favourite songs and rhymes with actions. Children: Children sing and do actions.

Step 2. Teacher: Invite children to show their book to a visitor or parent. Children: Child shares their book.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each child a sticker or certificate and say their name. Children: Child waves and says thank you.

### Practical activity: Celebration circle
1. Sit in a circle with visitors.
2. Sing, share books and say each name.
3. Hand out certificates or stickers.

**Safety:** Check for allergies before any snack and use non-toxic stickers. Supervise visitors and keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand and share a book together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose the last song.

### Assessment
Complete each child’s end-of-year literacy record from the year’s observations. Record the child’s strengths and a next step for Nursery 1.

- **Ask:** What is your favourite song?
  **Expected response:** Child sings or points.
- **Ask:** What do you like about your book?
  **Expected response:** A word or a point.

### Expected outcomes
- Has a completed end-of-year record
- Enjoys songs, rhymes, stories and books

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say "See you in Nursery 1."

## Day 5 (Friday): Transition note and records

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W10-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Celebration and end-of-year record

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will complete a transition note for each child for the Nursery 1 teacher.

### Success criteria
- Records what each child can do
- Records a next step

**Previous knowledge:** Children have worked on listening, speaking, rhymes, stories, early sounds, mark-making, names and books across the year.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, rhyme, sound, story, book, name

**Instructional materials:** Transition note template; Term records; Children’s portfolios; A quiet table

### Starter
Today the children enjoy free play with familiar materials while the teacher completes the transition notes.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set out free play with books, instruments, dough and puppets. Children: Children play freely and happily.

Step 2. Teacher: Review each child’s three term records and portfolio. Children: Children play while the teacher reviews.

Step 3. Teacher: Complete the note: strengths, interests, language, sounds known and next steps. Children: Children play and talk with the helper.

### Practical activity: Free play and records
1. Set out four play areas with a helper.
2. The teacher reviews records at a quiet table.
3. The teacher completes each transition note.

**Safety:** A second adult must supervise play while the teacher completes the notes. Keep all areas safe and tidy.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer familiar toys and a helper nearby.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to help a friend tidy up.

### Assessment
Write for each child: what they can do in listening, speaking, rhymes, sounds, early reading and mark-making; their interests; any support needed; and a next step. Use the four-level scale and facts you have seen.

- **Ask:** What do you like to play?
  **Expected response:** A favourite game or toy.
- **Ask:** What did you learn this year?
  **Expected response:** A word, sound or song.

### Expected outcomes
- Has a transition note for each child
- Plays happily in familiar activities

### Conclusion
Tidy up together and say goodbye to the year.

### Home connection
Share the end-of-year report with your child and keep reading together.
