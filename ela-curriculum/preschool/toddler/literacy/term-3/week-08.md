# Toddler Literacy — Term 3, Week 8: Books and print

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W08 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** My name card; The first letter of my name; Names on things I own

## Learning objectives
- Children will recognise their name card and the first letter of their name.
- Children will make marks to show their name with support.

**Key vocabulary:** name, letter, first, mine, card, write

**Content:** Recognising their name card and the first letter of their name, and making marks to show their name with support.

**Teaching activities:** Always write names with a capital first letter and lower-case letters; use the child’s photo; point to the first letter and say its name and sound only for the four taught sounds; never ask a toddler to write their whole name; accept any mark as name-writing.

**Learner activities:** Recognise their name card and the first letter of their name and make marks to show their name with support.

**Resources:** Name cards with photos; Name labels for pegs and trays; A tray of sand; Crayons and paper; A mirror

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child finds their name among several cards and points to the first letter with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Finds their name among several cards.
- Points to the first letter of their name with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Personal, Social and Emotional Development: identity; Understanding the World: belongings; Physical Development: mark-making

## Day 1 (Monday): Find my name

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** My name card

### Learning objectives
- Children will find their name card among several cards.

### Success criteria
- Finds their own name card
- Says their name

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find their name card, hear four sounds and make lines and circles.

**Key vocabulary:** name, letter, first, mine, card, write

**Instructional materials:** Name cards with photos; A tray; A basket

### Starter
Hold up a name card and say "Whose name is this?" Let the children guess.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each name card and say the name. Point to the photo and the word. Children: Children look and say the name.

Step 2. Teacher: Put five name cards on the floor and ask a child to find their own. Children: Child finds their card.

Step 3. Teacher: Take the photo away and ask again. Children: Child finds their card by the word or first letter.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child hold their card and say their name. Children: Child holds up the card and says the name.

### Practical activity: Name hunt
1. Put five name cards on the floor.
2. Each child finds their own card.
3. Hold it up and say the name.

**Safety:** Keep the cards smooth and large. Return cards to the basket at the end.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Keep the photo and use only three cards.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a friend’s name card without a photo.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is your name?
  **Expected response:** Child points to their card.
- **Ask:** Whose name is this?
  **Expected response:** A friend’s name.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds their own name card
- Finds a friend’s name card with support

### Conclusion
Put the cards in the basket and say each name.

### Home connection
Show your child their name written clearly and let them find it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find their name card among several cards" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): The first letter

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** The first letter of my name

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to the first letter of their name.

### Success criteria
- Points to the first letter of their name with support
- Says that it is the start of their name

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find their name card, hear four sounds and make lines and circles.

**Key vocabulary:** name, letter, first, mine, card, write

**Instructional materials:** Name cards; Counters; A pointer

### Starter
Say "Look at the start of my name" and point to the first letter on your name card.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a name card and put a counter under the first letter. Say "This is where the name starts." Children: Children look at the first letter.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child their card and put a counter under the first letter. Children: Child touches the first letter.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Where does your name start?" Children: Child points to the first letter.

Step 4. Teacher: For the taught sounds s, a, t and p say the sound if it matches. Children: Child says the sound.

### Practical activity: Where it starts
1. Give each child their name card.
2. Find the first letter with a pointer.
3. Put a counter under the first letter.

**Safety:** Use large counters that cannot be swallowed. Supervise counters and put them away after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Put a counter under the letter and point together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a friend whose name starts with the same letter.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where does your name start?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the first letter.
- **Ask:** Is it a big letter?
  **Expected response:** Yes, with a nod.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to the first letter of their name
- Knows it is where the name starts

### Conclusion
Put the counters away and say each name.

### Home connection
Point to the first letter of your child’s name when you write it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to the first letter of their name" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Names on my things

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Names on things I own

### Learning objectives
- Children will find their name on their peg, tray and belongings.

### Success criteria
- Finds their name on a peg or tray
- Says that it belongs to them

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find their name card, hear four sounds and make lines and circles.

**Key vocabulary:** name, letter, first, mine, card, write

**Instructional materials:** Name labels on pegs and trays; A bag and a water bottle with names; A basket

### Starter
Hold up a bag with a name label and say "Whose bag is this? It has a name on it."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a peg with a name label and say "This is Tola’s peg." Children: Children look at the label.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask each child to find their peg or tray. Children: Child finds their peg.

Step 3. Teacher: Put names on cups and bags and ask "Whose is this?" Children: Child says "mine" or the name.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child put their bag on their peg. Children: Child puts their bag on their peg.

### Practical activity: My peg
1. Find your name on a peg.
2. Put your bag on your peg.
3. Find your name on your tray.

**Safety:** Make sure pegs are at child height and clear of sharp edges. Supervise movement.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk with the child to the peg and point to the name.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a friend’s peg and say whose it is.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which is your peg?
  **Expected response:** Child points to their peg.
- **Ask:** Whose bag is this?
  **Expected response:** A name or mine.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds their name on belongings
- Says mine and the name

### Conclusion
Put all bags back on pegs and say each name.

### Home connection
Label your child’s things at home with their name.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find their name on their peg, tray and belongings" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MINE
- MY PEG

**Picture suggestions:**
- A peg with a name label

## Day 4 (Thursday): Marks for my name

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** The first letter of my name

### Learning objectives
- Children will make marks to show their name with support.

### Success criteria
- Makes marks on paper to show their name
- Shows their marks to a friend

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find their name card, hear four sounds and make lines and circles.

**Key vocabulary:** name, letter, first, mine, card, write

**Instructional materials:** Large paper; Fat crayons; Name cards; A tray of sand

### Starter
Say "We can make marks to show our names." Make a mark and say "This is my name."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a name card and say "I can make marks like my name." Children: Children look at the card.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child paper and a crayon and say "Make marks for your name." Children: Child makes marks on paper.

Step 3. Teacher: Write the child’s name neatly beside their marks and say it. Children: Child watches and says their name.

Step 4. Teacher: Let the children show their marks to a friend. Children: Child shows and says the name.

### Practical activity: Name marks
1. Give each child paper and a crayon.
2. Make marks for their name.
3. The teacher writes the name beside it.

**Safety:** Accept any mark as a name and do not correct. Keep crayons out of mouths and supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the crayon together and make marks.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find the first letter on their name card and copy it with a finger.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What are your marks?
  **Expected response:** My name.
- **Ask:** Can you show a friend?
  **Expected response:** Child shows their marks.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes marks to show their name
- Shows their marks to a friend

### Conclusion
Display the name marks on the wall and say "Well done."

### Home connection
Let your child make marks to show their name on a card or a drawing.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make marks to show their name with support" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MY MARKS

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child’s name marks

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 name check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** My name card

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child finds and talks about their name.

### Success criteria
- Finds their name among several cards
- Points to the first letter with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find their name card, hear four sounds and make lines and circles.

**Key vocabulary:** name, letter, first, mine, card, write

**Instructional materials:** Name cards; Pegs and labels; Crayons and paper; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "Where is your name?" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: name cards, pegs and name marks. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask the child to find their name. Children: Child finds their name.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Name stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use large cards and soft crayons.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and keep the photo.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to find a friend’s name.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: finds their name among cards; points to the first letter; makes marks for their name.

- **Ask:** Where is your name?
  **Expected response:** Child points to their name.
- **Ask:** Where does it start?
  **Expected response:** The first letter.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Finds their name among several cards

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say each child’s name.

### Home connection
Let your child find their name at home on labels.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child finds and talks about their name" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Names

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
