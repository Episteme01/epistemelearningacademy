# Toddler Literacy — Term 3, Week 3: First sounds

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Listening for /t/; Listening for /p/; Which sound do you hear first?

## Learning objectives
- Children will hear and say the sounds /t/ and /p/ at the start of words.
- Children will tell /t/ from /p/ with support.

**Key vocabulary:** tap, tin, pot, pan, sound, first

**Content:** Listening for the sounds /t/ and /p/ at the start of words and telling which sound they hear first.

**Teaching activities:** Say /t/ and /p/ as short, crisp sounds with no vowel added; /t/ is made with the tongue behind the teeth and /p/ with the lips; hold a tissue in front of your mouth to show the puff of /p/; keep the sounds clean and brief.

**Learner activities:** Hear and say /t/ and /p/ at the start of words and tell /t/ from /p/ with support.

**Resources:** A tap picture, a tin, a toy tiger; A pot, a pan, a pen; A tissue; Two baskets

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says /t/ and /p/ clearly and points to the correct picture for each sound.

## Expected outcomes
- Says /t/ and /p/ clearly.
- Points to the right picture for /t/ or /p/.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: kitchen objects; Music: sound songs; Physical Development: lips and tongue

## Day 1 (Monday): The tap says t, t, t

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Listening for /t/

### Learning objectives
- Children will hear and say the sound /t/ as the dripping tap sound.

### Success criteria
- Says the sound t when shown the tap
- Watches the teacher’s mouth

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say /s/ and /a/ and find objects that start with them.

**Key vocabulary:** tap, tin, pot, pan, sound, first

**Instructional materials:** A picture or toy tap; A cup of water; A mirror

### Starter
Hold up the tap picture and say "A dripping tap goes t, t, t." Tap your finger softly in the air.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "t, t, t" in short crisp sounds with your tongue behind your teeth. Show your mouth. Children: Children watch your mouth and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "t, t, t" together while you tap the table gently. Children: Children say "t" and tap their finger.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "t" softly and quickly. Children: Children copy a soft quick t.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child look in the mirror and say t. Children: Child watches their mouth and says t.

### Practical activity: Dripping tap
1. Give each child a finger to tap on their hand.
2. Say t, t, t with each tap.
3. Stop and listen quietly.

**Safety:** Keep taps soft and do not let children tap hard on tables. Use a damp cloth for any water drips.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Tap the child’s hand softly and say t together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend what the tap says.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the tap say?
  **Expected response:** T, t, t.
- **Ask:** Can you tap and say t?
  **Expected response:** Child taps and says t.

### Expected outcomes
- Says the sound /t/ clearly
- Links /t/ to the tap

### Conclusion
Turn off the pretend tap with a last quiet t.

### Home connection
Listen to a dripping tap at home and say t, t, t.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hear and say the sound /t/ as the dripping tap sound" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- T (a tap)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A tap
- A child saying t

**Song or rhyme:** The tap says t, t, t. Drip, drip, drip.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): The pot says p, p, p

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Listening for /p/

### Learning objectives
- Children will hear and say the sound /p/ as the popping sound.

### Success criteria
- Says the sound p when shown the pot
- Feels the puff of air on a hand

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say /s/ and /a/ and find objects that start with them.

**Key vocabulary:** tap, tin, pot, pan, sound, first

**Instructional materials:** A pot or a picture of a pot; A tissue; A mirror

### Starter
Hold up the pot and say "Popcorn is popping in the pot. p, p, p." Make a popping sound with your lips.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "p, p, p" in short pops. Hold a tissue in front of your mouth and let it move. Children: Children watch the tissue move.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "p, p, p" together and hold a hand in front of the mouth. Children: Children feel a puff on their hand.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "p" softly and loudly. Children: Children copy a soft p and a loud p.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child look in the mirror and say p. Children: Child watches their lips and says p.

### Practical activity: Popping corn
1. Give each child a scarf to hold.
2. Pretend to be popcorn popping and say p, p, p.
3. Jump gently on each p.

**Safety:** Use only pretend popcorn and do not use real popcorn due to choking risk. Keep jumps small and soft.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand and feel the puff with them.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to pop a pretend popcorn and tell a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the pot say?
  **Expected response:** P, p, p.
- **Ask:** Can you feel the puff?
  **Expected response:** Child says yes or shows the hand.

### Expected outcomes
- Says the sound /p/ clearly
- Links /p/ to the pot

### Conclusion
Put the lid on the pot and say a last p.

### Home connection
Pop pretend popcorn with your child and say p, p, p.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hear and say the sound /p/ as the popping sound" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- P (a pot)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A pot with popcorn
- A child saying p

**Song or rhyme:** The pot says p, p, p. Pop, pop, pop.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Tin, tap, pot, pan

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Which sound do you hear first?

### Learning objectives
- Children will hear whether a word starts with t or p.

### Success criteria
- Points to the t picture or the p picture
- Says the first sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say /s/ and /a/ and find objects that start with them.

**Key vocabulary:** tap, tin, pot, pan, sound, first

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards: tin, tap, tiger, pot, pan, pen; Two baskets, one for t and one for p

### Starter
Show the two baskets and say "One is for t and one is for p. Listen for the first sound."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "tin" and stress the first sound "t-in". Put the tin in the t basket. Children: Children listen and say t.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "pot" and stress the first sound "p-ot". Put the pot in the p basket. Children: Children listen and say p.

Step 3. Teacher: Say a word and let a child choose the basket. Children: Child chooses the t or p basket.

Step 4. Teacher: Continue with all six cards. Children: Children sort the cards with support.

### Practical activity: T and p baskets
1. Put two baskets on the mat.
2. Each child picks a card and says the word.
3. Put the card in the t or the p basket.

**Safety:** Use large soft cards and supervise sorting. Make sure each child has a turn.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say the first sound loudly and show the card for the child to place.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a new t or p word in the room.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What sound does tin start with?
  **Expected response:** The sound t.
- **Ask:** What sound does pot start with?
  **Expected response:** The sound p.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts pictures by first sound t or p with support
- Says the first sound

### Conclusion
Count the cards in each basket and say t and p.

### Home connection
Say a word and ask your child if it starts with t or p.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hear whether a word starts with t or p" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- TIN
- TAP
- POT
- PAN

**Picture suggestions:**
- A tin
- A tap
- A pot
- A pan

## Day 4 (Thursday): The t and p song

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Which sound do you hear first?

### Learning objectives
- Children will sing a song about t and p and do the matching actions.

### Success criteria
- Sings or hums the t and p song
- Does tap or pop for each sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say /s/ and /a/ and find objects that start with them.

**Key vocabulary:** tap, tin, pot, pan, sound, first

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards: tap, pot; A drum; A scarf

### Starter
Say "t, t, t" and tap. Say "p, p, p" and pop. Ask "Which one is it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing "Tap, tap, tap says t, t, t. Pop, pop, pop says p, p, p." Show the cards. Children: Children listen and look at the cards.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing again and do a tap action for t and a jump for p. Children: Children copy the tap and the pop.

Step 3. Teacher: Say a sound and let children do the action. Children: Child taps for t and pops for p.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child lead and choose t or p. Children: Child leads and friends follow.

### Practical activity: Tap and pop game
1. Sit in a circle with the two picture cards.
2. Say t or p and children tap or pop.
3. Change the order faster each time.

**Safety:** Keep jumps small and soft. Clear the floor and rest if children are tired.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Do the action together and say the sound loudly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose a sound and lead the game.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do for t?
  **Expected response:** Tap.
- **Ask:** What do we do for p?
  **Expected response:** Pop.

### Expected outcomes
- Tells t from p with actions
- Sings the song with actions

### Conclusion
Sing the song once quietly and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Play tap and pop at home: tap for t, pop for p.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sing a song about t and p and do the matching actions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- TAP – T
- POP – P

**Picture suggestions:**
- A tap
- A pot

**Song or rhyme:** Tap, tap, tap says t, t, t. Pop, pop, pop says p, p, p.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 t and p check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Which sound do you hear first?

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child hears and says /t/ and /p/.

### Success criteria
- Says /t/ and /p/ clearly
- Points to the right picture for t or p

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear and say /s/ and /a/ and find objects that start with them.

**Key vocabulary:** tap, tin, pot, pan, sound, first

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards; Two baskets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say t and p and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: t sound, p sound and sorting. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station say a word and ask "Is it t or p?" Children: Child says the sound or points.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: T and p stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use large soft cards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two pictures.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to sort all six cards alone.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says /t/ clearly; says /p/ clearly; tells /t/ from /p/ with support.

- **Ask:** What sound does tap start with?
  **Expected response:** The sound t.
- **Ask:** What sound does pan start with?
  **Expected response:** The sound p.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Says /t/ and /p/ clearly

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye with a t and a p.

### Home connection
Play listening games with t and p at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child hears and says /t/ and /p/" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- T and P

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
