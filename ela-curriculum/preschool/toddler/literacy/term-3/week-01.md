# Toddler Literacy — Term 3, Week 1: First sounds

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Listening for /s/; Things that start with /s/; The /s/ song

## Learning objectives
- Children will hear and say the sound /s/ at the start of words.
- Children will find objects that start with /s/ with support.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, sock, snake, sound, sss

**Content:** Listening for the sound /s/ at the start of words, finding things that start with /s/, and the /s/ song.

**Teaching activities:** Say /s/ as a pure sound, a long hiss "sss", with no vowel added; say "the sound sss" and never "es" or "suh"; show your mouth and teeth; use real objects; children hear and say sounds only, with no letter formation.

**Learner activities:** Hear and say the sound /s/ at the start of words and point to objects that start with /s/ with support.

**Resources:** A toy sun, a sock, a toy snake, a spoon; A tray; A bag; A picture of a snake; A mirror

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says /s/ clearly and points to an object that starts with /s/.

## Expected outcomes
- Says /s/ clearly.
- Points to an object that starts with /s/.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Understanding the World: everyday objects; Music: sound songs; Physical Development: mouth movements

## Day 1 (Monday): The snake says sss

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Listening for /s/

### Learning objectives
- Children will hear and say the sound /s/ as the snake sound.

### Success criteria
- Says the sound sss when shown the snake
- Watches the teacher’s mouth

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear rhyming words, clap the beats in words and join in songs; they have not yet been taught any letter sounds.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, sock, snake, sound, sss

**Instructional materials:** A toy snake or a picture of a snake; A mirror; A mat

### Starter
Wiggle the toy snake along the mat and say "The snake goes sss." Hold a finger to your lips.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "sss" clearly, a long hissing sound. Show how your teeth come together and the air flows out. Children: Children watch your mouth and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "sss" together while the snake wiggles. Say "the sound is sss". Children: Children say "sss" with you.

Step 3. Teacher: Make the snake go quiet and loud and say sss softly and loudly. Children: Children copy a soft sss and a loud sss.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child look in the mirror and say sss. Children: Child watches their mouth and says sss.

### Practical activity: Snake sound
1. Give each child a pretend snake made from a scarf or a hand.
2. Wiggle the snake along the floor and say sss.
3. Stop the snake and listen quietly.

**Safety:** Use a soft toy snake and tell children it is a toy. Keep the sound gentle and do not let children blow hard to avoid dizziness.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Wiggle the snake with the child and say sss together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend what the snake says.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does the snake say?
  **Expected response:** Sss.
- **Ask:** Can you make a long sss?
  **Expected response:** Child makes a long hissing sound.

### Expected outcomes
- Says the sound /s/ clearly
- Links /s/ to the snake

### Conclusion
Settle the snake to sleep with a very quiet sss.

### Home connection
Make a snake sound at home, a long sss, and wiggle a hand like a snake.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hear and say the sound /s/ as the snake sound" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SSS (a snake)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A snake
- A child saying sss

**Song or rhyme:** The snake says sss, sss, sss. The snake says sss, sss, sss.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Sun, sock and spoon

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Things that start with /s/

### Learning objectives
- Children will hear that sun, sock and spoon start with the sound /s/.

### Success criteria
- Repeats the word and the first sound
- Points to an object that starts with /s/

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear rhyming words, clap the beats in words and join in songs; they have not yet been taught any letter sounds.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, sock, snake, sound, sss

**Instructional materials:** A toy sun, a sock, a spoon; A tray; A cloth bag

### Starter
Hold up the sun and say "Sun. Sss-un. It starts with sss." Show the sock and say "Sock. Sss-ock."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say each word slowly and stress the first sound: sss-un, sss-ock, sss-poon. Children: Children repeat the word and the first sound.

Step 2. Teacher: Put the three objects on a tray and say "These all start with sss." Children: Children touch each object and say sss.

Step 3. Teacher: Add a ball to the tray and ask "Does ball start with sss?" Children: Child shakes their head or says no.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick an s-object and say the word. Children: Child picks and says sss and the word.

### Practical activity: S tray
1. Put the sun, sock, spoon and a ball on a tray.
2. Ask each child to take an object that starts with sss.
3. Say the word and the first sound.

**Safety:** Use large, clean objects. Keep the spoon plastic and blunt. Supervise handling.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects, one that starts with sss, and say the sound first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another sss object in the classroom.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What sound does sun start with?
  **Expected response:** Sss.
- **Ask:** Does ball start with sss?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Hears /s/ at the start of words
- Picks objects that start with /s/ with support

### Conclusion
Put the objects in the bag and say sss.

### Home connection
Look for things that start with sss, such as a sock or a spoon, and say the sound.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hear that sun, sock and spoon start with the sound /s/" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SUN
- SOCK
- SPOON

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): The sss song

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** The /s/ song

### Learning objectives
- Children will sing a song about the sound /s/ with actions.

### Success criteria
- Sings or hums the sss song
- Does the snake action

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear rhyming words, clap the beats in words and join in songs; they have not yet been taught any letter sounds.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, sock, snake, sound, sss

**Instructional materials:** A toy snake; Picture cards: sun, sock, snake; A scarf

### Starter
Wiggle the snake and sing "sss, sss, sss". Ask "Who knows the snake song?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing the song slowly: "Sss, sss, sss, sun and sock and snake. Sss, sss, sss, what can you take?" Show the cards. Children: Children listen and look at the cards.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing again and do the snake action with your arm. Children: Children copy the snake action and the sound.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing again and let children hold up a card at the right word. Children: Child holds up the sun, sock or snake card.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child lead the song with the snake. Children: Child leads and friends sing.

### Practical activity: Sing and slither
1. Give each child a scarf snake.
2. Sing the song and move the scarf.
3. Hold up a picture card at each word.

**Safety:** Clear the floor so children can move freely. Keep the volume gentle.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sing softly with the child and do the actions together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sing the song to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What sound do we sing?
  **Expected response:** Sss.
- **Ask:** Which things start with sss?
  **Expected response:** Sun, sock and snake.

### Expected outcomes
- Sings the sss song with actions
- Names sun, sock and snake

### Conclusion
Sing a quiet last verse and put the snake to bed.

### Home connection
Sing the sss song at home and wiggle like a snake.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sing a song about the sound /s/ with actions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SUN
- SOCK
- SNAKE

**Picture suggestions:**
- A sun
- A sock
- A snake

**Song or rhyme:** Sss, sss, sss, sun and sock and snake. Sss, sss, sss, what can you take?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Find the sss things

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Things that start with /s/

### Learning objectives
- Children will find objects that start with /s/ in a sound hunt.

### Success criteria
- Finds an s-object with support
- Says the word and the sound

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear rhyming words, clap the beats in words and join in songs; they have not yet been taught any letter sounds.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, sock, snake, sound, sss

**Instructional materials:** Six objects, three starting with /s/ and three that do not; A basket; A bag

### Starter
Say "Let us hunt for sss things. Sss, sss, sss." Show the empty basket.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hide the objects around the room in easy places. Children: Children watch where you put the objects.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Find a sss thing" and let each child look. Children: Child finds an object and brings it back.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Does it start with sss?" and say the word. Children: Child says yes or no and the word.

Step 4. Teacher: Put the sss things in the basket and the others on the mat. Children: Child sorts the objects.

### Practical activity: Sss hunt
1. Hide six objects in easy places in the room.
2. Children find objects and bring them to the mat.
3. Sort the sss objects into the basket.

**Safety:** Hide objects only in clear, low places away from hazards. Supervise the search and keep children together.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk with the child and point to the object after saying the sound.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to hide an s-object for a friend to find.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does sock start with sss?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** Does cup start with sss?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds objects starting with /s/ with support
- Says the word and sound

### Conclusion
Count the objects in the basket and say sss.

### Home connection
Play a sound hunt at home for things that start with sss.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find objects that start with /s/ in a sound hunt" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SSS HUNT

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children searching
- A basket of objects

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 1 sss check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Things that start with /s/

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child hears and says /s/.

### Success criteria
- Says /s/ clearly
- Points to an object that starts with /s/

**Previous knowledge:** Children can hear rhyming words, clap the beats in words and join in songs; they have not yet been taught any letter sounds.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, sock, snake, sound, sss

**Instructional materials:** S-objects; Other objects; A toy snake; Class record sheet

### Starter
Wiggle the snake and say "sss". Show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: snake sound, s-objects and the song. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What sound?" and "Which one starts with sss?" Children: Child says sss and points.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Sound stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use large, clean objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two objects.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to name two more sss words.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says /s/ clearly; points to an object starting with /s/; joins in the song.

- **Ask:** What sound does the snake make?
  **Expected response:** Sss.
- **Ask:** Show me something that starts with sss.
  **Expected response:** Sun, sock or spoon.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Says /s/ clearly

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the snake with a soft sss.

### Home connection
Play sound games at home with the sound sss.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child hears and says /s/" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Sss

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
