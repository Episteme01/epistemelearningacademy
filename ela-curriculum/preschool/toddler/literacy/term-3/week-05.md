# Toddler Literacy — Term 3, Week 5: Pre-writing

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W05 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Straight lines up and down; Round and curly patterns; Zigzag and wave

## Learning objectives
- Children will make straight lines, circles and zigzags in sand, paint and on paper.
- Children will copy a simple pattern with support.

**Key vocabulary:** line, circle, zigzag, wave, up, down

**Content:** Making straight lines up and down, round and curly patterns, and zigzag and wave patterns in sand, paint and on paper.

**Teaching activities:** Draw big in the air first, then on large surfaces; say a rhyme for each pattern; never correct pencil grip or correct the direction; let children use whole arm movements; do not teach letter formation.

**Learner activities:** Make straight lines, circles and zigzags in sand, paint and on paper and copy a simple pattern with support.

**Resources:** A tray of sand or flour; Paint and large brushes; Large paper; Fat crayons; Chalk; Ribbon sticks

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child makes straight and round marks on purpose and copies a simple pattern.

## Expected outcomes
- Makes straight and round marks on purpose.
- Copies a simple pattern.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Creative Development: pattern and art; Physical Development: gross and fine motor; Mathematics: shape and pattern

## Day 1 (Monday): Lines up and down

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Straight lines up and down

### Learning objectives
- Children will make straight lines up and down and across.

### Success criteria
- Makes a line up and down
- Makes a line across

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons and have been listening to the sounds s, a, t and p.

**Key vocabulary:** line, circle, zigzag, wave, up, down

**Instructional materials:** A tray of sand; A ribbon stick for each child; Large paper; Fat crayons

### Starter
Draw a straight line in the air from top to bottom and say "Down, down, down." Draw across and say "Across."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Draw a line in the air and say "up" and "down". Do it slowly. Children: Children draw the line in the air with a finger.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a ribbon stick and draw lines in the air. Children: Children make lines with the ribbon.

Step 3. Teacher: Draw lines in the sand: down, up and across. Children: Child makes lines in the sand with a finger.

Step 4. Teacher: Give crayons and large paper and let the children draw lines. Children: Child makes lines on paper.

### Practical activity: Line drawing
1. Draw lines in the air with ribbons.
2. Make lines in the sand with a finger.
3. Draw lines on large paper with crayons.

**Safety:** Use ribbon sticks with soft ends and keep children apart. Supervise sand and crayons and wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move the child’s hand to make the line together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to draw five straight lines and count them.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you draw a line down?
  **Expected response:** Child draws a line down.
- **Ask:** Can you draw a line across?
  **Expected response:** Child draws across.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes straight lines on purpose
- Uses arm movement

### Conclusion
Smooth the sand and wipe hands.

### Home connection
Draw lines in sand or flour with your child at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make straight lines up and down and across" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- DOWN
- ACROSS
- UP again

**Picture suggestions:**
- A line going down
- A line across

**Song or rhyme:** Down, down, down, across, across, across.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Circles and curls

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Round and curly patterns

### Learning objectives
- Children will make round and curly patterns.

### Success criteria
- Makes a circle shape
- Makes a curly line

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons and have been listening to the sounds s, a, t and p.

**Key vocabulary:** line, circle, zigzag, wave, up, down

**Instructional materials:** Paint and large brushes; Large paper; Chalk; A tray of flour

### Starter
Draw a big circle in the air and say "Round and round." Draw a curl and say "Curly, curly."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Draw big circles in the air with your arm and say "Round and round". Children: Children draw circles in the air.

Step 2. Teacher: Draw a circle in the flour tray with a finger. Children: Child draws a circle in the flour.

Step 3. Teacher: Give paint and brushes and let the children paint circles and curls. Children: Child paints round shapes.

Step 4. Teacher: Draw a spiral and say "Round and round to the middle." Children: Child copies the spiral with a finger.

### Practical activity: Round and round
1. Draw circles in the air.
2. Draw circles in flour.
3. Paint circles on large paper.

**Safety:** Use washable paint and aprons and keep paint off faces. Supervise brushes and wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move the child’s hand in a circle together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to draw a spiral and a circle.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you draw a circle?
  **Expected response:** Child draws a round shape.
- **Ask:** What shape is this?
  **Expected response:** A circle.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes round shapes on purpose
- Makes curly lines

### Conclusion
Clean up the paint and look at the circles on the wall.

### Home connection
Draw circles with a finger in sand or with a crayon at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Zigzag and wave

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Zigzag and wave

### Learning objectives
- Children will make zigzag and wave patterns.

### Success criteria
- Makes a zigzag line
- Makes a wave line

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons and have been listening to the sounds s, a, t and p.

**Key vocabulary:** line, circle, zigzag, wave, up, down

**Instructional materials:** Chalk; Fat crayons; Large paper; A scarf

### Starter
Draw a zigzag in the air and say "Zig, zag, zig, zag." Draw a wave and say "Up and down like the sea."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Draw a zigzag in the air and let children copy. Children: Children draw zigzags in the air.

Step 2. Teacher: Draw a wave in the air and let children copy. Children: Children draw waves in the air.

Step 3. Teacher: Draw zigzags and waves with chalk on the ground or on paper. Children: Child draws zigzags and waves.

Step 4. Teacher: Wave a scarf like a sea wave and move to music. Children: Children wave the scarf up and down.

### Practical activity: Zig and wave
1. Draw zigzags and waves in the air.
2. Draw them with chalk on a safe surface.
3. Draw them on large paper with crayons.

**Safety:** Use chalk on a paved area away from roads and wash hands after. Keep scarves soft and children apart.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move the child’s hand along the zigzag together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to draw a zigzag and a wave side by side.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you make a zigzag?
  **Expected response:** Child draws a zigzag.
- **Ask:** What does a wave look like?
  **Expected response:** Up and down like the sea.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes zigzags and waves
- Copies a pattern with support

### Conclusion
Wave the scarf slowly and put it away.

### Home connection
Draw zigzags and waves in sand or with chalk at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Copy my pattern

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Round and curly patterns

### Learning objectives
- Children will copy a simple pattern made of lines and circles.

### Success criteria
- Copies a line and circle pattern
- Tells about their pattern

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons and have been listening to the sounds s, a, t and p.

**Key vocabulary:** line, circle, zigzag, wave, up, down

**Instructional materials:** Large paper; Fat crayons; A pattern card with a line and a circle; Chalk

### Starter
Draw a line, a circle, a line, a circle. Say "I made a pattern. Can you copy it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Draw a pattern slowly: line, circle, line, circle. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Now you make it" and give each child a crayon. Children: Child draws a pattern on paper.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Tell me about your pattern." Children: Child says a word or shows.

Step 4. Teacher: Display the patterns together. Children: Child shows their pattern to a friend.

### Practical activity: Pattern making
1. Show a simple pattern card.
2. Each child copies the pattern with a crayon.
3. Show the pattern to a friend.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic crayons and keep crayons out of mouths. Supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Draw the first part of the pattern and let the child continue.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a new pattern with three shapes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is in your pattern?
  **Expected response:** Lines and circles.
- **Ask:** Can you make it again?
  **Expected response:** Child draws the pattern.

### Expected outcomes
- Copies a simple pattern with support
- Talks about their pattern

### Conclusion
Hang the patterns on the wall and say "Well done."

### Home connection
Draw a simple pattern at home and let your child copy it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "copy a simple pattern made of lines and circles" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 patterns check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Round and curly patterns

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child makes lines and patterns.

### Success criteria
- Makes straight and round marks on purpose
- Copies a simple pattern

**Previous knowledge:** Children can make marks with fingers, chalk and crayons and have been listening to the sounds s, a, t and p.

**Key vocabulary:** line, circle, zigzag, wave, up, down

**Instructional materials:** Sand tray; Paint and paper; Crayons; Class record sheet

### Starter
Draw a big line in the air and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: sand, paint and crayons. Children: Children rotate every five minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch what marks the child makes. Children: Child makes lines, circles and zigzags.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Pattern stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise paint and sand closely. Use washable paint and wash hands after each station.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and offer one tool.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to make a pattern for friends to copy.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: makes straight lines; makes circles; copies a simple pattern.

- **Ask:** Can you make a line?
  **Expected response:** Child makes a line.
- **Ask:** Can you make a circle?
  **Expected response:** Child makes a circle.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Makes lines and circles on purpose

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Make patterns in sand or flour at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child makes lines and patterns" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
