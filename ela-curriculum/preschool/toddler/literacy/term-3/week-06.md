# Toddler Literacy — Term 3, Week 6: Listening

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** One-step instructions; Two-step instructions; Giving an instruction to a friend

## Learning objectives
- Children will follow a two-part instruction.
- Children will give a simple instruction to a friend.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, go, bring, put, listen

**Content:** Following one-step and two-step instructions and giving a simple instruction to a friend.

**Teaching activities:** Give one instruction at a time first; use the words first and then; show with actions and wait before repeating; use real objects; never rush a child who needs time to think.

**Learner activities:** Follow a two-part instruction and give a simple instruction to a friend.

**Resources:** A basket of toys; A box and a mat; A ball, a cup, a spoon and a teddy; A bell; Pictures of instructions

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child follows a two-step instruction and gives a one-step instruction.

## Expected outcomes
- Follows a two-step instruction.
- Gives a one-step instruction.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Social Habits: listening and following directions; Physical Development: movement; Mathematics: position words

## Day 1 (Monday): One thing at a time

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** One-step instructions

### Learning objectives
- Children will follow a one-step instruction such as "Bring me the ball".

### Success criteria
- Listens to the instruction
- Does what the teacher asks

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow one-step instructions, hear the sounds s, a, t and p and answer simple questions.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, go, bring, put, listen

**Instructional materials:** A ball, a cup, a spoon and a teddy; A basket; A mat

### Starter
Say "Listen, then do. Bring me the ball." Wait and look at the children.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "Bring me the ball" and show a hand gesture. Wait for a child to bring it. Children: Child brings the ball.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Put the cup in the basket." Show the basket. Children: Child puts the cup in the basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Give instructions to each child with a different object. Children: Child listens and does the action.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise each child by saying what they did. Children: Child smiles and shows what they did.

### Practical activity: Listen and do
1. Put four objects on the mat.
2. Give each child a one-step instruction.
3. Say well done and put the object back.

**Safety:** Keep objects soft and safe and the floor clear. Do not rush the child and repeat the instruction once calmly.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the object and say the instruction slowly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give a friend an instruction.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you bring me the ball?
  **Expected response:** Child brings the ball.
- **Ask:** Where did you put the cup?
  **Expected response:** In the basket.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows a one-step instruction
- Listens before acting

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say "Good listening."

### Home connection
Give your child one-step instructions at home such as "Put your shoes here".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "follow a one-step instruction such as "Bring me the ball"" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- LISTEN
- DO it

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child bringing a ball
- A child putting a cup in a basket

## Day 2 (Tuesday): First and then

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Two-step instructions

### Learning objectives
- Children will follow a two-part instruction with first and then.

### Success criteria
- Does both parts of the instruction
- Understands first and then

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow one-step instructions, hear the sounds s, a, t and p and answer simple questions.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, go, bring, put, listen

**Instructional materials:** A ball, a cup and a basket; A bell; A mat

### Starter
Say "First clap, then stamp." Do it slowly and show the order with fingers: one, two.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "First clap, then stamp" and do the actions with the children. Children: Children clap and then stamp.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "First touch your head, then touch your toes." Children: Children touch their head, then their toes.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "First pick up the ball, then put it in the basket." Children: Child does both actions in order.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child have a turn with a new instruction. Children: Child listens and does both parts.

### Practical activity: Two things
1. Put a ball, a cup and a basket on the mat.
2. Say a two-part instruction.
3. The child does both parts in order.

**Safety:** Keep the instructions simple and the space clear. If the child forgets, repeat gently and help with a gesture.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Show the first action, wait, then show the second.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to remember three things in order.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you do first?
  **Expected response:** The first thing said.
- **Ask:** What do you do then?
  **Expected response:** The second thing said.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows two-part instructions
- Uses first and then

### Conclusion
Say "First tidy up, then sit down" and do it together.

### Home connection
Give two-part instructions at home such as "First wash hands, then eat".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "follow a two-part instruction with first and then" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FIRST
- THEN

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two steps on a path

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Go and bring

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Two-step instructions

### Learning objectives
- Children will go to a place and bring back an object.

### Success criteria
- Goes to the right place
- Brings back the correct object

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow one-step instructions, hear the sounds s, a, t and p and answer simple questions.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, go, bring, put, listen

**Instructional materials:** A box on a table; A teddy; A spoon; A cup

### Starter
Say "Go to the box, then bring me the teddy." Point to the box.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give the instruction and let one child try while others watch. Children: Child goes to the box and brings the teddy.

Step 2. Teacher: Change the instruction: "Go to the table, then bring the spoon." Children: Child goes to the table and brings the spoon.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child have a turn with a new instruction. Children: Child goes and brings an object.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise each child by saying what they did. Children: Child smiles and shows what they brought.

### Practical activity: Fetch game
1. Put four objects in different places.
2. Give each child a go-and-bring instruction.
3. The child goes and brings the object back.

**Safety:** Keep the objects within easy reach and the path clear. Walk, do not run, and supervise movement.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk with the child and point at the object.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to bring two objects in one trip.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where did you go?
  **Expected response:** To the box or the table.
- **Ask:** What did you bring?
  **Expected response:** The teddy or spoon.

### Expected outcomes
- Goes and brings an object on instruction
- Follows two-part instructions

### Conclusion
Put the objects back in the right places.

### Home connection
Ask your child to go and bring something, such as "Go to the kitchen and bring a spoon".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "go to a place and bring back an object" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- GO there
- BRING back

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child going to a box
- A child bringing a teddy

## Day 4 (Thursday): I am the leader

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Giving an instruction to a friend

### Learning objectives
- Children will give a simple instruction to a friend.

### Success criteria
- Says an instruction to a friend
- Waits for the friend to do it

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow one-step instructions, hear the sounds s, a, t and p and answer simple questions.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, go, bring, put, listen

**Instructional materials:** A leader’s hat; A ball, a cup and a basket; A mat

### Starter
Put on the leader’s hat and say "I am the leader. Bring me the cup." Give the hat to a child.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to be a leader: say "Clap" and everyone claps. Children: Children clap on the instruction.

Step 2. Teacher: Give the hat to a child and help them say an instruction. Children: Child says an instruction such as "clap".

Step 3. Teacher: Let the friend follow the instruction. Children: Friend does what the leader says.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap the hat and let others be the leader. Children: Child leads and friends follow.

### Practical activity: Leader game
1. Choose a leader with the hat.
2. The leader tells a friend what to do.
3. Swap the hat and play again.

**Safety:** Keep instructions kind and safe. Do not allow instructions that could hurt a friend and supervise each pair.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Whisper the instruction to the leader and let them say it.
- **Extension:** Ask the leader to give a two-part instruction.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did the leader say?
  **Expected response:** The instruction given.
- **Ask:** Can you be the leader?
  **Expected response:** Child gives an instruction.

### Expected outcomes
- Gives a simple instruction to a friend
- Listens as a friend leads

### Conclusion
Give the hat back and say "Well done, leaders."

### Home connection
Let your child give you an instruction and follow it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "give a simple instruction to a friend" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- LEADER

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child wearing a hat

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 instructions check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Two-step instructions

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child follows and gives instructions.

### Success criteria
- Follows a two-step instruction
- Gives a one-step instruction

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow one-step instructions, hear the sounds s, a, t and p and answer simple questions.

**Key vocabulary:** first, then, go, bring, put, listen

**Instructional materials:** Objects; A leader’s hat; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "First listen, then do" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: one-step, two-step and leader. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station give an instruction and watch. Children: Child does the action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Instruction stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use soft objects only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one-step instructions.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to give a two-step instruction.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: follows one-step instructions; follows two-step instructions; gives an instruction to a friend.

- **Ask:** What do you do first?
  **Expected response:** The first part of the instruction.
- **Ask:** What do you do then?
  **Expected response:** The second part.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Follows two-step instructions

### Conclusion
Say "Good listening" and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Practise first and then at home with small tasks.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child follows and gives instructions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Listen

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
