# Toddler Literacy — Term 3, Week 9: Stories

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Choosing a topic; Making pages with pictures and marks; Sharing my book

## Learning objectives
- Children will make a simple book with pictures and marks.
- Children will share their book with a friend.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, cover, picture, share, author

**Content:** Choosing a topic, making pages with pictures and marks, and sharing the book with a friend.

**Teaching activities:** Make a model book first; let children choose their own topic; accept any picture or mark as a page; write down what children say beside their pages; celebrate every book as a real book.

**Learner activities:** Make a simple book with pictures and marks and share it with a friend.

**Resources:** Folded paper booklets of four pages; Crayons and stickers; Magazine pictures; Glue sticks; A stapler for the teacher only; A model book

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child makes a book with at least three pages and tells a friend about one page.

## Expected outcomes
- Makes a book with at least three pages.
- Tells a friend about one page.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Creative Development: art; Understanding the World: favourite things; Social Habits: sharing

## Day 1 (Monday): What is my book about?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Choosing a topic

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose what their book is about.

### Success criteria
- Chooses a topic
- Says or points to their choice

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, tell a story in order and make marks and pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, cover, picture, share, author

**Instructional materials:** A model book called "My Favourite Things"; Picture cards: animals, food, family, toys

### Starter
Show the model book and say "I made a book about my favourite things. What will your book be about?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Turn the pages of the model book and say what each page shows. Children: Children look and name the pictures.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the topic cards and ask "What is your book about?" Children: Child points to a topic card.

Step 3. Teacher: Talk to each child about their topic. Children: Child says a word about their topic.

Step 4. Teacher: Write each child’s topic on a card to put in their book. Children: Child watches and says the topic.

### Practical activity: Choose a topic
1. Show the topic cards.
2. Each child chooses a topic.
3. Write the topic on a card.

**Safety:** Accept any choice and do not hurry. Keep cards flat and supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two topics and say the words with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell what they will put in the book.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is your book about?
  **Expected response:** Animals, food, family or toys.
- **Ask:** What do you like?
  **Expected response:** A favourite thing.

### Expected outcomes
- Chooses a topic for their book
- Says a word about their topic

### Conclusion
Put the topic cards in a basket and say "Tomorrow we make our books."

### Home connection
Ask your child what their book will be about and look for pictures at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose what their book is about" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**

- ABOUT

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Making the pages

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Making pages with pictures and marks

### Learning objectives
- Children will make pages for their book with pictures and marks.

### Success criteria
- Makes at least two pages
- Uses pictures or marks on each page

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, tell a story in order and make marks and pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, cover, picture, share, author

**Instructional materials:** Folded paper booklets; Crayons; Magazine pictures; Glue sticks

### Starter
Show a blank booklet and say "Every page needs a picture. What will you put on page one?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the booklet and how to open it and turn the page. Children: Children look and turn a page.

Step 2. Teacher: Show how to glue a picture or draw on a page. Children: Children glue or draw on page one.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Tell me about your page" and write the words under it. Children: Child tells about their page.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children make a second page. Children: Child makes a second page.

### Practical activity: Page making
1. Give each child a booklet.
2. Glue or draw on page one.
3. Make a second page.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic glue and keep it off faces and mouths. Wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the paper steady and help to glue the picture.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a cover with the title and a picture.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is on this page?
  **Expected response:** The name of the picture.
- **Ask:** Can you make another page?
  **Expected response:** Child makes another page.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes pages with pictures or marks
- Tells about a page

### Conclusion
Put the books on a table to dry and say "Tomorrow more pages."

### Home connection
Look at magazines or old cards at home and cut pictures for your child’s book.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make pages for their book with pictures and marks" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- PAGE
- PICTURE

**Picture suggestions:**
- A booklet
- A glued picture

## Day 3 (Wednesday): The cover and the pages

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Making pages with pictures and marks

### Learning objectives
- Children will finish a book with a cover and at least three pages.

### Success criteria
- Makes a cover
- Has at least three pages

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, tell a story in order and make marks and pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, cover, picture, share, author

**Instructional materials:** Booklets; Crayons; Stickers; Name cards

### Starter
Show the model book cover and say "A book has a cover and a name on it. This is the cover."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to draw on the cover and put a name on it. Children: Children look at the cover.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child their booklet and help them write or stick their name on the cover. Children: Child decorates the cover.

Step 3. Teacher: Finish pages two and three. Children: Child adds pictures or marks.

Step 4. Teacher: Check each book has three pages. Children: Child counts or shows their pages.

### Practical activity: Finishing the book
1. Give each child their booklet.
2. Make a cover and finish three pages.
3. Count the pages together.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic materials and supervise stickers. Keep glue sticks capped.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Stick a name label on the cover and let the child decorate.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a fourth page.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is on the cover?
  **Expected response:** My name and a picture.
- **Ask:** How many pages do you have?
  **Expected response:** Three or four.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a book with a cover and three pages
- Shows the pages

### Conclusion
Staple each book for the child and say "You made a book."

### Home connection
Look at your child’s book together and ask them to tell you about each page.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "finish a book with a cover and at least three pages" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- COVER
- MY NAME

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Sharing my book

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Sharing my book

### Learning objectives
- Children will share their book with a friend.

### Success criteria
- Shows their book to a friend
- Tells about one page

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, tell a story in order and make marks and pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, cover, picture, share, author

**Instructional materials:** The finished books; Cushions; A book corner

### Starter
Hold up a finished book and say "I made this book. Would you like to see it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to share: open the book, turn a page and say "This is my dog." Children: Children look and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Pair the children and give them time to share. Children: Child shows their book to a friend.

Step 3. Teacher: Walk round and ask each child "What is on this page?" Children: Child tells about a page.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap partners and share again. Children: Child shares with a new friend.

### Practical activity: Book sharing
1. Pair children on cushions.
2. Each child shares their book.
3. Swap books and look at a friend’s book.

**Safety:** Remind children to hold books gently and say thank you. Supervise sharing and keep the corner tidy.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sit beside the child and point to the page together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to read their book to a younger friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is on this page?
  **Expected response:** The name of the picture.
- **Ask:** Did you like your friend’s book?
  **Expected response:** Yes with a nod or word.

### Expected outcomes
- Shares their book with a friend
- Tells about a page

### Conclusion
Say thank you to your friend and put the books in the book corner.

### Home connection
Let your child read their book to you and to family members.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "share their book with a friend" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 9 book check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-LIT-T3-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Sharing my book

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child makes and shares a book.

### Success criteria
- Makes a book with at least three pages
- Tells a friend about one page

**Previous knowledge:** Children can handle books, tell a story in order and make marks and pictures.

**Key vocabulary:** book, page, cover, picture, share, author

**Instructional materials:** The finished books; Class record sheet; A camera or notebook

### Starter
Say "Show me your book" and invite children to the book corner.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Invite each child to show their book and tell about one page. Children: Child shows the book and tells about it.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "What did you make?" and "What is your favourite page?" Children: Child answers with a word or point.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and photograph the book. Children: Children return books to the book corner.

### Practical activity: Book showing
1. Invite children one at a time.
2. Each child shows their book.
3. The teacher records what they say.

**Safety:** Never force a child to speak. Accept pointing and show appreciation for every book.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sit beside the child and point to the page together.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to read their book to the group.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: makes pages; completes a cover; tells a friend about one page.

- **Ask:** What is on your page?
  **Expected response:** The name of the picture.
- **Ask:** What is your favourite page?
  **Expected response:** A page they point to.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Makes and shares a book

### Conclusion
Put the books in a display and clap for all authors.

### Home connection
Make small books at home from folded paper with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child makes and shares a book" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
