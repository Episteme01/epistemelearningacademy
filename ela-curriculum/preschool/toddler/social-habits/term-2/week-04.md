# Toddler Social Habits — Term 2, Week 4: Good manners

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W04 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Saying sorry; Saying "it is okay"; Making things better

## Learning objectives
- Children will say sorry when they have hurt someone, with support.
- Children will say "it is okay" and help to make things better.

**Key vocabulary:** sorry, hurt, okay, forgive, help, kind

**Content:** Saying sorry, saying "it is okay", and making things better.

**Teaching activities:** Never force an empty sorry; first help the child notice the hurt ("Ada is crying, she is sad"); model sorry and make-it-better actions; teach the friend to say "it is okay" only if they feel it; keep it brief and kind; no shaming or time-out for toddlers.

**Learner activities:** Say sorry with support and help a friend who is upset.

**Resources:** Puppets; Pictures of hurt and sorry scenes; A plaster toy and a soft toy; A sorry song

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says sorry with a prompt and helps a friend who is upset.

## Expected outcomes
- Says sorry with a prompt.
- Helps a friend who is upset.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: sorry words; Creative Development: drama; Numeracy: counting actions

## Day 1 (Monday): Saying sorry

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Saying sorry

### Learning objectives
- Children will say sorry when they have hurt someone, with support.

### Success criteria
- Says sorry with a prompt
- Looks at the friend who was hurt

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, follow instructions and play with friends.

**Key vocabulary:** sorry, hurt, okay, forgive, help, kind

**Instructional materials:** Two puppets; A picture of a hurt friend; A sorry song

### Starter
Show one puppet bumping another and say "Oh no! The puppet is sad. We say sorry."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the puppet saying sorry in a kind voice. Children: Children watch and say sorry.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Sorry means I did not want to hurt you." Children: Children repeat sorry.

Step 3. Teacher: Practise saying sorry to a puppet. Children: Child says sorry to the puppet.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Thank you for saying sorry." Children: Children smile.

### Practical activity: Puppet sorry
1. Watch the puppets.
2. Say sorry to a puppet.
3. Say it kindly.

**Safety:** Keep puppet play gentle and never use a real child as the example. Never force sorry; help the child notice how the friend feels.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say sorry together softly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say why we say sorry.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say when we hurt someone?
  **Expected response:** Sorry.
- **Ask:** How does the friend feel?
  **Expected response:** Sad.

### Expected outcomes
- Says sorry with a prompt
- Looks at the friend who was hurt

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say sorry to the puppet.

### Home connection
Help your child notice another person’s feelings before saying sorry.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say sorry when they have hurt someone, with support" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SORRY
- HURT

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two puppets
- A sad face

**Song or rhyme:** Sorry, sorry, I am sorry. Let me help you feel better.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): It is okay

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Saying "it is okay"

### Learning objectives
- Children will say "it is okay" when a friend says sorry.

### Success criteria
- Says it is okay with a prompt
- Smiles at the friend

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, follow instructions and play with friends.

**Key vocabulary:** sorry, hurt, okay, forgive, help, kind

**Instructional materials:** Two puppets; A smiley face card; A picture of friends making up

### Starter
Let one puppet say sorry and the other say "It is okay. Let us play."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how the second puppet says it is okay. Children: Children watch and say it is okay.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "We forgive our friends." Children: Children repeat forgive with support.

Step 3. Teacher: Practise in pairs: one says sorry, one says it is okay. Children: Child says sorry or it is okay.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap roles so everyone has a go. Children: Child swaps and practises again.

### Practical activity: Sorry and okay
1. Work in pairs.
2. Say sorry.
3. Say it is okay.

**Safety:** Keep the practice make-believe with puppets or dolls and supervise closely. Never ask a hurt child to forgive before they are ready.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Practise with the puppet first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say another way to make up.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say after sorry?
  **Expected response:** It is okay.
- **Ask:** Do we stay cross?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Says it is okay with a prompt
- Smiles at the friend

### Conclusion
Shake hands or wave and say friends.

### Home connection
Say "it is okay" when your child says sorry and show forgiveness.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say "it is okay" when a friend says sorry" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Making it better

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Making things better

### Learning objectives
- Children will do something kind to help a friend who is upset.

### Success criteria
- Offers a comfort or help
- Gets an adult when a friend is hurt

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, follow instructions and play with friends.

**Key vocabulary:** sorry, hurt, okay, forgive, help, kind

**Instructional materials:** A soft toy; A toy plaster; A tissue; Pictures of ways to help

### Starter
Show a sad soft toy and say "How can we make it feel better?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show ways to help: a smile, a tissue, a toy or getting the teacher. Children: Children look and choose a way.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children comfort the soft toy. Children: Child comforts the toy gently.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "If a friend is hurt, get the teacher." Children: Children repeat get the teacher.

Step 4. Teacher: Practise running to the teacher at a walk. Children: Child walks to the teacher.

### Practical activity: Help a friend
1. Look at ways to help.
2. Comfort a soft toy.
3. Walk to get the teacher.

**Safety:** Keep play gentle and the floor clear. Never encourage children to treat real injuries; always get an adult.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Choose a way to help together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to think of a way to help a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How can we help a friend?
  **Expected response:** Smile or get the teacher.
- **Ask:** Who do we tell?
  **Expected response:** The teacher.

### Expected outcomes
- Offers comfort to a friend
- Knows to get an adult

### Conclusion
Hug the soft toy and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Show your child how to comfort someone who is upset.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "do something kind to help a friend who is upset" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Sorry story

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Saying sorry

### Learning objectives
- Children will act out a short story where sorry and forgiving put things right.

### Success criteria
- Joins the sorry story
- Uses sorry or it is okay in role play

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, follow instructions and play with friends.

**Key vocabulary:** sorry, hurt, okay, forgive, help, kind

**Instructional materials:** A story with simple pictures; Puppets; Toy blocks

### Starter
Tell a short story: "Two friends built a tower. One knocked it down by mistake. What happened next?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the story slowly with pictures and puppets. Children: Children listen and look.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "How did the friend feel?" Children: Child says sad.

Step 3. Teacher: Act out saying sorry and building again together. Children: Child acts out the story.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "They made things better." Children: Children say better.

### Practical activity: Tower story
1. Listen to the story.
2. Act it out with blocks.
3. Say sorry and build again.

**Safety:** Use large light blocks and supervise falling towers. Keep the story gentle and the ending kind.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Act the story with the child and help with words.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell the story to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did the friend say?
  **Expected response:** Sorry.
- **Ask:** What did they do?
  **Expected response:** Built again together.

### Expected outcomes
- Joins the sorry story
- Uses sorry and it is okay in role play

### Conclusion
Tidy the blocks and say "Friends."

### Home connection
Tell a short sorry story at bedtime.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "act out a short story where sorry and forgiving put things right" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 4 sorry check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Making things better

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child says sorry and helps a friend.

### Success criteria
- Says sorry with a prompt
- Helps a friend who is upset

**Previous knowledge:** Children can listen, follow instructions and play with friends.

**Key vocabulary:** sorry, hurt, okay, forgive, help, kind

**Instructional materials:** Puppets; Soft toys; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the sorry song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: sorry, it is okay and make it better. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child responds. Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Sorry stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep play gentle. Use puppets and soft toys only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to explain sorry to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says sorry; says it is okay; helps a friend who is upset; gets an adult when needed.

- **Ask:** What do we say when we hurt someone?
  **Expected response:** Sorry.
- **Ask:** What do we say after sorry?
  **Expected response:** It is okay.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Says sorry and helps with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep modelling sorry and forgiving at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child says sorry and helps a friend" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
