# Toddler Social Habits — Term 2, Week 3: Listening

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Listening ears; Following one instruction; Following two instructions

## Learning objectives
- Children will stop and listen when an adult speaks.
- Children will follow simple one-step and two-step instructions.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, ears, look, do, stop, quiet

**Content:** Listening ears, following one instruction and following two instructions.

**Teaching activities:** Get down to the child’s level and use their name before an instruction; give one instruction at a time at first; use gestures with words; praise listening; keep your voice calm; use games such as Simon Says.

**Learner activities:** Stop and look when called and follow one-step and two-step instructions.

**Resources:** A bell; Simon Says cards; Toys; Pictures of ears and eyes; A listening song

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child stops and looks when called and follows a one-step instruction.

## Expected outcomes
- Stops and looks at the teacher when called.
- Follows a one-step instruction.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: listening skills; Physical Development: action games; Numeracy: order words

## Day 1 (Monday): Listening ears

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Listening ears

### Learning objectives
- Children will stop, look and listen when an adult speaks.

### Success criteria
- Stops and looks at the teacher
- Touches ears on request

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with friends and use kind words.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, ears, look, do, stop, quiet

**Instructional materials:** A bell; Pictures of ears and eyes; A puppet

### Starter
Ring the bell and say "Stop, look and listen. Listening ears on."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to touch ears and look at the speaker. Children: Children touch their ears and look.

Step 2. Teacher: Ring the bell and say a child’s name. Children: Child stops and looks at the teacher.

Step 3. Teacher: Play a sound game: listen for the bell and clap. Children: Children listen and clap.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Good listening." Children: Children smile.

### Practical activity: Listening game
1. Listen for the bell.
2. Stop and look.
3. Clap when you hear it.

**Safety:** Use a bell that is not too loud. Keep the volume gentle, especially for sensitive children.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Get down to the child’s level and say their name first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the bell ringer.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we listen with?
  **Expected response:** Our ears.
- **Ask:** What do we do when we hear the bell?
  **Expected response:** Stop and look.

### Expected outcomes
- Stops and looks when called
- Touches ears on request

### Conclusion
Sit quietly and listen to a gentle sound.

### Home connection
Get your child’s attention gently by name before you give an instruction.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "stop, look and listen when an adult speaks" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): One instruction

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Following one instruction

### Learning objectives
- Children will follow a simple one-step instruction.

### Success criteria
- Follows one-step instructions
- Waits for the instruction to finish

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with friends and use kind words.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, ears, look, do, stop, quiet

**Instructional materials:** Simon Says cards; Toys; A basket

### Starter
Say "Simon says touch your nose" and show how to do it.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give a one-step instruction such as "Clap your hands". Children: Children clap their hands.

Step 2. Teacher: Give a one-step instruction with an object: "Put the ball in the basket." Children: Child puts the ball in the basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Praise children who follow it. Children: Children smile.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child give an instruction to the group. Children: Child gives an instruction.

### Practical activity: Simon says
1. Listen to the instruction.
2. Do it carefully.
3. Take turns being Simon.

**Safety:** Keep the floor clear and the actions simple and safe. Never punish a child who gets it wrong.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Show the action while you say it.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give an instruction to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you do?
  **Expected response:** The action.
- **Ask:** Who gave the instruction?
  **Expected response:** The teacher.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows one-step instructions
- Waits for the instruction to finish

### Conclusion
Clap and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Give your child one simple instruction at a time and praise them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "follow a simple one-step instruction" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Two instructions

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Following two instructions

### Learning objectives
- Children will follow two instructions in order.

### Success criteria
- Follows two instructions in order with support
- Says first and then

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with friends and use kind words.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, ears, look, do, stop, quiet

**Instructional materials:** Toys; Two baskets; Simon Says cards

### Starter
Say "First jump, then clap" and do both slowly.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give a two-step instruction: "Pick up the ball and give it to a friend." Children: Child picks up and gives.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "First and then" as you act. Children: Children repeat first and then.

Step 3. Teacher: Give another two-step instruction with a different object. Children: Child follows both steps.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise the listening. Children: Children smile.

### Practical activity: Two steps
1. Listen to two steps.
2. Do the first.
3. Do the second.

**Safety:** Keep steps safe and short and the floor clear. Go back to one step if a child finds two too hard.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Give one step at a time.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to remember three steps.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you do first?
  **Expected response:** The first action.
- **Ask:** What did you do then?
  **Expected response:** The second action.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows two instructions with support
- Uses first and then

### Conclusion
Do two actions together and wave.

### Home connection
Practise two-step instructions at home during routines.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "follow two instructions in order" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Listening games

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Listening ears

### Learning objectives
- Children will play listening games that need careful attention.

### Success criteria
- Plays a listening game
- Follows the signal

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with friends and use kind words.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, ears, look, do, stop, quiet

**Instructional materials:** A drum; A shaker; A bell; A scarf

### Starter
Say "Today we play listening games with sounds."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Play the drum fast and slow and let children move. Children: Children move fast and slow.

Step 2. Teacher: Stop the drum and say freeze. Children: Children freeze like statues.

Step 3. Teacher: Hide a bell and let children find it by listening. Children: Child listens and points.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "You are good listeners." Children: Children smile and clap and cheer.

### Practical activity: Sounds and freeze
1. Move to the drum.
2. Freeze when it stops.
3. Find the hidden bell.

**Safety:** Keep the volume gentle and the floor clear. Offer ear protection or a quiet space to a sensitive child.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move with the child and freeze together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the drum player.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do when the drum stops?
  **Expected response:** Freeze.
- **Ask:** What do we listen with?
  **Expected response:** Our ears.

### Expected outcomes
- Plays listening games
- Follows a signal

### Conclusion
Sit quietly and say "Thank you."

### Home connection
Play freeze games at home with music.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "play listening games that need careful attention" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 listening check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Following one instruction

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child listens and follows instructions.

### Success criteria
- Stops and looks when called
- Follows one or two instructions

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with friends and use kind words.

**Key vocabulary:** listen, ears, look, do, stop, quiet

**Instructional materials:** Bell; Toys; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the listening song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: bell, Simon Says and two steps. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station give instructions and watch. Children: Child follows with a prompt.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Listening stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep the volume gentle. Keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one-step instructions only.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to give instructions.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: stops and looks when called; follows one instruction; follows two instructions; listens in a group.

- **Ask:** What do we do when we are called?
  **Expected response:** Stop and look.
- **Ask:** What do we listen with?
  **Expected response:** Our ears.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Follows instructions with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep giving simple instructions at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child listens and follows instructions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

- Listening
