# Toddler Social Habits — Term 2, Week 9: Respect

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Respecting elders and teachers; Respecting helpers; Respecting different people

## Learning objectives
- Children will greet and speak kindly to elders and helpers.
- Children will accept that people are different and treat them kindly.

**Key vocabulary:** respect, elder, helper, different, kind, greet

**Content:** Respecting elders and teachers, respecting helpers, and respecting different people.

**Teaching activities:** Respect is shown in many ways across Nigerian homes (greeting, kneeling or bowing, using titles, listening); teach respect as kindness and listening, accept each family’s way, and never require a child to perform a gesture that their family does not use; use dolls and puppets to show respect for people who look, speak or move differently.

**Learner activities:** Greet and speak kindly to elders and helpers and treat people who are different from us with kindness.

**Resources:** Pictures of elders, teachers, cleaners, drivers and gatekeepers; Puppets; Dolls of different appearance; A respect song

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child greets elders and helpers and uses kind words with a child who is different.

## Expected outcomes
- Greets an elder or helper in a family-appropriate way.
- Uses kind words with a child who looks different.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: respect words; Understanding the World: people around us; Creative Development: role play

## Day 1 (Monday): Respecting elders and teachers

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Respecting elders and teachers

### Learning objectives
- Children will greet elders and teachers respectfully in a way their family uses.

### Success criteria
- Greets an elder with words or a gesture
- Listens to an elder

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use manners, tell the truth and care for belongings.

**Key vocabulary:** respect, elder, helper, different, kind, greet

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of elders and teachers; A puppet grandparent; A greeting song

### Starter
Show a puppet grandparent and say "Good morning, Grandma." Let the puppet answer warmly.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show pictures of elders and teachers and say who they are. Children: Children look and name them.

Step 2. Teacher: Show how to greet with a smile and good morning. Children: Children greet the puppet.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask children how they greet elders at home. Children: Child shows or says their greeting if they wish.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "We listen and speak kindly to elders." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

### Practical activity: Greeting elders
1. Look at the pictures.
2. Greet the puppet grandparent.
3. Show how you greet at home.

**Safety:** Accept every family’s way of greeting and never require a particular gesture. Keep puppet play gentle.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Greet together and use a simple word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach a friend a greeting from home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is an elder?
  **Expected response:** A grandparent or older person.
- **Ask:** What do we say?
  **Expected response:** Good morning.

### Expected outcomes
- Greets an elder respectfully
- Listens to an elder

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye with a respectful greeting.

### Home connection
Show your child how your family greets elders.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "greet elders and teachers respectfully in a way their family uses" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- ELDER
- RESPECT

**Picture suggestions:**
- An elderly woman

**Song or rhyme:** Good morning, Grandma, good morning, Grandpa.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Respecting helpers

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Respecting helpers

### Learning objectives
- Children will greet and thank people who help the school.

### Success criteria
- Greets a helper
- Says thank you to a helper

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use manners, tell the truth and care for belongings.

**Key vocabulary:** respect, elder, helper, different, kind, greet

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of the cleaner, gatekeeper, cook, driver and nurse; A thank-you card

### Starter
Show a picture of the school cleaner and say "This person keeps our school clean. We say thank you."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each helper picture and say what they do. Children: Children look and repeat the job.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "We greet helpers and say thank you." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

Step 3. Teacher: Walk to meet a helper and let children greet them. Children: Children say hello and thank you.

Step 4. Teacher: Make a thank-you card together. Children: Child makes a mark on the card.

### Practical activity: Thank our helpers
1. Look at the pictures.
2. Greet a helper.
3. Make a thank-you card.

**Safety:** Keep the walk short and supervised and ask the helper beforehand. Use non-toxic crayons.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk with the child and greet together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what a helper does.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who keeps our school clean?
  **Expected response:** The cleaner.
- **Ask:** What do we say to helpers?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Greets and thanks a helper
- Names what a helper does

### Conclusion
Give the thank-you card and wave.

### Home connection
Greet and thank helpers you meet with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "greet and thank people who help the school" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HELPER
- THANK YOU

**Picture suggestions:**
- A school cleaner
- A gatekeeper

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Everyone is different

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Respecting different people

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice that people are different and treat everyone kindly.

### Success criteria
- Says that friends are different
- Uses kind words to a child who is different

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use manners, tell the truth and care for belongings.

**Key vocabulary:** respect, elder, helper, different, kind, greet

**Instructional materials:** Dolls or pictures of children of different skin tones, abilities and clothing; A mirror

### Starter
Show two dolls that look different and say "These friends look different. They are both our friends."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show pictures of children who look and move differently. Children: Children look and say friend.

Step 2. Teacher: Look in a mirror and say "I am special." Children: Child looks and smiles.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "We are kind to everyone." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

Step 4. Teacher: Play a game where friends do different things. Children: Child plays with a friend.

### Practical activity: We are all friends
1. Look at the pictures.
2. Look in the mirror.
3. Play with a friend.

**Safety:** Answer children’s questions about differences simply and kindly and never allow teasing. Use an unbreakable mirror.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Look at the picture together and say friend.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say one way they are like a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Are all friends the same?
  **Expected response:** No, we are different.
- **Ask:** Are we kind to everyone?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Says that people are different
- Is kind to everyone

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say "We are friends."

### Home connection
Talk kindly about differences when your child notices them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice that people are different and treat everyone kindly" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- DIFFERENT
- KIND

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children of different skin tones

## Day 4 (Thursday): Respect role play

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Respecting helpers

### Learning objectives
- Children will act out respectful greetings and thanks in role play.

### Success criteria
- Acts a respectful greeting
- Uses thank you in role play

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use manners, tell the truth and care for belongings.

**Key vocabulary:** respect, elder, helper, different, kind, greet

**Instructional materials:** Hats for a teacher, a nurse and a driver; Toy phones; A play area

### Starter
Show three hats and say "Today we meet three helpers. Let us greet them."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put on a hat and say "I am the nurse." Children: Children greet the nurse.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child try a hat and be the helper. Children: Child takes a role.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Thank you for helping us." Children: Children say thank you.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap roles so everyone has a go. Children: Child swaps and plays again.

### Practical activity: Helper role play
1. Put on a hat.
2. Greet the helper.
3. Say thank you.

**Safety:** Use hats that are clean and shared fairly and supervise swapping. Keep play gentle.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Play the helper with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose the helper and greet them.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is the helper?
  **Expected response:** A nurse, teacher or driver.
- **Ask:** What do you say?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Acts a respectful greeting
- Says thank you in role play

### Conclusion
Take off the hats and say "Thank you, helpers."

### Home connection
Play helpers at home and practise greetings.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "act out respectful greetings and thanks in role play" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 9 respect check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Respecting elders and teachers

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child shows respect.

### Success criteria
- Greets an elder or helper
- Is kind to everyone

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use manners, tell the truth and care for belongings.

**Key vocabulary:** respect, elder, helper, different, kind, greet

**Instructional materials:** Pictures; Puppets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the respect song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: elders, helpers and different friends. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child behaves. Children: Child responds with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Respect stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep play gentle. Respect every family’s way of showing respect.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to greet a visitor.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: greets an elder; thanks a helper; is kind to a child who is different; listens to adults.

- **Ask:** What do we say to an elder?
  **Expected response:** Good morning.
- **Ask:** What do we say to helpers?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Shows respect with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep showing respect to everyone at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child shows respect" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Respect

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
