# Toddler Social Habits — Term 2 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Family**: Who is in my family; What my family does for me; Showing love to my family | Children will name people in their family. Children will say one way they show love to their family. | Who is in my family, what my family does for me, and showing love to my family. | Welcome every kind of family: children may live with parents, grandparents, aunts, uncles, guardians or siblings; never ask a child to explain a family situation; use family photographs only if the family agrees; use home-language words for family members and repeat them in English. | Name people in their family and show or say love to a family member. | Family photographs brought from home (optional); A doll family or picture cards; A heart cut-out; A family song; Crayons | Observe whether each child names at least two family members and shows love in a simple way. | Names at least two family members. Shows or says love to a family member. | Literacy: family words; Numeracy: counting family members; Creative Development: drawing |
| 2 | **Friends**: Playing with friends; Being a good friend; Making friends | Children will play beside and with friends. Children will say what a good friend does. | Playing with friends, being a good friend, and making friends. | Notice and name friendly acts; let children choose play partners; help shy children join with a simple script such as "Can I play?"; avoid forcing friendships; use pairs and small groups; reassure children when a friend is absent. | Play with a friend for a short time and say what a good friend does. | Matching toys; A friendship song; Puppets; Pictures of friends playing; A friendship chart | Observe whether each child plays with a friend for a short time and says one thing a good friend does. | Plays with a friend for a short time. Says one thing a good friend does. | Literacy: friend words; Numeracy: pairs; Creative Development: group songs |
| 3 | **Listening**: Listening ears; Following one instruction; Following two instructions | Children will stop and listen when an adult speaks. Children will follow simple one-step and two-step instructions. | Listening ears, following one instruction and following two instructions. | Get down to the child’s level and use their name before an instruction; give one instruction at a time at first; use gestures with words; praise listening; keep your voice calm; use games such as Simon Says. | Stop and look when called and follow one-step and two-step instructions. | A bell; Simon Says cards; Toys; Pictures of ears and eyes; A listening song | Observe whether each child stops and looks when called and follows a one-step instruction. | Stops and looks at the teacher when called. Follows a one-step instruction. | Literacy: listening skills; Physical Development: action games; Numeracy: order words |
| 4 | **Good manners**: Saying sorry; Saying "it is okay"; Making things better | Children will say sorry when they have hurt someone, with support. Children will say "it is okay" and help to make things better. | Saying sorry, saying "it is okay", and making things better. | Never force an empty sorry; first help the child notice the hurt ("Ada is crying, she is sad"); model sorry and make-it-better actions; teach the friend to say "it is okay" only if they feel it; keep it brief and kind; no shaming or time-out for toddlers. | Say sorry with support and help a friend who is upset. | Puppets; Pictures of hurt and sorry scenes; A plaster toy and a soft toy; A sorry song | Observe whether each child says sorry with a prompt and helps a friend who is upset. | Says sorry with a prompt. Helps a friend who is upset. | Literacy: sorry words; Creative Development: drama; Numeracy: counting actions |
| 5 | **Responsibility**: Helping my teacher; Helping at home; Our class helpers | Children will do small helping jobs. Children will say how they helped. | Helping my teacher, helping at home, and our class helpers. | Give every child a small real job; make jobs safe and short; praise the effort; rotate helpers daily; use a helpers chart; link jobs at school to jobs at home; never compare children. | Do small helping jobs and say "I helped". | A helpers chart; Small brooms and cloths; Baskets; A watering can; Pictures of helping at home | Observe whether each child does a small job with support and says "I helped". | Does a small job with support. Says "I helped". | Numeracy: counting helpers; Science: caring for plants; Literacy: job words |
| 6 | **Honesty**: What is true; Telling the truth when something goes wrong; Being brave to say the truth | Children will say what is true about simple pictures and events. Children will tell an adult the truth about a small accident with support. | What is true, telling the truth when something goes wrong, and being brave to say the truth. | Toddlers mix pretend and real, so teach "true" with simple real facts; never trap a child into lying by asking "Did you do it?" when you know; thank children for telling the truth even when they made a mess; respond calmly; use stories and puppets. | Say what is true about simple pictures and events and tell an adult about a spill or mistake. | Picture cards showing true and not-true things; A puppet; A spilled cup scene; A truth song; A star chart | Observe whether each child says what is true about a simple event and tells an adult about a mistake. | Says what is true about a simple event. Tells an adult about a spill or mistake. | Literacy: true and not true; Creative Development: story and drama; Numeracy: matching |
| 7 | **Responsibility**: My bag and my things; Caring for toys and books; Putting things back | Children will care for toys, books and their own belongings. Children will put things back in the right place. | My bag and my things, caring for toys and books, and putting things back. | Give every child a labelled hook and bag; show how to hold a book gently; use pictures on shelves so children can put things back; praise care not just tidiness; replace broken items without shame. | Care for toys, books and belongings and put things back in the right place. | Bags and hooks with names; Picture books; Toy baskets with picture labels; A shelf; A caring song | Observe whether each child puts things back and handles a book gently. | Puts a toy back where it belongs. Handles a book gently. | Literacy: book handling; Numeracy: matching and sorting; Science: caring for the environment |
| 8 | **Good manners**: Washing hands before eating; Sitting and eating nicely; Not wasting food | Children will wash hands before eating and sit to eat. Children will say please and thank you at mealtimes. | Washing hands before eating, sitting and eating nicely, and not wasting food. | Check allergies and dietary needs before any food activity; use real snack time where possible; never force a child to eat; respect families’ ways of eating (with hands, spoons or forks) and food customs; keep a second adult nearby for choking safety; avoid whole nuts, hard sweets and other choking hazards. | Wash hands before eating, sit to eat and say please and thank you at mealtimes. | Plastic plates and cups; A play kitchen or toy food; Washing basin, soap and towels; A snack-time song; Pictures of eating nicely | Observe whether each child washes hands before eating, sits to eat and says thank you. | Washes hands before eating with support. Sits to eat and says thank you. | Science: food and health; Literacy: mealtime words; Numeracy: one each |
| 9 | **Respect**: Respecting elders and teachers; Respecting helpers; Respecting different people | Children will greet and speak kindly to elders and helpers. Children will accept that people are different and treat them kindly. | Respecting elders and teachers, respecting helpers, and respecting different people. | Respect is shown in many ways across Nigerian homes (greeting, kneeling or bowing, using titles, listening); teach respect as kindness and listening, accept each family’s way, and never require a child to perform a gesture that their family does not use; use dolls and puppets to show respect for people who look, speak or move differently. | Greet and speak kindly to elders and helpers and treat people who are different from us with kindness. | Pictures of elders, teachers, cleaners, drivers and gatekeepers; Puppets; Dolls of different appearance; A respect song | Observe whether each child greets elders and helpers and uses kind words with a child who is different. | Greets an elder or helper in a family-appropriate way. Uses kind words with a child who looks different. | Literacy: respect words; Understanding the World: people around us; Creative Development: role play |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising family and friends; Revising manners and honesty; Revising helping and respect | Children will show Term 2 learning through play stations. Teachers will gather evidence for every child’s Term 2 record. | Revising family and friends, manners and honesty, and helping and respect, with end-of-term records. | Keep every activity familiar and joyful; observe quietly during play; record what children can do; share records with parents in plain language with a strength, a next step and a home idea. | Show learning from the term through play stations and a celebration. | Family cards; Puppets; A play kitchen; Helper hats; Baskets; Stickers; Class record sheets | Observe and record each child’s Term 2 social learning. | Completes familiar social tasks with growing independence. Each child has an observation record for Term 2. | Literacy: songs; Numeracy: counting; Creative Development: role play |
