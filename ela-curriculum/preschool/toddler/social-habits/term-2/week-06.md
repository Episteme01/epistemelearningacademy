# Toddler Social Habits — Term 2, Week 6: Honesty

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** What is true; Telling the truth when something goes wrong; Being brave to say the truth

## Learning objectives
- Children will say what is true about simple pictures and events.
- Children will tell an adult the truth about a small accident with support.

**Key vocabulary:** true, truth, honest, oops, brave, tell

**Content:** What is true, telling the truth when something goes wrong, and being brave to say the truth.

**Teaching activities:** Toddlers mix pretend and real, so teach "true" with simple real facts; never trap a child into lying by asking "Did you do it?" when you know; thank children for telling the truth even when they made a mess; respond calmly; use stories and puppets.

**Learner activities:** Say what is true about simple pictures and events and tell an adult about a spill or mistake.

**Resources:** Picture cards showing true and not-true things; A puppet; A spilled cup scene; A truth song; A star chart

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says what is true about a simple event and tells an adult about a mistake.

## Expected outcomes
- Says what is true about a simple event.
- Tells an adult about a spill or mistake.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: true and not true; Creative Development: story and drama; Numeracy: matching

## Day 1 (Monday): What is true?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** What is true

### Learning objectives
- Children will say whether a simple statement about a picture is true.

### Success criteria
- Says true or not true with support
- Points to what is real

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say sorry, help others and do small jobs.

**Key vocabulary:** true, truth, honest, oops, brave, tell

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards of a dog, a cat, a car and a tree; A puppet; A truth song

### Starter
Show a picture of a dog and say "This is a dog. That is true." Then say "This is a fish. That is not true."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a picture and say a true sentence. Children: Children say true.

Step 2. Teacher: Show a picture and say a silly untrue sentence. Children: Children say not true and laugh.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child say a true thing about a picture. Children: Child says a true thing.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "We tell what is true." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

### Practical activity: True or not true
1. Look at a picture.
2. Listen to a sentence.
3. Say true or not true.

**Safety:** Keep the examples silly and safe and never make a child feel wrong. Accept pretend play as pretend.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two pictures and say which is true.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say a true sentence about the room.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is this a dog?
  **Expected response:** Yes, it is true.
- **Ask:** Is this a fish?
  **Expected response:** No, not true.

### Expected outcomes
- Says true or not true with support
- Says a true thing about a picture

### Conclusion
Sing the truth song and wave.

### Home connection
Play a true or not true game at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say whether a simple statement about a picture is true" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- TRUE
- REAL

**Picture suggestions:**
- A dog picture
- A cat picture

**Song or rhyme:** Tell the truth, tell the truth, it is the best thing to do.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Telling the truth about a spill

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Telling the truth when something goes wrong

### Learning objectives
- Children will tell an adult when they spill or break something by accident.

### Success criteria
- Says oops and tells an adult
- Hears the adult stay calm

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say sorry, help others and do small jobs.

**Key vocabulary:** true, truth, honest, oops, brave, tell

**Instructional materials:** A plastic cup of water on a tray; A cloth; A puppet

### Starter
Show the puppet spilling a cup and say "Oops! The puppet tells the teacher."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the puppet telling the teacher in a small voice. Children: Children watch and say oops.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Thank you for telling me" calmly and wipe the spill. Children: Children watch and say thank you.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pretend to spill and tell the teacher. Children: Child says oops and tells.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Accidents happen. We tell the truth." Children: Children repeat tell the truth.

### Practical activity: Oops and tell
1. Spill a little water on a tray.
2. Say oops aloud.
3. Tell the teacher.

**Safety:** Use only a little water on a tray and wipe spills at once. Never scold a child who tells the truth about an accident.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say oops together and tell the teacher.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell what happened in one sentence.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say after a spill?
  **Expected response:** Oops.
- **Ask:** Who do we tell?
  **Expected response:** The teacher.

### Expected outcomes
- Says oops and tells an adult
- Understands that telling the truth is safe

### Conclusion
Wipe the table together and say thank you.

### Home connection
Thank your child for telling the truth even when they made a mess.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "tell an adult when they spill or break something by accident" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- OOPS
- TELL

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Being brave to tell the truth

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Being brave to say the truth

### Learning objectives
- Children will see that telling the truth is brave and makes people trust us.

### Success criteria
- Says that telling the truth is good
- Joins a story about telling the truth

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say sorry, help others and do small jobs.

**Key vocabulary:** true, truth, honest, oops, brave, tell

**Instructional materials:** A short story with pictures; A puppet; A brave badge

### Starter
Tell a very short story: "Kemi broke a cup. She was scared. She told her mother the truth. Mother said thank you for telling me."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the story with pictures slowly. Children: Children listen and look.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "How did Kemi feel at first?" Children: Child says scared.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What did Kemi do?" Children: Child says she told the truth.

Step 4. Teacher: Give a brave badge to the story and say "Truth is brave." Children: Children clap and cheer.

### Practical activity: Story and badge
1. Listen to the story.
2. Say what Kemi did.
3. Wear a brave badge.

**Safety:** Use a gentle story with a kind ending. Never use frightening punishments in stories.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Tell the story in two sentences with pictures.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what they would do.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did Kemi do?
  **Expected response:** Told the truth.
- **Ask:** Was it brave?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Understands that telling the truth is brave
- Retells part of the story

### Conclusion
Say "I tell the truth" together.

### Home connection
Read a story about honesty and talk about it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "see that telling the truth is brave and makes people trust us" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BRAVE
- TRUTH

**Picture suggestions:**
- A story picture of a girl and her mother

## Day 4 (Thursday): Truth and pretend

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** What is true

### Learning objectives
- Children will tell the difference between real and pretend in play.

### Success criteria
- Says real or pretend
- Joins pretend play

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say sorry, help others and do small jobs.

**Key vocabulary:** true, truth, honest, oops, brave, tell

**Instructional materials:** A toy phone; A real cup and a toy cup; Pictures of real and pretend things

### Starter
Hold a toy cup and say "This is a pretend cup. This one is a real cup."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a real and a pretend object and name them. Children: Children say real or pretend.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children pretend to drink from a toy cup. Children: Child pretends to drink.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Pretend is fun. Truth is real." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

Step 4. Teacher: Sort pictures into real and pretend. Children: Child sorts a picture.

### Practical activity: Real and pretend
1. Look at objects.
2. Say real or pretend.
3. Sort the pictures.

**Safety:** Use safe objects only. Keep play gentle and never call a child a liar for pretending.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects and say real or pretend.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a real thing and a pretend thing.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the toy cup real?
  **Expected response:** No, pretend.
- **Ask:** Is the water real?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Tells real from pretend
- Joins pretend play

### Conclusion
Put the toys away and say "Real or pretend."

### Home connection
Enjoy pretend play at home and name what is real.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "tell the difference between real and pretend in play" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- REAL
- PRETEND

**Picture suggestions:**
- A real cup
- A toy cup

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 truth check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T2-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** What is true

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child understands and says what is true.

### Success criteria
- Says true or not true
- Tells an adult about an accident

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say sorry, help others and do small jobs.

**Key vocabulary:** true, truth, honest, oops, brave, tell

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards; A puppet; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the truth song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: true or not true, oops and tell, and real or pretend. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child answers. Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Truth stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors dry. Never use a test that traps a child into a lie.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to say why we tell the truth.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says true or not true; tells an adult about an accident; tells real from pretend; shows trust in adults.

- **Ask:** What do we tell?
  **Expected response:** The truth.
- **Ask:** Who do we tell?
  **Expected response:** A grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Says what is true with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep welcoming the truth at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child understands and says what is true" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Truth

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
