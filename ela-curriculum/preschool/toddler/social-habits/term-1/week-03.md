# Toddler Social Habits — Term 1, Week 3: Good manners

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Saying please; Saying thank you; Polite asking and taking

## Learning objectives
- Children will say please when asking for something.
- Children will say thank you when they receive something.

**Key vocabulary:** please, thank you, give, take, ask, kind

**Content:** Saying please when asking, thank you when receiving, and polite asking and taking.

**Teaching activities:** Model please and thank you constantly in daily routines; praise the words, not only the result; never force a child to say them; use a puppet to show the right and wrong way; link to snack time and play.

**Learner activities:** Say please and thank you with prompting in play and snack routines.

**Resources:** Snack items such as biscuits or fruit; A puppet; Toys; A picture of a child asking nicely; A magic words song

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says please and thank you with a prompt.

## Expected outcomes
- Says please with a prompt.
- Says thank you with a prompt.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: magic words; Numeracy: one each; Physical Development: giving and receiving

## Day 1 (Monday): Saying please

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Saying please

### Learning objectives
- Children will say please when they ask for something.

### Success criteria
- Says please with a prompt
- Waits for the item

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet and say goodbye with a word or a wave.

**Key vocabulary:** please, thank you, give, take, ask, kind

**Instructional materials:** A puppet; A bowl of plastic fruit; A toy; A picture of a child asking nicely

### Starter
Show the puppet reaching for a toy and say "Please may I have the toy?" and give it.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the puppet snatching and then asking nicely with please. Children: Children watch and say please.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Please is a magic word." Children: Children repeat please.

Step 3. Teacher: Hold up a toy and say "Say please to get the toy." Children: Child says please and gets the toy.

Step 4. Teacher: Practise asking for things at the snack table. Children: Child asks with please.

### Practical activity: Asking with please
1. Put toys in a bowl.
2. Ask each child to say please for a toy.
3. Give the toy with a smile.

**Safety:** Use large toys with no small parts. Never withhold a basic need such as a drink or a toilet visit until a child says please.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say please together with a gesture.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to remind a friend to say please.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is the magic word?
  **Expected response:** Please.
- **Ask:** When do we say please?
  **Expected response:** When we ask for something.

### Expected outcomes
- Says please with a prompt
- Waits for an item after asking

### Conclusion
Say "Please and thank you" together.

### Home connection
Model please at home and wait for your child to say it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say please when they ask for something" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**
- PLEASE
- ASK

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child saying please

**Song or rhyme:** Please, please, the magic word. Please, please, nicely heard.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Saying thank you

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Saying thank you

### Learning objectives
- Children will say thank you when they receive something.

### Success criteria
- Says thank you with a prompt
- Smiles when thanking

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet and say goodbye with a word or a wave.

**Key vocabulary:** please, thank you, give, take, ask, kind

**Instructional materials:** A puppet; Small gifts such as stickers; A picture of a child saying thank you

### Starter
Give a sticker to a puppet and have it say "Thank you!" with a big smile.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to say thank you when someone gives something. Children: Children watch and say thank you.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Thank you is another magic word." Children: Children repeat thank you.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each child a sticker. Children: Child says thank you and takes the sticker.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children give a sticker to a friend and hear thank you. Children: Child gives and says you are welcome.

### Practical activity: Giving and thanking
1. Hold out a sticker.
2. The child says thank you.
3. Give a sticker to a friend.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic stickers and watch for children who put stickers in their mouths. Never force the words; model and wait.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say thank you together with a smile.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say thank you to a helper.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say when we get something?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.
- **Ask:** Who do we thank?
  **Expected response:** Anyone who helps or gives.

### Expected outcomes
- Says thank you with a prompt
- Smiles when thanking

### Conclusion
Say "Thank you, friends" together.

### Home connection
Say thank you for small things at home and let your child copy.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say thank you when they receive something" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Asking and taking nicely

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Polite asking and taking

### Learning objectives
- Children will ask for something with please and take it gently.

### Success criteria
- Asks with please
- Takes with a gentle hand

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet and say goodbye with a word or a wave.

**Key vocabulary:** please, thank you, give, take, ask, kind

**Instructional materials:** Toys and a snack tray; A puppet; Pictures of nice and not-nice taking

### Starter
Show two pictures: a child grabbing and a child asking nicely. Ask "Which is nice?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the puppet grabbing and say "Oh no, not nice." Children: Children say not nice.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the puppet asking with please and thank you. Children: Children say nice.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child ask for something at the table. Children: Child says please.

Step 4. Teacher: Give the item and wait for thank you. Children: Child says thank you nicely.

### Practical activity: Snack table manners
1. Put out a snack.
2. Ask with please.
3. Say thank you.

**Safety:** Check for allergies before any snack and keep the portion small. Supervise eating closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say the words together and show the picture.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the helper who gives to friends.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is grabbing nice?
  **Expected response:** No.
- **Ask:** What do we say when we ask?
  **Expected response:** Please.

### Expected outcomes
- Asks with please
- Takes gently and says thank you

### Conclusion
Say "Please and thank you" together.

### Home connection
Practise asking nicely at mealtimes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "ask for something with please and take it gently" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Please and thank you game

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Saying please

### Learning objectives
- Children will use please and thank you in a pretend shop.

### Success criteria
- Uses please and thank you in play
- Takes turns being shopkeeper

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet and say goodbye with a word or a wave.

**Key vocabulary:** please, thank you, give, take, ask, kind

**Instructional materials:** A play shop with toy food; Play money or tokens; A basket

### Starter
Set up a play shop and say "Today we visit the shop and use our magic words."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to buy something with please and thank you. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child be the shopkeeper or the customer. Children: Child asks or gives.

Step 3. Teacher: Remind children to say please and thank you. Children: Child uses the magic words.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap roles so everyone has a go. Children: Child changes roles.

### Practical activity: Play shop
1. Set up the shop.
2. Customers ask with please.
3. Shopkeepers say thank you.

**Safety:** Use only large toys that cannot be swallowed and supervise sharing. Keep the area clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Play the shopkeeper while the child is the customer.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the shopkeeper and remind customers.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you say to buy?
  **Expected response:** Please.
- **Ask:** What do you say when you get it?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses please and thank you in play
- Takes turns in a role play

### Conclusion
Tidy the shop and say thank you together.

### Home connection
Play shop at home and practise please and thank you.

- Which children could show "use please and thank you in a pretend shop" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 manners check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Polite asking and taking

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child uses please and thank you.

### Success criteria
- Says please with a prompt
- Says thank you with a prompt

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet and say goodbye with a word or a wave.

**Key vocabulary:** please, thank you, give, take, ask, kind

**Instructional materials:** Snack items; A play shop; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the magic words song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: snack, shop and puppet. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child asks and thanks. Children: Child uses magic words with a prompt.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Manners stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Check for allergies before snacks and supervise every station. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to remind a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says please; says thank you; asks and takes nicely; uses manners in play.

- **Ask:** What are the magic words?
  **Expected response:** Please and thank you.
- **Ask:** When do we say please?
  **Expected response:** When we ask for something.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Uses magic words with a prompt

### Conclusion
Say "Thank you, friends" and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Keep using please and thank you every day at home.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
