# Toddler Social Habits — Term 1, Week 5: Routines

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W05 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Circle time and listening; Lining up and walking; Tidy-up time

## Learning objectives
- Children will follow simple classroom routines.
- Children will help to tidy up when asked.

**Key vocabulary:** line, sit, listen, tidy, circle, walk

**Content:** Circle time and listening, lining up and walking, and tidy-up time.

**Teaching activities:** Use the same routine words and songs every day; show before you tell; give a warning before change; praise children who follow the routine; keep each routine short; let children have jobs.

**Learner activities:** Follow simple classroom routines such as sitting in the circle, lining up and tidying.

**Resources:** A mat for circle time; A tidy-up song; A picture timetable; Baskets for toys; A line of footprints on the floor

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child sits in the circle, joins a line and helps to tidy with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Sits in the circle and joins a line with support.
- Helps to tidy toys.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Physical Development: walking in a line; Literacy: routine songs; Numeracy: counting in line

## Day 1 (Monday): Circle time

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Circle time and listening

### Learning objectives
- Children will sit in the circle and listen for a short time.

### Success criteria
- Sits on the mat for two minutes
- Looks at the teacher when called

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet, use please and thank you and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** line, sit, listen, tidy, circle, walk

**Instructional materials:** A mat; A circle song; A puppet

### Starter
Sing "Come and sit, come and sit, sit in the circle" and show where to sit.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to sit on the mat with a quiet body. Children: Children sit and look.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Listening ears on" and touch your ears. Children: Children touch their ears and look.

Step 3. Teacher: Tell a very short story with the puppet. Children: Children listen and smile.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise children who sit well. Children: Children stay seated for a short time.

### Practical activity: Circle time practice
1. Sing the circle song.
2. Sit in a circle.
3. Listen to a short story.

**Safety:** Leave space between children and sit away from furniture. Never force a child to sit; offer a lap or a quiet spot.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sit beside the child and use a short time.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to help a friend find a place.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where do we sit?
  **Expected response:** On the mat.
- **Ask:** What do we use to listen?
  **Expected response:** Our ears.

### Expected outcomes
- Sits in the circle for a short time
- Looks at the teacher when called

### Conclusion
Sing the goodbye song and stand up quietly.

### Home connection
Practise sitting together for a short story at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sit in the circle and listen for a short time" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Lining up and walking

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Lining up and walking

### Learning objectives
- Children will line up and walk with the group.

### Success criteria
- Joins a line with support
- Walks beside the teacher

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet, use please and thank you and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** line, sit, listen, tidy, circle, walk

**Instructional materials:** Footprints on the floor; A rope with loops or a hand-hold; A line-up song

### Starter
Put footprints on the floor and say "Let us stand on the footprints and make a line."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to stand on a footprint. Children: Children stand on footprints.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Walk, walk, walk" and lead the line. Children: Children walk in a line.

Step 3. Teacher: Stop and start with a signal. Children: Children stop and wait.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk to the door and back. Children: Children walk with a friend.

### Practical activity: Line and walk
1. Stand on footprints.
2. Walk in a line.
3. Stop and start together.

**Safety:** Keep the route clear of hazards and keep children at a walking pace. An adult must be at the front and the back of the line.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand at the front of the line.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to be the line leader.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How do we walk?
  **Expected response:** In a line.
- **Ask:** What do we do at the signal?
  **Expected response:** Stop.

### Expected outcomes
- Joins a line with support
- Walks and stops on a signal

### Conclusion
Sing the line-up song and sit down.

### Home connection
Practise walking holding hands at home and outside.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "line up and walk with the group" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Tidy-up time

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Tidy-up time

### Learning objectives
- Children will help to put toys away when the tidy-up song plays.

### Success criteria
- Puts a toy in a basket
- Says tidy up

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet, use please and thank you and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** line, sit, listen, tidy, circle, walk

**Instructional materials:** Baskets for toys; Toys with pictures on the baskets; A tidy-up song

### Starter
Sing "Tidy up, tidy up, everybody tidy up" and point to the toys on the floor.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to put a block in the block basket. Children: Children watch and copy.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing the tidy-up song while children work. Children: Children put toys in baskets.

Step 3. Teacher: Check the floor together and say "All tidy." Children: Child looks at the floor and says tidy.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise the tidy room. Children: Children clap and cheer.

### Practical activity: Tidy up
1. Look at the toys on the floor.
2. Put each in its basket.
3. Check the floor.

**Safety:** Keep walkways clear and supervise lifting of large items. Use large, light toys only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Tidy together and name each toy.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend where a toy goes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where does this toy go?
  **Expected response:** In its basket.
- **Ask:** Is the floor tidy?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Puts toys in baskets
- Says tidy up with a prompt

### Conclusion
Look at the tidy room and say "Well done."

### Home connection
Give your child a small tidying job at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "help to put toys away when the tidy-up song plays" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Our daily routine

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Circle time and listening

### Learning objectives
- Children will follow the picture timetable and know what comes next.

### Success criteria
- Looks at the timetable pictures
- Moves to the next activity with a prompt

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet, use please and thank you and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** line, sit, listen, tidy, circle, walk

**Instructional materials:** A picture timetable with four to six pictures; A bell; Toys

### Starter
Show the picture timetable and say "First circle, then play, then snack, then home."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each picture and say what happens. Children: Children look and name the activity.

Step 2. Teacher: Ring the bell and move to the next picture. Children: Children move to the next activity.

Step 3. Teacher: Let a child turn over a picture to show what is next. Children: Child turns the picture.

Step 4. Teacher: Talk about how routines help us feel safe. Children: Children smile and nod.

### Practical activity: Following the timetable
1. Look at the timetable.
2. Do each activity in order.
3. Turn the picture.

**Safety:** Keep transitions calm and give a warning before changes. Stay near children who find change hard.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the picture and walk with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell what comes after snack.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What comes next?
  **Expected response:** The next activity.
- **Ask:** What do we do after play?
  **Expected response:** Tidy up.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows the picture timetable
- Moves to the next activity with a prompt

### Conclusion
Sing the goodbye song and wave.

### Home connection
Use a simple picture routine at home for morning and bedtime.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "follow the picture timetable and know what comes next" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 routines check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Tidy-up time

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child follows routines.

### Success criteria
- Sits in the circle
- Helps to tidy up

**Previous knowledge:** Children can greet, use please and thank you and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** line, sit, listen, tidy, circle, walk

**Instructional materials:** Mat; Baskets; Footprints; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the circle song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: circle, line and tidy. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child follows the routine. Children: Child follows with a prompt.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Routine stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep walkways clear. Use light, large objects only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one routine.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to lead the line.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: sits for circle time; joins a line; helps to tidy; follows the picture timetable.

- **Ask:** Where do we sit?
  **Expected response:** On the mat.
- **Ask:** What do we do with toys?
  **Expected response:** Put them away.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Follows classroom routines with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep using simple routines at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child follows routines" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
