# Toddler Social Habits — Term 1, Week 2: Greetings

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Saying good morning; Greeting my teacher and friends; Saying goodbye and waving

## Learning objectives
- Children will greet people with words or a wave.
- Children will say goodbye politely.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, good morning, goodbye, wave, smile, friend

**Content:** Saying good morning, greeting the teacher and friends, and saying goodbye with a wave.

**Teaching activities:** Greet every child by name each morning and expect a response of any kind; model "Good morning" with eye contact; respect that families greet elders in different ways (a wave, a bow, a kneel or a handshake) and never correct a family’s greeting; praise every attempt.

**Learner activities:** Greet people with a word or a wave and say goodbye politely.

**Resources:** A greeting song; Puppets; Pictures of morning, afternoon and evening; A teacher’s bell

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child greets the teacher with a word or gesture and waves goodbye.

## Expected outcomes
- Greets the teacher with a word or gesture.
- Waves goodbye.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: greeting words; Numeracy: counting the greeters; Creative Development: song and movement

## Day 1 (Monday): Good morning

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Saying good morning

### Learning objectives
- Children will say or show good morning to the teacher and friends.

### Success criteria
- Says or signs good morning with support
- Looks at the person they greet

**Previous knowledge:** Children know their names, their teacher and the classroom, and are practising goodbyes.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, good morning, goodbye, wave, smile, friend

**Instructional materials:** A puppet; A greeting song; A picture of the sun rising

### Starter
Use a puppet to say "Good morning, friends!" and let the puppet greet each child.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a picture of the sun rising and say "It is morning. We say good morning." Children: Children look and say good morning.

Step 2. Teacher: Greet each child by name: "Good morning, Ada." Children: Child answers with a word or wave.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing the good morning song with actions. Children: Children sing and do actions.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children greet the puppet. Children: Child says good morning to the puppet.

### Practical activity: Good morning circle
1. Sit in a circle.
2. Greet each child.
3. Let them greet the puppet.

**Safety:** Keep the circle small and quiet and sit children away from hazards. Never force a child to speak; accept a wave.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Greet the child softly and accept a smile.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to greet two friends.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say in the morning?
  **Expected response:** Good morning.
- **Ask:** Who do we say it to?
  **Expected response:** Teacher, friends and family.

### Expected outcomes
- Says or shows good morning
- Looks at the person being greeted

### Conclusion
Say "Goodbye" and wave together.

### Home connection
Greet your child with "Good morning" every day and wait for a reply.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say or show good morning to the teacher and friends" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- GOOD MORNING
- HELLO

**Picture suggestions:**
- A rising sun
- Children waving

**Song or rhyme:** Good morning, good morning, how are you today?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Greeting my teacher

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Greeting my teacher and friends

### Learning objectives
- Children will greet their teacher and friends in a polite way.

### Success criteria
- Greets the teacher at the door
- Greets a friend with a word or wave

**Previous knowledge:** Children know their names, their teacher and the classroom, and are practising goodbyes.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, good morning, goodbye, wave, smile, friend

**Instructional materials:** A toy door or a door; Puppets; A greeting song

### Starter
Stand at the door and say "Good morning!" to each child as they enter, with a big smile.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to greet the teacher with a wave and good morning. Children: Children watch and copy.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children knock on the toy door and greet the teacher inside. Children: Child greets the teacher.

Step 3. Teacher: Show how to greet a friend by name. Children: Child greets a friend.

Step 4. Teacher: Talk about how some families greet elders in special ways. Children: Children share a greeting from home if they wish.

### Practical activity: Door greeting
1. Set up a toy door.
2. Let children greet the teacher.
3. Greet a friend by name.

**Safety:** Keep the door area clear and supervise closely. Respect each family’s way of greeting and never correct a child’s home greeting.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Greet together and hold the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to greet a visitor at the door.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How do you greet your teacher?
  **Expected response:** Good morning or a wave.
- **Ask:** How do you greet a friend?
  **Expected response:** Hello and their name.

### Expected outcomes
- Greets the teacher
- Greets a friend with a word or gesture

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to everyone and say "See you tomorrow."

### Home connection
Greet family and visitors together and let your child copy.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "greet their teacher and friends in a polite way" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HELLO
- FRIEND

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Greeting at different times

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Saying good morning

### Learning objectives
- Children will know that we say good morning, good afternoon and good night at different times.

### Success criteria
- Says good morning at the start of the day
- Says good night at bedtime with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children know their names, their teacher and the classroom, and are practising goodbyes.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, good morning, goodbye, wave, smile, friend

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of morning, afternoon and night; A teddy bear; A greeting song

### Starter
Show a picture of the sun and say "Good morning." Show the moon and say "Good night."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each picture and say the greeting. Children: Children say the greeting.

Step 2. Teacher: Match the teddy bear to the time of day. Children: Child places the teddy next to the picture.

Step 3. Teacher: Pretend to put the teddy to bed and say good night. Children: Child says good night to the teddy.

Step 4. Teacher: Sing the greeting song. Children: Children sing with actions.

### Practical activity: Greeting pictures
1. Put the three pictures on the floor.
2. Say the greeting for each.
3. Put the teddy to bed.

**Safety:** Keep the floor clear of tripping hazards. Be gentle with the teddy.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say the greeting together and show the picture.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose the right greeting for a picture.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say in the morning?
  **Expected response:** Good morning.
- **Ask:** What do we say at night?
  **Expected response:** Good night.

### Expected outcomes
- Knows different greetings
- Says good morning and good night with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say good afternoon.

### Home connection
Say good morning and good night each day.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "know that we say good morning, good afternoon and good night at different times" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MORNING
- NIGHT

**Picture suggestions:**
- The sun
- The moon

## Day 4 (Thursday): Waving goodbye

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Saying goodbye and waving

### Learning objectives
- Children will say goodbye politely with a wave.

### Success criteria
- Says goodbye or waves
- Says goodbye to the teacher at home time

**Previous knowledge:** Children know their names, their teacher and the classroom, and are practising goodbyes.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, good morning, goodbye, wave, smile, friend

**Instructional materials:** A goodbye song; A toy bus; Puppets

### Starter
Sing "Goodbye, goodbye, wave your hand goodbye" and wave to the puppet.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to wave goodbye and say bye. Children: Children copy the wave.

Step 2. Teacher: Let the toy bus go away and wave goodbye. Children: Child waves to the bus.

Step 3. Teacher: Practise goodbyes to friends. Children: Child waves to a friend.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Goodbye, teacher" together at the door. Children: Child waves and says bye.

### Practical activity: Goodbye practice
1. Wave goodbye to the toy bus.
2. Wave goodbye to a friend.
3. Say goodbye to the teacher.

**Safety:** Keep the area clear. Stay close to any child who becomes upset.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Wave together and sing the goodbye song.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say goodbye to every friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do when we go?
  **Expected response:** Wave and say goodbye.
- **Ask:** Who do you say goodbye to?
  **Expected response:** Teacher, friends and family.

### Expected outcomes
- Says goodbye with a wave
- Says goodbye to the teacher

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the class.

### Home connection
Practise a happy goodbye whenever someone leaves the house.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say goodbye politely with a wave" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- GOODBYE
- WAVE

**Picture suggestions:**

**Song or rhyme:** Goodbye, goodbye, wave your hand goodbye.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 greetings check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Greeting my teacher and friends

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child greets and says goodbye.

### Success criteria
- Greets the teacher with a word or gesture
- Waves goodbye

**Previous knowledge:** Children know their names, their teacher and the classroom, and are practising goodbyes.

**Key vocabulary:** hello, good morning, goodbye, wave, smile, friend

**Instructional materials:** Puppets; A toy door; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the greeting song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: good morning, door greeting and goodbye. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station greet the child and watch the response. Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children join the goodbye song.

### Practical activity: Greeting stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep the floor clear. Accept every form of greeting from the child.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to greet a new friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: greets the teacher; greets a friend; says or waves goodbye; looks at the person being greeted.

- **Ask:** What do you say in the morning?
  **Expected response:** Good morning.
- **Ask:** What do you do when you go?
  **Expected response:** Wave and say goodbye.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Greets and says goodbye with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Share how your child greets and keep practising each day.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child greets and says goodbye" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
