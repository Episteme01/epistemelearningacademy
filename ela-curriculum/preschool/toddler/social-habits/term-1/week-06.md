# Toddler Social Habits — Term 1, Week 6: Sharing

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Sharing toys; Taking turns; Waiting for my turn

## Learning objectives
- Children will share a toy with a friend with support.
- Children will take turns in a simple game.

**Key vocabulary:** share, turn, wait, my turn, your turn, toy

**Content:** Sharing toys, taking turns, and waiting for my turn.

**Teaching activities:** Expect toddlers to say "mine"; do not force sharing but teach turns with a timer or song; praise every turn taken; keep duplicate toys available; use "my turn, your turn" language; stay close to guide gently.

**Learner activities:** Share a toy with support, take turns in a simple game and wait for a short time.

**Resources:** Two of each toy; A ball; A sand timer or song; A puppet; A turn-taking mat with footprints

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child shares with support and waits for a turn for a short time.

## Expected outcomes
- Shares a toy with adult support.
- Waits for a turn for a short time.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Numeracy: one each and first, next; Literacy: my turn and your turn; Physical Development: rolling and passing

## Day 1 (Monday): Sharing toys

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Sharing toys

### Learning objectives
- Children will share a toy with a friend with adult support.

### Success criteria
- Hands a toy to a friend with support
- Says share with a prompt

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow simple routines and use magic words with a prompt.

**Key vocabulary:** share, turn, wait, my turn, your turn, toy

**Instructional materials:** A basket of large toys; A puppet; A picture of children sharing

### Starter
Show the puppet holding a toy and say "I have a toy. I can share it with a friend."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the puppet sharing a toy with another puppet. Children: Children watch and say share.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Sharing makes friends happy." Children: Children smile and repeat share.

Step 3. Teacher: Give two children one toy and help them share. Children: Child passes the toy to a friend.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise the sharing with a smile and thank you. Children: Children say thank you.

### Practical activity: Sharing a toy
1. Give two children one toy.
2. Help them pass it.
3. Say thank you.

**Safety:** Use large toys without small parts and watch closely for grabbing. Never force a child to give up a toy; use turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the toy together and hand it over together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a toy to share with a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do with a toy?
  **Expected response:** Share it.
- **Ask:** How does a friend feel?
  **Expected response:** Happy.

### Expected outcomes
- Shares a toy with support
- Says share with a prompt

### Conclusion
Put the toys away and say "We shared."

### Home connection
Practise sharing a snack or a toy with a sibling or friend.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "share a toy with a friend with adult support" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Taking turns

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Taking turns

### Learning objectives
- Children will take turns in a simple game.

### Success criteria
- Takes a turn when asked
- Says my turn or your turn with a prompt

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow simple routines and use magic words with a prompt.

**Key vocabulary:** share, turn, wait, my turn, your turn, toy

**Instructional materials:** A soft ball; A turn-taking mat; A puppet

### Starter
Roll a soft ball to one child and say "My turn, your turn."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to roll the ball and say my turn. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Roll the ball to a child and say your turn. Children: Child rolls the ball back.

Step 3. Teacher: Move around the circle with the ball. Children: Each child takes a turn.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Everyone had a turn." Children: Children clap and cheer.

### Practical activity: Ball turns
1. Sit in a circle.
2. Roll the ball to a friend.
3. Say my turn, your turn.

**Safety:** Use a very soft ball and keep the circle away from hazards. Never leave a child waiting too long.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Roll the ball together with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to roll the ball to a friend by name.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Whose turn is it?
  **Expected response:** The child named.
- **Ask:** What do we say?
  **Expected response:** My turn, your turn.

### Expected outcomes
- Takes a turn in a ball game
- Uses my turn and your turn with a prompt

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the ball.

### Home connection
Take turns rolling a ball or stacking blocks with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "take turns in a simple game" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Waiting for my turn

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Waiting for my turn

### Learning objectives
- Children will wait for a short time for their turn.

### Success criteria
- Waits while a friend has a turn
- Takes a turn when it is theirs

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow simple routines and use magic words with a prompt.

**Key vocabulary:** share, turn, wait, my turn, your turn, toy

**Instructional materials:** A sand timer or a short song; A slide or toy car track; A turn-taking mat

### Starter
Say "We wait for our turn. While we wait, we sing." and start a short song.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how a friend has a turn on the slide or track. Children: Children watch and wait.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing a short song while waiting. Children: Children sing and wait.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Now it is your turn." Children: Child takes a turn.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise waiting with a smile. Children: Children wave or clap.

### Practical activity: Waiting game
1. Stand in a line on the mat.
2. Sing while you wait.
3. Take your turn.

**Safety:** Supervise close to the slide or track and keep the line short. Never make children wait long.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Wait with the child and sing quietly together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count to three while they wait.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do while we wait?
  **Expected response:** Sing or watch.
- **Ask:** When is it my turn?
  **Expected response:** When the teacher says.

### Expected outcomes
- Waits for a short time
- Takes a turn when it is theirs

### Conclusion
Say "Thank you for waiting."

### Home connection
Practise waiting for a short time at the table or in a queue.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "wait for a short time for their turn" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Sharing and turns game

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Sharing toys

### Learning objectives
- Children will share and take turns in a small group game.

### Success criteria
- Shares a toy in a group
- Takes a turn with a friend

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow simple routines and use magic words with a prompt.

**Key vocabulary:** share, turn, wait, my turn, your turn, toy

**Instructional materials:** Large building blocks; A basket; A puppet

### Starter
Show a basket of blocks and say "We have blocks to share. Let us build together."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to pass a block to a friend. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children take turns adding a block to a tower. Children: Child adds a block.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "My block, your block." Children: Child passes a block.

Step 4. Teacher: Look at the tower and say "We did it." Children: Children clap and cheer.

### Practical activity: Tower together
1. Sit in a small group.
2. Take turns adding blocks.
3. Share the blocks.

**Safety:** Use large, light blocks only and keep children seated safely. Watch for falling blocks.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Build beside the child and take turns together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give a block to a friend who has none.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did we do?
  **Expected response:** We shared.
- **Ask:** Whose turn is it?
  **Expected response:** The next friend.

### Expected outcomes
- Shares and takes turns in a group
- Says we did it

### Conclusion
Put the blocks away and say "We shared."

### Home connection
Play a building game and take turns adding blocks.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 sharing check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Taking turns

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child shares and takes turns.

### Success criteria
- Shares a toy with support
- Waits for a short turn

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow simple routines and use magic words with a prompt.

**Key vocabulary:** share, turn, wait, my turn, your turn, toy

**Instructional materials:** Toys; A ball; Blocks; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the sharing song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: toys, ball and blocks. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child shares and waits. Children: Child plays with a friend.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Sharing stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep duplicates of favourite toys. Step in gently if a child grabs.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to teach a friend to take turns.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: shares a toy; takes turns; waits for a short time; uses my turn and your turn.

- **Ask:** What do we do with toys?
  **Expected response:** Share them.
- **Ask:** What do we say?
  **Expected response:** My turn, your turn.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Shares and takes turns with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say "Well done."

### Home connection
Keep practising sharing and turns at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child shares and takes turns" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
