# Toddler Social Habits — Term 1, Week 7: Kindness

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Kind hands; Kind words; Helping a friend

## Learning objectives
- Children will use gentle hands with friends and toys.
- Children will say a kind word or help a friend.

**Key vocabulary:** kind, gentle, help, hug, nice, friend

**Content:** Kind hands, kind words and helping a friend.

**Teaching activities:** Teach "gentle hands" with a soft toy; notice and name every kind act ("You helped Ada, that was kind"); never label a child as unkind; show kind words with a puppet; use a kindness chart with stickers.

**Learner activities:** Use gentle hands, say a kind word and help a friend with support.

**Resources:** A soft toy or doll; A puppet; A kindness chart; Stickers; Pictures of kind actions

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child uses gentle hands and says or does a kind thing with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Uses gentle hands with a friend.
- Says or does a kind thing with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: kind words; Creative Development: singing and acting; Numeracy: counting kind acts

## Day 1 (Monday): Kind hands

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Kind hands

### Learning objectives
- Children will use gentle hands with a soft toy and a friend.

### Success criteria
- Strokes a soft toy gently
- Uses gentle hands with a friend with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can share and take turns with support.

**Key vocabulary:** kind, gentle, help, hug, nice, friend

**Instructional materials:** A soft toy or doll; A puppet; A picture of hands

### Starter
Hold a soft toy gently and say "I use kind hands. I do not hit or push."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to stroke a soft toy gently. Children: Children copy the gentle touch.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Kind hands help. Kind hands share." Children: Children look at their hands.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children pat a friend’s hand gently if they wish. Children: Child pats a friend’s hand.

Step 4. Teacher: Use the puppet to show what happens if hands are not kind. Children: Children say gentle.

### Practical activity: Gentle hands
1. Hold a soft toy.
2. Stroke it gently.
3. Pat a friend’s hand gently.

**Safety:** Never force touch. Let children choose a wave or high five instead. Stay close to children who hit or push and gently redirect them.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Guide the child’s hand gently on the soft toy.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to show a friend gentle hands.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What are kind hands?
  **Expected response:** Gentle hands.
- **Ask:** Do kind hands hit?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses gentle hands
- Says gentle or kind with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye with kind hands.

### Home connection
Praise your child for gentle hands and show how to be gentle with a baby or a pet.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use gentle hands with a soft toy and a friend" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Kind words

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Kind words

### Learning objectives
- Children will say a kind word to a friend.

### Success criteria
- Says a kind word with a prompt
- Smiles when saying it

**Previous knowledge:** Children can share and take turns with support.

**Key vocabulary:** kind, gentle, help, hug, nice, friend

**Instructional materials:** A puppet; Pictures of kind words; A kindness chart

### Starter
Let the puppet say "You are my friend" and give a big smile.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show kind words: hello, well done, thank you, you are my friend. Children: Children say the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Use the puppet to say a kind word to a child. Children: Child smiles or says thank you.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child say a kind word to a friend. Children: Child says a kind word.

Step 4. Teacher: Add a sticker to the kindness chart. Children: Child places a sticker.

### Practical activity: Kind words round
1. Sit in a circle.
2. Say a kind word to a friend.
3. Add a sticker to the chart.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic stickers and watch that none are put in mouths. Never force a child to speak; accept a smile.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say the kind word together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say a kind word to every friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is a kind word?
  **Expected response:** Well done or thank you.
- **Ask:** Who do you say it to?
  **Expected response:** A friend.

### Expected outcomes
- Says a kind word with a prompt
- Smiles when saying a kind word

### Conclusion
Say "You are kind" to a friend.

### Home connection
Use kind words with your child and ask them to say one to a family member.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Helping a friend

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Helping a friend

### Learning objectives
- Children will help a friend in a simple way.

### Success criteria
- Helps a friend pick up something
- Says help with a prompt

**Previous knowledge:** Children can share and take turns with support.

**Key vocabulary:** kind, gentle, help, hug, nice, friend

**Instructional materials:** Blocks; A bag; A puppet who drops things

### Starter
Let the puppet drop its bag and say "Oh no! Who can help me?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to pick up the bag and give it back. Children: Children watch and help.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "We help our friends." Children: Children repeat help.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child help the puppet or a friend pick up toys. Children: Child picks up toys.

Step 4. Teacher: Say thank you to helpers. Children: Children say you are welcome.

### Practical activity: Helping hands
1. Let a puppet drop things.
2. Children help to pick them up.
3. Say thank you.

**Safety:** Use light, large objects and keep the floor clear. Supervise carefully so no child is hurt while helping.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Help together and hold the object.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a friend who needs help.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do for friends?
  **Expected response:** Help them.
- **Ask:** Who needs help?
  **Expected response:** A friend who dropped something.

### Expected outcomes
- Helps a friend in a simple way
- Says help with a prompt

### Conclusion
Look at the helpful class and say "Thank you."

### Home connection
Ask your child to help with a small job and say thank you.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "help a friend in a simple way" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Kindness every day

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Helping a friend

### Learning objectives
- Children will use kind hands, kind words and helping in play.

### Success criteria
- Uses a kind action in play
- Receives a sticker for kindness

**Previous knowledge:** Children can share and take turns with support.

**Key vocabulary:** kind, gentle, help, hug, nice, friend

**Instructional materials:** A kindness chart; Stickers; A play area with toys

### Starter
Show the kindness chart and say "When we are kind, we get a sticker."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Watch children play and name kind acts you see. Children: Children play together.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "I saw you share. That was kind." Children: Child smiles and receives a sticker.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children say a kind thing they did. Children: Child says or shows what they did.

Step 4. Teacher: Count the stickers together. Children: Children count with support.

### Practical activity: Kindness spotting
1. Play in small groups.
2. The teacher spots kind acts.
3. Add stickers to the chart.

**Safety:** Give praise for kind acts and never remove a sticker. Use non-toxic stickers and supervise play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Name a kind act the child did and give a sticker.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to spot a kind act by a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you do that was kind?
  **Expected response:** Shared or helped.
- **Ask:** How many stickers?
  **Expected response:** A number with support.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses a kind action in play
- Says a kind thing they did

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye with a smile.

### Home connection
Notice and praise your child’s kind acts at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use kind hands, kind words and helping in play" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 kindness check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Kind words

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child shows kindness.

### Success criteria
- Uses gentle hands
- Says or does a kind thing

**Previous knowledge:** Children can share and take turns with support.

**Key vocabulary:** kind, gentle, help, hug, nice, friend

**Instructional materials:** Soft toys; Puppets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the kind hands song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: gentle hands, kind words and helping. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child shows kindness. Children: Child plays with a friend.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Kindness stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station closely and step in gently if a child is rough. Keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to show a friend how to help.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: uses gentle hands; says a kind word; helps a friend; plays kindly with others.

- **Ask:** What are kind hands?
  **Expected response:** Gentle hands.
- **Ask:** What do we do for friends?
  **Expected response:** Help them.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Shows kindness with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep praising kind acts at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child shows kindness" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
