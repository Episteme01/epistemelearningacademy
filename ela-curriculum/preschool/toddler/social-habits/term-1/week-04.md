# Toddler Social Habits — Term 1, Week 4: Feelings

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W04 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Happy and sad; Angry and scared; Showing feelings with faces

## Learning objectives
- Children will name happy, sad, angry and scared.
- Children will show feelings with their faces and bodies.

**Key vocabulary:** happy, sad, angry, scared, face, feel

**Content:** Naming happy and sad, angry and scared, and showing feelings with faces and bodies.

**Teaching activities:** Use real faces, mirrors and picture cards; name feelings as they happen ("You look sad"); never say a feeling is wrong; keep feelings words simple; model calm responses; use a feelings chart every day.

**Learner activities:** Name happy, sad, angry and scared and show them with the face and body.

**Resources:** Feelings face cards; A mirror; A feelings chart; Pictures of children with different feelings; A puppet

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names two or more feelings and shows a feeling with their face.

## Expected outcomes
- Names two or more feelings.
- Shows a feeling with the face.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: feelings words; Creative Development: drama and faces; Numeracy: sorting feelings cards

## Day 1 (Monday): Happy and sad

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Happy and sad

### Learning objectives
- Children will name happy and sad and show them with their faces.

### Success criteria
- Says or shows happy
- Says or shows sad

**Previous knowledge:** Children have practised greetings, please and thank you.

**Key vocabulary:** happy, sad, angry, scared, face, feel

**Instructional materials:** Feelings face cards: happy and sad; A mirror; A puppet

### Starter
Show a happy face and a sad face and say "I feel happy. I feel sad. Everyone has feelings."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the happy face and smile together. Children: Children smile and say happy.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the sad face and make a sad face. Children: Children make a sad face and say sad.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children look in the mirror and show each feeling. Children: Child shows happy and sad in the mirror.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What makes you happy?" Children: Child says or points to something.

### Practical activity: Mirror faces
1. Use a mirror.
2. Show a happy face.
3. Show a sad face.

**Safety:** Use a safe unbreakable mirror and supervise closely. Never say that sad is bad; say that all feelings are okay.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Show the face and name the feeling together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say why a friend might be sad.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How do you feel when you play?
  **Expected response:** Happy.
- **Ask:** How does a sad face look?
  **Expected response:** Mouth turned down.

### Expected outcomes
- Names happy and sad
- Shows happy and sad with the face

### Conclusion
Smile and say "I feel happy."

### Home connection
Name feelings at home: "You look happy" or "You look sad."

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name happy and sad and show them with their faces" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

**Picture suggestions:**
- A happy face

**Song or rhyme:** If you are happy and you know it, clap your hands.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Angry and scared

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Angry and scared

### Learning objectives
- Children will name angry and scared and show them with their faces.

### Success criteria
- Says or shows angry
- Says or shows scared

**Previous knowledge:** Children have practised greetings, please and thank you.

**Key vocabulary:** happy, sad, angry, scared, face, feel

**Instructional materials:** Feelings face cards: angry and scared; A mirror; A puppet

### Starter
Show an angry face and a scared face and say "Sometimes we feel cross or scared. That is okay."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the angry face and make a cross face. Children: Children make an angry face and say angry.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the scared face and make a scared face. Children: Children make a scared face and say scared.

Step 3. Teacher: Use the puppet to show what scared feels like and give it a hug. Children: Children say it is okay.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "A grown-up can help us when we feel big feelings." Children: Child points to a trusted adult.

### Practical activity: Feelings faces
1. Look at the cards.
2. Make each face in the mirror.
3. Point to a helper.

**Safety:** Keep the activity gentle and calm so it does not frighten children. Stop if a child is upset and comfort them.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Show the face and name the feeling together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what helps when they are scared.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How do you look when you are angry?
  **Expected response:** Cross face.
- **Ask:** Who can help you?
  **Expected response:** A grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Names angry and scared
- Knows a grown-up can help

### Conclusion
Take a deep breath and smile.

### Home connection
Name big feelings at home and say "I can help you."

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Feelings faces

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Showing feelings with faces

### Learning objectives
- Children will match faces to feeling words and show feelings with their bodies.

### Success criteria
- Matches a face to a feeling word
- Shows a feeling with the body

**Previous knowledge:** Children have practised greetings, please and thank you.

**Key vocabulary:** happy, sad, angry, scared, face, feel

**Instructional materials:** Feelings cards; A feelings chart; A puppet

### Starter
Show a feelings card and say "How does this child feel?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold up a feelings card and name it. Children: Children say the feeling.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Show me happy" and let children show it. Children: Child shows happy with face and body.

Step 3. Teacher: Play "Show me" for sad, angry and scared. Children: Child shows each feeling.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a card and show the feeling. Children: Friends guess the feeling.

### Practical activity: Show me a feeling
1. Show feelings cards.
2. Children make the face.
3. Friends guess.

**Safety:** Keep the floor clear and supervise movement. Stop the game if a child becomes upset.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two cards and name the feeling.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to show a feeling and say why.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How does this face feel?
  **Expected response:** Happy, sad, angry or scared.
- **Ask:** Show me a happy face.
  **Expected response:** Child smiles.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches faces to feeling words
- Shows feelings with the body

### Conclusion
Smile and wave to friends.

### Home connection
Play feelings faces at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match faces to feeling words and show feelings with their bodies" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): How do I feel today?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Showing feelings with faces

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose a face on the feelings chart to show how they feel.

### Success criteria
- Chooses a feelings face
- Says a feeling word with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children have practised greetings, please and thank you.

**Key vocabulary:** happy, sad, angry, scared, face, feel

**Instructional materials:** A feelings chart with four faces; Name tags or pegs; A feelings song

### Starter
Show the feelings chart and say "Every morning we show how we feel."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the four faces on the chart. Children: Children look and name them.

Step 2. Teacher: Call each child to put their name tag on a face. Children: Child chooses a face and places the tag.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Thank you for telling me" and listen. Children: Child says a word or points.

Step 4. Teacher: Talk about how to help someone who is sad. Children: Children give a hug or a smile if they wish.

### Practical activity: Feelings chart
1. Look at the feelings chart.
2. Put your name on a face.
3. Say how you feel.

**Safety:** Never judge a feeling. Offer comfort and let children choose whether to be touched. Offer a wave or a high five instead of a hug.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two faces and help the child choose.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say why they chose that face.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How do you feel today?
  **Expected response:** Happy, sad, angry or scared.
- **Ask:** Why do you feel that?
  **Expected response:** Child gives a reason or points.

### Expected outcomes
- Chooses a feelings face
- Says how they feel with support

### Conclusion
Sing a happy song together.

### Home connection
Ask your child how they feel each day.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose a face on the feelings chart to show how they feel" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 4 feelings check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Showing feelings with faces

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names and shows feelings.

### Success criteria
- Names two feelings
- Shows a feeling with the face

**Previous knowledge:** Children have practised greetings, please and thank you.

**Key vocabulary:** happy, sad, angry, scared, face, feel

**Instructional materials:** Feelings cards; A mirror; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the feelings song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: faces, chart and story. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How does this feel?" and watch. Children: Child answers with a word or face.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Feelings stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep the floor clear. Stop any activity that distresses a child.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two feelings.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to teach a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names happy and sad; names angry and scared; shows feelings with the face; points to a helper.

- **Ask:** How do you feel?
  **Expected response:** A feeling word.
- **Ask:** Who helps you?
  **Expected response:** A grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Names and shows feelings

### Conclusion
Smile and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Keep naming feelings at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child names and shows feelings" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

- Feelings
