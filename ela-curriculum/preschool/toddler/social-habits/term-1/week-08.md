# Toddler Social Habits — Term 1, Week 8: Health habits

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Telling the teacher I need the toilet; Washing my hands; Wiping and flushing with help

## Learning objectives
- Children will tell an adult when they need the toilet.
- Children will wash their hands after using the toilet.

**Key vocabulary:** toilet, wash, soap, flush, clean, towel

**Content:** Telling the teacher when I need the toilet, washing my hands, and wiping and flushing with help.

**Teaching activities:** Treat accidents as normal and never shame a child; ask every child regularly and let them go when they need to; use simple words agreed with parents; model hand washing with a song; ensure a second adult is available; follow the school hygiene and safeguarding policy for toilet help.

**Learner activities:** Tell an adult about toilet needs, use the toilet with support and wash hands with soap.

**Resources:** A potty or child toilet; A doll; Soap, a basin and towels; A hand washing song; Pictures of toilet steps; Spare clothes

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child tells an adult they need the toilet and washes hands with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Tells an adult they need the toilet with a word or sign.
- Washes hands with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: keeping clean; Literacy: toilet words; Physical Development: self-care

## Day 1 (Monday): Telling the teacher

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Telling the teacher I need the toilet

### Learning objectives
- Children will tell an adult when they need the toilet with a word or sign.

### Success criteria
- Says or signs toilet
- Goes to the toilet with an adult

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow routines, share and show kindness.

**Key vocabulary:** toilet, wash, soap, flush, clean, towel

**Instructional materials:** A doll; A picture of a toilet; A toilet song

### Starter
Show the doll and say "Dolly needs the toilet. She tells her teacher: toilet please."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how the doll tells the teacher "toilet please". Children: Children watch and say toilet.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "We tell the teacher when we need the toilet." Children: Children repeat toilet please.

Step 3. Teacher: Practise the word or sign with each child. Children: Child says or signs toilet.

Step 4. Teacher: Take small groups to see the toilet room. Children: Children visit and look.

### Practical activity: Toilet words
1. Use the doll to practise.
2. Say toilet please.
3. Visit the toilet room.

**Safety:** Use only school-approved toilet routines with a second adult nearby and keep doors open or as the policy says. Never shame a child; accidents happen.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Practise the word or sign with a doll first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to remind a friend to tell the teacher.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we say?
  **Expected response:** Toilet please.
- **Ask:** Who do we tell?
  **Expected response:** The teacher.

### Expected outcomes
- Says or signs toilet
- Visits the toilet room with an adult

### Conclusion
Sing the toilet song and wave.

### Home connection
Agree the same toilet word with your child at home and school.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "tell an adult when they need the toilet with a word or sign" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Washing my hands

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Washing my hands

### Learning objectives
- Children will wash their hands with soap after using the toilet.

### Success criteria
- Washes hands with soap with support
- Dries hands on a towel

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow routines, share and show kindness.

**Key vocabulary:** toilet, wash, soap, flush, clean, towel

**Instructional materials:** A basin and water; Soap; Towels; A hand washing song

### Starter
Show your hands and say "Hands must be clean. We wash with soap after the toilet."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to wet, soap, rub and rinse hands. Children: Children watch and copy the actions.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing the hand washing song while rubbing. Children: Children sing and rub.

Step 3. Teacher: Show how to dry hands on a clean towel. Children: Child dries hands.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Clean hands. Well done." Children: Children show their clean hands.

### Practical activity: Hand washing
1. Wet hands.
2. Rub with soap while singing.
3. Rinse and dry.

**Safety:** Use mild soap and shallow water and wipe up spills. Supervise every child at the basin and use a clean towel for each child.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Wash hands together with a hand over the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach a friend to wash hands.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** When do we wash hands?
  **Expected response:** After the toilet.
- **Ask:** What do we use?
  **Expected response:** Soap and water.

### Expected outcomes
- Washes hands with soap with support
- Dries hands on a towel

### Conclusion
Show clean hands and say "Clean."

### Home connection
Wash hands together after every toilet visit and before meals.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "wash their hands with soap after using the toilet" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Wiping and flushing

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Wiping and flushing with help

### Learning objectives
- Children will learn the steps after using the toilet with adult help.

### Success criteria
- Names the toilet steps in order
- Flushes with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow routines, share and show kindness.

**Key vocabulary:** toilet, wash, soap, flush, clean, towel

**Instructional materials:** Picture cards for the toilet steps; A doll and a toy toilet; A towel

### Starter
Show the picture cards and say "Toilet, wipe, flush, wash. Four steps to stay clean."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each picture and say the step. Children: Children look and say the step.

Step 2. Teacher: Use the doll and toy toilet to act out the steps. Children: Children copy the actions.

Step 3. Teacher: Put the cards in order. Children: Child places the cards in order.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "I did it" when finished. Children: Children say I did it.

### Practical activity: Toilet steps
1. Look at the cards.
2. Act the steps with the doll.
3. Put the cards in order.

**Safety:** Use a doll and cards only and never ask a child to demonstrate on their own body. Keep to the school policy for toilet help.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to each card and say the step together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend the steps.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do after the toilet?
  **Expected response:** Wipe, flush and wash.
- **Ask:** What comes last?
  **Expected response:** Wash hands.

### Expected outcomes
- Names the toilet steps
- Flushes with support

### Conclusion
Sing the washing song and wave.

### Home connection
Talk through the toilet steps at home and praise each one.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "learn the steps after using the toilet with adult help" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Clean hands every time

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Washing my hands

### Learning objectives
- Children will wash hands without being reminded after a toilet visit or before eating.

### Success criteria
- Goes to the basin when prompted
- Says clean hands

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow routines, share and show kindness.

**Key vocabulary:** toilet, wash, soap, flush, clean, towel

**Instructional materials:** A basin and soap; Towels; A hand washing chart

### Starter
Show the hand washing chart and say "We wash hands before snack."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Remind children to wash before snack. Children: Children line up at the basin.

Step 2. Teacher: Watch each child wash and sing together. Children: Child washes with the song.

Step 3. Teacher: Add a tick to the chart. Children: Child ticks the chart.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Clean hands help us stay well." Children: Children say clean hands.

### Practical activity: Wash before snack
1. Line up at the basin.
2. Wash with soap.
3. Dry and tick the chart.

**Safety:** Supervise closely and keep the floor dry. Use mild soap and clean towels.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Wash together and name each step.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to remind a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** When do we wash hands?
  **Expected response:** Before snack and after the toilet.
- **Ask:** Are your hands clean?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Washes hands when prompted
- Says clean hands

### Conclusion
Show clean hands and wave.

### Home connection
Make hand washing a family routine.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "wash hands without being reminded after a toilet visit or before eating" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 hygiene check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T1-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Washing my hands

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child manages toilet routines and hand washing.

### Success criteria
- Tells an adult about toilet needs
- Washes hands with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can follow routines, share and show kindness.

**Key vocabulary:** toilet, wash, soap, flush, clean, towel

**Instructional materials:** Basin, soap and towels; Picture cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the hand washing song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: toilet words, steps and hand washing. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch what the child can do. Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Hygiene stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and wipe spills at once. Use mild soap and clean towels and follow the school hygiene policy.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to explain hand washing to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: tells an adult about toilet needs; names toilet steps; washes hands with soap; dries hands.

- **Ask:** What do we say?
  **Expected response:** Toilet please.
- **Ask:** What do we wash?
  **Expected response:** Our hands.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Practises toilet and hand routines

### Conclusion
Show clean hands and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Share toilet progress with the teacher and keep routines the same.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child manages toilet routines and hand washing" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

- Hygiene

- Photos of stations
