# Toddler Social Habits

**Programme ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft (not yet approved by an ELA reviewer)

## Subject philosophy
Social Habits is where ELA’s motto, "Where Learning Meets Morality", begins. Toddlers learn to belong: to greet, to share, to say please and thank you, to name how they feel, to tell the truth, to help and to stay safe. These habits are not taught by talking. They are taught by daily routine, by gentle adult modelling, by songs, stories and play, and by warm repetition until kind behaviour becomes natural.

## Rationale
The earliest years set the pattern for how a child relates to others. Toddlers who feel secure, who have words for their feelings and who practise kindness and good manners in small, repeated moments are ready to learn everything else. This programme draws on values families in Ilorin and across Nigeria already hold, such as respect for elders, greeting properly, honesty, sharing food and helping at home, and it makes them visible and practised in the classroom. Teachers should respect the different ways families greet and show respect, and never shame a child.

## Aims
- To help each child feel safe, happy and a valued member of the class.
- To teach simple good manners: greetings, please, thank you and sorry.
- To help children name and manage basic feelings with adult support.
- To practise sharing, taking turns, kindness and helping others.
- To build independence and responsibility in daily routines, toileting, hygiene and tidying.
- To introduce honesty, respect, gratitude and care for others and for belongings.
- To teach simple safety habits at school, at the road and with people.

## Learning outcomes by the end of the year
- Separates from a parent with support and joins class routines happily.
- Greets people and says please, thank you and sorry with prompting.
- Names basic feelings (happy, sad, angry, scared) and uses a calming strategy with an adult.
- Shares and takes turns with support.
- Uses the toilet and washes hands with growing independence.
- Follows simple instructions and classroom routines.
- Shows kindness, helps others and says what is true with support.
- Tidies up belongings and shows care for toys and books.
- Follows basic safety rules at school and near roads.

## Developmental expectations
- Toddlers are self-centred by nature; sharing and turn-taking are learnt slowly and need many gentle repetitions.
- Feelings are big and sudden; children need words and an adult nearby, not punishment.
- Behaviour improves through modelling, routine and praise for what is done well.
- Toddlers often cannot yet explain why; they do the right thing because a trusted adult shows and expects it.
- Separation anxiety, toileting accidents and conflict over toys are normal and are handled calmly.

## Key competencies
- Self-confidence and independence.
- Managing feelings and behaviour.
- Making relationships and cooperating.
- Showing good manners and respect.
- Keeping safe and healthy.

## Curriculum strands
- **Me and my feelings** (SF): Belonging, naming feelings and calming down.
- **Relationships and kindness** (RL): Family, friends, sharing, turn-taking and kindness.
- **Manners and respect** (MN): Greetings, please, thank you, sorry and respect for others.
- **Routines and responsibility** (RT): Classroom routines, tidying, helping and independence.
- **Values** (VL): Honesty, gratitude, generosity and caring for others.
- **Safety and health habits** (SH): Hygiene, toileting, eating well and keeping safe.

## Prior knowledge
None assumed. Children arrive with very different home experiences. Use the first two weeks to learn about each child, their family words for greeting and toileting, and any separation worries.

## Progression across the year
- **Term 1:** Term 1 focuses on belonging and basic habits: settling in, greetings, please and thank you, feelings, routines, sharing, kindness, toileting and safety.
- **Term 2:** Term 2 focuses on relationships and responsibility: family, friends, listening, saying sorry, helping, honesty, looking after things, eating manners and respect.
- **Term 3:** Term 3 focuses on confidence and community: bravery, calming big feelings, teamwork, helpers, road safety, body safety, gratitude, culture and independence.

## Assessment principles
- Assessment is by watching and recording behaviour during real routines and play. There are no written tests and no child is ever labelled as naughty.
- Levels Emerging, Developing, Secure and Exceeding follow the lesson success criteria (see ASSESSMENT_FRAMEWORK.md).
- Record what a child does in familiar situations, with a strength, a next step and a home idea for parents.
- Share concerns about a child’s behaviour or wellbeing privately and kindly with parents and the school lead, following the school’s safeguarding policy.

## Inclusion and differentiation
- Every lesson has a support and an extension version.
- Expect and welcome different ways of greeting and showing respect from different families and cultures.
- For children who find groups hard, offer a quiet corner and a short, adult-led version of the activity.
- Never force hugging, kissing or touch. Offer a wave, a smile or a high five as a choice.
- Accept home-language words for feelings and manners and repeat them in English.

## Cross-curricular connections
- Literacy: stories, songs and rhymes about manners and feelings.
- Numeracy: turn-taking and sharing equally, counting helpers and objects.
- Science: hygiene, caring for living things and keeping the environment clean.
- EYFS mapping: Personal, Social and Emotional Development, with Communication and Language (see STANDARDS_ALIGNMENT.md).

## Parent connection
- Every lesson ends with a short home idea so that manners and routines are practised in the same way at home.
- Parents are invited to share how their family greets and shows respect and to use the same words with their child.
- Teachers and parents agree gentle, consistent expectations and share successes with a short note.
