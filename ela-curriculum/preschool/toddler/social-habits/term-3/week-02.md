# Toddler Social Habits — Term 3, Week 2: Feelings

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Big feelings in my body; Breathing and calming; Asking for help

## Learning objectives
- Children will notice big feelings in their bodies.
- Children will use slow breathing or a calm place with an adult.

**Key vocabulary:** calm, breathe, cross, upset, help, quiet

**Content:** Big feelings in my body, breathing and calming, and asking for help.

**Teaching activities:** Stay calm yourself; name the feeling first ("You are cross"); never punish a child for a big feeling; offer a calm corner as a safe place, not a punishment; teach slow breathing with a pinwheel or a teddy; use a feelings chart; teach asking for help in words or a gesture.

**Learner activities:** Notice big feelings in the body, use slow breathing with an adult and ask for help when upset.

**Resources:** A calm corner with cushions; A pinwheel or feather; A teddy; A feelings chart; A calming song

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child uses a calming strategy with adult support and asks for help.

## Expected outcomes
- Uses a calming strategy with adult support.
- Asks an adult for help when upset.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Physical Development: breathing; Literacy: feelings words; Creative Development: calm music

## Day 1 (Monday): Big feelings in my body

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Big feelings in my body

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice where big feelings are in their bodies.

### Success criteria
- Points to where a feeling is in the body
- Names a big feeling

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name feelings and try new things.

**Key vocabulary:** calm, breathe, cross, upset, help, quiet

**Instructional materials:** A feelings chart; A drawing of a body; A puppet

### Starter
Say "When I am cross, my face is hot and my fists are tight. Show me."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how a cross body looks: tight fists, hot face. Children: Children copy the body shape.

Step 2. Teacher: Show how a calm body looks: loose hands and soft face. Children: Children copy the calm body.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Where do you feel it?" and point to the body picture. Children: Child points to a body part.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "All feelings are okay. We can calm down." Children: Children repeat calm down.

### Practical activity: Body feelings
1. Make a cross body.
2. Make a calm body.
3. Point to where you feel it.

**Safety:** Keep the movements gentle and never push a child to show strong feelings. Stop if a child becomes upset and comfort them.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Show the body shape and copy together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what makes them feel cross.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How does a cross body look?
  **Expected response:** Tight fists and hot face.
- **Ask:** How does a calm body look?
  **Expected response:** Soft and loose.

### Expected outcomes
- Notices big feelings in the body
- Names a big feeling

### Conclusion
Take a calm body pose and wave.

### Home connection
Name your child’s feelings and where you see them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice where big feelings are in their bodies" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Breathing and calming

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Breathing and calming

### Learning objectives
- Children will use slow breathing to calm down.

### Success criteria
- Takes slow breaths with an adult
- Says calm with a prompt

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name feelings and try new things.

**Key vocabulary:** calm, breathe, cross, upset, help, quiet

**Instructional materials:** A pinwheel or a feather; A teddy; A calm music track

### Starter
Hold a feather and say "Blow slowly and the feather floats."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to breathe in through the nose and out slowly like blowing a feather. Children: Children copy slowly.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children blow a pinwheel slowly. Children: Child blows the pinwheel gently.

Step 3. Teacher: Place a teddy on the tummy and watch it rise and fall. Children: Child breathes with the teddy.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Breathe in, breathe out. I am calm." Children: Children repeat calm.

### Practical activity: Teddy breathing
1. Lie down or sit.
2. Put a teddy on your tummy.
3. Breathe slowly.

**Safety:** Offer a seat if lying down is not comfortable and keep each breath gentle. Never ask children to hold their breath.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Breathe beside the child and count slowly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach a friend to breathe slowly.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How do we breathe to calm?
  **Expected response:** Slowly.
- **Ask:** What can help us calm?
  **Expected response:** A teddy or a feather.

### Expected outcomes
- Takes slow breaths
- Says calm with a prompt

### Conclusion
Take one more slow breath together.

### Home connection
Practise slow breathing with your child at bedtime.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use slow breathing to calm down" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Asking for help

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Asking for help

### Learning objectives
- Children will ask an adult for help when they feel upset.

### Success criteria
- Says help or points to an adult
- Goes to a calm corner with an adult

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name feelings and try new things.

**Key vocabulary:** calm, breathe, cross, upset, help, quiet

**Instructional materials:** A calm corner; A help card; A puppet

### Starter
Show a puppet that is upset and say "The puppet is upset. It says: help please."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the puppet asking a grown-up for help. Children: Children watch and say help.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the calm corner and say "You can sit here with a grown-up." Children: Children look at the corner.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children practise handing the help card to an adult. Children: Child gives the card.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Asking for help is brave." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

### Practical activity: Help card
1. Give the help card to the teacher.
2. Go to the calm corner.
3. Breathe slowly with the teacher.

**Safety:** Keep the calm corner a safe, warm place and never use it as punishment. Stay with the child at all times.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hand over the card together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to remind a friend to ask for help.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who do we ask?
  **Expected response:** A grown-up.
- **Ask:** Where can we go?
  **Expected response:** The calm corner.

### Expected outcomes
- Asks for help when upset
- Uses the calm corner with an adult

### Conclusion
Sit quietly and then wave.

### Home connection
Set up a calm place at home and visit it together.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 4 (Thursday): My calm plan

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Breathing and calming

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose a calming strategy and use it in a pretend upset.

### Success criteria
- Chooses a calming idea
- Uses it with an adult

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name feelings and try new things.

**Key vocabulary:** calm, breathe, cross, upset, help, quiet

**Instructional materials:** Calming picture cards: breathe, hug a teddy, go to the corner, ask for help; A puppet

### Starter
Show four calming cards and say "When we are upset, we choose one."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each card and act it out. Children: Children copy each action.

Step 2. Teacher: Let the puppet feel cross and ask a child to choose a card. Children: Child chooses a card.

Step 3. Teacher: Act out using the chosen idea. Children: Child acts out the idea.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "I can calm down." Children: Children say I can calm down.

### Practical activity: Calm cards
1. Look at the cards.
2. Choose one.
3. Act it out with the puppet.

**Safety:** Keep the scene make-believe with the puppet. Stop if a child becomes distressed and comfort them.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Choose between two cards.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach the puppet a calming idea.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What can we do to calm?
  **Expected response:** Breathe or hug a teddy.
- **Ask:** Who can help?
  **Expected response:** A grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Chooses a calming idea
- Uses it with an adult

### Conclusion
Pick one calming idea and do it together.

### Home connection
Make a calm card set for home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose a calming strategy and use it in a pretend upset" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 calm check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Asking for help

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child manages big feelings.

### Success criteria
- Uses a calming strategy
- Asks for help

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name feelings and try new things.

**Key vocabulary:** calm, breathe, cross, upset, help, quiet

**Instructional materials:** Calm corner; Cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the calm song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: breathing, calm corner and cards. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child responds. Children: Child responds with a prompt.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Calm stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station closely and keep the mood quiet and kind. Stop any activity that upsets a child.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to guide a friend to the calm corner.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: notices big feelings; uses slow breathing; asks for help; uses the calm corner.

- **Ask:** How do we calm down?
  **Expected response:** Breathe slowly.
- **Ask:** Who do we ask?
  **Expected response:** A grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Uses calming strategies with support

### Conclusion
Take a slow breath and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Keep practising calm breathing together.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child manages big feelings" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
