# Toddler Social Habits — Term 3, Week 7: Values

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Saying thank you for gifts and help; Giving to others; Sharing food and toys

## Learning objectives
- Children will say thank you and say what they are thankful for.
- Children will give or share with others.

**Key vocabulary:** thank you, give, gift, thankful, share, kind

**Content:** Saying thank you for gifts and help, giving to others, and sharing food and toys.

**Teaching activities:** Link gratitude to real things children notice (food, rain, family, teachers); let children give freely and never force giving; model generosity and thanks; respect families who have less and avoid asking for gifts or money; keep any food activity allergy-safe.

**Learner activities:** Say what they are thankful for and give or share with a friend.

**Resources:** A thank-you tree made of paper; Paper leaves; A basket of fruit pictures; Toys to share; A gratitude song

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says what they are thankful for and gives or shares with a friend.

## Expected outcomes
- Says what they are thankful for.
- Gives or shares an item with a friend.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: thankful words; Numeracy: counting and sharing; Creative Development: drawing

## Day 1 (Monday): Being thankful

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Saying thank you for gifts and help

### Learning objectives
- Children will say what they are thankful for.

### Success criteria
- Says one thing they are thankful for
- Says thank you with a smile

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say thank you, share and help others.

**Key vocabulary:** thank you, give, gift, thankful, share, kind

**Instructional materials:** A paper tree; Paper leaves; Pictures of food, family, rain and friends

### Starter
Show a paper tree with no leaves and say "Our tree is bare. We will add leaves for things we are thankful for."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show pictures of food, family, rain and friends and say "I am thankful for this." Children: Children look and say thankful.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "What are you thankful for?" and listen. Children: Child names or points to something.

Step 3. Teacher: Help each child draw or place a leaf on the tree. Children: Child places a leaf.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "We say thank you for good things." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

### Practical activity: Thank-you tree
1. Choose something you are thankful for.
2. Draw it on a leaf.
3. Put the leaf on the tree.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic crayons and keep paper away from mouths. Accept every answer and never compare what children have.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two pictures and help the child choose.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say why they are thankful.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What are you thankful for?
  **Expected response:** Food, family or friends.
- **Ask:** What do we say?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Says what they are thankful for
- Says thank you with a smile

### Conclusion
Look at the full tree and say "Thank you."

### Home connection
Say one thing you are thankful for with your child each evening.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say what they are thankful for" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- THANKFUL
- TREE

**Picture suggestions:**
- A paper tree
- A family picture

**Song or rhyme:** Thank you for the sun, thank you for the rain.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Giving to others

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Giving to others

### Learning objectives
- Children will give something to a friend or helper.

### Success criteria
- Gives a drawing or toy to a friend
- Says this is for you

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say thank you, share and help others.

**Key vocabulary:** thank you, give, gift, thankful, share, kind

**Instructional materials:** Paper and crayons; Small toys; A gift basket

### Starter
Show a drawing and say "I made this for you. This is a gift."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to draw something for a friend. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child draw or pick something to give. Children: Child draws or chooses.

Step 3. Teacher: Help children give their gift and say this is for you. Children: Child gives and says this is for you.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Giving makes us happy." Children: Children smile.

### Practical activity: A gift for a friend
1. Draw a picture.
2. Choose a friend.
3. Give it and say this is for you.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic crayons and make sure every child gets a gift so none is left out. Never ask for money or valuable gifts.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Draw together and give together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give a gift to a helper.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you give?
  **Expected response:** A drawing or toy.
- **Ask:** Who is it for?
  **Expected response:** A friend.

### Expected outcomes
- Gives a gift to a friend
- Says this is for you

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say "Thank you."

### Home connection
Let your child give a drawing to someone.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "give something to a friend or helper" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Sharing food and toys

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Sharing food and toys

### Learning objectives
- Children will share toys and, with care, food in a small group.

### Success criteria
- Shares a toy
- Passes pretend food to a friend

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say thank you, share and help others.

**Key vocabulary:** thank you, give, gift, thankful, share, kind

**Instructional materials:** Toys; Toy food; A picnic blanket; Plates

### Starter
Spread a picnic blanket and say "We are having a picnic. We share with friends."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to pass a plate and say please and thank you. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child toy food to share. Children: Child passes toy food.

Step 3. Teacher: Share toys at the picnic. Children: Child shares a toy.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Sharing is kind." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

### Practical activity: Picnic sharing
1. Sit on the blanket.
2. Pass the toy food.
3. Say thank you.

**Safety:** Use toy food only for this activity. If real snacks are shared, check allergies and seat children while they eat.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Pass the plate together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to serve every friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do with food?
  **Expected response:** Share it.
- **Ask:** What do we say?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Shares toys and pretend food
- Uses thank you

### Conclusion
Tidy the picnic and say "Thank you."

### Home connection
Share a snack with a sibling and say thank you.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "share toys and, with care, food in a small group" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Thank you circle

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Saying thank you for gifts and help

### Learning objectives
- Children will thank a friend for something kind in a circle.

### Success criteria
- Says thank you to a friend
- Names what the friend did

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say thank you, share and help others.

**Key vocabulary:** thank you, give, gift, thankful, share, kind

**Instructional materials:** A soft ball; A circle song; Pictures of kind acts

### Starter
Roll the ball to a child and say "Thank you for being kind today."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to pass the ball and thank the friend for something. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Pass the ball around the circle. Children: Child says thank you to the next friend.

Step 3. Teacher: Help children say what the friend did. Children: Child says shared or helped.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Thank you, everyone." Children: Children clap and cheer.

### Practical activity: Thank you ball
1. Sit in a circle.
2. Pass the ball.
3. Say thank you.

**Safety:** Use a soft ball and sit children away from furniture. Make sure every child receives thanks.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Pass the ball together and say thank you.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to thank a friend for something specific.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who do you thank?
  **Expected response:** A friend.
- **Ask:** Why do you thank?
  **Expected response:** They shared or helped.

### Expected outcomes
- Says thank you to a friend
- Names a kind act

### Conclusion
Hold hands and say "Thank you."

### Home connection
Thank each family member for something small.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "thank a friend for something kind in a circle" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 gratitude check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Giving to others

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child shows gratitude and generosity.

### Success criteria
- Says what they are thankful for
- Gives or shares

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say thank you, share and help others.

**Key vocabulary:** thank you, give, gift, thankful, share, kind

**Instructional materials:** Paper tree; Gift basket; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the thank you song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: thankful tree, giving and sharing. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child behaves. Children: Child responds with a prompt.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Gratitude stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use toy food only and non-toxic materials.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to thank a helper.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says what they are thankful for; says thank you; gives a gift; shares toys.

- **Ask:** What are you thankful for?
  **Expected response:** A thing or person.
- **Ask:** What do we do with toys?
  **Expected response:** Share them.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Shows gratitude and generosity with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep saying thank you and sharing at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child shows gratitude and generosity" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**
- Gratitude
