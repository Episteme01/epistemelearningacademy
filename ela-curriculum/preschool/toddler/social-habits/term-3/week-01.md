# Toddler Social Habits — Term 3, Week 1: Confidence

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Trying something new; Saying "I can try"; Keeping going

## Learning objectives
- Children will try a new activity with encouragement.
- Children will say "I can try" and keep going for a short time.

**Key vocabulary:** try, brave, new, again, can, proud

**Content:** Trying something new, saying "I can try", and keeping going.

**Teaching activities:** Praise effort rather than result ("You kept trying"); offer a small first step; stay beside a hesitant child; never compare children; let children refuse and return; model trying and making mistakes yourself.

**Learner activities:** Try a new activity with encouragement, say "I can try" and keep going for a short time.

**Resources:** A balance beam on the floor; Stacking cups; A simple puzzle; A brave badge; A puppet who is afraid and then tries

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child tries a new activity with encouragement and says "I can try".

## Expected outcomes
- Tries a new activity with encouragement.
- Says "I can try" with a prompt.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Physical Development: balance; Literacy: I can words; Numeracy: stacking and counting

## Day 1 (Monday): Trying something new

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Trying something new

### Learning objectives
- Children will try a new activity with encouragement.

### Success criteria
- Tries a new activity
- Smiles after trying

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt manners, kindness, honesty, helping and respect over two terms.

**Key vocabulary:** try, brave, new, again, can, proud

**Instructional materials:** Stacking cups; A puppet; A brave badge

### Starter
Show a puppet looking at a tower of cups and say "I have never done this. I will try."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to stack two cups and say "I tried." Children: Children watch and clap.

Step 2. Teacher: Invite each child to try stacking one cup. Children: Child tries stacking a cup.

Step 3. Teacher: Praise the trying, not only the tower. Children: Child smiles and tries again.

Step 4. Teacher: Give the brave badge to the puppet and say "Brave to try." Children: Children cheer for the puppet.

### Practical activity: Stack and try
1. Pick up a cup.
2. Stack it on another.
3. Try again if it falls.

**Safety:** Use large light plastic cups only and keep children seated. Never force a child to try; stay close and offer a first step.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Stack the first cup together and let the child add one.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to try stacking four cups.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you try?
  **Expected response:** Stacking cups.
- **Ask:** Did it fall?
  **Expected response:** Yes, and I tried again.

### Expected outcomes
- Tries a new activity with encouragement
- Smiles after trying

### Conclusion
Say "I tried" together and wave.

### Home connection
Praise your child for trying new things, not only for succeeding.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "try a new activity with encouragement" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- TRY
- BRAVE

**Picture suggestions:**
- Stacking cups
- A brave badge

**Song or rhyme:** I can try, I can try, I can try again.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): I can try

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Saying "I can try"

### Learning objectives
- Children will say "I can try" before an activity.

### Success criteria
- Says I can try with a prompt
- Starts a task after saying it

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt manners, kindness, honesty, helping and respect over two terms.

**Key vocabulary:** try, brave, new, again, can, proud

**Instructional materials:** A simple puzzle; A ball and basket; A puppet

### Starter
Say "When something looks hard, we say: I can try."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Model saying "I can try" before a puzzle. Children: Children repeat I can try.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child say it before a task. Children: Child says I can try and begins.

Step 3. Teacher: Notice effort and say "You are trying." Children: Child continues with the task.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Well done for trying." Children: Children smile.

### Practical activity: I can try tasks
1. Choose a task.
2. Say I can try.
3. Do the task.

**Safety:** Choose tasks that children can mostly do and use large pieces. Never say a task is easy or hard.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say the words together and start together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say it to a friend before they start.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you say before you try?
  **Expected response:** I can try.
- **Ask:** Did you try?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Says I can try with a prompt
- Starts a task after saying it

### Conclusion
Say "I can try" together and clap.

### Home connection
Teach your child to say "I can try" before new tasks.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say "I can try" before an activity" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Keeping going

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Keeping going

### Learning objectives
- Children will keep going with a task for a short time when it is not easy.

### Success criteria
- Tries again after a mistake
- Asks for help when needed

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt manners, kindness, honesty, helping and respect over two terms.

**Key vocabulary:** try, brave, new, again, can, proud

**Instructional materials:** A tower of blocks; A puzzle with big pieces; A puppet

### Starter
Build a tower that falls and say "Oops. Let us try again."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a tower that falls and rebuild it. Children: Children watch and say try again.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "If it is hard, I can ask for help." Children: Children repeat ask for help.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children build or do a puzzle. Children: Child tries and tries again.

Step 4. Teacher: Praise the trying with specific words. Children: Child smiles and nods.

### Practical activity: Try again
1. Build a tower.
2. If it falls, try again.
3. Ask for help if you need it.

**Safety:** Use large light blocks and keep a safe distance from falling towers. Step in only when a child asks for help.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Build the first layer together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to help a friend who is stuck.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we do if it falls?
  **Expected response:** Try again.
- **Ask:** What can we ask for?
  **Expected response:** Help.

### Expected outcomes
- Tries again after a mistake
- Asks for help when needed

### Conclusion
Look at the finished work and say "You kept trying."

### Home connection
Let your child try again before you help.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "keep going with a task for a short time when it is not easy" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Brave steps

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Trying something new

### Learning objectives
- Children will try a physical challenge with a friendly adult beside them.

### Success criteria
- Tries to walk along a line or beam
- Says I did it

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt manners, kindness, honesty, helping and respect over two terms.

**Key vocabulary:** try, brave, new, again, can, proud

**Instructional materials:** A line of tape or a low beam on the floor; Soft mats; A badge

### Starter
Show a line on the floor and say "Can you walk on the line? Let us try."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to walk along the line with arms out. Children: Children watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Hold hands and walk the line with each child. Children: Child tries to walk the line.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children try with less help. Children: Child walks with less support.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "I did it" and give a badge. Children: Child says I did it.

### Practical activity: Walk the line
1. Stand at the start.
2. Walk on the line slowly.
3. Say I did it.

**Safety:** Use tape on the floor or a very low beam with soft mats beside it. Stay close and keep a second adult watching.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hand all the way along.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to walk backwards or carry a bean bag.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Did you try?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** How do you feel?
  **Expected response:** Proud or happy.

### Expected outcomes
- Tries a physical challenge
- Says I did it

### Conclusion
Give a badge and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Let your child try a safe challenge and praise the effort.

- Which children could show "try a physical challenge with a friendly adult beside them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 1 trying check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Saying "I can try"

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child tries new things and keeps going.

### Success criteria
- Tries a new activity
- Says I can try with a prompt

**Previous knowledge:** Children have learnt manners, kindness, honesty, helping and respect over two terms.

**Key vocabulary:** try, brave, new, again, can, proud

**Instructional materials:** Cups; A puzzle; A line on the floor; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the try song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: cups, puzzle and line. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child tries. Children: Child tries with a prompt.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Trying stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station closely and keep floors clear. Use large light items only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to encourage a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: tries a new activity; says I can try; keeps going after a mistake; asks for help.

- **Ask:** What do you say?
  **Expected response:** I can try.
- **Ask:** What do you do if it falls?
  **Expected response:** Try again.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Tries new things with support

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep praising effort at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child tries new things and keeps going" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
