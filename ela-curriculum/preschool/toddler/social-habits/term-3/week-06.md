# Toddler Social Habits — Term 3, Week 6: Safety

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** My body is mine; Saying no and telling a trusted adult; My safe grown-ups

## Learning objectives
- Children will say "my body is mine" and say "stop" to touch they do not like.
- Children will name trusted adults they can tell.

**Key vocabulary:** body, mine, stop, no, tell, safe

**Content:** My body is mine, saying no and telling a trusted adult, and my safe grown-ups.

**Teaching activities:** Teach this week gently and briefly using puppets, songs and pictures; use simple words and never describe abuse or ask children questions about what has happened to them; follow the school’s safeguarding and child protection policy and tell parents in advance what is taught; if a child says anything that worries you, listen calmly, do not question, and report it to the designated safeguarding lead the same day; never promise to keep a secret.

**Learner activities:** Say "my body is mine", say stop to touch they do not like and name trusted adults they can tell.

**Resources:** Puppets; A stop hand picture; A body outline picture; Pictures of trusted adults such as teacher, parent and nurse; A safe grown-ups song

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says stop with a hand sign and names a trusted adult. Never assess through personal questions.

## Expected outcomes
- Says stop to touch they do not like.
- Names a trusted adult.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: stop and tell; Creative Development: songs and puppets; Social Habits: kindness and respect

## Day 1 (Monday): My body is mine

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** My body is mine

### Learning objectives
- Children will learn that their body belongs to them and that they can choose who touches it.

### Success criteria
- Says my body is mine with a prompt
- Shows a stop hand

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stay safe on the road, ask for help and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** body, mine, stop, no, tell, safe

**Instructional materials:** A body outline picture; A puppet; A stop hand picture

### Starter
Show a puppet and say "This is my body. It is mine. I look after it and I say who can touch it."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the body outline and say "This is your body. It is yours." Children: Children look and touch their own arm.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "You can say stop if you do not like a touch." Children: Children show a stop hand.

Step 3. Teacher: Show the puppet saying stop to a tickle and the other puppet stopping. Children: Children watch and say stop.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "My body is mine." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

### Practical activity: Stop hand
1. Show a stop hand.
2. Say stop loudly.
3. Say my body is mine.

**Safety:** Use only puppets and pictures and never demonstrate on a child’s body. Keep the tone calm and positive and follow the school’s safeguarding policy.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Show the stop hand together and say stop.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell the puppet to stop.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Whose body is it?
  **Expected response:** Mine.
- **Ask:** What can you say?
  **Expected response:** Stop.

### Expected outcomes
- Says my body is mine with a prompt
- Shows a stop hand

### Conclusion
Sing the body song and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Respect your child’s no to hugs and tickles, and say "Your body is yours."

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "learn that their body belongs to them and that they can choose who touches it" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MINE
- STOP

**Picture suggestions:**
- A stop hand
- A body outline

**Song or rhyme:** My body is mine, my body is mine, I can say stop.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Saying no and stop

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Saying no and telling a trusted adult

### Learning objectives
- Children will practise saying no and stop to things they do not like and learn to tell an adult.

### Success criteria
- Says no or stop in play
- Says tell a grown-up

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stay safe on the road, ask for help and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** body, mine, stop, no, tell, safe

**Instructional materials:** Two puppets; A stop hand picture; A trusted adult picture

### Starter
Use two puppets: one takes a toy and the other says "No, stop. That is mine."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the puppet saying no and stop to a toy being taken. Children: Children watch and say stop.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "If someone does something we do not like, we say stop and tell a grown-up." Children: Children repeat tell a grown-up.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children practise saying no and stop to a puppet. Children: Child says no and stop.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "A grown-up will listen." Children: Children nod and smile.

### Practical activity: No and stop
1. Hold up a stop hand.
2. Say no and stop to the puppet.
3. Say tell a grown-up.

**Safety:** Keep the practice about toys and play only and use puppets. If a child says something that worries you, listen calmly and report it to the safeguarding lead the same day.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Say stop together with a stop hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name who they would tell.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you say if you do not like something?
  **Expected response:** No or stop.
- **Ask:** Who do you tell?
  **Expected response:** A grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Says no or stop in play
- Knows to tell a grown-up

### Conclusion
Show a stop hand and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Tell your child they can always tell you if something worries them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "practise saying no and stop to things they do not like and learn to tell an adult" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SAY NO
- TELL

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two puppets

## Day 3 (Wednesday): My safe grown-ups

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** My safe grown-ups

### Learning objectives
- Children will name the grown-ups they can tell when they feel worried.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to a safe grown-up
- Says I can tell

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stay safe on the road, ask for help and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** body, mine, stop, no, tell, safe

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a teacher, a parent, a grandparent and a nurse; A hand-shaped chart; Stickers

### Starter
Show a hand-shaped chart and say "On each finger, we put a grown-up we can tell."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the trusted adult pictures and name each person. Children: Children look and say who.

Step 2. Teacher: Help each child choose people they can tell and place stickers on fingers. Children: Child places a sticker on a finger.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "I can tell my safe grown-ups." Children: Children repeat the sentence.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Grown-ups help us stay safe." Children: Children smile.

### Practical activity: Safe hand chart
1. Look at the pictures.
2. Choose safe grown-ups.
3. Put stickers on the fingers.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic stickers and never ask children questions about their families beyond who helps them. Respect each family’s situation.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to a picture and name the grown-up together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend who their safe grown-ups are.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is a safe grown-up?
  **Expected response:** Teacher, parent or nurse.
- **Ask:** What do you do if you are worried?
  **Expected response:** Tell a safe grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Names a safe grown-up
- Says I can tell

### Conclusion
Hold up the safe hand and wave.

### Home connection
Talk to your child about who they can tell when they feel worried.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name the grown-ups they can tell when they feel worried" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SAFE
- TELL

**Picture suggestions:**
- A safe hand chart

## Day 4 (Thursday): Safe and happy

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** My body is mine

### Learning objectives
- Children will sing and act songs that reinforce stop, tell and safe grown-ups.

### Success criteria
- Joins the safe song
- Shows stop and tell actions

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stay safe on the road, ask for help and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** body, mine, stop, no, tell, safe

**Instructional materials:** A safety song; Puppets; Pictures of happy children

### Starter
Sing the safe song and do the actions for stop and tell.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Teach the safe song with actions for stop and tell. Children: Children do the actions.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing the song again slowly. Children: Children sing with the actions.

Step 3. Teacher: Use the puppet to say "I feel safe." Children: Children say I feel safe.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Everyone is safe at school." Children: Children smile and wave.

### Practical activity: Safe song
1. Learn the song.
2. Do the actions.
3. Say I feel safe.

**Safety:** Keep the mood warm and light and the actions simple. Never press a child to share personal experiences.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Do the actions together and sing quietly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead the song.

### Assessment

- **Ask:** What do you say to stop?
  **Expected response:** Stop.
- **Ask:** Who do you tell?
  **Expected response:** A grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Joins the safe song
- Does stop and tell actions

### Conclusion
Sing the song once more and wave goodbye.

### Home connection

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 safety check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SOC-T3-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** My safe grown-ups

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child shows stop and names a safe grown-up.

### Success criteria
- Shows a stop hand and says stop
- Names or points to a safe grown-up

**Previous knowledge:** Children can stay safe on the road, ask for help and name feelings.

**Key vocabulary:** body, mine, stop, no, tell, safe

**Instructional materials:** Puppets; Pictures; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the safe song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: stop, tell and safe grown-ups. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station watch how the child responds. Children: Child responds with a word or sign.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level in a general way only. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Safety stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and use puppets and pictures only. Record observations only and report any concern to the safeguarding lead.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to show a friend the stop hand.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: shows a stop hand; says no or stop in play; names a safe grown-up; says I can tell. Do not question children about their own experiences.

- **Ask:** What do you say?
  **Expected response:** Stop.
- **Ask:** Who can you tell?
  **Expected response:** A safe grown-up.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Shows stop and names a safe grown-up

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and say well done.

### Home connection
Keep reminding your child that they can tell you anything.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child shows stop and names a safe grown-up" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
