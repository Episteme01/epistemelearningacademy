# Toddler Social Habits — Term 3 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Confidence**: Trying something new; Saying "I can try"; Keeping going | Children will try a new activity with encouragement. Children will say "I can try" and keep going for a short time. | Trying something new, saying "I can try", and keeping going. | Praise effort rather than result ("You kept trying"); offer a small first step; stay beside a hesitant child; never compare children; let children refuse and return; model trying and making mistakes yourself. | Try a new activity with encouragement, say "I can try" and keep going for a short time. | A balance beam on the floor; Stacking cups; A simple puzzle; A brave badge; A puppet who is afraid and then tries | Observe whether each child tries a new activity with encouragement and says "I can try". | Tries a new activity with encouragement. Says "I can try" with a prompt. | Physical Development: balance; Literacy: I can words; Numeracy: stacking and counting |
| 2 | **Feelings**: Big feelings in my body; Breathing and calming; Asking for help | Children will notice big feelings in their bodies. Children will use slow breathing or a calm place with an adult. | Big feelings in my body, breathing and calming, and asking for help. | Stay calm yourself; name the feeling first ("You are cross"); never punish a child for a big feeling; offer a calm corner as a safe place, not a punishment; teach slow breathing with a pinwheel or a teddy; use a feelings chart; teach asking for help in words or a gesture. | Notice big feelings in the body, use slow breathing with an adult and ask for help when upset. | A calm corner with cushions; A pinwheel or feather; A teddy; A feelings chart; A calming song | Observe whether each child uses a calming strategy with adult support and asks for help. | Uses a calming strategy with adult support. Asks an adult for help when upset. | Physical Development: breathing; Literacy: feelings words; Creative Development: calm music |
| 3 | **Cooperation**: Building together; Carrying together; Group games | Children will work with a friend or a small group on a simple task. Children will say "we did it". | Building together, carrying together, and group games. | Choose tasks that need two or more children; give each child a clear part; praise the team ("We did it"); keep groups small; step back as children cooperate; rotate partners. | Work with a friend on a simple task and say "we did it". | Large cardboard boxes; Large blocks; A light mat to carry; A parachute; A teamwork song | Observe whether each child works with a friend on a simple task and joins a group game. | Works with a friend on a simple task. Joins a group game. | Physical Development: carrying; Numeracy: counting together; Literacy: we words |
| 4 | **Community**: Teachers and nurses; Drivers, farmers and market sellers; Thanking helpers | Children will name community helpers and what they do. Children will thank a helper. | Teachers and nurses, drivers, farmers and market sellers, and thanking helpers. | Use local helpers children know (the nurse at the clinic, the bus driver, the market seller, the farmer, the gateman); invite a helper to visit if possible; keep role play positive; avoid gender stereotypes about jobs; use real objects such as a stethoscope toy. | Name community helpers and what they do and say thank you to a helper. | Pictures of community helpers; Dress-up items and hats; Toy medical kit; Toy vegetables and a play market; A thank-you card | Observe whether each child names two helpers and says thank you to a helper. | Names two or more community helpers. Says thank you to a helper. | Understanding the World: people and jobs; Literacy: job words; Creative Development: role play |
| 5 | **Safety**: Holding hands near roads; Stop, look and listen; Safe walking | Children will hold an adult’s hand near roads. Children will stop at a pretend kerb and look and listen. | Holding hands near roads, stop, look and listen, and safe walking. | Always practise in a safe, closed play space with chalk roads and toy cars; never practise on a real road with children as the lesson; stress holding an adult’s hand; use short rules; link to what families do on the way to school; invite parents to hold hands at the gate. | Hold an adult’s hand near roads and stop, look and listen at a pretend kerb. | Chalk to draw roads; Toy cars; A stop sign; Pictures of roads and crossings; A road safety song | Observe whether each child holds a hand to walk and stops at a pretend kerb on request. | Holds an adult’s hand to walk. Stops at a pretend kerb on request. | Literacy: safety words; Physical Development: stopping and starting; Numeracy: counting cars |
| 6 | **Safety**: My body is mine; Saying no and telling a trusted adult; My safe grown-ups | Children will say "my body is mine" and say "stop" to touch they do not like. Children will name trusted adults they can tell. | My body is mine, saying no and telling a trusted adult, and my safe grown-ups. | Teach this week gently and briefly using puppets, songs and pictures; use simple words and never describe abuse or ask children questions about what has happened to them; follow the school’s safeguarding and child protection policy and tell parents in advance what is taught; if a child says anything that worries you, listen calmly, do not question, and report it to the designated safeguarding lead the same day; never promise to keep a secret. | Say "my body is mine", say stop to touch they do not like and name trusted adults they can tell. | Puppets; A stop hand picture; A body outline picture; Pictures of trusted adults such as teacher, parent and nurse; A safe grown-ups song | Observe whether each child says stop with a hand sign and names a trusted adult. Never assess through personal questions. | Says stop to touch they do not like. Names a trusted adult. | Literacy: stop and tell; Creative Development: songs and puppets; Social Habits: kindness and respect |
| 7 | **Values**: Saying thank you for gifts and help; Giving to others; Sharing food and toys | Children will say thank you and say what they are thankful for. Children will give or share with others. | Saying thank you for gifts and help, giving to others, and sharing food and toys. | Link gratitude to real things children notice (food, rain, family, teachers); let children give freely and never force giving; model generosity and thanks; respect families who have less and avoid asking for gifts or money; keep any food activity allergy-safe. | Say what they are thankful for and give or share with a friend. | A thank-you tree made of paper; Paper leaves; A basket of fruit pictures; Toys to share; A gratitude song | Observe whether each child says what they are thankful for and gives or shares with a friend. | Says what they are thankful for. Gives or shares an item with a friend. | Literacy: thankful words; Numeracy: counting and sharing; Creative Development: drawing |
| 8 | **Community**: Our clothes and food; Songs and dances; Festivals and special days | Children will enjoy and name familiar cultural clothes, foods, songs and dances. Children will say that families celebrate in different ways. | Our clothes and food, songs and dances, and festivals and special days. | Celebrate the cultures of every family in the class (Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, and others, and different faiths); invite parents to share clothes, foods, songs and stories; never rank cultures or religions; use real objects and songs; keep any food tasting allergy-safe and halal or dietary needs respected. | Join in songs and dances and name a food or clothing from home. | Traditional clothes and fabric; Pictures of local foods; Drums and shakers; Recorded or live songs; Pictures of festivals | Observe whether each child joins a song or dance and names a food or clothing from home. | Joins in a song or dance. Names a food or clothing from home. | Literacy: songs and stories; Creative Development: music and dance; Understanding the World: people and communities |
| 9 | **Independence**: Putting on shoes and bag; Washing and drying hands; Packing away and helping | Children will do simple self-help tasks with less help. Children will say "I can do it". | Putting on shoes and bag, washing and drying hands, and packing away and helping. | Allow time; wait before helping; give just enough help; praise effort ("You did it yourself"); use real shoes, bags and jackets; set up a dress-up area; avoid taking over when a child struggles; link to routines at arrival and home time. | Do simple self-help tasks with less help and say "I can do it". | Shoes with Velcro and with laces for practice; Bags and jackets; A basin and soap; Baskets; A dressing board | Observe whether each child does one self-help task with little support and says "I can do it". | Does one self-help task with little support. Says "I can do it". | Physical Development: fine motor skills; Literacy: I can do it; Numeracy: pairs of shoes |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising feelings and bravery; Revising safety and helping; Celebration and end-of-year record | Children will show Year learning through games and a class celebration. Teachers will complete every child’s end-of-year record and transition note. | Revising feelings and bravery, safety and helping, and celebrating the year with end-of-year records and a transition note. | Keep every activity familiar and joyful; observe quietly; record what children can do; invite parents to the celebration; complete the transition note honestly for the Nursery 1 teacher; handle any safeguarding or welfare concern through the school’s policy and not through the transition note alone. | Show learning from the year through games and a class celebration. | Feelings cards; Calm corner; Helper hats; Road safety signs; Certificates or stickers; End-of-year record sheets | Observe and record each child’s end-of-year social learning and complete the transition note. | Takes part confidently in familiar routines and games. Each child has an end-of-year observation record. | Literacy: songs and stories; Creative Development: music; Numeracy: counting and turns |
