# Toddler Numeracy

**Programme ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft (not yet approved by an ELA reviewer)

## Subject philosophy
Toddlers meet mathematics through their hands, eyes, bodies and voices. They handle real things, compare them, sort them, count them in songs and games, and hear adults use precise mathematical words in meaningful moments. ELA treats early numeracy as a way of making sense of the world, not as a set of sums to be written. Every idea is met first through play with real objects from a Nigerian home, market and compound, and only later through pictures and numerals.

## Rationale
The first three years of mathematical learning set up confidence and vocabulary for everything that follows. Research-informed early years practice, including the British Early Years Foundation Stage and Nigerian early childhood curriculum aims, agrees that young children need many repeated, playful experiences of comparing, sorting, matching, counting and noticing patterns before formal number work makes sense. This programme gives ELA toddlers a deliberate, ambitious but developmentally honest start: secure counting to 3 and growing counting to 5 with real objects, rhymes to 10, and a rich bank of comparison words.

## Aims
- To build joy, curiosity and confidence in handling and talking about quantity, size, shape, position and pattern.
- To establish the foundations of counting: a stable rhyme, touching one object for each word, and knowing that the last word tells how many.
- To teach children to sort, match and compare real objects and to say what they notice.
- To grow mathematical vocabulary through songs, stories and everyday routines.
- To prepare children for Nursery 1 numeracy without introducing formal written work.

## Learning outcomes by the end of the year
- Sorts familiar objects into two or three groups by colour, size or kind, and says one reason.
- Matches identical objects and finds objects that are the same and different.
- Compares two objects by size, length, weight and capacity using words such as big, small, long, short, heavy, light, full and empty.
- Recites the number rhyme from one to five, and with support to ten.
- Counts up to three objects reliably by touching each one once, and up to five with support.
- Recognises and matches the numerals 1 to 5 to groups of objects with support.
- Names and finds circles, squares and triangles in the environment.
- Continues a simple two-part pattern (AB) with colours, objects or sounds with support.
- Uses position words (in, on, under, up, down, behind, in front) and routine time words (morning, afternoon, night).

## Developmental expectations
- Children learn best when numeracy is physical: carrying, pouring, stacking, lining up, hiding and finding.
- Attention spans are short, so each lesson is built from short, active parts and repeats key ideas on different days.
- Language comes before symbols: children say, show and act before they read or write numerals.
- Counting reliability grows slowly. Expect children to skip, double count or lose track, and keep numbers small.
- Independence in this programme means choosing an activity, tidying it away and trying a turn without an adult leading every move.

## Key competencies
- Observing and noticing similarities and differences.
- Communicating mathematical ideas in single words, phrases and gestures.
- Persisting with a hands-on task for a short time.
- Cooperating by taking turns and sharing materials.
- Early reasoning: choosing, giving a reason and checking.

## Curriculum strands
- **Sorting and matching** (SM): Matching identical objects, sorting by one feature, saying what is the same and different.
- **Comparing and measuring** (CM): Big and small, long and short, heavy and light, full and empty, more and the same.
- **Number and counting** (NC): Number rhymes, one-to-one counting, how many, numerals 1 to 5, one more.
- **Shape and space** (SS): Circles, squares and triangles; position words and movement.
- **Pattern and time** (PT): Simple colour, sound and movement patterns; the order of the day.
- **Mathematical language and reasoning** (ML): Using precise words, answering how and why in simple ways, and using maths in play such as market games.

## Prior knowledge
None is assumed beyond ordinary play and routines. Children may arrive knowing a counting rhyme at home. Teachers should observe in the first fortnight what each child already does and record it as the baseline.

## Progression across the year
- **Term 1:** Term 1 builds the habits of looking closely: size, same and different, sorting, one and lots, rhymes to three, counting to three and the circle. Number is kept to three.
- **Term 2:** Term 2 widens number to five and links quantity to numerals 1 to 5. Children compare length and weight, make simple patterns and meet the square and triangle.
- **Term 3:** Term 3 deepens and applies learning: capacity, the daily routine, shape hunts, position, sharing one-to-one, one more, counting with reasons, a simple class graph and a role-play market.

## Assessment principles
- Assessment is by observation during play, short talk with a child and collected examples such as photographs and the child’s own work. There are no written tests.
- Each child is recorded as Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding against the lesson’s success criteria (definitions in ASSESSMENT_FRAMEWORK.md).
- The Friday lesson each week and Week 10 of each term are for review, catch-up and gathering evidence.
- Parents receive a short, positive, jargon-free summary each term.

## Inclusion and differentiation
- Every lesson names a supported and an extended version of the main task.
- Offer the same ideas through touch, sight, sound and movement so children with different strengths can take part.
- Allow children who speak little English or another home language to show with actions and objects, and name things in the home language too.
- Children with additional needs follow the same activity with adapted materials, more time or an adult partner.

## Cross-curricular connections
- Literacy: rhymes, songs and picture books carry the counting and comparing words.
- Science: sorting living and non-living things, comparing weights and pouring water.
- Social Habits: sharing, taking turns, tidying away and caring for materials.
- EYFS mapping: mainly Mathematics, with Communication and Language, Physical Development and Personal, Social and Emotional Development (see STANDARDS_ALIGNMENT.md).

## Parent connection
- Each lesson ends with a two-minute home idea using ordinary household things and no purchases.
- Parents are invited to count stairs, spoons, fruit and steps aloud during daily routines.
- Families are encouraged to use the home language for number rhymes and counting; the idea transfers to English.
