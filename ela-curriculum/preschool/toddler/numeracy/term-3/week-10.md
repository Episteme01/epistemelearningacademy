# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 10: Revision and assessment

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W10 · **Week type:** revision-assessment · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Revising number, measures and shapes; Revising position and time; Celebration and end-of-year record

## Learning objectives
- Children will show Year learning through games and stations.
- Teachers will complete every child’s end-of-year record and transition note.

**Key vocabulary:** count, one more, full, empty, morning, night, circle, square, triangle

**Content:** Revising the year’s numeracy learning through games and stations, celebrating achievements and completing each child’s end-of-year record.

**Teaching activities:** Keep the week relaxed and celebratory; observe quietly; record evidence; prepare a transition note for the next class.

**Learner activities:** Play favourite maths games, show what they know, celebrate with friends and share their learning with families.

**Resources:** Materials from the year; Class record sheets; A camera or phone; Stickers or small certificates; A celebration cloth and music

**Assessment:** Use the three term records and this week’s evidence to complete the end-of-year record for every strand: sorting and matching, comparing and measuring, number and counting, shape and space, pattern and time, and mathematical language. Record Secure only with evidence from two or more occasions.

## Expected outcomes
- Takes part confidently in familiar maths games.
- Each child has an end-of-year observation record.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: songs and rhymes from the year; Social Habits: celebrating together and thanking friends; Expressive arts: music and dance

## Day 1 (Monday): Counting and number games

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W10-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** revision · **Subtopic:** Revising number, measures and shapes

### Learning objectives
- Children will revisit counting to five, one more and sharing through games.

### Success criteria
- Counts up to five objects by touching
- Adds one more or shares one each

**Previous knowledge:** Children have met counting to five, numerals, measures, patterns, shapes, position, time, one more and sharing during the year.

**Key vocabulary:** count, one more, full, empty, morning, night, circle, square, triangle

**Instructional materials:** Blocks and trays; Toy fruits and baskets; Number cards 1 to 5; Class record sheet

### Starter
Beat the drum five times and say "One, two, three, four, five." Say "Today we play our favourite counting games."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Play "Count the blocks" with a tray of up to five blocks. Children: Children touch and count the blocks.

Step 2. Teacher: Play "One more" with fruits and ask "How many now?" Children: Children add one more and say the new number.

Step 3. Teacher: Play "One for you, one for me" with toys and plates. Children: Children share one each with friends.

Step 4. Teacher: Match numeral cards to groups of objects. Children: Children match numerals to groups with support.

### Practical activity: Number game stations
1. Set up stations for counting, one more and sharing.
2. Children play with a friend.
3. Teacher records at one station.

**Safety:** Use large toy items only; supervise stations; keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count only to three and play with an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count to five and tell a friend how many.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many blocks?
  **Expected response:** The correct number up to five.
- **Ask:** What is one more?
  **Expected response:** The next number.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts, adds one more and shares
- Matches numerals with support

### Conclusion
Sing a counting song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Count and share at home with a family member.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "revisit counting to five, one more and sharing through games" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Count, add one, share

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of counting activities

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Shape, size and position games

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W10-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** revision · **Subtopic:** Revising position and time

### Learning objectives
- Children will revisit shapes, comparing size and position through games.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to circle, square and triangle
- Uses a comparison or position word

**Previous knowledge:** Children have met counting to five, numerals, measures, patterns, shapes, position, time, one more and sharing during the year.

**Key vocabulary:** count, one more, full, empty, morning, night, circle, square, triangle

**Instructional materials:** Cardboard shapes; Ropes, sticks and baskets; A tunnel and a chair; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "Show me a circle! Show me a long rope! Go behind the chair!" Say "Today we play games about shapes and size."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Play shape hunt: find a circle, a square and a triangle. Children: Children find and name shapes.

Step 2. Teacher: Compare long and short ropes and heavy and light baskets. Children: Children choose the longer rope and heavier basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Play position games with the chair and tunnel. Children: Children follow instructions and say position words.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask the children to tell a friend one thing they can do. Children: Child shows or says one skill.

### Practical activity: Shape and position circuit
1. Set up a shape hunt, a measure station and a position course.
2. Children visit each with a friend.
3. Teacher records.

**Safety:** Use low equipment; supervise stations; keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Do one activity at a time with an adult.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach a friend a game.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Show me a triangle.
  **Expected response:** Child points to a triangle.
- **Ask:** Go behind the chair.
  **Expected response:** Child goes behind the chair.

### Expected outcomes
- Names shapes and uses position words
- Compares measures

### Conclusion
Sing the position song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Play shape and position games at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "revisit shapes, comparing size and position through games" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Shape, measure, position

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of shape and position games

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Full, empty, morning and night

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W10-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Revising position and time

### Learning objectives
- Children will revisit capacity and time words through play.

### Success criteria
- Says full or empty correctly
- Sorts day and night pictures

**Previous knowledge:** Children have met counting to five, numerals, measures, patterns, shapes, position, time, one more and sharing during the year.

**Key vocabulary:** count, one more, full, empty, morning, night, circle, square, triangle

**Instructional materials:** Cups and a basin of water; Sun and moon pictures; Picture cards of the day; Class record sheet

### Starter
Show a full cup and an empty cup. Say "Full! Empty!" Show the sun and the moon and say "Day! Night!"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pour water into cups and ask "Full or empty?" Children: Children pour and say the word.

Step 2. Teacher: Sort day and night picture cards under the sun and the moon. Children: Children sort cards and say "day" or "night".

Step 3. Teacher: Put the cards of the school day in order. Children: Children place the cards in order with help.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What do we do in the morning?" and "What do we do at night?" Children: Children answer with a word or action.

### Practical activity: Water and time stations
1. Set up a water station and a time sorting station.
2. Children play at each station.
3. Teacher records.

**Safety:** Supervise water play; wipe spills at once; wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use full and empty only, with an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell the story of their day in order.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the cup full or empty?
  **Expected response:** The correct word.
- **Ask:** When do we sleep?
  **Expected response:** Night.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses full and empty correctly
- Sorts day and night

### Conclusion
Sing the day song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Talk about the parts of the day at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "revisit capacity and time words through play" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Full, empty, day, night

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of water and time activities

## Day 4 (Thursday): Our maths party

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W10-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Revising number, measures and shapes

### Learning objectives
- Children will use number, shape and sharing words in a pretend party.

### Success criteria
- Counts out items for guests
- Uses shape or number words in play

**Previous knowledge:** Children have met counting to five, numerals, measures, patterns, shapes, position, time, one more and sharing during the year.

**Key vocabulary:** count, one more, full, empty, morning, night, circle, square, triangle

**Instructional materials:** Plates, cups and toy food; Soft toys as guests; A party cloth; Shape cards

### Starter
Spread the party cloth and say "Our toys are having a maths party. Who can set the table for four guests?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Ask children to put one plate and one cup for each guest. Children: Children place one plate and one cup at each place.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Can you find a round plate and a square tray?" Children: Children find shapes at the party table.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask children to share five biscuits among the guests. Children: Children share and count what is left.

Step 4. Teacher: Sing a party song with a clapping pattern. Children: Children clap in a pattern and sing.

### Practical activity: Maths party table
1. Give each child a role.
2. Set the table with one plate and cup for each guest.
3. Count and share the food.

**Safety:** Use toy items; supervise role play; wash hands after play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Set the table for two guests only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to set the table for five guests and say how many plates.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many plates for four guests?
  **Expected response:** Four.
- **Ask:** Which plate is round?
  **Expected response:** The circle-shaped plate.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses number and shape words in play
- Shares one each

### Conclusion
Say thank you to the guests and tidy the party table together.

### Home connection
Help to set the table at home and count the plates.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use number, shape and sharing words in a pretend party" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Party table

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Show and celebrate our year

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W10-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** assessment · **Subtopic:** Celebration and end-of-year record

### Learning objectives
- Children will show their year of numeracy learning in familiar games and celebrate their work.

### Success criteria
- Takes part confidently in at least three familiar tasks
- Shows or says one thing they have learned this year

**Previous knowledge:** Children have met counting to five, numerals, measures, patterns, shapes, position, time, one more and sharing during the year.

**Key vocabulary:** count, one more, full, empty, morning, night, circle, square, triangle

**Instructional materials:** Station materials from the year; Class record sheets; A camera or phone; Stickers or small certificates; Music

### Starter
Play a favourite song from the year. Say "Today we show what we know and celebrate how much we have learned this year."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up six quick stations: sorting, comparing, counting, shapes, position and one more. Spend three minutes at each. Children: Children visit stations in pairs.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask a simple question and wait. Children: Child answers or shows the response.

Step 3. Teacher: Take photographs of each child at work and record levels against the three-term record. Children: Children continue playing.

Step 4. Teacher: Give each child a certificate or sticker and say one thing they did well this year. Children: Children receive their award and clap for friends.

### Practical activity: Year celebration stations
1. Set up six stations.
2. Children visit each with a friend.
3. Teacher records evidence and takes photos.

**Safety:** Remove small items; supervise all stations; never compare children aloud.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Visit three stations with an adult partner at each.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to be a station helper.

### Assessment
Observe each child at the six stations and complete the end-of-year record. Record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for each strand: sorting and matching, comparing and measuring, number and counting, shape and space, pattern and time, and mathematical language and reasoning. Write one transition sentence for the Nursery 1 teacher.

- **Ask:** How many blocks?
  **Expected response:** The correct number up to five.
- **Ask:** Which is longer?
  **Expected response:** The longer object.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an end-of-year observation record
- Celebrates their own learning

### Conclusion
Clap for the whole class and sing the closing song. Send the end-of-year note home.

### Home connection
Parents receive an end-of-year note with strengths, next steps and home ideas for the holiday.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show their year of numeracy learning in familiar games and celebrate their work" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Well done this year!

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of the celebration
