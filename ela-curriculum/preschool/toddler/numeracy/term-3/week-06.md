# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 6: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Adding one more; One more game with fingers; One more in stories and songs

## Learning objectives
- Children will add one more object to a small group.
- Children will say or show how many there are now, with support.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, now, how many, add, altogether

**Content:** Adding one more object to a small group and saying how many there are now, using objects, fingers and stories.

**Teaching activities:** Count the group first, add one object slowly, say "one more", and recount from one; keep groups to one to four.

**Learner activities:** Add one more to a group, recount, show the new number on fingers and act out one more in songs and stories.

**Resources:** Large blocks; Toy fruits; Cups; Soft toys; A basket and a cloth

**Assessment:** Check whether each child adds one more to a group of one to three and says or shows how many now.

## Expected outcomes
- Adds one more to a group of one to three.
- Shows how many now with fingers.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: sentences with one more; Social Habits: asking politely for more; Physical Development: handling objects

## Day 1 (Monday): One more block

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Adding one more

### Learning objectives
- Children will add one more block to a small group and say how many now.

### Success criteria
- Adds exactly one block
- Counts the new group

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, give one to each friend and say more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, now, how many, add, altogether

**Instructional materials:** Large wooden blocks; A tray; A mat

### Starter
Put two blocks on the tray and ask "How many?" Add one block and say "One more block. Now how many?" Count together.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Start with one block and count it. Add one more and say "One more. Now two." Children: Children count one, two with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Start with two blocks, add one more and say "One more. Now three." Count again. Children: Children count to three and say "three".

Step 3. Teacher: Ask a child to add one more to a group of three and say how many. Children: Child adds one block and counts to four with help.

Step 4. Teacher: Play "One more, please" with the children asking for one more block. Children: Children say "one more, please" and add a block.

### Practical activity: One more tray
1. Give each pair a tray with two blocks.
2. Add one more and count.
3. Swap and try again with three blocks.

**Safety:** Use blocks larger than a child’s fist; keep trays on the floor; supervise group play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Start with one block and add one more with hand-over-hand help.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to add one more to four blocks and say how many.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many blocks now?
  **Expected response:** The correct number after adding one.
- **Ask:** Can you add one more?
  **Expected response:** Child adds exactly one block.

### Expected outcomes
- Adds one more to a small group
- Says how many now

### Conclusion
Count the final group aloud and say "One more, one more, well done."

### Home connection
Add one more spoon or one more shoe to a pile at home and count.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "add one more block to a small group and say how many now" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 + one more = 2
- Add one more

**Picture suggestions:**
- A block added to a group

**Song or rhyme:** One more block, one more block, count them all, one more block.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): One more finger

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** One more game with fingers

### Learning objectives
- Children will show one more finger and say the new number.

### Success criteria
- Shows one more finger
- Says the number

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, give one to each friend and say more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, now, how many, add, altogether

**Instructional materials:** Finger picture cards; A drum; A mat

### Starter
Show one finger and say "One." Raise another finger and say "One more finger. Now two."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold up two fingers and say "Two." Add one more finger and say "Three." Children: Children copy and say the numbers.

Step 2. Teacher: Call "Show me three." Then say "Add one more" and hold up four fingers. Children: Children add one finger and say "four".

Step 3. Teacher: Play "one more" game: show a number and ask for one more. Children: Children show one more finger and say the new number.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask pairs to take turns: one shows fingers, the other shows one more. Children: Pairs play and say the numbers.

### Practical activity: Finger one more
1. Sit in pairs facing each other.
2. One shows two fingers; the partner shows one more.
3. Say the number together.

**Safety:** Keep hands away from faces; sit comfortably; the leader stops the game if it becomes loud.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use one and two fingers only with an adult adding the finger.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to show one more than four fingers using both hands.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is one more than two?
  **Expected response:** Three.
- **Ask:** Show me one more finger.
  **Expected response:** Child shows one extra finger.

### Expected outcomes
- Shows one more with fingers
- Says the next number

### Conclusion
Fold the fingers away and say "One more, one more, sleep."

### Home connection
Play "one more" with your child using fingers while waiting.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show one more finger and say the new number" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 2, then one more is 3

**Picture suggestions:**
- Hands with two and three fingers

## Day 3 (Wednesday): One more fruit in the basket

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** One more in stories and songs

### Learning objectives
- Children will add one more toy fruit to a basket and count how many now.

### Success criteria
- Adds one fruit
- Recounts and says how many now

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, give one to each friend and say more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, now, how many, add, altogether

**Instructional materials:** Toy fruits; A basket; A cloth

### Starter
Put two toy fruits in the basket and cover them with a cloth. Say "Two fruits. One more fruit comes into the basket. Now how many?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Add one fruit while the children watch. Lift the cloth and count. Children: Children count the fruits and say "three".

Step 2. Teacher: Cover the basket again and add another fruit. Ask "How many now?" Children: Children guess, then count to check.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each child a turn to add one fruit to the basket. Children: Child adds one fruit and the group counts.

Step 4. Teacher: Take one fruit away and ask "Now how many?" Children: Children count and say the new number.

### Practical activity: Fruit basket one more
1. Start with two fruits in a basket.
2. Add one more and count.
3. Say the number aloud.

**Safety:** Use toy fruits larger than a child’s fist; keep baskets on the table.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Start with one fruit and add one more with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to add one more twice and say how many.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many fruits now?
  **Expected response:** The correct number after adding one.
- **Ask:** What happens if I take one away?
  **Expected response:** There is one less.

### Expected outcomes
- Adds one more to a basket
- Counts the new total

### Conclusion
Count the fruits in the basket one last time and say the number.

### Home connection
Count items in a bowl and add one more each time.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "add one more toy fruit to a basket and count how many now" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- One more fruit

**Picture suggestions:**
- A basket of fruits

## Day 4 (Thursday): The hungry caterpillar friend

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** One more in stories and songs

### Learning objectives
- Children will listen to a story about adding one more and act it out.

### Success criteria
- Says one more at the right moment
- Acts out adding one more

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, give one to each friend and say more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, now, how many, add, altogether

**Instructional materials:** A soft toy caterpillar or a green cloth; Three toy leaves; A basket

### Starter
Show the caterpillar toy and say "Little Caterpillar is hungry. He eats one more leaf each day. Let us count his leaves."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the first day and feed one leaf to the caterpillar. Say "One leaf." Children: Children say "one" and munch.

Step 2. Teacher: Feed one more leaf and ask "How many now?" Children: Children count and say "two".

Step 3. Teacher: Feed another leaf and ask the same question. Children: Children count and say "three".

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child feed the caterpillar one more leaf while the group says the number. Children: Child feeds a leaf and says "one more".

### Practical activity: Feed the caterpillar
1. Give each pair a caterpillar and three leaves.
2. Feed one leaf at a time.
3. Count after each leaf.

**Safety:** Use toy or paper leaves; keep them away from mouths; supervise group play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Feed only two leaves and say the numbers with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell the story with four leaves.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many leaves did he eat on Tuesday?
  **Expected response:** Two altogether.
- **Ask:** What did he say after the third leaf?
  **Expected response:** Three, and he was full.

### Expected outcomes
- Adds one more in a story
- Says the new number

### Conclusion
Put the caterpillar to sleep under a cloth and say "Goodnight."

### Home connection
Tell the caterpillar story at home using beans or buttons for leaves, held by an adult.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "listen to a story about adding one more and act it out" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1, 2, 3 leaves

**Picture suggestions:**
- A caterpillar eating leaves

**Song or rhyme:** Munch, munch, munch, one more leaf. Munch, munch, munch, now we have two.

**Short story:** Little Caterpillar was hungry. On Monday he ate one leaf. Munch! On Tuesday he ate one more leaf. Now he had eaten two. Munch, munch! On Wednesday he ate one more leaf. Now three! Munch, munch, munch! Little Caterpillar was full. He rolled up and went to sleep.

## Day 5 (Friday): One more stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Adding one more

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can add one more at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Adds one more to a group
- Says how many now

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, give one to each friend and say more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one more, now, how many, add, altogether

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: blocks and tray; Station 2: finger games; Station 3: fruit basket; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the one more song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Add one more" and "How many now?" Children: Child adds and says the number.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use large objects only; supervise stations; keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to be the "one more" leader.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: adds one more to a group of one to three; says or shows how many now.

- **Ask:** How many now?
  **Expected response:** The correct number after adding one.
- **Ask:** Can you add one more?
  **Expected response:** Child adds one object.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Adds one more with support

### Conclusion
Sing the one more song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Practise one more at home with fruit or blocks.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show they can add one more at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

- One more
