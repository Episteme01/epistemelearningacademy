# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 7: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Counting without missing any; The last number tells how many; Checking our counting

## Learning objectives
- Children will count up to five objects without skipping any.
- Children will check a count by counting again.

**Key vocabulary:** count, again, check, how many, last

**Content:** Counting up to five objects without skipping or repeating any, answering how many with the last number and checking the count.

**Teaching activities:** Show how to move counted objects to a "done" tray; model a mistake and fix it; praise careful touching rather than speed.

**Learner activities:** Count scattered and lined-up objects, check by counting again, find mistakes and say how many.

**Resources:** Large blocks; Toy fruits; Two trays; A cloth; A teddy puppet who makes counting mistakes

**Assessment:** Check one-to-one touching, order of number words and whether the child says how many with the last number.

## Expected outcomes
- Counts to three reliably and to five with growing accuracy.
- Says the last number as the answer to how many.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: number words and questions; Social Habits: helping a friend; Physical Development: moving objects carefully

## Day 1 (Monday): Count without missing any

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Counting without missing any

### Learning objectives
- Children will count up to five objects by moving each one from a "to count" tray to a "done" tray.

### Success criteria
- Moves each object once
- Says the numbers in order

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three reliably and to five with support, and add one more to a small group.

**Key vocabulary:** count, again, check, how many, last

**Instructional materials:** Five blocks; Two trays labelled to count and done; A mat

### Starter
Spread five blocks on the mat and say "Let me count them. I must not miss any!" Count while pointing quickly and miss one. Say "Oh no, I missed one!"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the "to count" tray and the "done" tray. Move one block at a time, saying the number. Children: Children watch and say the numbers with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "When all the blocks are in the done tray, the last number tells how many." Children: Children say "five" when the last block is moved.

Step 3. Teacher: Let a child move the blocks while the group counts. Children: Child moves each block and the group counts.

Step 4. Teacher: Try with three blocks and then four. Children: Children count each group carefully.

### Practical activity: To count and done
1. Give each pair two trays and up to five blocks.
2. Move each block from one tray to the other while saying the numbers.
3. Say the last number as the answer.

**Safety:** Use blocks larger than a child’s fist; keep trays on the floor; supervise group play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count three blocks and move each one hand-over-hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count five blocks without the trays by pointing.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many blocks did you count?
  **Expected response:** The correct number, up to five.
- **Ask:** Did you touch each one?
  **Expected response:** Child nods.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts without skipping objects
- Says the last number as the answer

### Conclusion
Put all the blocks back and say "Careful counting, well done."

### Home connection
Count spoons or shoes at home by moving each one aside as you count.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count up to five objects by moving each one from a "to count" tray to a "done" tray" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- To count
- Done

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two trays and blocks

**Song or rhyme:** Touch one, move one, say the number. Touch two, move two, count with me.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Teddy makes a mistake

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Checking our counting

### Learning objectives
- Children will help Teddy find and fix counting mistakes.

### Success criteria
- Spots a counting mistake
- Helps to count again correctly

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three reliably and to five with support, and add one more to a small group.

**Key vocabulary:** count, again, check, how many, last

**Instructional materials:** A teddy puppet; Five toy fruits; A tray

### Starter
Hold the teddy puppet and say "Teddy is learning to count but he makes mistakes. Can you help him?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Teddy counts four fruits but says "one, two, four". Ask "Is that right?" Children: Children shake their heads and say "no".

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "What number did Teddy forget?" and count again together. Children: Children say "three" and count correctly.

Step 3. Teacher: Teddy touches one fruit twice. Ask children to spot the mistake. Children: Children point to the fruit counted twice.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child be the teacher and show Teddy how to count. Children: Child counts and the group checks.

### Practical activity: Help Teddy count
1. Give each pair a teddy and a tray of fruits.
2. One child makes the teddy count with a mistake.
3. The partner spots and fixes it.

**Safety:** Use toy fruits larger than a fist; keep the tray on the table; supervise play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Let the teddy make only one mistake, such as forgetting a number.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give the teddy a hint to count correctly.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Did Teddy count right?
  **Expected response:** No, he missed one.
- **Ask:** How can we help Teddy?
  **Expected response:** Count again slowly and touch each one.

### Expected outcomes
- Spots and fixes counting mistakes
- Counts again to check

### Conclusion
Thank Teddy for learning and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Count a set of objects at home and check by counting again.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "help Teddy find and fix counting mistakes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Check by counting again

**Picture suggestions:**
- A teddy puppet counting fruits

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Scattered or in a line?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** The last number tells how many

### Learning objectives
- Children will count the same objects when they are scattered and when they are in a line and see the number stays the same.

### Success criteria
- Counts scattered objects
- Says the same number for a line

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three reliably and to five with support, and add one more to a small group.

**Key vocabulary:** count, again, check, how many, last

**Instructional materials:** Five blocks; A mat; Two trays

### Starter
Spread five blocks randomly on the mat and ask "How many?" Count them together. Then line them up and ask "How many now? Did the number change?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count the blocks while they are scattered. Say "Five." Children: Children touch and count to five.

Step 2. Teacher: Line them up and count again. Say "Still five." Children: Children count the line and say "five".

Step 3. Teacher: Spread them out wide and count again. Ask "Is it still five?" Children: Children count and say "still five".

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child spread and line up the blocks and count them. Children: Child rearranges the blocks and counts to five.

### Practical activity: Scatter and line
1. Give each child five blocks.
2. Scatter them, count, line them up, count again.
3. Compare the two counts.

**Safety:** Use large blocks; keep them on the mat; supervise.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use three blocks and count them together before and after.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count seven objects with help and say how many.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many blocks?
  **Expected response:** Five, in both arrangements.
- **Ask:** Did the number change?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts scattered and lined-up objects
- Knows the number stays the same

### Conclusion
Put the blocks in the basket together saying the numbers.

### Home connection
Count the same objects in different arrangements and see if the number changes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count the same objects when they are scattered and when they are in a line and see the number stays the same" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Still five

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Count the class

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** The last number tells how many

### Learning objectives
- Children will count friends, chairs and shoes in the room.

### Success criteria
- Counts people or objects in the room
- Says how many

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three reliably and to five with support, and add one more to a small group.

**Key vocabulary:** count, again, check, how many, last

**Instructional materials:** Chairs; A mat; A clipboard or chart

### Starter
Say "Today we count real things in our classroom. How many chairs? How many friends are sitting?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count the chairs together and move each one to the side as you count. Children: Children count each chair.

Step 2. Teacher: Count the children sitting in a circle by tapping each shoulder. Children: Children count each friend.

Step 3. Teacher: Count the shoes in a pile by pairing them. Children: Children count the shoes and say how many.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Are there more chairs or more children?" Children: Children compare and say "more".

### Practical activity: Classroom counting
1. Count chairs, friends and shoes in the room.
2. Say each number aloud.
3. Compare which group is more.

**Safety:** Keep walkways clear; children count calmly; no pushing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count only up to three with an adult.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count items outdoors, such as leaves or stones too large to swallow.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many chairs?
  **Expected response:** The number of chairs, up to five.
- **Ask:** Are there more chairs or friends?
  **Expected response:** The correct answer by comparison.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts real things in the room
- Compares counts

### Conclusion
Count the whole class together and clap.

### Home connection
Count people at the table or shoes at the door.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count friends, chairs and shoes in the room" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Careful counting stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Checking our counting

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can count up to five carefully at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Counts up to five without skipping
- Says how many

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three reliably and to five with support, and add one more to a small group.

**Key vocabulary:** count, again, check, how many, last

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: to count and done trays; Station 2: Teddy mistakes; Station 3: scatter and line; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the counting song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "How many?" and "Can you check?" Children: Child counts and checks.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use large items only; supervise stations; keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to count objects for a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: counts to three reliably and to five with growing accuracy; says how many with the last number; checks by counting again.

- **Ask:** How many are there?
  **Expected response:** The correct number up to five.
- **Ask:** Can you count again to check?
  **Expected response:** Child recounts and gets the same.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Counts carefully to five

### Conclusion
Sing the counting song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Count things carefully at home this week.
