# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 5: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W05 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Sharing one each; Giving out to friends; Enough or not enough

## Learning objectives
- Children will give one object to each friend.
- Children will show when there is not enough with support.

**Key vocabulary:** one each, share, enough, friend, give

**Content:** One-to-one correspondence in sharing: giving one object to each friend and noticing when there is not enough.

**Teaching activities:** Model "one for you, one for me" slowly; use real friends and toys; ask "Does everyone have one?" and let children check.

**Learner activities:** Hand out one object to each friend, check that everyone has one, and notice who has none.

**Resources:** Toy biscuits and plates; Cups and a tray; Soft toys as friends; A basket of toy fruits; Name cards or pictures

**Assessment:** Check whether each child gives one object to each of up to four friends and notices when someone has none.

## Expected outcomes
- Hands out one object to each of up to four friends.
- Notices when someone has none.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Social Habits: sharing and fairness; Literacy: sentence "one for you"; Understanding the world: family meals

## Day 1 (Monday): One for you, one for me

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Sharing one each

### Learning objectives
- Children will give one object to a friend and keep one for themselves.

### Success criteria
- Gives one object to a friend
- Says "one for you" or "one for me"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can give one to a friend, count to five with support and use more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one each, share, enough, friend, give

**Instructional materials:** Toy biscuits; Two plates; A soft toy

### Starter
Hold two biscuits and say "One for you, one for me!" Give one to the soft toy and keep one. Say "We both have one."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Share four biscuits between yourself and the soft toy, saying "one for you, one for me" each time. Children: Children watch and say the sentence with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Do we both have the same?" and count the biscuits on each plate. Children: Children count and say "same".

Step 3. Teacher: Invite a child to share with a friend using two biscuits. Children: Child gives one biscuit to the friend and keeps one.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap partners and repeat. Children: Each child shares with a new partner.

### Practical activity: Sharing pairs
1. Give each pair a plate with two toy biscuits.
2. Share one each and say the sentence.
3. Swap roles.

**Safety:** Use toy biscuits larger than a child’s fist. If using real food, wash hands and check allergies first.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two biscuits and model the sharing hand-over-hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to share six biscuits between two friends.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who has one?
  **Expected response:** Both friends have one.
- **Ask:** What do we say when we share?
  **Expected response:** One for you, one for me.

### Expected outcomes
- Gives one to a friend
- Uses the sharing sentence

### Conclusion
Hold up the plates and say "One for you, one for me. We share."

### Home connection
Share a snack with a sibling using one for you and one for me.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "give one object to a friend and keep one for themselves" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- One for you
- One for me

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two children sharing biscuits

**Song or rhyme:** One for you, one for me, one for you, one for me. Sharing is kind, sharing is fun.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Everyone has one

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Giving out to friends

### Learning objectives
- Children will give out one cup to each friend at a table.

### Success criteria
- Gives one cup to each friend
- Checks that everyone has one

**Previous knowledge:** Children can give one to a friend, count to five with support and use more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one each, share, enough, friend, give

**Instructional materials:** Four cups; Four soft toys seated around a mat; A tray

### Starter
Seat four soft toys around the mat. Say "The toys are thirsty. Each toy needs one cup. Let us give out the cups."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give one cup to the first toy and say "one cup for Teddy". Children: Children say "one" and watch.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask a child to give a cup to the next toy. Continue around the circle. Children: Child gives one cup to each toy.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Does every toy have one cup?" and check each toy. Children: Children point to each toy and say "yes" or "no".

Step 4. Teacher: Remove one cup and ask "Who has no cup?" Children: Children point to the toy without a cup.

### Practical activity: Cups for the toys
1. Seat the toys in a circle.
2. Give one cup to each toy.
3. Check that every toy has one.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable plastic cups; children walk carefully around the circle.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two toys and two cups.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give out cups to five toys.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does every toy have a cup?
  **Expected response:** Yes when every toy has one.
- **Ask:** Who has no cup?
  **Expected response:** The toy without one.

### Expected outcomes
- Gives one to each friend
- Notices who has none

### Conclusion
Collect the cups one by one and say "Thank you, toys."

### Home connection
When setting the table, give one plate and one cup to each person.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "give out one cup to each friend at a table" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- One cup each

**Picture suggestions:**
- Four toys with four cups

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Enough or not enough?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Enough or not enough

### Learning objectives
- Children will check whether there is enough for everyone and say when there is not enough.

### Success criteria
- Notices when someone has none
- Says "not enough" or points

**Previous knowledge:** Children can give one to a friend, count to five with support and use more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one each, share, enough, friend, give

**Instructional materials:** Five toy fruits; Four soft toys or children as guests; A basket

### Starter
Show a basket with only three fruits and four toys. Say "Oh no! There are four toys but only three fruits. Is there enough?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give one fruit to each toy and stop when the fruit runs out. Say "Teddy has none." Children: Children point to the toy with no fruit.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "How can we fix it?" and add one more fruit. Children: Children say "more" or give the extra fruit.

Step 3. Teacher: Try with different numbers of fruits and toys. Children: Children give one fruit to each toy and check.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Enough!" when each toy has one. Children: Children say "enough" and clap.

### Practical activity: Enough for everyone
1. Give each group four toys and a basket of fruits.
2. Give one fruit to each toy.
3. Say if there is enough or if more are needed.

**Safety:** Use large toy fruits; keep baskets on the table; supervise group play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two toys and two fruits, and point out when each has one.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count how many more fruits are needed.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does everyone have a fruit?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no, with a gesture.
- **Ask:** What do we need?
  **Expected response:** One more fruit.

### Expected outcomes
- Notices when there is not enough
- Asks for or gives one more

### Conclusion
Put the extra fruit in the basket and say "Enough for everyone."

### Home connection
Count whether there is enough chairs for everyone at the table.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "check whether there is enough for everyone and say when there is not enough" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Enough
- Not enough

**Picture suggestions:**
- Four toys and three fruits

## Day 4 (Thursday): Party for friends

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Sharing one each

### Learning objectives
- Children will plan a pretend party and share one item with each guest.

### Success criteria
- Gives one item to each guest
- Says "one for you"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can give one to a friend, count to five with support and use more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one each, share, enough, friend, give

**Instructional materials:** Soft toys as guests; Plates, cups and toy biscuits; A party cloth

### Starter
Spread a party cloth. Say "We are having a party for our toy friends! Let us make sure every friend gets one plate, one cup and one biscuit."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Place a plate in front of each toy and say "one plate each". Children: Children place one plate for each guest.

Step 2. Teacher: Place a cup on each plate and say "one cup each". Children: Children place one cup on each plate.

Step 3. Teacher: Give one biscuit to each guest and say "one biscuit each". Children: Children give out the biscuits and say "one for you".

Step 4. Teacher: Sing a party song and clap as each guest is served. Children: Children clap and sing.

### Practical activity: Toy party
1. Seat soft toys around the cloth.
2. Give each toy a plate, a cup and a biscuit.
3. Say "one for you".

**Safety:** Use toy food and unbreakable plates; children walk carefully; wash hands after play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Serve only two guests with one item each.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give out items to five guests and count them.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many plates for four guests?
  **Expected response:** Four.
- **Ask:** Who has a biscuit?
  **Expected response:** Every guest has one.

### Expected outcomes
- Shares one item with each guest
- Uses one-to-one in role play

### Conclusion
Say "Thank you" to each guest and clear the party cloth together.

### Home connection
Help to set the table for a family meal with one item for each person.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "plan a pretend party and share one item with each guest" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- One plate, one cup, one biscuit

**Picture suggestions:**
- A toy party table

## Day 5 (Friday): Sharing stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Giving out to friends

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can share one each at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Gives one to each friend
- Notices who has none

**Previous knowledge:** Children can give one to a friend, count to five with support and use more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one each, share, enough, friend, give

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: biscuits and plates; Station 2: cups for toys; Station 3: enough or not enough; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the sharing song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Does everyone have one?" and "Who needs more?" Children: Child checks and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use toy items only; supervise stations; keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to show a friend how to share fairly.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: gives one to each of up to four friends; checks that everyone has one; notices when there is not enough.

- **Ask:** Does everyone have one?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no, with a gesture.
- **Ask:** Can you share these with a friend?
  **Expected response:** Child shares one each.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Shares one each with growing accuracy

### Conclusion
Sing the sharing song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Practise one for you, one for me at mealtimes.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
