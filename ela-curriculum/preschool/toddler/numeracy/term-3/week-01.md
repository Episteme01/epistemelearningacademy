# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 1: Measuring

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Full and empty containers; Pouring water and sand; Nearly full, half full

## Learning objectives
- Children will say whether a container is full or empty.
- Children will pour to fill a container with support.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, pour, fill, water, half

**Content:** Comparing containers that are full, empty and nearly full, and pouring water or sand to fill them.

**Teaching activities:** Pour slowly so children can see the level rise; use the words full and empty each time; let children pour with a jug while you hold the container.

**Learner activities:** Fill and empty cups and bowls, say full or empty, pour carefully and notice half full.

**Resources:** Plastic cups, bowls and jugs; A tray or large basin; Water or dry sand; Towels and a sponge; Two clear plastic bottles

**Assessment:** Check whether each child says full or empty correctly and can fill a cup with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Identifies full and empty containers.
- Fills a cup and says full.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: water and materials; Physical Development: pouring and carrying; Social Habits: cleaning up spills

## Day 1 (Monday): Full cup, empty cup

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Full and empty containers

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to a full cup and an empty cup when asked.

### Success criteria
- Points to the full container on request
- Points to the empty container on request

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small, long and short, heavy and light, and count to five with support.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, pour, fill, water, half

**Instructional materials:** Two clear plastic cups, one full of water and one empty; A tray; A towel

### Starter
Hold up a cup full of water and say "Full! This cup is full of water." Turn the empty cup upside down and say "Empty! Nothing inside."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the full and empty cups side by side on the tray. Say "This one is full. This one is empty." Tilt each so children can see. Children: Children look closely and point to each cup when named.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Where is the full cup?" Then "Where is the empty cup?" Swap the sides and ask again. Children: Children point to the correct cup each time.

Step 3. Teacher: Pretend to drink from the full cup and say "Glug, glug, all gone. Now it is empty." Children: Children copy the drinking action and say "empty".

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child look into the cups and choose one for you to carry to the table. Children: Child chooses a cup and says "full" or "empty".

### Practical activity: Full or empty?
1. Place four clear cups on a tray, some full of water and some empty.
2. Each child points to the full cups and the empty cups.
3. Move the full cups to one side of the tray and the empty cups to the other.

**Safety:** Use shatterproof plastic cups, water in a shallow tray, and a towel ready for spills. Wipe spills at once so no one slips.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Compare one very full cup and one empty cup and say the word with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a container in the room that is full or empty and tell a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which cup is full?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the cup with water.
- **Ask:** Is this cup empty?
  **Expected response:** Yes if nothing is inside.

### Expected outcomes
- Identifies full and empty containers
- Says full or empty with support

### Conclusion
Tip the water into the basin and say "Empty, all gone." Dry the cups with a towel.

### Home connection
At bath time, show your child a full cup and an empty cup and say full and empty.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to a full cup and an empty cup when asked" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FULL (a full cup)
- EMPTY (an empty cup)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A full cup and an empty cup
- A child looking into a cup

**Song or rhyme:** Full, full, full of water. Empty, empty, nothing inside.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Pouring and filling

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Pouring water and sand

### Learning objectives
- Children will pour water or sand into a cup until it is full.

### Success criteria
- Pours from a jug into a cup
- Says full when the cup is full

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small, long and short, heavy and light, and count to five with support.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, pour, fill, water, half

**Instructional materials:** A shallow basin of water or sand; Small jugs and cups; Towels; A sponge

### Starter
Pour a little water into a cup and say "Not full yet." Pour more and say "Nearly full." Pour to the top and say "Full!" Invite the children to try.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pour water slowly into a clear cup from a small jug. Stop at nearly full and say "Nearly full." Children: Children watch the water rise and say "more" or "stop".

Step 2. Teacher: Stop at full and say "Full!" Tip the cup out into the basin. Children: Children say "full" and watch the cup empty.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pour a cup from the jug while you hold the cup steady. Children: Child pours until the cup is full and says "full".

Step 4. Teacher: Compare a big cup and a small cup: ask "Which holds more water?" Children: Children point to the bigger cup and say "more".

### Practical activity: Water play basin
1. Set up a basin with cups and jugs on a waterproof mat.
2. Children fill and empty cups for several minutes.
3. Name each cup as full, nearly full or empty.

**Safety:** Keep water shallow and always supervise water play. Use a waterproof apron, wipe spills at once, and wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a small cup and hold the jug together with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to fill two cups to the top and say which is the bigger cup.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the cup full yet?
  **Expected response:** Yes or not yet, with a word or gesture.
- **Ask:** Which cup holds more?
  **Expected response:** The larger cup.

### Expected outcomes
- Fills a cup to the top with support
- Says full, nearly full or empty

### Conclusion
Pour all the water back into the basin and say "Empty. All done."

### Home connection
Let your child fill a cup with water from a bucket outside and say when it is full.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "pour water or sand into a cup until it is full" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Full
- Nearly full
- Empty

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Sand scoop

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Pouring water and sand

### Learning objectives
- Children will scoop sand into a bowl and say when it is full or empty.

### Success criteria
- Scoops sand into a container
- Says full or empty

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small, long and short, heavy and light, and count to five with support.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, pour, fill, water, half

**Instructional materials:** A tray of dry sand; Scoops or cups; Two bowls, one big and one small; A brush

### Starter
Scoop sand into a small bowl and say "How many scoops to fill this bowl?" Count each scoop together.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Scoop sand into the small bowl while counting each scoop. Say "Full!" when it is full. Children: Children count the scoops and say "full".

Step 2. Teacher: Pour the sand back and try the big bowl. Count the scoops again. Children: Children count again and say "more scoops".

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child fill their own small bowl and tell a friend "full". Children: Child fills a bowl and says "full".

Step 4. Teacher: Tip out the sand and say "empty". Brush the tray clean. Children: Children say "empty" and help to brush.

### Practical activity: Scoop and fill
1. Give each child a bowl and a scoop.
2. Count scoops until the bowl is full.
3. Compare with a friend’s bowl.

**Safety:** Use clean, dry sand and tell children not to throw it or put it in their mouths. Wash hands afterwards and wipe the floor.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a small bowl and scoop together hand-over-hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess how many scoops will fill the bowl and then check.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the bowl full?
  **Expected response:** Yes when it reaches the top.
- **Ask:** How many scoops did we use?
  **Expected response:** The number counted, up to five.

### Expected outcomes
- Fills a bowl with sand
- Uses full and empty with scoops

### Conclusion
Brush the sand into the tray together and say "Empty trays, clean hands."

### Home connection
Scoop rice or beans into a bowl at home with a grown-up and say full or empty.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "scoop sand into a bowl and say when it is full or empty" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Scoop 1, 2, 3

**Picture suggestions:**
- A bowl full of sand

## Day 4 (Thursday): Half full and nearly full

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Nearly full, half full

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice a cup that is half full and nearly full.

### Success criteria
- Points to a half-full cup
- Says "half" or "nearly full" with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small, long and short, heavy and light, and count to five with support.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, pour, fill, water, half

**Instructional materials:** Clear cups with water at different levels; A tray; A towel

### Starter
Hold up three cups: one full, one half full and one empty. Say "These cups have different amounts of water. Let us look carefully."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the three cups in a row. Point to each and say "full, half full, empty." Children: Children touch each cup and repeat the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which cup is nearly full?" and pour a cup to just below the top. Children: Children point to the nearly full cup.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pour water to a line you have marked on the cup. Children: Child pours up to the mark and says "half".

Step 4. Teacher: Put the cups in order from empty to full. Children: Children help to line up the cups from empty to full.

### Practical activity: Cups in a row
1. Draw a line on clear cups with a marker.
2. Children pour water to the line.
3. Put the cups in order from empty to full.

**Safety:** Use shatterproof plastic cups; keep trays on a waterproof mat; wipe spills at once.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Compare only full and empty cups until the child is ready for half.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to fill a cup halfway without a mark and check with a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which cup is half full?
  **Expected response:** The cup with water at the middle.
- **Ask:** Can you make this cup nearly full?
  **Expected response:** Child pours up to near the top.

### Expected outcomes
- Recognises half full and nearly full
- Orders cups by how full they are

### Conclusion
Pour the water out and say "From full to empty." Dry the cups.

### Home connection
At mealtimes, notice cups that are full, half full or empty.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice a cup that is half full and nearly full" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Empty
- Half full
- Full

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three cups with different water levels

## Day 5 (Friday): Full and empty stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Full and empty containers

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can recognise and make full and empty containers at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Identifies full and empty containers
- Fills a cup with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small, long and short, heavy and light, and count to five with support.

**Key vocabulary:** full, empty, pour, fill, water, half

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: full or empty cups; Station 2: water pouring basin; Station 3: sand scoop; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the full and empty song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Divide the children into three groups and rotate every four minutes. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: At the cup station ask "Which is full? Which is empty?" Children: Child points to the full and empty cups.

Step 3. Teacher: At the pouring station ask "Can you fill the cup?" Children: Child fills the cup and says "full".

Step 4. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy their station at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations on waterproof mats.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes one station per round.

**Safety:** Supervise water and sand stations at all times; wipe spills immediately; wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner at each station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to teach a friend how to fill a cup.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: identifies full and empty; fills a container with support; uses the words full, half and empty.

- **Ask:** Is the cup full or empty?
  **Expected response:** The correct word.
- **Ask:** Can you fill it?
  **Expected response:** Child fills the cup.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Compares full and empty containers

### Conclusion
Sing the song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Notice full and empty containers at home this week.

- Which children could show "show they can recognise and make full and empty containers at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Full, empty

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
