# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Measuring**: Full and empty containers; Pouring water and sand; Nearly full, half full | Children will say whether a container is full or empty. Children will pour to fill a container with support. | Comparing containers that are full, empty and nearly full, and pouring water or sand to fill them. | Pour slowly so children can see the level rise; use the words full and empty each time; let children pour with a jug while you hold the container. | Fill and empty cups and bowls, say full or empty, pour carefully and notice half full. | Plastic cups, bowls and jugs; A tray or large basin; Water or dry sand; Towels and a sponge; Two clear plastic bottles | Check whether each child says full or empty correctly and can fill a cup with support. | Identifies full and empty containers. Fills a cup and says full. | Science: water and materials; Physical Development: pouring and carrying; Social Habits: cleaning up spills |
| 2 | **Time**: Morning and night; Afternoon; What comes first and next in our day | Children will tell morning from night using pictures and routines. Children will say what happens first and next in the school day with support. | Telling morning, afternoon and night apart using familiar routines and pictures, and saying what happens first and next. | Link each time word to a clear daily event; use picture cards in order; refer to the routine each day using the time words. | Sort day and night pictures, order the school day with picture cards, act out routines and sing a day song. | Picture cards: waking up, eating breakfast, school, lunch, nap, dinner, bedtime; A sun and moon picture; A soft toy; A drum | Check whether children sort day and night pictures and name one thing that happens in the morning. | Sorts day and night pictures correctly. Names one activity that happens in the morning. | Literacy: sequencing and time words; Social Habits: routines and hygiene; Science: day and night |
| 3 | **Shape**: Circle, square, triangle revision; Shape hunt indoors and outdoors; Building with shapes | Children will find and name a circle, a square and a triangle in the environment. Children will build a simple picture using shapes with support. | Revising circle, square and triangle, finding shapes indoors and outdoors and building simple pictures with shapes. | Ask children to describe the shape using touch and simple talk; use the shape names every time; leave shape cards on show for the week. | Hunt for shapes, match shape cards to real objects, build pictures and say which shape they see. | Large cardboard shapes; Shape cards; Blocks of different shapes; A hoop or tape; Paper and glue sticks | Check whether each child names or points to circle, square and triangle with growing accuracy. | Names or points to the three shapes with growing accuracy. Makes a shape picture. | Literacy: shape words and describing; Physical Development: building and carrying; Understanding the world: shapes in buildings |
| 4 | **Position**: Up and down; In front and behind; Position games and obstacle courses | Children will follow instructions using up, down, in front and behind. Children will describe where they are using one position word. | Understanding and using up, down, in front and behind in movement games, hide and find and obstacle courses. | Say the position word as the child moves; use landmarks such as a chair or a tree; keep instructions to one at a time and then two. | Move up and down, stand in front and behind objects and friends, follow instructions on an obstacle course and say where they are. | A low bench or step; A chair, a box and a soft toy; Cones and a tunnel; A ball | Check whether each child follows position instructions and says where an object or friend is. | Moves to a position on request. Says where an object is using one word. | Physical Development: balance and movement; Literacy: prepositions in talk; Social Habits: waiting and taking turns in a line |
| 5 | **Counting**: Sharing one each; Giving out to friends; Enough or not enough | Children will give one object to each friend. Children will show when there is not enough with support. | One-to-one correspondence in sharing: giving one object to each friend and noticing when there is not enough. | Model "one for you, one for me" slowly; use real friends and toys; ask "Does everyone have one?" and let children check. | Hand out one object to each friend, check that everyone has one, and notice who has none. | Toy biscuits and plates; Cups and a tray; Soft toys as friends; A basket of toy fruits; Name cards or pictures | Check whether each child gives one object to each of up to four friends and notices when someone has none. | Hands out one object to each of up to four friends. Notices when someone has none. | Social Habits: sharing and fairness; Literacy: sentence "one for you"; Understanding the world: family meals |
| 6 | **Counting**: Adding one more; One more game with fingers; One more in stories and songs | Children will add one more object to a small group. Children will say or show how many there are now, with support. | Adding one more object to a small group and saying how many there are now, using objects, fingers and stories. | Count the group first, add one object slowly, say "one more", and recount from one; keep groups to one to four. | Add one more to a group, recount, show the new number on fingers and act out one more in songs and stories. | Large blocks; Toy fruits; Cups; Soft toys; A basket and a cloth | Check whether each child adds one more to a group of one to three and says or shows how many now. | Adds one more to a group of one to three. Shows how many now with fingers. | Literacy: sentences with one more; Social Habits: asking politely for more; Physical Development: handling objects |
| 7 | **Counting**: Counting without missing any; The last number tells how many; Checking our counting | Children will count up to five objects without skipping any. Children will check a count by counting again. | Counting up to five objects without skipping or repeating any, answering how many with the last number and checking the count. | Show how to move counted objects to a "done" tray; model a mistake and fix it; praise careful touching rather than speed. | Count scattered and lined-up objects, check by counting again, find mistakes and say how many. | Large blocks; Toy fruits; Two trays; A cloth; A teddy puppet who makes counting mistakes | Check one-to-one touching, order of number words and whether the child says how many with the last number. | Counts to three reliably and to five with growing accuracy. Says the last number as the answer to how many. | Literacy: number words and questions; Social Habits: helping a friend; Physical Development: moving objects carefully |
| 8 | **Data**: Choosing and standing in lines; Which line has more?; Counting a line | Children will sort themselves or objects into groups by a simple choice. Children will say which group has more. | Sorting children or objects by a simple choice, forming lines and saying which line has more. | Make real graphs with children and objects; line up neatly at one end; ask "Which line has more?" and count to check. | Choose between two things, stand or place objects in lines, compare lines and count a line. | Two baskets; Picture cards of two favourite fruits; Large blocks; A string line on the floor; Stickers or counters (large) | Check whether each child joins a group according to a choice and says which group has more. | Joins a group according to a choice. Points to the group with more. | Social Habits: choosing and respecting differences; Literacy: asking and answering questions; Science: sorting and recording |
| 9 | **Applying number**: Setting up our market; Buying and selling up to five; Counting change in sharing games | Children will count out up to three or five items in a play market. Children will use number, more and one each words in role play. | Using number, more, same and sharing words in a pretend market, counting out up to five items for customers. | Take the role of a customer; ask for a number of items; model polite words; ask "How many?" and let children check. | Set up a stall, count out items for customers, say please and thank you, and compare baskets. | A table or cloth for the stall; Toy fruits and vegetables; Baskets and bags; Number cards 1 to 5; A toy cash tin with large play coins | Check whether children count out the number asked for and use number words naturally in role play. | Counts out items on request in play. Uses number words naturally in role play. | Social Habits: politeness and cooperation; Literacy: asking and answering; Understanding the world: markets and community helpers |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising number, measures and shapes; Revising position and time; Celebration and end-of-year record | Children will show Year learning through games and stations. Teachers will complete every child’s end-of-year record and transition note. | Revising the year’s numeracy learning through games and stations, celebrating achievements and completing each child’s end-of-year record. | Keep the week relaxed and celebratory; observe quietly; record evidence; prepare a transition note for the next class. | Play favourite maths games, show what they know, celebrate with friends and share their learning with families. | Materials from the year; Class record sheets; A camera or phone; Stickers or small certificates; A celebration cloth and music | Use the three term records and this week’s evidence to complete the end-of-year record for every strand: sorting and matching, comparing and measuring, number and counting, shape and space, pattern and time, and mathematical language. Record Secure only with evidence from two or more occasions. | Takes part confidently in familiar maths games. Each child has an end-of-year observation record. | Literacy: songs and rhymes from the year; Social Habits: celebrating together and thanking friends; Expressive arts: music and dance |
