# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 4: Position

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W04 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Up and down; In front and behind; Position games and obstacle courses

## Learning objectives
- Children will follow instructions using up, down, in front and behind.
- Children will describe where they are using one position word.

**Key vocabulary:** up, down, in front, behind, next to

**Content:** Understanding and using up, down, in front and behind in movement games, hide and find and obstacle courses.

**Teaching activities:** Say the position word as the child moves; use landmarks such as a chair or a tree; keep instructions to one at a time and then two.

**Learner activities:** Move up and down, stand in front and behind objects and friends, follow instructions on an obstacle course and say where they are.

**Resources:** A low bench or step; A chair, a box and a soft toy; Cones and a tunnel; A ball

**Assessment:** Check whether each child follows position instructions and says where an object or friend is.

## Expected outcomes
- Moves to a position on request.
- Says where an object is using one word.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Physical Development: balance and movement; Literacy: prepositions in talk; Social Habits: waiting and taking turns in a line

## Day 1 (Monday): Up and down

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Up and down

### Learning objectives
- Children will move their bodies and objects up and down on request.

### Success criteria
- Raises and lowers an object when asked
- Says up or down

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use in, on and under and follow simple one-step instructions in games.

**Key vocabulary:** up, down, in front, behind, next to

**Instructional materials:** A ball; A balloon or soft scarf; A low step or bench

### Starter
Raise your arms and say "Up!" Lower them and say "Down!" Hold a scarf high and let it float down. Say "Down it comes."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Do the actions: arms up, arms down, stand up, sit down. Say the words as you move. Children: Children copy the actions and say "up" and "down".

Step 2. Teacher: Hold a ball high and say "Up". Lower it and say "Down". Let children try. Children: Children raise and lower the ball when asked.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child step up onto a low bench with help and step down. Children: Child steps up and down, saying the words.

Step 4. Teacher: Play "Up and down" with a scarf: toss it up and watch it float down. Children: Children toss the scarf up and say "down" as it lands.

### Practical activity: Up and down game
1. Stand with space between children.
2. Call up or down and children move their bodies or objects.
3. Change the speed of the calls.

**Safety:** Use a low, stable bench with an adult beside each child; keep the floor clear; no jumping from the bench.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move together with an adult and use one word at a time.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give "up" and "down" instructions to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you put your hands up?
  **Expected response:** Child raises their hands.
- **Ask:** Where is the ball now?
  **Expected response:** Up or down, depending on where it is.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows up and down instructions
- Says up and down

### Conclusion
Lower arms slowly and say "Down, down, sit down."

### Home connection
Play "up and down" with a toy or a scarf at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "move their bodies and objects up and down on request" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- UP (arrow pointing up)
- DOWN (arrow pointing down)

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children with arms up
- A scarf floating down

**Song or rhyme:** Up, up, up, we stretch so high. Down, down, down, we sit on the ground.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): In front and behind

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** In front and behind

### Learning objectives
- Children will stand in front of and behind a chair or a friend when asked.

### Success criteria
- Moves in front of or behind an object
- Says in front or behind with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use in, on and under and follow simple one-step instructions in games.

**Key vocabulary:** up, down, in front, behind, next to

**Instructional materials:** A chair; A soft toy; A line of cones

### Starter
Put a soft toy on the floor in front of a chair and say "Teddy is in front of the chair." Move it behind and say "Teddy is behind the chair."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Stand in front of the chair and say "I am in front." Walk behind it and say "I am behind." Children: Children watch and copy the movements.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask each child to stand in front of the chair, then behind it. Children: Child moves as instructed.

Step 3. Teacher: Place the soft toy in front, behind and say "Where is Teddy?" Children: Children say "in front" or "behind".

Step 4. Teacher: Line up in a queue and say "Who is in front? Who is behind?" Children: Children point to the child in front or behind them.

### Practical activity: Chair game
1. Place a chair in the middle.
2. Children take turns to stand in front of or behind it.
3. Say where they are.

**Safety:** Use a stable chair; children walk calmly; keep the area clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a large object and an adult to guide the movement.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put two toys in front and behind and describe.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is Teddy?
  **Expected response:** In front or behind.
- **Ask:** Can you go behind the chair?
  **Expected response:** Child goes behind the chair.

### Expected outcomes
- Understands in front and behind
- Uses the words with support

### Conclusion
Queue up and say "Who is in front?" before moving to the next activity.

### Home connection
Use in front and behind when lining up shoes or toys.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "stand in front of and behind a chair or a friend when asked" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- IN FRONT
- BEHIND

**Picture suggestions:**
- A toy in front of and behind a chair

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Obstacle course words

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Position games and obstacle courses

### Learning objectives
- Children will follow position instructions on a simple obstacle course.

### Success criteria
- Completes the course following the instructions
- Uses a position word

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use in, on and under and follow simple one-step instructions in games.

**Key vocabulary:** up, down, in front, behind, next to

**Instructional materials:** A tunnel, a low bench, cones, a mat; A soft toy to carry

### Starter
Show the obstacle course. Say "Today we crawl through the tunnel, walk around the cone and step up on the bench."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Walk the course once yourself and say each position word: "through", "around", "up", "down". Children: Children watch and say the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child try the course with an adult walking beside them. Children: Child follows the course and hears the words.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask the child to say what they are doing: "I am going up." Children: Child says a position word.

Step 4. Teacher: Change the course by moving a cone in front or behind and ask "Where is the cone?" Children: Children say where the cone is.

### Practical activity: Position course
1. Set up a tunnel, cones and a bench in a line.
2. Each child goes through the course.
3. They say one position word.

**Safety:** Use low, stable equipment; crawl only; an adult stays at each obstacle; no running.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Take one obstacle at a time with an adult.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to design and explain a course for a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where did you go?
  **Expected response:** Through the tunnel, up the bench.
- **Ask:** Is the cone in front or behind you?
  **Expected response:** In front or behind.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows position instructions on a course
- Uses position words

### Conclusion
Walk the course slowly together one more time and say each word.

### Home connection
Make a small obstacle course with cushions and a chair at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "follow position instructions on a simple obstacle course" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Through, around, up, down

**Picture suggestions:**
- An obstacle course

## Day 4 (Thursday): Follow the leader positions

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Position games and obstacle courses

### Learning objectives
- Children will give and follow position instructions in a game.

### Success criteria
- Follows a position instruction
- Gives a position instruction

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use in, on and under and follow simple one-step instructions in games.

**Key vocabulary:** up, down, in front, behind, next to

**Instructional materials:** A drum; Soft toys; A chair and a box

### Starter
Say "Let us play follow the leader! I say where to go and you do it." Stand behind the chair and call "Behind!"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Call an instruction: "Stand behind the chair." "Put the toy in front of the box." Children: Children follow the instruction.

Step 2. Teacher: Choose a leader. Let the leader call "Up" or "Down" or "In front". Children: Leader calls an instruction and the group follows.

Step 3. Teacher: Change leaders every minute so everyone has a turn. Children: Each child leads one instruction.

Step 4. Teacher: Finish with a calm game: "Sit down, stand up, sit down." Children: Children follow the slow instructions.

### Practical activity: Position follow the leader
1. Stand in a circle.
2. The leader gives one instruction at a time.
3. Swap leaders after each round.

**Safety:** Give children space; keep movements gentle; stop if the game becomes too boisterous.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Pair the child with an adult leader and use one word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give two instructions in a row.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is the toy?
  **Expected response:** In front of or behind something.
- **Ask:** What did the leader say?
  **Expected response:** The position word.

### Expected outcomes
- Gives and follows position instructions
- Takes turns as a leader

### Conclusion
Slow the game down and finish with "Sit down together."

### Home connection
Play follow the leader at home using up, down, in front and behind.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "give and follow position instructions in a game" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Up, down, in front, behind

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Position stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Up and down

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they understand up, down, in front and behind at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Follows position instructions
- Says a position word

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use in, on and under and follow simple one-step instructions in games.

**Key vocabulary:** up, down, in front, behind, next to

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: up and down with scarves; Station 2: chair and toy; Station 3: obstacle course; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the position song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Put the toy in front of the chair" and "Go up the bench." Children: Child follows the instruction.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use low equipment; supervise each station; keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to run the obstacle course game for a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: follows up and down instructions; follows in front and behind instructions; says where an object is.

- **Ask:** Where is Teddy?
  **Expected response:** In front or behind the chair.
- **Ask:** Can you go up?
  **Expected response:** Child moves up safely.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Uses position words with growing accuracy

### Conclusion
Sing the position song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Use position words in talk at home this week.
