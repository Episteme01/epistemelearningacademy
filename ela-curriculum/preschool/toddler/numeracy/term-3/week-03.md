# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 3: Shape

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Circle, square, triangle revision; Shape hunt indoors and outdoors; Building with shapes

## Learning objectives
- Children will find and name a circle, a square and a triangle in the environment.
- Children will build a simple picture using shapes with support.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, shape, build, find

**Content:** Revising circle, square and triangle, finding shapes indoors and outdoors and building simple pictures with shapes.

**Teaching activities:** Ask children to describe the shape using touch and simple talk; use the shape names every time; leave shape cards on show for the week.

**Learner activities:** Hunt for shapes, match shape cards to real objects, build pictures and say which shape they see.

**Resources:** Large cardboard shapes; Shape cards; Blocks of different shapes; A hoop or tape; Paper and glue sticks

**Assessment:** Check whether each child names or points to circle, square and triangle with growing accuracy.

## Expected outcomes
- Names or points to the three shapes with growing accuracy.
- Makes a shape picture.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: shape words and describing; Physical Development: building and carrying; Understanding the world: shapes in buildings

## Day 1 (Monday): Shape hunt indoors

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Shape hunt indoors and outdoors

### Learning objectives
- Children will find circles, squares and triangles in the room.

### Success criteria
- Finds a shape that matches the card
- Names the shape with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles, squares and triangles with support and sort them into three groups.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, shape, build, find

**Instructional materials:** Large shape cards: circle, square, triangle; A basket; Objects of different shapes around the room

### Starter
Hold up the circle card and ask "Where is a circle in our room?" Wait for a child to point and say "Yes! A clock, a circle."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each shape card and name it. Trace the edge slowly with a finger. Children: Children trace the shape in the air.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each pair a shape card and say "Go and find the same shape." Children: Pairs walk around and find a matching object.

Step 3. Teacher: When children find a match, ask "What shape is it?" Children: Child names or shows the shape.

Step 4. Teacher: Gather in a circle and let each pair show what they found. Children: Pairs show their object and say the shape.

### Practical activity: Room shape hunt
1. Give each pair a shape card.
2. Find an object in the room with the same shape.
3. Bring it to the mat or point to it.

**Safety:** Keep the walkways clear; children walk and carry only one object each; no climbing to reach objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hunt for only one shape, such as the circle, with an adult.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find two different objects with the same shape.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is the clock?
  **Expected response:** Circle.
- **Ask:** Can you find a square?
  **Expected response:** Child finds a square object.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds shapes in the room
- Names circle, square or triangle

### Conclusion
Put all found objects in a row and say the shape of each one.

### Home connection
Find circles, squares and triangles at home on plates, windows and tiles.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find circles, squares and triangles in the room" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Circle, square, triangle

**Picture suggestions:**
- A clock, a window and a roof

**Song or rhyme:** Shape, shape, where is the shape? Round, square, triangle, find the shape.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Shape hunt outdoors

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Shape hunt indoors and outdoors

### Learning objectives
- Children will find shapes in the school compound or playground.

### Success criteria
- Points to a shape outside
- Says the shape name

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles, squares and triangles with support and sort them into three groups.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, shape, build, find

**Instructional materials:** Shape cards; A clipboard or card with three shapes; A safe outdoor area

### Starter
Take the shape cards outside and say "Let us look for shapes outside. A wheel, a window, a roof. Which shape?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Stand together and hold up each shape card. Ask "What can we find like this?" Children: Children look around and point to objects.

Step 2. Teacher: Walk to a wheel or a round gate and say "A circle!" Trace it in the air. Children: Children trace the shape and say "circle".

Step 3. Teacher: Find a square window and a triangle roof with the children. Children: Children point and name each shape.

Step 4. Teacher: Give each child a shape card and let them find a match. Children: Child finds a matching shape outside and shows a friend.

### Practical activity: Outdoor shape walk
1. Walk with a partner around a safe area.
2. Look for shapes that match the card.
3. Tell the group what you found.

**Safety:** Keep the walk within a fenced area with an adult for every four children; stay away from roads and water; wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk beside the child and point to suitable shapes.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count how many circles they can find.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is the roof?
  **Expected response:** Triangle.
- **Ask:** What shape is the wheel?
  **Expected response:** Circle.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds shapes outdoors
- Names shapes in the environment

### Conclusion
Return indoors and wash hands. Say "Shape hunters, well done."

### Home connection
On the way home, point out shapes of doors, signs and wheels.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find shapes in the school compound or playground" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Outdoor shapes

**Picture suggestions:**
- Wheel, window, roof

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Build with shapes

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Building with shapes

### Learning objectives
- Children will build a simple house or vehicle using shapes.

### Success criteria
- Uses at least two shapes to build
- Names one shape they used

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles, squares and triangles with support and sort them into three groups.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, shape, build, find

**Instructional materials:** Large wooden blocks of different shapes; Shape cards; A mat

### Starter
Build a quick house with a square block and a triangle block on top. Say "A square for the walls and a triangle for the roof. What shapes can you use?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a house made of shapes and name each shape you used. Children: Children point to each shape and name it.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a few blocks and ask them to build their own house. Children: Child builds a simple house.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What shapes did you use?" and walk around. Children: Child names or points to the shapes used.

Step 4. Teacher: Invite children to visit a friend’s house and say one shape they see. Children: Children look at each other’s buildings and name a shape.

### Practical activity: Shape houses
1. Give each pair a basket of blocks.
2. Build a house or a car using at least two shapes.
3. Name the shapes used.

**Safety:** Use large blocks and build on the floor only; keep the area clear and walk around buildings carefully.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Build together hand-over-hand using only a square and a triangle.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to build a bridge or a tower and name the shapes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is your roof?
  **Expected response:** Triangle, or the shape used.
- **Ask:** Can you use a circle?
  **Expected response:** Child adds a circular piece.

### Expected outcomes
- Builds with shapes
- Names shapes in a building

### Conclusion
Take a photograph of each building and then tidy the blocks together.

### Home connection
Build with boxes and cups at home and name the shapes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "build a simple house or vehicle using shapes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- House: square and triangle

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Shape pictures with friends

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Building with shapes

### Learning objectives
- Children will make a group picture using shapes and say which shapes they used.

### Success criteria
- Adds a shape to a group picture
- Names a shape

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles, squares and triangles with support and sort them into three groups.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, shape, build, find

**Instructional materials:** A large sheet of paper; Cardboard shapes; Glue sticks; Crayons

### Starter
Say "Today we make one big picture together, a village! What shapes can a village have?" Show the large paper.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Place a square and a triangle on the paper to make a first house. Say the shapes. Children: Children watch and say the shapes.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask each child to add a shape to make a house, a car or a tree. Children: Child places a shape on the paper and names it.

Step 3. Teacher: Glue the shapes down while naming each one. Children: Children help glue with supervision.

Step 4. Teacher: Look at the finished picture and count how many circles, squares and triangles there are. Children: Children point and count the shapes.

### Practical activity: Village picture
1. Spread a large paper on the floor.
2. Each child adds a shape.
3. Name the shapes together.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic glue sticks, supervised; wash hands afterwards; keep scissors out of reach.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer one shape at a time and show where to place it.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to add details such as a door or a window and name the shape.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape did you add?
  **Expected response:** The name of the shape.
- **Ask:** How many circles are in our picture?
  **Expected response:** The number counted, up to five.

### Expected outcomes
- Contributes to a group shape picture
- Names shapes in a picture

### Conclusion
Display the picture on the wall and say "Our shape village!"

### Home connection
Make a picture with paper shapes cut from old cartons.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make a group picture using shapes and say which shapes they used" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Shape village

**Picture suggestions:**
- A village made of shapes

## Day 5 (Friday): Shape hunt stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Shape hunt indoors and outdoors

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can find, name and use shapes at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to circle, square and triangle
- Builds with shapes

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles, squares and triangles with support and sort them into three groups.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, square, triangle, shape, build, find

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: shape cards and objects; Station 2: block building; Station 3: shape picture; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the shape song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Show me a triangle" and "What shape is this?" Children: Child points to or names a shape.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use large shapes and blocks only; supervise glue; keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to teach a friend the three shapes.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names or points to circle, square and triangle; finds shapes in the environment; builds with shapes.

- **Ask:** What shape is this?
  **Expected response:** Circle, square or triangle.
- **Ask:** Where is a triangle?
  **Expected response:** Child points to a triangle.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Names the three shapes with growing accuracy

### Conclusion
Sing the shape song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Keep naming shapes you see this week.

- Which children could show "show they can find, name and use shapes at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
