# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 8: Data

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W08 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Choosing and standing in lines; Which line has more?; Counting a line

## Learning objectives
- Children will sort themselves or objects into groups by a simple choice.
- Children will say which group has more.

**Key vocabulary:** more, fewer, line, group, choose

**Content:** Sorting children or objects by a simple choice, forming lines and saying which line has more.

**Teaching activities:** Make real graphs with children and objects; line up neatly at one end; ask "Which line has more?" and count to check.

**Learner activities:** Choose between two things, stand or place objects in lines, compare lines and count a line.

**Resources:** Two baskets; Picture cards of two favourite fruits; Large blocks; A string line on the floor; Stickers or counters (large)

**Assessment:** Check whether each child joins a group according to a choice and says which group has more.

## Expected outcomes
- Joins a group according to a choice.
- Points to the group with more.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Social Habits: choosing and respecting differences; Literacy: asking and answering questions; Science: sorting and recording

## Day 1 (Monday): Which fruit do you like?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Choosing and standing in lines

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose between two fruits and stand in the line for their choice.

### Success criteria
- Chooses a fruit
- Stands in the matching line

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort objects, count to five with support and say more and fewer.

**Key vocabulary:** more, fewer, line, group, choose

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of an orange and a mango; Two strings on the floor; A mat

### Starter
Show pictures of an orange and a mango. Say "Which do you like? Orange or mango? Stand by the picture you like."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the two pictures at the end of two lines. Say "If you like oranges, stand here." Children: Children choose a picture and walk to it.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask the children in each line to stand neatly one behind another. Children: Children line up behind the picture.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Let us look at the lines. Which line is longer?" Children: Children look and point to the longer line.

Step 4. Teacher: Count the children in each line together. Children: Children count each line aloud.

### Practical activity: Fruit lines
1. Make two lines on the floor.
2. Each child chooses a fruit and stands in a line.
3. Count each line.

**Safety:** Keep lines short with space between children; walk calmly; no pushing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Choose with an adult and stand together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess which line will be longer before counting.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which fruit did you choose?
  **Expected response:** The fruit chosen.
- **Ask:** Which line has more children?
  **Expected response:** The longer line.

### Expected outcomes
- Chooses and joins a line
- Compares lines

### Conclusion
Count both lines and say "More oranges" or "More mangoes."

### Home connection
Ask family members which food they like and count how many choose each.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose between two fruits and stand in the line for their choice" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Blocks in lines

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Which line has more?

### Learning objectives
- Children will place blocks in two lines and say which line has more.

### Success criteria
- Places blocks in lines
- Says which has more

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort objects, count to five with support and say more and fewer.

**Key vocabulary:** more, fewer, line, group, choose

**Instructional materials:** Red and blue blocks; A mat; Two strings

### Starter
Show a pile of red and blue blocks and say "Let us put the reds in one line and the blues in another line. Which will have more?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sort the blocks by colour into two lines starting at the same end. Children: Children sort the blocks into lines.

Step 2. Teacher: Look at the two lines. Say "This line has more." Children: Children point to the longer line.

Step 3. Teacher: Count both lines and say the numbers. Children: Children count and compare.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children choose two colours and make their own lines. Children: Pairs make lines and say which has more.

### Practical activity: Block graph
1. Give each pair a pile of blocks in two colours.
2. Sort into two lines.
3. Compare and count.

**Safety:** Use large blocks only; keep them on the mat; supervise group play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sort two colours and compare lines with one block in each.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count each line and say how many more.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which line has more blocks?
  **Expected response:** The longer line.
- **Ask:** How many red blocks?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes lines of objects
- Says which has more

### Conclusion
Count both lines one more time and say "More red" or "More blue."

### Home connection
Sort spoons and cups into lines at home and compare.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "place blocks in two lines and say which line has more" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Red line
- Blue line

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two lines of blocks

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Our shoes graph

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Counting a line

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort shoes into two groups and count each line.

### Success criteria
- Sorts shoes by type
- Counts a line of up to five

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort objects, count to five with support and say more and fewer.

**Key vocabulary:** more, fewer, line, group, choose

**Instructional materials:** Children’s shoes (sandals and closed shoes); Two labelled mats; A mat

### Starter
Say "Let us make a shoe graph. Who is wearing sandals? Who is wearing closed shoes?" Ask children to take off one shoe.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the sandal picture and the closed shoe picture on the floor. Place your own shoe in a line. Children: Children place their shoes under the matching picture.

Step 2. Teacher: Line up the shoes neatly one beside another. Say "Let us count each line." Children: Children count the shoes in each line.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which line has more shoes?" Children: Children point to the line with more.

Step 4. Teacher: Remove two shoes from one line and count again. Children: Children count again and say the new number.

### Practical activity: Shoe lines
1. Children place one shoe in the correct line.
2. Count each line.
3. Say which has more.

**Safety:** Keep shoes in neat lines and away from walkways; children wash hands after handling shoes.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count only the shorter line with an adult.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess how many shoes are in each line and then count.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which line has more shoes?
  **Expected response:** The longer line.
- **Ask:** How many sandals?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts and counts real objects
- Compares two lines

### Conclusion
Put shoes back and say "Sandals and shoes, all done."

### Home connection
Sort shoes by type at the door and count each group.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort shoes into two groups and count each line" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Sandals
- Closed shoes

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two lines of shoes

## Day 4 (Thursday): Our favourite game graph

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Which line has more?

### Learning objectives
- Children will vote with a block for a favourite game and say which has more.

### Success criteria
- Chooses a favourite
- Places a block in the matching stack

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort objects, count to five with support and say more and fewer.

**Key vocabulary:** more, fewer, line, group, choose

**Instructional materials:** Two pictures of games (ball and dance); Blocks; A mat

### Starter
Show pictures of ball play and dancing. Say "Which game do you like best? Put a block on your favourite."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give each child a block. Ask them to put it by their favourite picture. Children: Children place a block under a picture.

Step 2. Teacher: Build a tall tower under each picture, one block at a time. Children: Children watch the towers grow.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which tower is taller?" and "Which has more blocks?" Children: Children point to the taller tower.

Step 4. Teacher: Count the blocks in each tower. Children: Children count and say the numbers.

### Practical activity: Block vote
1. Give each child a block.
2. Place it in a stack for their favourite game.
3. Count both stacks.

**Safety:** Use large blocks; build towers on the floor; supervise group play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Choose with an adult and place the block together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say which game won and how they know.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which game has more blocks?
  **Expected response:** The taller stack.
- **Ask:** How many chose dancing?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.

### Expected outcomes
- Votes with a block
- Compares tall and short stacks

### Conclusion
Say "More blocks, more friends like it" and then play the winning game.

### Home connection
Make a family graph of favourite foods using spoons or sticks held by an adult.

### Teacher reflection (private)

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Graph stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Which line has more?

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can sort, compare and count lines at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Sorts into two groups
- Says which group has more

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort objects, count to five with support and say more and fewer.

**Key vocabulary:** more, fewer, line, group, choose

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: block lines; Station 2: shoe lines; Station 3: favourite vote; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the sorting song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which line has more?" and "How many are in this line?" Children: Child compares and counts.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use large items only; supervise stations; keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to run a vote for a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: joins a group according to a choice; says which line has more; counts a short line.

- **Ask:** Which line has more?
  **Expected response:** The longer line.
- **Ask:** How many in this line?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Compares groups with growing confidence

### Conclusion
Sing the sorting song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Make a simple graph at home with family members.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show they can sort, compare and count lines at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
