# Toddler Numeracy — Term 3, Week 9: Applying number

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Setting up our market; Buying and selling up to five; Counting change in sharing games

## Learning objectives
- Children will count out up to three or five items in a play market.
- Children will use number, more and one each words in role play.

**Key vocabulary:** buy, sell, market, how many, please, thank you

**Content:** Using number, more, same and sharing words in a pretend market, counting out up to five items for customers.

**Teaching activities:** Take the role of a customer; ask for a number of items; model polite words; ask "How many?" and let children check.

**Learner activities:** Set up a stall, count out items for customers, say please and thank you, and compare baskets.

**Resources:** A table or cloth for the stall; Toy fruits and vegetables; Baskets and bags; Number cards 1 to 5; A toy cash tin with large play coins

**Assessment:** Check whether children count out the number asked for and use number words naturally in role play.

## Expected outcomes
- Counts out items on request in play.
- Uses number words naturally in role play.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Social Habits: politeness and cooperation; Literacy: asking and answering; Understanding the world: markets and community helpers

## Day 1 (Monday): Set up our market

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Setting up our market

### Learning objectives
- Children will help to set up a market and sort goods by kind.

### Success criteria
- Sorts goods into groups
- Names goods and counts a few

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, share one each, add one more and use position and shape words.

**Key vocabulary:** buy, sell, market, how many, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** A table or cloth; Toy fruits and vegetables; Baskets; Name signs with pictures

### Starter
Say "We are opening a market today! Who will help me set up the stall? We need baskets for oranges, tomatoes and yams."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a basket and name an item to place in it: "Oranges go in this basket." Children: Children name the items and place them in baskets.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask children to count how many items are in each basket, up to five. Children: Children count items in their basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Put a picture sign on each basket. Children: Children place a picture sign on the right basket.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk around the market and say what you can buy. Children: Children say what they see.

### Practical activity: Market set-up
1. Give each pair a basket and a set of toy items.
2. Sort the items and count them.
3. Place them on the stall.

**Safety:** Use toy items only; keep the stall low and stable; children walk calmly.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sort two kinds of item and count only three.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to label the basket with a picture and tell a friend how many.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is in this basket?
  **Expected response:** The kind of item.
- **Ask:** How many oranges are there?
  **Expected response:** The number counted.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts and counts market goods
- Names market items

### Conclusion
Stand back and admire the market. Say "Our market is ready!"

### Home connection
Talk about what you buy at the market and how many.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "help to set up a market and sort goods by kind" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Buying and selling

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Buying and selling up to five

### Learning objectives
- Children will buy and sell up to three items, saying please and thank you.

### Success criteria
- Counts out the number of items requested
- Uses polite words

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, share one each, add one more and use position and shape words.

**Key vocabulary:** buy, sell, market, how many, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** A market stall; Toy fruits; Baskets; Number cards 1 to 3

### Starter
Act as a customer. Say "Good morning. Please may I have two oranges?" Wait for the trader to count out two oranges.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Model the buying and selling exchange with a child as the trader. Children: Child counts out two oranges and says "thank you".

Step 2. Teacher: Ask for three tomatoes and let the trader check by counting. Children: Child counts three tomatoes and gives them.

Step 3. Teacher: Let pairs take turns as customer and trader with number cards. Children: Pairs buy and sell and count aloud.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap roles and try a number up to five. Children: Children count out up to five items.

### Practical activity: Market role play
1. Set up a stall for each pair.
2. The customer shows a number card.
3. The trader counts out that many items.

**Safety:** Use toy items only; keep stalls stable; supervise role play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use number cards 1 and 2 only and count together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to buy two different things and say how many in all.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many tomatoes did you buy?
  **Expected response:** The number on the card.
- **Ask:** What do we say when we receive something?
  **Expected response:** Thank you.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts out items on request
- Uses polite words in play

### Conclusion
Close the market stall and say "Goodbye, see you tomorrow."

### Home connection
Pretend to shop at home using real items and ask for numbers.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "buy and sell up to three items, saying please and thank you" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Please
- Thank you

**Picture suggestions:**
- A trader and customer

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Which basket has more?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Buying and selling up to five

### Learning objectives
- Children will compare baskets from the market and say which has more or the same.

### Success criteria
- Compares two baskets
- Says more or same

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, share one each, add one more and use position and shape words.

**Key vocabulary:** buy, sell, market, how many, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** Two baskets of toy fruits; Number cards; A mat

### Starter
Show two baskets of fruits and say "Who has more fruits? Amina or Tunde? Let us count."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Count the fruits in each basket by moving one at a time. Children: Children count each basket aloud.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Amina has four. Tunde has three. Amina has more." Children: Children say "more" and point to Amina’s basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Make the baskets the same by giving one more to Tunde. Children: Children add a fruit and say "same".

Step 4. Teacher: Let pairs make baskets and compare them. Children: Pairs compare and say more or same.

### Practical activity: Compare baskets
1. Give each pair two baskets.
2. Count out different numbers.
3. Say which has more.

**Safety:** Use toy fruits only; keep baskets on the mat; supervise group play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Compare baskets of one and four items.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say how many more one basket has.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which basket has more?
  **Expected response:** The one with more fruits.
- **Ask:** Are they the same?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no with a reason.

### Expected outcomes
- Compares quantities in baskets
- Makes baskets equal

### Conclusion
Count both baskets and say "More, same."

### Home connection
Compare two plates of food at home and say which has more.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "compare baskets from the market and say which has more or the same" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Amina 4
- Tunde 3

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Sharing market goods

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Counting change in sharing games

### Learning objectives
- Children will share market goods fairly among friends and count them.

### Success criteria
- Shares goods one each
- Counts what is left

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, share one each, add one more and use position and shape words.

**Key vocabulary:** buy, sell, market, how many, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** Toy fruits; Plates for friends; A basket

### Starter
Say "After the market, the family shares the fruits. One for you, one for you, one for you. How many do we have left?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give out one fruit to each plate and count how many are left in the basket. Children: Children count the fruits left.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Is there enough for everyone?" Add more if needed. Children: Children check and add fruits.

Step 3. Teacher: Share fruit among three plates and say "one each, one each". Children: Child shares one fruit to each plate.

Step 4. Teacher: Count the fruits on each plate and say they are the same. Children: Children count and say "same".

### Practical activity: Share the shopping
1. Give each group a basket and plates.
2. Share fruits one each.
3. Count what is left.

**Safety:** Use toy fruits; keep baskets on the table; supervise group play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Share between two plates only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to share five fruits among two plates and say what is left.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many are left?
  **Expected response:** The number remaining.
- **Ask:** Does each plate have the same?
  **Expected response:** Yes if shared evenly.

### Expected outcomes
- Shares goods one each
- Counts what is left

### Conclusion
Pack the baskets away and say "Thank you, market."

### Home connection
Help share fruit or biscuits fairly at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "share market goods fairly among friends and count them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Share and count

**Picture suggestions:**
- Plates with fruits

## Day 5 (Friday): Market stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T3-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Buying and selling up to five

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can count, compare and share in a market game at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Counts out up to three or five items
- Uses number and comparison words

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to five with support, share one each, add one more and use position and shape words.

**Key vocabulary:** buy, sell, market, how many, please, thank you

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: buying and selling; Station 2: compare baskets; Station 3: sharing goods; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the market song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "How many would you like?" and "Which has more?" Children: Child counts and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use toy items only; supervise stations; keep floors clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to be the market manager and help a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: counts out items on request; uses number, more and same words in role play; shares one each.

- **Ask:** Can you give me three oranges?
  **Expected response:** Child counts out three oranges.
- **Ask:** Which basket has more?
  **Expected response:** The one with more.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Uses number words in role play

### Conclusion
Sing the market song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Play market with real objects at home.
