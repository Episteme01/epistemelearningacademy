# Toddler Numeracy — Term 2 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Comparing quantity**: More; The same amount; No more, all gone | Children will point to the group that has more. Children will say more when they want another. | Comparing two groups of real objects and saying which has more, which is the same, and when none are left. | Place groups side by side, lined up so the difference is obvious; use the words more and same every time; ask "How do you know?" and accept pointing. | Choose the group with more, make two groups the same, ask for more using the word, and say all gone. | Large wooden blocks; Toy biscuits and plates; Two trays or baskets; Toy oranges; Soft toys as guests | Note whether each child picks the group with more when the difference is clear and uses the word more in play. | Chooses the group with more when the difference is clear. Uses the word more purposefully. | Literacy: asking politely with more please; Social Habits: fair sharing and saying please; Science: comparing amounts of water or sand |
| 2 | **Counting**: Five little rhymes with fingers; Count down and count up; Jumping to five | Children will join in counting rhymes to five. Children will show up to five fingers with help. | Saying one to five in order in action rhymes, showing up to five fingers, counting down and counting up. | Lead rhymes with a steady beat and finger actions; sing each rhyme slowly first, then faster; show each finger as it is named. | Say numbers to five with actions, show fingers, count down by lowering fingers, and jump to five. | A drum; Five plastic cups; Five toy birds or paper birds; A mango tree picture; Number picture cards with dots to five | Listen for who joins rhymes to five and who shows up to five fingers; note on the class record. | Says the numbers one to five in order with the group. Shows five fingers with support. | Literacy: rhythm, rhyme and voice play; Science: birds and trees; Physical Development: finger and body movement |
| 3 | **Counting**: Touch and count four and five; How many now?; Counting while moving | Children will count up to five objects with one touch for each. Children will say how many using the last number word. | Counting four and five real objects by touching each one once, answering how many, and counting while moving. | Count slowly with a clear touch and slide for every object; repeat the last number as the answer; keep groups small and lined up. | Touch and move objects one at a time, count four and five, say how many, and count while moving. | Large wooden blocks; Toy fruits; A basket; Five plastic cups; Soft balls | Check one-to-one touching, order of number words and whether the child says how many with the last number. | Counts up to three objects reliably and up to five with support. Gives how many with support. | Literacy: number words and questions; Physical Development: moving objects one at a time; Social Habits: taking turns to count |
| 4 | **Numerals**: Finding numerals in our world; Matching numerals to groups; Feeling and tracing numerals | Children will recognise the numerals 1, 2 and 3 with support. Children will match a numeral card to a group of one, two or three objects. | Seeing the numerals 1, 2 and 3 in the environment, matching them to groups of one, two and three, and tracing them with a finger. | Show the numeral and the group together; say the number while pointing to both; trace the numeral large in the air before the child traces; keep numerals big and clear. | Spot numerals around the room, match numeral cards to groups, trace numerals in sand and on cards. | Large numeral cards 1, 2 and 3; Dot cards for one, two and three; Large blocks; A tray of sand or flour; A calendar and a clock picture | Check whether children match a numeral to the right group of up to three and point to a named numeral from three. | Matches a numeral to the right group of up to three. Points to a named numeral from three. | Literacy: print awareness; shapes of marks; Physical Development: tracing and arm movements; Social Habits: sharing materials |
| 5 | **Numerals**: Meeting 4 and 5; Putting numerals in order; Numeral and quantity games | Children will recognise the numerals 4 and 5 with support. Children will place numeral cards 1 to 5 in order with help. | Meeting the numerals 4 and 5, matching numerals 1 to 5 to groups, and putting numeral cards in order. | Show each new numeral large and trace it in the air; always pair a numeral with a group; make the number line from left to right as children say the numbers. | Match numerals to groups to five, place cards in order, and play numeral and quantity games. | Numeral cards 1 to 5; Dot cards to five; Large blocks; A string or paper strip as a number line; Soft toys | Check numeral-to-group matching to five with support and ordering of numeral cards from one to five. | Matches numerals 1 to 5 to groups with support. Orders 1 to 3 independently and 1 to 5 with help. | Literacy: left to right order and sequencing; Physical Development: carrying and placing cards; Social Habits: taking turns |
| 6 | **Measuring**: Long and short objects; Comparing two lengths side by side; Long and short in stories and songs | Children will say which of two things is long or short. Children will line up two objects at one end to compare them. | Comparing the length of two objects and saying which is long and which is short, lining them up at one end. | Hold objects side by side with the ends lined up on the floor; say longer and shorter with a stretching gesture; model the line-up before children try. | Find long and short things, line up two objects to compare, make a long and a short snake, and sing about long and short. | Lengths of rope or cloth strips; Wooden sticks or rolled newspaper (large); Two ribbons; Playdough; A toy snake | Check whether each child chooses the longer or shorter of two objects and lines up objects at one end. | Chooses the longer or shorter of two objects. Begins to line up objects to compare. | Literacy: comparing words and rhymes; Physical Development: stretching and rolling; Science: measuring with hands and body |
| 7 | **Measuring**: Holding and comparing weight; Heavy and light baskets; Guess, feel and check | Children will say which of two objects feels heavy and which feels light. Children will predict which is heavy and check by holding. | Feeling and comparing the weight of two objects, and saying which is heavy and which is light. | Let children hold each object before naming it; use strong body language for heavy and gentle for light; ask them to guess, feel and check. | Hold, carry and compare objects, sort into heavy and light baskets, and make and check guesses. | A heavy basket (filled with large blocks) and a light basket (filled with scarves); Balloons and stones too large to swallow; A bucket and a sponge; Two bags | Check whether each child identifies the heavier object when the difference is clear and makes and checks a guess. | Identifies the heavier object when the difference is clear. Makes a prediction and checks it. | Science: forces and materials; Physical Development: lifting and carrying; Literacy: opposites |
| 8 | **Pattern**: Sound and clap patterns; Colour patterns AB; What comes next? | Children will copy a clap or movement pattern. Children will continue a two-colour pattern with support. | Copying and continuing simple two-part patterns with claps, movements, colours and objects. | Say the pattern aloud as you make it, "red, blue, red, blue"; point to each item; pause before the next item and ask "What comes next?" | Copy clap and stamp patterns, continue a colour pattern, make an AB pattern with objects and dance a pattern. | Red and blue blocks or cups; Coloured cloths; A drum; Large wooden beads on a string with a knot (too large to swallow); Leaves and stones too large to swallow | Check whether each child copies a two-part pattern and adds the next object with support. | Copies a two-part clap pattern. Adds the next object to an AB pattern with support. | Literacy: rhythm and sequencing words; Physical Development: clapping and stamping; Expressive arts: dance and music |
| 9 | **Shape**: Squares in our world; Triangles in our world; Circle, square or triangle? | Children will find squares and triangles around them. Children will tell a circle from a square with support. | Finding and naming squares and triangles in the environment and telling circle, square and triangle apart. | Trace each shape slowly with a finger; count corners and sides only for squares and triangles by touching; place shapes against real objects. | Find squares and triangles, trace the shapes, sort circle, square and triangle, and make shape pictures. | Large cardboard shapes (circle, square, triangle); Boxes, tiles, triangle roof pictures, a slice of bread; Shape sorting trays; A shape bag | Check whether each child names or points to a square, triangle and circle with support. | Names or points to a square and a circle. Points to a triangle when asked. | Literacy: shape words and descriptive talk; Physical Development: tracing and carrying shapes; Understanding the world: buildings and objects |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising numerals and counting; Revising comparing and patterns; Revising shapes | Children will show Term 2 learning through play stations. Teachers will gather evidence for every child’s Term 2 record. | Revising Term 2 learning through play stations and gathering evidence for the Term 2 record. | Keep stations relaxed; observe quietly; give help only when asked; praise effort and kindness. | Revisit familiar games, play with a friend and show what they know in many ways. | Materials from Weeks 1 to 9 of Term 2; Class record sheets; A camera or phone; Stickers | Use the term record to judge each strand: comparing quantity, counting to five, numerals, measures, patterns and shapes. Use two or more occasions before recording Secure. | Completes familiar tasks with growing independence. Each child has an observation record for Term 2. | Literacy: songs and rhymes from the term; Social Habits: working with a friend and tidying up; Physical Development: movement games |
