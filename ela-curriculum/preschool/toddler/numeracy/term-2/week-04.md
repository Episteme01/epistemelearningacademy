# Toddler Numeracy — Term 2, Week 4: Numerals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W04 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Finding numerals in our world; Matching numerals to groups; Feeling and tracing numerals

## Learning objectives
- Children will recognise the numerals 1, 2 and 3 with support.
- Children will match a numeral card to a group of one, two or three objects.

**Key vocabulary:** number, numeral, one, two, three, match

**Content:** Seeing the numerals 1, 2 and 3 in the environment, matching them to groups of one, two and three, and tracing them with a finger.

**Teaching activities:** Show the numeral and the group together; say the number while pointing to both; trace the numeral large in the air before the child traces; keep numerals big and clear.

**Learner activities:** Spot numerals around the room, match numeral cards to groups, trace numerals in sand and on cards.

**Resources:** Large numeral cards 1, 2 and 3; Dot cards for one, two and three; Large blocks; A tray of sand or flour; A calendar and a clock picture

**Assessment:** Check whether children match a numeral to the right group of up to three and point to a named numeral from three.

## Expected outcomes
- Matches a numeral to the right group of up to three.
- Points to a named numeral from three.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: print awareness; shapes of marks; Physical Development: tracing and arm movements; Social Habits: sharing materials

## Day 1 (Monday): Numbers all around us

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Finding numerals in our world

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice numerals in the environment and match 1, 2 and 3 to groups.

### Success criteria
- Points to a numeral when asked
- Says one, two or three while looking at a numeral

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to three reliably and to five with support, and say how many for small groups.

**Key vocabulary:** number, numeral, one, two, three, match

**Instructional materials:** Large numeral cards 1, 2 and 3; A calendar; A picture of a clock; A house number card

### Starter
Hold up the house number card and say "This is a number on a house. Numbers are everywhere! Let us look for numbers in our room."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the numerals 1, 2 and 3 on cards. Say each number and trace it in the air. Children: Children trace the numerals in the air with a finger.

Step 2. Teacher: Point to the calendar and clock picture. Say "These have numbers too." Children: Children look and point to a numeral they notice.

Step 3. Teacher: Put one block beside the numeral 1, two beside the 2 and three beside the 3. Children: Children touch each block and say the number.

Step 4. Teacher: Hold up a numeral and ask "Which group matches?" Children: Children point to the group of blocks that matches the numeral.

### Practical activity: Numeral walk
1. Stick large numeral cards on the wall at child height.
2. Walk with a partner and find each numeral.
3. Say the number and touch the card.

**Safety:** Fix cards firmly at child height with no sharp edges; children walk, not run.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use numerals 1 and 2 only with one and two blocks beside them.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find numerals at home or in the classroom and tell the group.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you show me the number 2?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the numeral 2.
- **Ask:** How many blocks go with this number?
  **Expected response:** The correct number of blocks, up to three.

### Expected outcomes
- Notices numerals in the environment
- Matches a numeral to a group

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the numerals and say "One, two, three, goodbye."

### Home connection
Spot numbers at home on a phone, a clock or a house door and say them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice numerals in the environment and match 1, 2 and 3 to groups" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1  2  3 (large numerals)
- One block, two blocks, three blocks

**Picture suggestions:**
- A house number
- A calendar and a clock

**Song or rhyme:** One is a stick, two is a swan, three is two bumps, count with me.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Match the number to the dots

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Matching numerals to groups

### Learning objectives
- Children will match numeral cards 1, 2 and 3 to dot cards or groups of objects.

### Success criteria
- Places the numeral beside the right group
- Says the number while matching

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to three reliably and to five with support, and say how many for small groups.

**Key vocabulary:** number, numeral, one, two, three, match

**Instructional materials:** Numeral cards 1 to 3; Dot cards for one, two and three; Large blocks; A mat

### Starter
Place one dot card and ask "How many dots?" Lay the numeral 1 beside it. Say "One dot, one numeral. They match."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the dot card with two dots. Count them. Place the numeral 2 beside it. Children: Children count the dots and say "two".

Step 2. Teacher: Mix the cards and ask a child to find the numeral for three dots. Children: Child matches the numeral 3 with the three dots.

Step 3. Teacher: Use blocks: put two blocks on the mat and ask "Which numeral?" Children: Child places the numeral 2 beside the blocks.

Step 4. Teacher: Let children make their own matches from a basket of cards and blocks. Children: Children match numerals and groups, saying the number each time.

### Practical activity: Numeral and dots match
1. Lay out three numeral cards in a row.
2. Children place the correct dot card or blocks under each numeral.
3. They check by counting aloud.

**Safety:** Use large, rounded cards and blocks larger than a child’s fist. Keep everything on the mat.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Match numerals 1 and 2 only, and use dot cards with large dots.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put the numerals in order and match groups to each.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which numeral goes with three dots?
  **Expected response:** The numeral 3.
- **Ask:** How many blocks for the number 2?
  **Expected response:** Two.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches numerals 1 to 3 to groups
- Says the numbers while matching

### Conclusion
Line up the matched pairs and say "One, two, three. All matched."

### Home connection
Show your child a number card and ask them to find that many spoons.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match numeral cards 1, 2 and 3 to dot cards or groups of objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 (one dot)
- 2 (two dots)
- 3 (three dots)

**Picture suggestions:**
- Numeral cards with dot cards

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Feel and trace the numbers

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Feeling and tracing numerals

### Learning objectives
- Children will trace the numerals 1, 2 and 3 with a finger in sand or on textured cards.

### Success criteria
- Traces the shape of a numeral
- Says the number while tracing

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to three reliably and to five with support, and say how many for small groups.

**Key vocabulary:** number, numeral, one, two, three, match

**Instructional materials:** A tray of dry sand or flour; Large sandpaper or textured numeral cards; Paper numerals

### Starter
Trace the numeral 1 in the air with a big arm movement and say "One is a straight line." Invite the children to trace with you.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Draw the numeral 1 in the sand slowly with your finger. Say "Down, one." Children: Children trace the numeral on their own trays.

Step 2. Teacher: Draw the numeral 2 with a curve and a line along the bottom and say "Curve and slide, two." Children: Children copy the movement in the sand.

Step 3. Teacher: Draw the numeral 3 with two curves and say "Bump, bump, three." Children: Children trace the numeral 3 and say "three".

Step 4. Teacher: Place the numeral cards beside each tray and let children check their tracing. Children: Child compares the traced numeral with the card.

### Practical activity: Sand numerals
1. Give each child a shallow tray of sand.
2. Trace each numeral three times.
3. Shake the tray to wipe it smooth.

**Safety:** Use clean, dry sand or flour; tell children not to eat or throw it. Wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use hand-over-hand tracing on textured cards.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to trace the numeral and then place the matching number of blocks beside it.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you trace the number 3?
  **Expected response:** Child traces the numeral 3 in the sand.
- **Ask:** What number is this?
  **Expected response:** The correct numeral name.

### Expected outcomes
- Traces the numerals 1 to 3
- Says the number while tracing

### Conclusion
Shake the trays to make the numerals disappear. Say "All gone numbers. Goodbye."

### Home connection
Trace numbers on a tray of flour or on your child’s back with a finger.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "trace the numerals 1, 2 and 3 with a finger in sand or on textured cards" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Trace: 1
- Trace: 2
- Trace: 3

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child tracing in sand
- Numeral cards with arrows

## Day 4 (Thursday): Number hunt

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Finding numerals in our world

### Learning objectives
- Children will find numerals in the room and outdoors and say the number.

### Success criteria
- Finds a numeral 1, 2 or 3
- Says the number

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to three reliably and to five with support, and say how many for small groups.

**Key vocabulary:** number, numeral, one, two, three, match

**Instructional materials:** Numeral cards hidden around the room; A basket; Number cards for each child

### Starter
Say "Some numbers are hiding in our room. Let us find them." Show a numeral card to remind the children what to look for.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give each child a card with a numeral 1, 2 or 3. Say "Find the same number." Children: Children walk around and look for a matching numeral.

Step 2. Teacher: When a child finds a match, say the number together and cheer. Children: Child shows the numeral and says the number.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask each child to find the group of blocks that matches their numeral. Children: Child finds the matching group and counts it.

Step 4. Teacher: Collect the cards and put them in order. Children: Children help place the cards in a row 1, 2, 3.

### Practical activity: Hidden number hunt
1. Hide large numeral cards at low heights around the room.
2. Children find a card and bring it to the basket.
3. They say the number.

**Safety:** Hide cards only at child height, never on high shelves; keep the area clear of obstacles.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hide cards in plain sight and walk with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find all three numerals and put them in order.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What number did you find?
  **Expected response:** The correct numeral name.
- **Ask:** Can you find a number 3?
  **Expected response:** Child finds the numeral 3.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds and names numerals 1 to 3
- Matches a numeral with a group

### Conclusion
Put all numerals in a line on the floor and say "One, two, three."

### Home connection
Look for numbers on doors, clocks and phones with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find numerals in the room and outdoors and say the number" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Number hunt

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children hunting numerals

## Day 5 (Friday): Numeral stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Matching numerals to groups

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can recognise, match and trace the numerals 1, 2 and 3 at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Matches a numeral to a group of up to three
- Points to a named numeral

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count up to three reliably and to five with support, and say how many for small groups.

**Key vocabulary:** number, numeral, one, two, three, match

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: numeral and dot match; Station 2: sand numerals; Station 3: number hunt cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the numeral song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Show me the number 2" and "Which numeral goes with these blocks?" Children: Child points to numerals and matches them.

Step 3. Teacher: Look at the traced numerals and talk about them with each child. Children: Child shows and names a traced numeral.

Step 4. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use clean sand or flour; supervise the sand tray; remove small items.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a child to quiz a friend: "Show me the number 3."

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: points to a named numeral from three; matches numerals 1 to 3 to groups; traces numerals.

- **Ask:** Which numeral is this?
  **Expected response:** The correct number name.
- **Ask:** Can you match the number to the blocks?
  **Expected response:** Child places the numeral beside the right group.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Recognises numerals 1 to 3 with support

### Conclusion
Sing the numeral song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Show your child numbers at home and ask "what number is this?"

- Which children could show "show they can recognise, match and trace the numerals 1, 2 and 3 at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 2 3

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
