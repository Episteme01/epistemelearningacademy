# Toddler Numeracy — Term 2, Week 7: Measuring

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Holding and comparing weight; Heavy and light baskets; Guess, feel and check

## Learning objectives
- Children will say which of two objects feels heavy and which feels light.
- Children will predict which is heavy and check by holding.

**Key vocabulary:** heavy, light, hold, feel, guess

**Content:** Feeling and comparing the weight of two objects, and saying which is heavy and which is light.

**Teaching activities:** Let children hold each object before naming it; use strong body language for heavy and gentle for light; ask them to guess, feel and check.

**Learner activities:** Hold, carry and compare objects, sort into heavy and light baskets, and make and check guesses.

**Resources:** A heavy basket (filled with large blocks) and a light basket (filled with scarves); Balloons and stones too large to swallow; A bucket and a sponge; Two bags

**Assessment:** Check whether each child identifies the heavier object when the difference is clear and makes and checks a guess.

## Expected outcomes
- Identifies the heavier object when the difference is clear.
- Makes a prediction and checks it.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: forces and materials; Physical Development: lifting and carrying; Literacy: opposites

## Day 1 (Monday): Feel heavy, feel light

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Holding and comparing weight

### Learning objectives
- Children will hold two objects and say which feels heavy and which feels light.

### Success criteria
- Holds both objects
- Points to the heavy one on request

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small and long and short and use words such as more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** heavy, light, hold, feel, guess

**Instructional materials:** A large stone or a bag of sand and a balloon or scarf; Two baskets, one heavy and one light; A mat

### Starter
Pick up a heavy bag with a big groan and say "Heavy!" Pick up a scarf and float it and say "Light!" Let the children feel both.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold out the two baskets and let each child lift one in each hand. Children: Children lift both baskets and feel the difference.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "This one is heavy. This one is light." Use a deep voice for heavy and a high voice for light. Children: Children copy the voices and the words.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which basket is heavy?" and "Which is light?" Children: Children point to the correct basket.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap the contents of the baskets without telling the children and ask again. Children: Children lift and decide which is heavy now.

### Practical activity: Heavy and light baskets
1. Place a heavy and a light basket on the floor.
2. Each child lifts both and says which is heavy.
3. Move the heavy basket with a friend.

**Safety:** Keep heavy items to a safe weight children can lift with both hands; bend the knees to lift; never carry a heavy item alone.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use one very heavy and one very light item, and say the word as the child lifts.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a third object that is heavier or lighter than both.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which basket is heavy?
  **Expected response:** The heavy basket.
- **Ask:** Is the scarf heavy or light?
  **Expected response:** Light.

### Expected outcomes
- Identifies heavy and light
- Says heavy or light with support

### Conclusion
Lift the heavy basket together and put it down gently. Then float the scarf and say "Heavy, light."

### Home connection
Let your child lift a full water bottle and an empty one and say which is heavy.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "hold two objects and say which feels heavy and which feels light" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HEAVY (a big stone)
- LIGHT (a feather)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A stone and a feather
- A child lifting a basket

**Song or rhyme:** Heavy, heavy, down we go. Light, light, up we float.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Guess, feel, check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Guess, feel and check

### Learning objectives
- Children will guess which of two objects is heavier and check by holding them.

### Success criteria
- Makes a guess before lifting
- Checks by holding both

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small and long and short and use words such as more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** heavy, light, hold, feel, guess

**Instructional materials:** Pairs of objects: block and sponge, shoe and sock, bag of rice and empty bag; Two baskets; A mat

### Starter
Show a block and a sponge. Say "Which do you think is heavy? Make a guess, then we will feel."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "I think the block is heavy." Lift both and show your surprise or agreement. Children: Children watch and say whether the guess was right.

Step 2. Teacher: Let a child guess and check with another pair, such as a shoe and a sock. Children: Child guesses, lifts both and says which is heavy.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Were you right?" and accept a nod or "yes". Children: Child nods or says yes or no.

Step 4. Teacher: Place heavy objects in one basket and light in the other. Children: Children sort objects after lifting them.

### Practical activity: Guess and check pairs
1. Give each pair three pairs of objects.
2. They guess which is heavier, then lift and check.
3. They sort into heavy and light baskets.

**Safety:** Use only soft or lightweight objects and moderate weights; keep fingers clear when putting objects down.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects with a very big weight difference and hold both for the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to explain: "The stone is heavy because it is big."

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which one do you think is heavy?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the one they guess.
- **Ask:** Were you right?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no, with a nod.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a guess and checks it
- Sorts objects as heavy or light

### Conclusion
Count how many heavy and light things there are and say the words together.

### Home connection
Guess which of two shopping items is heavy and lift them to check.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "guess which of two objects is heavier and check by holding them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Guess
- Feel
- Check

**Picture suggestions:**
- A block and a sponge

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Heavy and light carriers

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Heavy and light baskets

### Learning objectives
- Children will carry heavy and light items from one place to another and say which was harder.

### Success criteria
- Carries both items
- Says heavy or light for each one

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small and long and short and use words such as more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** heavy, light, hold, feel, guess

**Instructional materials:** Two bags, one heavy, one light; A small bucket; A path marked with cones

### Starter
Say "Mama is carrying shopping from the market. She has a heavy bag and a light bag. Who can help her?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the two bags and ask a child to carry each to the table. Children: Child carries the light bag and the heavy bag and says which was heavy.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which bag was easy to carry? Which was hard?" Children: Child points to the light and heavy bags.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children work in pairs to carry the heavy bag together. Children: Pairs carry the heavy bag together and say "heavy".

Step 4. Teacher: Put the light bag on a high shelf and the heavy bag on the floor and ask why. Children: Children say or gesture that the heavy bag is safer low.

### Practical activity: Market carry
1. Mark a short path with cones.
2. Children carry a light bag then a heavy bag along the path.
3. Talk about which was harder.

**Safety:** Fill the heavy bag only with soft heavy things such as cloth and a little sand in a closed bag; children carry together and bend their knees; no running.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Carry the heavy bag with an adult.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess which of two bags is heavy by looking, then check by lifting.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which bag is heavy?
  **Expected response:** The heavier bag.
- **Ask:** Can two friends carry it?
  **Expected response:** Yes, together.

### Expected outcomes
- Carries and compares weights
- Uses the words heavy and light in play

### Conclusion
Put both bags down gently and say "Heavy, light, well done."

### Home connection
Help carry a light and a heavy shopping bag at home with a grown-up.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "carry heavy and light items from one place to another and say which was harder" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Heavy bag
- Light bag

**Picture suggestions:**
- A mother with a heavy bag at the market

## Day 4 (Thursday): Water bucket and sponge

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Heavy and light baskets

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice that a sponge or cloth is light dry and heavy when wet.

### Success criteria
- Lifts a dry and a wet sponge
- Says which one is heavy

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small and long and short and use words such as more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** heavy, light, hold, feel, guess

**Instructional materials:** A bucket of water; Two sponges or cloths; A tray; Towels

### Starter
Hold a dry sponge and say "Light!" Squeeze water into a bowl and say "Now it is heavy! Why?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give each child a dry sponge to hold and say "light". Children: Children hold the dry sponge and say "light".

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child dip the sponge in the bucket and hold it up. Children: Children hold the wet sponge and say "heavy".

Step 3. Teacher: Squeeze the sponge over the tray and feel it become light again. Children: Children squeeze and feel it get lighter.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Why was the wet sponge heavy?" Accept answers such as "water". Children: Children say "water" or gesture.

### Practical activity: Wet and dry sponge
1. Give each pair a bucket of shallow water and two sponges.
2. Hold a dry sponge and a wet sponge.
3. Say which is heavy.

**Safety:** Use only shallow water, under adult supervision; wipe spills at once; keep towels ready and wash hands after play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use one sponge and ask "light or heavy?" before and after wetting it.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess how heavy a sponge will be when it is full of water.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which sponge is heavy?
  **Expected response:** The wet sponge.
- **Ask:** What makes it heavy?
  **Expected response:** Water.

### Expected outcomes
- Notices that water makes a sponge heavy
- Compares weights in play

### Conclusion
Dry hands with the towels and say "Heavy water, light sponge."

### Home connection
At home, lift a dry cloth and a wet cloth and say which is heavy.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice that a sponge or cloth is light dry and heavy when wet" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Dry: light
- Wet: heavy

**Picture suggestions:**
- A wet sponge dripping

## Day 5 (Friday): Weight stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Guess, feel and check

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can compare weights at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Identifies the heavier object
- Makes a guess and checks it

**Previous knowledge:** Children can compare big and small and long and short and use words such as more and same.

**Key vocabulary:** heavy, light, hold, feel, guess

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: heavy and light baskets; Station 2: guess and check pairs; Station 3: wet and dry sponges; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the heavy and light song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which is heavy?" and "Can you guess before you lift?" Children: Child guesses and checks by lifting.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use soft and light objects only; keep water supervised; wipe spills at once.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to explain why one object is heavy.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: identifies the heavier object; makes a guess and checks it; uses heavy and light.

- **Ask:** Which is heavy?
  **Expected response:** The heavier object.
- **Ask:** Can you find a light one?
  **Expected response:** Child finds a light object.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Compares weight with growing confidence

### Conclusion
Sing the heavy and light song and tidy up.

### Home connection
Ask your child to guess which of two things is heavier.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show they can compare weights at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)

- Heavy, light

**Picture suggestions:**
