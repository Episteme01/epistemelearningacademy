# Toddler Numeracy — Term 2, Week 5: Numerals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W05 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Meeting 4 and 5; Putting numerals in order; Numeral and quantity games

## Learning objectives
- Children will recognise the numerals 4 and 5 with support.
- Children will place numeral cards 1 to 5 in order with help.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, in order, next, number line

**Content:** Meeting the numerals 4 and 5, matching numerals 1 to 5 to groups, and putting numeral cards in order.

**Teaching activities:** Show each new numeral large and trace it in the air; always pair a numeral with a group; make the number line from left to right as children say the numbers.

**Learner activities:** Match numerals to groups to five, place cards in order, and play numeral and quantity games.

**Resources:** Numeral cards 1 to 5; Dot cards to five; Large blocks; A string or paper strip as a number line; Soft toys

**Assessment:** Check numeral-to-group matching to five with support and ordering of numeral cards from one to five.

## Expected outcomes
- Matches numerals 1 to 5 to groups with support.
- Orders 1 to 3 independently and 1 to 5 with help.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: left to right order and sequencing; Physical Development: carrying and placing cards; Social Habits: taking turns

## Day 1 (Monday): Meet four and five

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Meeting 4 and 5

### Learning objectives
- Children will recognise the numerals 4 and 5 and match them to groups of four and five.

### Success criteria
- Points to the numeral 4 or 5 when asked
- Matches a numeral to a group

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match numerals 1 to 3 to groups, count to five with support and trace numerals in sand.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, in order, next, number line

**Instructional materials:** Numeral cards 4 and 5; Dot cards for four and five; Large blocks

### Starter
Hold up four blocks and ask "How many?" Count them together. Show the numeral 4 and say "This is how we write four."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Trace the numeral 4 in the air and say "Down, across, down." Place four blocks beside the card. Children: Children trace in the air and count the blocks.

Step 2. Teacher: Do the same for the numeral 5 with five blocks. Say "Five. A straight line, a belly, a hat." Children: Children trace the numeral 5 and count five blocks.

Step 3. Teacher: Mix the numeral cards 1 to 5 and ask a child to find the 4. Children: Child points to the numeral 4 and says "four".

Step 4. Teacher: Show a dot card and ask "Which numeral?" Children: Children match the dot card to the numeral card.

### Practical activity: Four and five match
1. Give each pair numeral cards 4 and 5 and a basket of blocks.
2. One child takes a card; the partner counts out that number of blocks.
3. Swap and repeat.

**Safety:** Use blocks larger than a child’s fist. Keep cards and blocks on the mat.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Match numeral 4 only with a group of four lined up beside it.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find the numeral for a group chosen by a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you show me the number 5?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the numeral 5.
- **Ask:** How many blocks go with the number 4?
  **Expected response:** Four.

### Expected outcomes
- Recognises numerals 4 and 5 with support
- Matches numerals 4 and 5 to groups

### Conclusion
Hold up the numeral 5 and five fingers. Say "Five!" and clap five times.

### Home connection
Find numbers four and five on a clock or a phone and say them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "recognise the numerals 4 and 5 and match them to groups of four and five" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 4 (four dots)
- 5 (five dots)

**Picture suggestions:**
- Numeral cards 4 and 5 with dots

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Putting numbers in order

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Putting numerals in order

### Learning objectives
- Children will put the numeral cards 1 to 3 in order and 1 to 5 with help.

### Success criteria
- Places cards 1, 2 and 3 in order
- Says the numbers as they place them

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match numerals 1 to 3 to groups, count to five with support and trace numerals in sand.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, in order, next, number line

**Instructional materials:** Numeral cards 1 to 5; A string number line on the floor; A basket

### Starter
Show the cards in a jumble and say "Oh no, the numbers are mixed up! Can you help me put them in order?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Start the line by placing the numeral 1 at the left. Say "One goes first." Children: Children watch and say "one".

Step 2. Teacher: Ask a child to find the next numeral, 2. Place it beside the 1. Children: Child chooses the 2 and places it in the line.

Step 3. Teacher: Continue to 3, then 4 and 5, with children placing each card. Children: Children place cards and say the numbers.

Step 4. Teacher: Mix the cards and let pairs build the number line again. Children: Pairs order the cards and check by counting.

### Practical activity: Number line build
1. Lay a string line on the floor.
2. Children take turns placing a numeral card in order.
3. They count along the line when finished.

**Safety:** Fix the string to the floor with tape so it does not trip anyone. Keep cards large and smooth.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Order only 1, 2 and 3 with the cards already near the line.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a missing number after you remove one card.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What number comes after two?
  **Expected response:** Three.
- **Ask:** Can you put the cards in order?
  **Expected response:** Child places cards one to three or five in order.

### Expected outcomes
- Orders numerals 1 to 3
- Orders 1 to 5 with help

### Conclusion
Point along the number line and count from one to five together.

### Home connection
Count along a row of items on the table or step on numbered stones.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "put the numeral cards 1 to 3 in order and 1 to 5 with help" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 2 3 4 5 (in a row)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A string number line with cards

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Which numeral, which group?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Numeral and quantity games

### Learning objectives
- Children will match numerals 1 to 5 to groups of objects.

### Success criteria
- Counts out the number shown on a numeral card
- Matches a numeral to a group

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match numerals 1 to 3 to groups, count to five with support and trace numerals in sand.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, in order, next, number line

**Instructional materials:** Numeral cards 1 to 5; Large blocks; Five small trays

### Starter
Hold a numeral card behind your back and say "I am thinking of a number. It tells how many blocks to take." Reveal the card.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the numeral 3 and count out three blocks into a tray. Children: Children count with you and check.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the numeral 5 and let a child count out five blocks. Children: Child counts five blocks into a tray.

Step 3. Teacher: Show a tray with a group and ask "Which numeral matches?" Children: Children point to the matching numeral.

Step 4. Teacher: Play "numeral and tray": each child picks a card and fills a tray. Children: Child counts out blocks to match their numeral.

### Practical activity: Fill the tray
1. Place numeral cards face down.
2. A child picks a card and fills a tray with that many blocks.
3. The partner checks by counting.

**Safety:** Use blocks that are too large to swallow; sit on the floor mat; supervise all trays.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use only numerals 1 to 3 and place the blocks in a row beside the card.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find two different numerals that make the same group when added by counting on.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you put four blocks in the tray?
  **Expected response:** Child places four blocks.
- **Ask:** Which numeral matches this tray?
  **Expected response:** The numeral for the group.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts out a group to match a numeral
- Matches numerals 1 to 5 to groups with support

### Conclusion
Count each tray aloud and say "The numbers match."

### Home connection
Show your child a number and ask them to bring that many spoons.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match numerals 1 to 5 to groups of objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Take 3
- Take 5

**Picture suggestions:**
- A tray with five blocks beside the numeral 5

## Day 4 (Thursday): Numeral line walk

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Putting numerals in order

### Learning objectives
- Children will walk along a floor number line saying the numbers from one to five.

### Success criteria
- Steps on each numeral and says the number
- Stops at the numeral called

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match numerals 1 to 3 to groups, count to five with support and trace numerals in sand.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, in order, next, number line

**Instructional materials:** Numeral cards 1 to 5 taped to the floor in a line; A drum; A soft toy

### Starter
Beat the drum and say "A number path! Let us walk along it and say the numbers."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Walk slowly along the line stepping on each card and saying the number. Children: Children watch, then walk after you.

Step 2. Teacher: Call "Stand on 3!" and "Stand on 5!" Children: Children walk to the numeral called and stand on it.

Step 3. Teacher: Place a soft toy on a numeral and ask "Where is Teddy? On which number?" Children: Children say the number where the toy is.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child be the caller while the others stand on the numeral. Children: Child calls a number and the group moves to it.

### Practical activity: Number path game
1. Tape five numeral cards to the floor.
2. Children walk along them saying each number.
3. They stop at the numeral called by a friend.

**Safety:** Tape the cards securely and check the floor is dry; one child at a time on the line with an adult nearby.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use only three numerals and walk beside the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to walk backwards from five to one.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What number are you standing on?
  **Expected response:** The numeral on the card under the child.
- **Ask:** Can you stand on number 4?
  **Expected response:** Child finds and stands on the numeral 4.

### Expected outcomes
- Walks along a number line saying numbers
- Finds a named numeral

### Conclusion
Walk the line one last time saying "one, two, three, four, five" and sit down.

### Home connection
Draw numbers with chalk on the ground and hop along them with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "walk along a floor number line saying the numbers from one to five" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 2 3 4 5 (path)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A floor number path

## Day 5 (Friday): Numerals one to five stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Numeral and quantity games

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can recognise, order and match numerals 1 to 5 at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Matches numerals 1 to 5 to groups with support
- Orders numerals 1 to 3 independently

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match numerals 1 to 3 to groups, count to five with support and trace numerals in sand.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, in order, next, number line

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: numeral and tray; Station 2: number line; Station 3: number path; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the numeral song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which numeral goes here?" and "What comes next?" Children: Child answers by pointing or saying.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Remove small items; tape floor cards securely; supervise each station.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to lead the number path game.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: matches numerals 1 to 5 to groups; orders 1 to 3 independently and 1 to 5 with help.

- **Ask:** What number comes after four?
  **Expected response:** Five.
- **Ask:** Which numeral matches these blocks?
  **Expected response:** The correct numeral, up to five.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Recognises and orders numerals to five

### Conclusion
Sing the numeral song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Count numbers on a phone keypad with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show they can recognise, order and match numerals 1 to 5 at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 2 3 4 5

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
