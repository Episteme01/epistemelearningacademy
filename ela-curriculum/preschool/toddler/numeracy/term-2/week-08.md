# Toddler Numeracy — Term 2, Week 8: Pattern

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Sound and clap patterns; Colour patterns AB; What comes next?

## Learning objectives
- Children will copy a clap or movement pattern.
- Children will continue a two-colour pattern with support.

**Key vocabulary:** pattern, next, again, clap, stamp

**Content:** Copying and continuing simple two-part patterns with claps, movements, colours and objects.

**Teaching activities:** Say the pattern aloud as you make it, "red, blue, red, blue"; point to each item; pause before the next item and ask "What comes next?"

**Learner activities:** Copy clap and stamp patterns, continue a colour pattern, make an AB pattern with objects and dance a pattern.

**Resources:** Red and blue blocks or cups; Coloured cloths; A drum; Large wooden beads on a string with a knot (too large to swallow); Leaves and stones too large to swallow

**Assessment:** Check whether each child copies a two-part pattern and adds the next object with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Copies a two-part clap pattern.
- Adds the next object to an AB pattern with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: rhythm and sequencing words; Physical Development: clapping and stamping; Expressive arts: dance and music

## Day 1 (Monday): Clap, stamp, clap, stamp

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Sound and clap patterns

### Learning objectives
- Children will copy a clap and stamp pattern.

### Success criteria
- Copies the clap and stamp pattern
- Keeps going for at least two repeats

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour, match, and copy clapping, counting and movement actions.

**Key vocabulary:** pattern, next, again, clap, stamp

**Instructional materials:** A drum; An open floor space

### Starter
Clap and stamp in a steady beat: "Clap, stamp, clap, stamp." Say "I made a pattern. Can you do it with me?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Do the pattern slowly four times while saying "clap, stamp" and look at the children. Children: Children watch and join in on clap and stamp.

Step 2. Teacher: Stop and ask "What comes next after clap?" Children: Children stamp or say "stamp".

Step 3. Teacher: Try a new pattern: pat knees, clap, pat knees, clap. Children: Children copy the new pattern.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child lead a pattern for the group. Children: Child makes a two-part pattern and the group copies.

### Practical activity: Pattern circle
1. Stand in a circle with space between children.
2. Make a clap and stamp pattern.
3. Pass the leader role around the circle.

**Safety:** Give children space to avoid bumping; stamp softly on a clear floor; stop if the group becomes too excited.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the child’s hands to clap, then guide the foot to stamp.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to invent a new two-part pattern and lead the group.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** After clap, stamp, clap, what comes next?
  **Expected response:** Stamp.
- **Ask:** Can you make a pattern?
  **Expected response:** Child makes a two-part pattern.

### Expected outcomes
- Copies a two-part clap pattern
- Continues the pattern with support

### Conclusion
Beat a slow drum pattern and tiptoe away. Say "Pattern finished."

### Home connection
Make clap and tap patterns with your child on the table or a bucket.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "copy a clap and stamp pattern" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Clap, stamp, clap, stamp

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children clapping and stamping

**Song or rhyme:** Clap, stamp, clap, stamp, that is a pattern. Clap, stamp, clap, stamp, what comes next?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Red, blue, red, blue

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Colour patterns AB

### Learning objectives
- Children will continue a red and blue pattern with blocks or cups.

### Success criteria
- Adds the next object in the pattern
- Says the colours while building

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour, match, and copy clapping, counting and movement actions.

**Key vocabulary:** pattern, next, again, clap, stamp

**Instructional materials:** Red and blue blocks or cups; A mat; A basket

### Starter
Lay out three objects: red, blue, red. Ask "What comes next?" and wait.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Make a pattern with blocks: red, blue, red, blue. Say each colour as you place it. Children: Children say the colours with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Stop and ask "What comes next?" Hold up a red block and a blue block. Children: Child chooses the correct block and places it.

Step 3. Teacher: Say the pattern from the start and point to each block. Children: Children say the pattern aloud.

Step 4. Teacher: Give each pair a basket of blocks and let them make a pattern. Children: Pairs make a red and blue pattern.

### Practical activity: Block patterns
1. Give each pair red and blue blocks.
2. One child starts the pattern with two blocks.
3. The partner adds the next blocks.

**Safety:** Use large blocks, not small ones; keep everything on the mat.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Start the pattern and leave only one block to add.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a pattern with three colours.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What comes after red, blue, red?
  **Expected response:** Blue.
- **Ask:** Can you make a pattern?
  **Expected response:** Child builds a red and blue pattern.

### Expected outcomes
- Continues a colour pattern
- Says the colours in the pattern

### Conclusion
Say the pattern together from start to end and point to each block.

### Home connection
Make a pattern with two kinds of socks or spoons.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "continue a red and blue pattern with blocks or cups" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Red, blue, red, blue

**Picture suggestions:**
- A red and blue block pattern

## Day 3 (Wednesday): What comes next?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** What comes next?

### Learning objectives
- Children will find the missing object in a two-part pattern.

### Success criteria
- Chooses the next object
- Explains with a word or gesture

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour, match, and copy clapping, counting and movement actions.

**Key vocabulary:** pattern, next, again, clap, stamp

**Instructional materials:** A pattern strip with cups or leaves; A basket of extra objects; A cloth

### Starter
Say "I am hiding the next one. Can you guess what is under the cloth?" Cover the last object in a pattern.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Make a pattern with cups: tall, short, tall, short. Cover the last one with a cloth. Children: Children look at the pattern and guess what is under the cloth.

Step 2. Teacher: Lift the cloth and check the guess. Children: Children say "yes" or "no" and say the pattern aloud.

Step 3. Teacher: Give each child a pattern strip with the last item missing. Children: Child chooses the next object from a basket.

Step 4. Teacher: Switch to a sound pattern and ask what comes next. Children: Children say or play the next sound.

### Practical activity: Missing one
1. Lay out a pattern of five objects.
2. Cover the last one.
3. Children choose the right object to put there.

**Safety:** Use large objects only; supervise the basket; no small items.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer only the correct object and one clearly different one.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a mistake in a pattern and fix it.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What comes next?
  **Expected response:** The object that continues the pattern.
- **Ask:** Is this the same pattern?
  **Expected response:** Yes if repeating, no if not.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds the next item in a pattern
- Explains with gestures

### Conclusion
Say the whole pattern aloud with the group and clap on the last item.

### Home connection
Ask your child to guess what comes next in a row of fruit or spoons.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find the missing object in a two-part pattern" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- What comes next?

**Picture suggestions:**
- A pattern with a hidden object

## Day 4 (Thursday): Pattern dance

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Sound and clap patterns

### Learning objectives
- Children will make and follow patterns in a dance using movements and drums.

### Success criteria
- Follows a movement pattern
- Makes a pattern with a friend

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour, match, and copy clapping, counting and movement actions.

**Key vocabulary:** pattern, next, again, clap, stamp

**Instructional materials:** A drum; Coloured scarves in two colours; An open floor space

### Starter
Beat the drum and say "Let us dance a pattern!" Hold up a red and a blue scarf alternately.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Dance a pattern: hop, spin, hop, spin. Say the actions aloud. Children: Children copy the dance pattern.

Step 2. Teacher: Hold up red then blue scarves. Say "Wave red, wave blue." Pause. Children: Children wave the scarf that matches.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child make a two-move dance for a friend to copy. Children: Child makes a pattern and the friend copies it.

Step 4. Teacher: Dance a pattern with the whole class to the drum. Children: Children dance and repeat the pattern.

### Practical activity: Pattern dance
1. Give each child two scarves.
2. Follow the leader’s two-move pattern.
3. Change leader every minute.

**Safety:** Allow lots of space; use light scarves and keep them away from necks; no running.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Dance beside the child and copy their moves first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a three-move pattern.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is the pattern?
  **Expected response:** Child names or shows the two moves.
- **Ask:** What comes after hop?
  **Expected response:** Spin, or the next move in the pattern.

### Expected outcomes
- Follows and makes a movement pattern
- Works with a partner

### Conclusion
Slow the drum and finish with a calm pattern: "sit, stand, sit, stand."

### Home connection
Make a dance pattern with a family member and teach it to the family.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make and follow patterns in a dance using movements and drums" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Hop, spin, hop, spin

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Pattern stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Colour patterns AB

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can copy and continue patterns at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Copies a two-part pattern
- Adds the next item with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour, match, and copy clapping, counting and movement actions.

**Key vocabulary:** pattern, next, again, clap, stamp

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: clap and drum patterns; Station 2: block patterns; Station 3: missing one game; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the pattern song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "What comes next?" and watch for the child’s choice. Children: Child chooses or says the next item.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use large objects only; supervise stations; keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to invent a new pattern and teach a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: copies a two-part clap or movement pattern; adds the next object to an AB pattern with support.

- **Ask:** What comes next?
  **Expected response:** The next item in the pattern.
- **Ask:** Can you make a pattern?
  **Expected response:** Child makes a pattern.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Copies and continues patterns

### Conclusion
Sing the pattern song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Make patterns with objects at home this week.

- Which children could show "show they can copy and continue patterns at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
