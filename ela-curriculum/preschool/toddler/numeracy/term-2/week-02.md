# Toddler Numeracy — Term 2, Week 2: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Five little rhymes with fingers; Count down and count up; Jumping to five

## Learning objectives
- Children will join in counting rhymes to five.
- Children will show up to five fingers with help.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, count, up, down, fingers

**Content:** Saying one to five in order in action rhymes, showing up to five fingers, counting down and counting up.

**Teaching activities:** Lead rhymes with a steady beat and finger actions; sing each rhyme slowly first, then faster; show each finger as it is named.

**Learner activities:** Say numbers to five with actions, show fingers, count down by lowering fingers, and jump to five.

**Resources:** A drum; Five plastic cups; Five toy birds or paper birds; A mango tree picture; Number picture cards with dots to five

**Assessment:** Listen for who joins rhymes to five and who shows up to five fingers; note on the class record.

## Expected outcomes
- Says the numbers one to five in order with the group.
- Shows five fingers with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: rhythm, rhyme and voice play; Science: birds and trees; Physical Development: finger and body movement

## Day 1 (Monday): Five little fingers

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Five little rhymes with fingers

### Learning objectives
- Children will join in a finger rhyme to five and show each finger as it is named.

### Success criteria
- Shows fingers one at a time as the rhyme goes
- Says some of the numbers four and five

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say one, two, three in rhymes, show up to three fingers and take part in counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, count, up, down, fingers

**Instructional materials:** A drum; A mat; Dot cards for four and five

### Starter
Wave one hand. Say "I have fingers on my hand! Let us count them: one, two, three, four, five." Raise each finger slowly.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Teach the rhyme "One finger, two fingers, three fingers, four, five fingers waving, waving, waving more." Lift each finger as it is named. Children: Children watch and copy the finger actions.

Step 2. Teacher: Say the rhyme again and invite the children to say the numbers they know. Children: Children say the numbers one to three and listen for four and five.

Step 3. Teacher: Show the dot cards for four and five. Count the dots slowly with your finger. Children: Children touch the dots and say the numbers.

Step 4. Teacher: Play "freeze fingers": call a number from one to five and children show that many fingers. Children: Children show the number of fingers called, looking at a friend to check.

### Practical activity: Finger rhyme circle
1. Sit in a circle with hands ready.
2. Say the rhyme and lift a finger for each number.
3. Pass a drum and let each child beat once for each number.

**Safety:** Keep hands away from faces to avoid poking eyes; the drum is played softly.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use only three fingers, and lift each finger for the child while saying the number.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say the rhyme alone and show the correct number of fingers.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Show me five fingers.
  **Expected response:** Child shows five fingers, possibly after copying.
- **Ask:** What comes after three?
  **Expected response:** Four.

### Expected outcomes
- Shows up to five fingers
- Joins the rhyme to five

### Conclusion
Close your fingers into a fist and say "Five fingers sleep." Open and wiggle them to wake up.

### Home connection
Say the finger rhyme at bath time or while waiting in the car.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "join in a finger rhyme to five and show each finger as it is named" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 2 3 4 5 (five dots in a row)
- Five fingers (hand picture)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A hand with five fingers
- Children in a circle showing fingers

**Song or rhyme:** One finger, two fingers, three fingers, four, five fingers waving, waving, waving more.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Count up, count down

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Count down and count up

### Learning objectives
- Children will join in counting up from one to five and counting down from five.

### Success criteria
- Joins counting up to five
- Lowers a finger for each number when counting down

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say one, two, three in rhymes, show up to three fingers and take part in counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, count, up, down, fingers

**Instructional materials:** Five plastic cups; A tray; A soft toy

### Starter
Build a tower of five cups slowly and count up: "One, two, three, four, five!" Then say "Now let us knock the tower down and count backwards."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Stack the cups one at a time while saying each number aloud, holding the last cup high on "five". Children: Children say the numbers with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Take cups away one at a time. Say "Five, four, three, two, one, all gone!" Children: Children lower a finger for each number as the cups disappear.

Step 3. Teacher: Let a child stack the cups while the group counts up. Children: Child stacks cups and the group says the numbers.

Step 4. Teacher: Let another child take them away while the group counts down. Children: Child removes cups and the group counts down.

### Practical activity: Cup tower count
1. Give each pair five cups.
2. One child builds the tower while the partner counts up.
3. Then they take it apart while counting down.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable cups and build on a low surface on the floor. Keep cups out of mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count up and down only to three, and say the numbers with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count down from five alone and say "blast off" at the end.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many cups are left?
  **Expected response:** The correct number as the tower is taken down.
- **Ask:** What comes before three?
  **Expected response:** Two.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts up to five with a group
- Joins counting down

### Conclusion
Hold the last cup and say "All gone!" Tidy the cups together.

### Home connection
Count down from five at bedtime: "Five more minutes, four, three, two, one."

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "join in counting up from one to five and counting down from five" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Up: 1 2 3 4 5
- Down: 5 4 3 2 1

**Picture suggestions:**
- A tower of five cups

**Song or rhyme:** Five, four, three, two, one, all gone! One, two, three, four, five, up again!

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Jump to five

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Jumping to five

### Learning objectives
- Children will jump or step once for each number up to five.

### Success criteria
- Moves once for each number said
- Stops at five

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say one, two, three in rhymes, show up to three fingers and take part in counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, count, up, down, fingers

**Instructional materials:** An open floor space; Five paper footprints; A drum

### Starter
Jump one, two, three, four, five times with the children watching. Say "Five jumps! Can you do it?" Invite them to join.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Lay five paper footprints in a line. Step on each and say the number. Children: Children watch, then step with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Beat the drum five times slowly. Say "Jump with the drum." Children: Children jump gently once for each beat.

Step 3. Teacher: Call "Jump three!" or "Jump five!" and show the number on your fingers. Children: Children jump the number called and then stop.

Step 4. Teacher: Finish with a calm game: "Tiptoe five steps to your seat." Children: Children tiptoe five steps and sit down.

### Practical activity: Footprint jumps
1. Tape five footprints in a line.
2. Each child steps on them while the group counts.
3. Repeat, stopping at a number called.

**Safety:** Tape footprints securely; check the floor is dry and clear; children go one at a time with an adult beside them.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use three footprints and hold the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to jump to five, then count back to one.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many jumps did you make?
  **Expected response:** Five, or the number called.
- **Ask:** Can you stop at three?
  **Expected response:** Child stops after the third jump.

### Expected outcomes
- Moves once for each number up to five
- Stops at a named number

### Conclusion
Tiptoe to the mat saying "Five steps, sit down."

### Home connection
Count five steps on the stairs or to the door at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "jump or step once for each number up to five" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Jump 1 2 3 4 5

**Picture suggestions:**
- Five footprints in a line

## Day 4 (Thursday): Five birds on the mango tree

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Count down and count up

### Learning objectives
- Children will count five birds going up and down in a story with actions.

### Success criteria
- Says a number for each bird
- Joins in counting down

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say one, two, three in rhymes, show up to three fingers and take part in counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, count, up, down, fingers

**Instructional materials:** Five toy or paper birds; A picture of a mango tree; Sticky tack or a flannel board

### Starter
Show the mango tree picture. Say "Look! A big mango tree. Who lives in the tree? Birds! Let us count them."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Place five birds on the tree. Count them slowly: "One, two, three, four, five." Children: Children count the birds with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell the story. Remove one bird at a time and ask "How many birds now?" Children: Children look and say the number of birds left.

Step 3. Teacher: When no birds are left, ask "Where are the birds?" and wait. Children: Children say "gone" or "all gone".

Step 4. Teacher: Bring the birds back one at a time as you count up. Children: Children count up and flap their arms like wings.

### Practical activity: Bird tree counting
1. Give each pair a tree picture and five paper birds.
2. One child removes birds while the partner counts down.
3. Then they bring the birds back and count up.

**Safety:** Use large paper or card birds with rounded corners. Keep sticky tack away from mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use three birds only and count each one aloud with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell the story using fingers and hide one finger at a time.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many birds were on the tree at the start?
  **Expected response:** Five.
- **Ask:** How many birds are left when two fly away from five?
  **Expected response:** Three, counted by looking.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts down from five in a story
- Counts up to five again

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the birds and say "Tweet tweet, goodnight." Place the birds in a basket.

### Home connection
Count birds, trees or chickens in your compound and say the numbers.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count five birds going up and down in a story with actions" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 5 birds
- 4 birds
- 3 birds
- 2 birds
- 1 bird
- No birds

**Picture suggestions:**
- A mango tree with five birds

**Song or rhyme:** Five little birds on the mango tree, tweet, tweet, tweet. One flew away, now there are four, tweet, tweet, tweet.

**Short story:** Five little birds sat on the mango tree. Tweet, tweet! One flew away to find a worm. Now there were four. Tweet, tweet! Another flew to the river. Now there were three. Two flew home. Now there was one. Then the last bird flew away. No birds left! In the morning the birds came back. One, two, three, four, five. Tweet, tweet!

## Day 5 (Friday): Rhyme stations to five

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Five little rhymes with fingers

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can say, show and count to five at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Shows up to five fingers
- Says numbers in order to five with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say one, two, three in rhymes, show up to three fingers and take part in counting games.

**Key vocabulary:** four, five, count, up, down, fingers

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: finger rhyme cards; Station 2: cup tower; Station 3: five birds and tree; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the finger rhyme and show the three stations. Say "At each station we say, show and count to five."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Divide the children into three groups and rotate every four minutes. Children: Children play at each station with a partner.

Step 2. Teacher: At the finger station, ask "Show me five fingers" and "Show me four." Children: Child shows the number of fingers called.

Step 3. Teacher: At the cup station, ask "Can you count the cups up and down?" Children: Child counts cups and says the numbers.

Step 4. Teacher: Record each child’s level and photograph their work. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes one station per round.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable cups and large birds; supervise each station closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Keep the child at one station with an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to lead the count at the cup station.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: joins the rhyme to five; shows up to five fingers; counts down from five with support.

- **Ask:** Show me four fingers.
  **Expected response:** Child shows four fingers.
- **Ask:** What comes after four?
  **Expected response:** Five.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Joins rhymes to five

### Conclusion
Sing the finger rhyme once more and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Say the finger rhyme every day this week.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show they can say, show and count to five at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 1 2 3 4 5

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of the stations
