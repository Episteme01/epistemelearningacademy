# Toddler Numeracy — Term 2, Week 3: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Touch and count four and five; How many now?; Counting while moving

## Learning objectives
- Children will count up to five objects with one touch for each.
- Children will say how many using the last number word.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, four, five, last

**Content:** Counting four and five real objects by touching each one once, answering how many, and counting while moving.

**Teaching activities:** Count slowly with a clear touch and slide for every object; repeat the last number as the answer; keep groups small and lined up.

**Learner activities:** Touch and move objects one at a time, count four and five, say how many, and count while moving.

**Resources:** Large wooden blocks; Toy fruits; A basket; Five plastic cups; Soft balls

**Assessment:** Check one-to-one touching, order of number words and whether the child says how many with the last number.

## Expected outcomes
- Counts up to three objects reliably and up to five with support.
- Gives how many with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: number words and questions; Physical Development: moving objects one at a time; Social Habits: taking turns to count

## Day 1 (Monday): Touch, slide and count four and five

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Touch and count four and five

### Learning objectives
- Children will touch and slide each of four or five objects while saying the numbers in order.

### Success criteria
- Touches each object once
- Says the numbers in order up to four or five

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say the numbers to five in rhymes and count up to three objects by touching each one.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, four, five, last

**Instructional materials:** Five large wooden blocks; Two trays; A mat

### Starter
Show five blocks in a line. Say "I have blocks. How many? Let us count them one at a time and slide them." Touch the first block.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Touch and slide each block to the second tray as you say the numbers one to four slowly. Children: Children watch and say the numbers with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Add a fifth block and count again to five. Say "Five blocks. There are five." Children: Children say "five" and show five fingers.

Step 3. Teacher: Invite a child to touch and slide while the group counts aloud. Children: Child touches and slides each block while the others say the numbers.

Step 4. Teacher: Count again from the other end of the line and ask "Is it still five?" Children: Children nod or say "five" to show it is the same.

### Practical activity: Block slide count
1. Give each pair a tray of blocks and an empty tray.
2. One child slides and says each number; the partner shows the fingers.
3. Swap roles and try four, then five blocks.

**Safety:** Use blocks larger than a child’s fist. Keep trays on the floor with children seated.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count only to three and guide the child’s finger touch by touch.
- **Extension:** Count to five and then show the number on a numeral card without help.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many blocks are on the tray?
  **Expected response:** Four or five, as counted.
- **Ask:** Can you touch each block just once?
  **Expected response:** Child touches each block once.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts four and five objects with one touch for each
- Says the last number as the answer

### Conclusion
Slide all the blocks back saying "five, four, three, two, one, home."

### Home connection
Count five spoons or five shoes at home by touching and sliding each one.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "touch and slide each of four or five objects while saying the numbers in order" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Touch, slide, say
- Five blocks

**Picture suggestions:**
- Five blocks in a row
- A child sliding blocks

**Song or rhyme:** Touch one, touch two, touch three, touch four, touch five, blocks and me.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Five fruits in the basket

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Touch and count four and five

### Learning objectives
- Children will count five toy fruits into a basket and say how many.

### Success criteria
- Counts fruits one at a time into the basket
- Says how many when all are in

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say the numbers to five in rhymes and count up to three objects by touching each one.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, four, five, last

**Instructional materials:** Five toy fruits (orange, mango, banana, pawpaw, lemon); A basket; A market cloth

### Starter
Spread a market cloth and place five fruits in a row. Say "A market stall! Let us put the fruits into the basket and count."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pick up one fruit, name it, and put it in the basket saying "one". Continue to five. Children: Children watch and say the numbers.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "How many fruits are in the basket?" Wait. Children: Children say or show five.

Step 3. Teacher: Take the fruits out one by one and count them again. Children: Children count the fruits as they come out.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask each child to put in three fruits and say how many, then five. Children: Child places the fruits and counts them.

### Practical activity: Fruit basket count
1. Give each pair a basket and five toy fruits.
2. One child counts fruits into the basket; the other checks by counting again.
3. Swap and try again.

**Safety:** Toy fruits must be bigger than a child’s fist. Do not use real small fruits for counting games.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count only three fruits and say each number as the fruit goes in.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count five fruits into the basket and then take away two and say how many are left.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many fruits are in the basket?
  **Expected response:** Five, or the number counted.
- **Ask:** Can you put in three fruits?
  **Expected response:** Child counts out three fruits.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts up to five objects into a basket
- Says how many in a game

### Conclusion
Pretend to carry the basket home from the market. Say "Five fruits. Thank you!"

### Home connection
Count fruits at home, such as five oranges in a bowl, when helping to wash them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count five toy fruits into a basket and say how many" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- 5 fruits
- Count and say

**Picture suggestions:**
- A basket with five fruits

## Day 3 (Wednesday): How many now?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** How many now?

### Learning objectives
- Children will count a group, see one object added or removed, and say how many now.

### Success criteria
- Recounts after a change
- Says the new number

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say the numbers to five in rhymes and count up to three objects by touching each one.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, four, five, last

**Instructional materials:** Five cups; A tray; A cloth to cover objects

### Starter
Put three cups on a tray and ask "How many?" Then add a cup behind your back and say "Something changed. Let us count again."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show three cups, count them, then add one in front of the children. Say "Now count again." Children: Children count and say "four".

Step 2. Teacher: Remove one cup and ask "How many now?" Children: Children count and say "three".

Step 3. Teacher: Cover the cups with a cloth, lift it and ask "How many now?" Let them check by counting. Children: Children count and give the new number.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child be the teacher: add or remove a cup and ask the group. Children: Child changes the cups and the group counts.

### Practical activity: Change and count
1. Give each pair a tray with up to five cups.
2. One child adds or removes a cup while the other covers their eyes.
3. The partner counts and says how many now.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable cups and keep them on the tray; supervise to keep cups out of mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Change by one cup only between two and three.
- **Extension:** Change by two cups and ask the child to say how many were added or removed.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many cups now?
  **Expected response:** The correct number after the change.
- **Ask:** Did I add or take away?
  **Expected response:** Child points or says add or take away.

### Expected outcomes
- Recounts after a change
- Says how many now

### Conclusion
Gather the cups and count them one last time together, saying the final number.

### Home connection
Ask "how many now?" when a biscuit is eaten or added at snack time.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count a group, see one object added or removed, and say how many now" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- How many now?

**Picture suggestions:**
- Cups added and removed

## Day 4 (Thursday): Collect five

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Counting while moving

### Learning objectives
- Children will collect up to five objects while moving and count them.

### Success criteria
- Collects the number of objects asked for
- Counts them aloud or with fingers

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say the numbers to five in rhymes and count up to three objects by touching each one.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, four, five, last

**Instructional materials:** Ten soft balls scattered on the floor; A large basket; A drum

### Starter
Roll the soft balls around the room. Say "Let us go on a ball hunt. How many balls can we collect in the basket?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "Collect three balls." Pick up each ball and say its number as it goes in the basket. Children: Children collect three balls and count aloud.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Now collect five." Watch as each child collects and counts. Children: Children collect five balls and say the numbers.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "How many are in the basket?" and let the group check by counting. Children: Children count again and say the final number.

Step 4. Teacher: Change the task: "Collect four balls and take them to the shelf." Children: Child collects four balls, counts, and carries them to the shelf.

### Practical activity: Ball hunt
1. Scatter ten soft balls around the room.
2. Call a number from one to five.
3. Children collect that many and place them in a basket.

**Safety:** Use soft balls larger than a fist and keep the floor free of other objects. Children walk, not run.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Collect only two balls at a time with an adult beside the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to collect five balls without help and tell the group how many.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many balls are in your basket?
  **Expected response:** The number asked for, up to five.
- **Ask:** Can you collect three?
  **Expected response:** Child collects exactly three.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts while moving
- Collects up to five objects

### Conclusion
Tip the balls out and roll them back to the middle while saying the numbers.

### Home connection
Ask your child to collect five spoons or five toys and count them as they bring them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "collect up to five objects while moving and count them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Collect 5

**Picture suggestions:**
- Children collecting balls

## Day 5 (Friday): Counting to five stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** How many now?

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can count to five and say how many at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Touches each object once
- Says how many for up to five

**Previous knowledge:** Children can say the numbers to five in rhymes and count up to three objects by touching each one.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, four, five, last

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: blocks and trays; Station 2: fruit basket; Station 3: cups and cloth; Class record sheet

### Starter
Show the three stations and say "Today we count to five at each station. Remember to touch each one."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children count at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How many?" and watch for one touch per object. Children: Child counts and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "How many now?" after adding or removing an object at one station. Children: Child recounts and says the new number.

Step 4. Teacher: Note each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Remove small items and keep walkways clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner at each station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to be the counting leader.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: touches one object for each number; counts to three reliably and to five with support; says how many with the last number.

- **Ask:** How many fruits?
  **Expected response:** The correct number up to five.
- **Ask:** Can you count the blocks again?
  **Expected response:** Child counts again and gets the same number.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Counts to five with support

### Conclusion
Count the children in the circle together, up to five at a time.

### Home connection
Count five things a day with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show they can count to five and say how many at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Count to 5

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of the stations
