# Toddler Numeracy — Term 2, Week 9: Shape

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Squares in our world; Triangles in our world; Circle, square or triangle?

## Learning objectives
- Children will find squares and triangles around them.
- Children will tell a circle from a square with support.

**Key vocabulary:** square, triangle, circle, corner, side

**Content:** Finding and naming squares and triangles in the environment and telling circle, square and triangle apart.

**Teaching activities:** Trace each shape slowly with a finger; count corners and sides only for squares and triangles by touching; place shapes against real objects.

**Learner activities:** Find squares and triangles, trace the shapes, sort circle, square and triangle, and make shape pictures.

**Resources:** Large cardboard shapes (circle, square, triangle); Boxes, tiles, triangle roof pictures, a slice of bread; Shape sorting trays; A shape bag

**Assessment:** Check whether each child names or points to a square, triangle and circle with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Names or points to a square and a circle.
- Points to a triangle when asked.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: shape words and descriptive talk; Physical Development: tracing and carrying shapes; Understanding the world: buildings and objects

## Day 1 (Monday): Square things

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Squares in our world

### Learning objectives
- Children will find and name squares in the environment.

### Success criteria
- Points to a square on request
- Says square

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles and round things, make patterns and use position words.

**Key vocabulary:** square, triangle, circle, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** A large cardboard square; A box, a floor tile and a window picture; A mat

### Starter
Hold up the cardboard square and say "This is a square. It has four straight sides and four corners." Trace its edge with a finger.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Trace the square and count the four sides by touching each one. Count the corners. Children: Children touch each side and corner and count to four.

Step 2. Teacher: Place the square against a box, a tile and a window picture. Say "Look, a square!" Children: Children point to the square shapes.

Step 3. Teacher: Put a circle and a triangle beside the square and ask "Which is the square?" Children: Child points to the square.

Step 4. Teacher: Send children to find a square in the room. Children: Children find square objects and show a friend.

### Practical activity: Square hunt
1. Give each pair a cardboard square.
2. Find things in the room that match its shape.
3. Bring one example back or point to it.

**Safety:** Use cardboard shapes with rounded corners; children walk and do not climb to reach objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer a square and a circle only and hold them near the objects.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find three squares in different places.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you find a square?
  **Expected response:** Child points to a square.
- **Ask:** How many corners has a square?
  **Expected response:** Four, counted by touch.

### Expected outcomes
- Names or points to a square
- Finds squares in the room

### Conclusion
Hold up the square and say "Square, square, square." Trace it in the air.

### Home connection
Look for squares at home such as tiles, windows or boxes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find and name squares in the environment" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SQUARE (4 sides, 4 corners)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A square, a box and a tile

**Song or rhyme:** Square, square, four straight sides. Square, square, four corners.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Triangle things

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Triangles in our world

### Learning objectives
- Children will find and name triangles in the environment.

### Success criteria
- Points to a triangle on request
- Says triangle with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles and round things, make patterns and use position words.

**Key vocabulary:** square, triangle, circle, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** A large cardboard triangle; A roof picture, a sandwich cut in half, a drum stand picture; A mat

### Starter
Hold up the triangle. Say "This is a triangle. It has three straight sides and three corners." Trace its edge slowly.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Trace the triangle and count three sides and three corners with the children. Children: Children touch each side and corner and count to three.

Step 2. Teacher: Show a roof picture and a bread slice cut in half. Say "Triangle roof! Triangle bread!" Children: Children point to the triangle shapes.

Step 3. Teacher: Place the triangle beside a circle and a square. Ask "Which is the triangle?" Children: Child points to the triangle.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk around the room and find triangle shapes. Children: Children point to triangle shapes in the room.

### Practical activity: Triangle hunt
1. Give each pair a cardboard triangle.
2. Match it to roof shapes, slices and pictures.
3. Tell a friend what you found.

**Safety:** Use cardboard shapes with rounded corners; no real knives; slices of bread cut by an adult before the lesson.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer a triangle and a circle only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a triangle and a square and say how they are different.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you show me a triangle?
  **Expected response:** Child points to a triangle.
- **Ask:** How many sides has a triangle?
  **Expected response:** Three, counted by touch.

### Expected outcomes
- Names or points to a triangle
- Finds triangles in pictures

### Conclusion
Trace a triangle in the air and say "Triangle, triangle, three sides."

### Home connection
Look for triangles on roofs, signs or sandwiches.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find and name triangles in the environment" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- TRIANGLE (3 sides, 3 corners)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A triangle roof
- A sandwich cut in half

**Song or rhyme:** Triangle, triangle, three straight sides. Triangle, triangle, three sharp corners.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Circle, square or triangle?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Circle, square or triangle?

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort circles, squares and triangles into three groups.

### Success criteria
- Puts shapes in the right group
- Names or points to each shape

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles and round things, make patterns and use position words.

**Key vocabulary:** square, triangle, circle, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** Large cardboard shapes in several colours; Three trays labelled with a circle, a square and a triangle; A shape bag

### Starter
Pull a shape from the bag and say "What shape is this? Let us find out." Feel it with your fingers first.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pull out a shape, trace its edge and say its name, then place it on the matching tray. Children: Children watch and say the shape name.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child pull a shape from the bag, feel it and decide where it goes. Children: Child places the shape on the correct tray.

Step 3. Teacher: Mix up the shapes on the mat. Ask "Find all the circles." Children: Children collect all circles.

Step 4. Teacher: Repeat for the squares and triangles. Children: Children sort each group into its tray.

### Practical activity: Shape sorting trays
1. Give each pair a bag of shapes and three trays.
2. Take turns to pull a shape and place it.
3. Check each tray together.

**Safety:** Use large cardboard or plastic shapes with smooth edges; keep them on the mat; supervise to avoid mouthing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two shapes only, a circle and a square, with a clear tray for each.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sort shapes by shape and by colour and explain.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is this?
  **Expected response:** Circle, square or triangle.
- **Ask:** Which tray does it go in?
  **Expected response:** The tray with the same shape.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts three shapes
- Names shapes with support

### Conclusion
Count the shapes in each tray together and say the shape name.

### Home connection
Sort plates, tiles and slices of bread by shape.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort circles, squares and triangles into three groups" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Circle
- Square
- Triangle

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three trays with shapes

## Day 4 (Thursday): Shape pictures

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Circle, square or triangle?

### Learning objectives
- Children will make a simple picture using circles, squares and triangles.

### Success criteria
- Uses at least two different shapes in a picture
- Names a shape they used

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles and round things, make patterns and use position words.

**Key vocabulary:** square, triangle, circle, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** Large cardboard shapes; A large sheet of paper; Glue sticks; Crayons

### Starter
Show a picture of a house made of shapes. Say "This house has a square, a triangle roof and a round window. Let us make our own."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Build a house on the paper with a square and a triangle roof. Say the shape names. Children: Children watch and point to the shapes.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a few shapes and ask them to make a house or a car. Children: Child places the shapes to make a picture.

Step 3. Teacher: Walk around and ask "What shape is this?" for each child. Children: Child names or points to a shape in their picture.

Step 4. Teacher: Display the pictures on a table and let children look at each other’s pictures. Children: Children point to the shapes in a friend’s picture.

### Practical activity: Shape house pictures
1. Give each child a sheet of paper and shapes.
2. Arrange the shapes to make a house.
3. Glue them down if they wish.

**Safety:** Use non-toxic glue sticks; supervise glue use; keep shapes large; wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two shapes and show where to place them.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a picture of a boat or a car and name the shapes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What shape is the roof?
  **Expected response:** Triangle.
- **Ask:** Can you find a square in your picture?
  **Expected response:** Child points to a square.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a picture using shapes
- Names shapes in the picture

### Conclusion
Put the pictures on a display and say "Well done, shape builders."

### Home connection
Make a picture with cut-out shapes from old cartons.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make a simple picture using circles, squares and triangles" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- House: square and triangle

**Picture suggestions:**
- A house made of shapes

## Day 5 (Friday): Shape stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T2-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Circle, square or triangle?

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can find and name circles, squares and triangles at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Points to a named shape
- Sorts shapes into groups

**Previous knowledge:** Children can find circles and round things, make patterns and use position words.

**Key vocabulary:** square, triangle, circle, corner, side

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: shape hunt cards; Station 2: sorting trays; Station 3: shape pictures; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the three shape songs and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Show me a triangle" and "Which shape is this?" Children: Child points to or names a shape.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Use smooth large shapes only; supervise stations; keep glue supervised.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to quiz a friend: "Show me a square."

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names or points to a square and a circle; points to a triangle; sorts shapes.

- **Ask:** Show me the triangle.
  **Expected response:** Child points to the triangle.
- **Ask:** What shape is this?
  **Expected response:** The shape name.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Recognises circle, square and triangle with support

### Conclusion
Sing the shape songs and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Look for shapes on the way home and name them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show they can find and name circles, squares and triangles at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

- Circle, square, triangle
