# Toddler Numeracy — Term 1, Week 1: Big and small

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Big and small objects in the classroom; Big and small in my family and compound; Finding the big one and the small one

## Learning objectives
- Children will point to the big or the small one when asked.
- Children will say or sign big and small with support.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, little, bigger, show me

**Content:** Comparing two objects by size and using the words big and small in play, songs and routines.

**Teaching activities:** Model big and small with exaggerated voice and gesture; hold up two objects and ask "Where is the big one?"; narrate children’s actions with the words.

**Learner activities:** Handle, carry and roll big and small objects; point, show and say big or small; match big and small in a basket game.

**Resources:** Adult and child sandals; Big and small plastic cups and bowls; Big and small balls; Big and small drums or tins; Two puppets or soft toys

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child chooses the big or small object on request; record on the class record sheet.

## Expected outcomes
- Chooses the big or small object on request in most turns.
- Uses the words big and small with prompting.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: rhyme and picture book; Social Habits: tidying away in two baskets; Physical Development: carrying and rolling

## Day 1 (Monday): Daddy’s sandal, baby’s sandal

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Big and small objects in the classroom

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to the big sandal or the small sandal when asked.

### Success criteria
- Points to the big sandal on request
- Looks at both sandals before choosing

**Previous knowledge:** No prior mathematics is needed. Children have seen big and small things at home, for example an adult’s sandal and a baby’s sandal.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, little, bigger, show me

**Instructional materials:** One adult sandal and one child sandal; Two puppets or soft toys (one large, one small); Floor mat

### Starter
Sit the children in a half circle on a mat. Put on the adult sandal and say in a big, slow voice, "This is a big sandal. Big!" Then hold up the small sandal and whisper, "This is a small sandal. Small!"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Place the adult sandal and the child sandal side by side on the mat. Point to each and say "big" in a deep voice and "small" in a tiny voice, three times. Children: Children copy the voices and gestures: arms wide for big, fingers close together for small.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Where is the big sandal?" and wait. Gently guide a hand if a child is unsure. Children: Each child takes a turn to touch the big sandal.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Where is the small sandal?" Swap the positions of the sandals and ask again. Children: Children touch the small sandal and say or whisper "small".

Step 4. Teacher: Bring in the two puppets. Say "Big puppet wears the big sandal. Small puppet wears the small sandal." Let each child try putting a sandal on the correct puppet. Children: Children put the sandal on the correct puppet and show the puppet to a friend.

### Practical activity: Sandal parade
1. Line up the children’s own sandals with an adult’s sandal on the mat.
2. Ask each child, one at a time, to find "your small sandal" and then "the big sandal".
3. Children carry their small sandal to the shelf and the big one to the teacher.

**Safety:** Check the floor is dry and clear. Children sit while handling sandals; no walking while wearing adult sandals.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer only two items with a very obvious size difference and use a hand-over-hand point. Say the word for the child and wait for any sign or sound in reply.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another big thing and another small thing in the room and bring them to the mat.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Show me the big sandal.
  **Expected response:** Child points to or touches the adult sandal.
- **Ask:** Which one is small?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the child sandal and may say "small".

### Expected outcomes
- Chooses the big sandal in most turns
- Says or signs big or small with prompting

### Conclusion
Hold up the two sandals one last time. Clap and say "Big! Small!" together. Children put the sandals back in two places: big on the left, small on the right.

### Home connection
At home, find a grown-up’s shoe and a child’s shoe. Say "big" and "small" while you point, then ask your child to find the small one.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to the big sandal or the small sandal when asked" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BIG (large drawing of an adult sandal)
- SMALL (small drawing of a child sandal)

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photo of an adult sandal beside a child sandal
- Two puppets, one large and one small

**Song or rhyme:** Big, big, big (stretch arms wide). Small, small, small (hold fingers close). Big, big, big. Small, small, small. Show me big. Show me small.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Rolling big and small balls

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Big and small objects in the classroom

### Learning objectives
- Children will push and roll a big ball and a small ball and say which is big and which is small with support.

### Success criteria
- Rolls both balls
- Points to the big ball when asked

**Previous knowledge:** No prior mathematics is needed. Children have seen big and small things at home, for example an adult’s sandal and a baby’s sandal.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, little, bigger, show me

**Instructional materials:** One big soft ball and one small soft ball; Two baskets, one large and one small; Cones or a cloth line (optional)

### Starter
Roll the big ball slowly across the floor to a child and say "Here comes the big ball!" Then roll the small ball and say "Here comes the small ball!" Let each child feel them.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sit in a circle. Roll the big ball to a child, say "big ball", and ask them to roll it back. Children: Child rolls the big ball back; the group says "big" as it travels.

Step 2. Teacher: Repeat with the small ball and say "small ball". Children: Children roll the small ball and say or whisper "small".

Step 3. Teacher: Show the two baskets. Say "Big basket for the big ball. Small basket for the small ball." Children: Children take turns to put each ball in the matching basket.

Step 4. Teacher: Mix the balls and ask "Can you find the big ball?" while pointing to neither. Children: Children point to and pick up the big ball.

### Practical activity: Ball and basket game
1. Spread two big and two small soft balls on the floor.
2. Say "Find a big ball." Child brings it to the big basket.
3. Say "Find a small ball." Child brings it to the small basket.

**Safety:** Use soft balls larger than a child’s fist. Keep the floor clear and the group seated except when it is a child’s turn.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use only one ball at a time and say the size word as the ball is placed in the basket.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a bigger ball than the small one by choosing from three sizes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which ball goes in the big basket?
  **Expected response:** The big ball.
- **Ask:** Can you show me the small ball?
  **Expected response:** Child holds up or points to the small ball.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches big ball to big basket
- Says "big" or "small" with prompting

### Conclusion
Sing the "Big, small" rhyme from Day 1 while rolling the balls one more time. Tidy up by placing the big ball and small ball in their baskets together.

### Home connection
Use a small orange and a big orange, or a big and a small spoon, and ask your child to give you the big one.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "push and roll a big ball and a small ball and say which is big and which is small with support" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BIG ball (large circle)
- SMALL ball (small circle)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A big and a small ball
- Two baskets of different sizes

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Find the big cup, find the small cup

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Finding the big one and the small one

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose the big or small cup when asked and say the word with support.

### Success criteria
- Chooses the correct cup in at least two of three turns
- Says or signs one size word

**Previous knowledge:** No prior mathematics is needed. Children have seen big and small things at home, for example an adult’s sandal and a baby’s sandal.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, little, bigger, show me

**Instructional materials:** Four plastic cups: two big, two small; A tray; A bucket of water and a sponge for the outdoor extension (optional)

### Starter
Hold up a big cup and a small cup and say "I am thirsty! Which cup holds more water? The big cup!" Pretend to drink from each cup and let children join in.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the big and small cup on the tray. Ask "Show me the big cup." Children: Child points to or lifts the big cup.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Now show me the small cup." Swap places and ask again. Children: Child points to the small cup and says "small" if able.

Step 3. Teacher: Hold the cups behind your back and ask "Which hand has the big cup?" Reveal slowly. Children: Children point to the hand and shout "big!" or "small!".

Step 4. Teacher: Give each pair of children a tray with a big and small cup. Say "Give your friend the small cup." Children: Child gives the small cup to a friend and says "small" or "for you".

### Practical activity: Cup trays
1. Each child receives a tray with two cups.
2. Teacher calls "big" or "small" and children lift that cup.
3. Swap trays with a friend and play again.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable plastic cups with no sharp edges and no water inside indoors; supervise at all times.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer one very big and one very small cup and place them far apart.
- **Extension:** Introduce a third, middle-sized cup and ask "Which is the biggest?" with gestures.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Show me the small cup.
  **Expected response:** Child lifts or points to the small cup.
- **Ask:** Is this cup big or small?
  **Expected response:** Child says or shows the correct word.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds the big or small cup on request
- Gives the small cup to a friend

### Conclusion
Stack the cups together, big at the bottom, small on the top. Say "Big goes first!" and tidy them into the basket together.

### Home connection
At mealtime, ask your child to pass you the big cup or the small plate and name it together.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose the big or small cup when asked and say the word with support" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BIG cup
- SMALL cup

**Picture suggestions:**
- Big and small cups
- A child drinking from a small cup

## Day 4 (Thursday): The big yam and the small yam

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Big and small in my family and compound

### Learning objectives
- Children will use the words big and small while listening to a story and acting it out.

### Success criteria
- Joins in saying big or small at the right moment
- Acts big or small with their body

**Previous knowledge:** No prior mathematics is needed. Children have seen big and small things at home, for example an adult’s sandal and a baby’s sandal.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, little, bigger, show me

**Instructional materials:** A story picture of a big yam and a small yam (or two toy yams, or a big and small cassava); A basket; A drum or shaker

### Starter
Walk in carrying a basket on your head with a big yam in it. Say "Mama went to the market!" in a storytelling voice and let the children guess what is in the basket.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell the story slowly, holding up the big yam on "big" and the small yam on "small". Pause before each word and look at the children. Children: Children say "big" or "small" when you pause and show it with their arms or fingers.

Step 2. Teacher: Tell it again and invite children to be the big yam (stretch up) or the small yam (curl small) when they hear the word. Children: Children stretch tall or curl down according to the word.

Step 3. Teacher: Place the big yam and small yam on the mat and ask "Which one fits in baby’s bag?" Children: Children point to the small yam and say or whisper "small".

### Practical activity: Be the yam
1. Play the drum slowly for big and fast for small.
2. Children walk with big steps for slow drum beats and tiny steps for fast beats.
3. Stop and call "big!" or "small!" for children to freeze in that shape.

**Safety:** Space children well apart and walk, never run. Use toy or large real yams that cannot be swallowed and keep them off the floor.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Pair the child with an adult for each movement and use an exaggerated gesture.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find something big and something small at home to tell the class on Monday.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which yam was too big for the bag?
  **Expected response:** The big yam.
- **Ask:** What fitted into the bag?
  **Expected response:** The small yam.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses big and small words in a story
- Moves in a big or small way on request

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the yams. Say together, "Big yam, small yam. Goodbye!" Children carry the pretend yams to the basket.

### Home connection
Tell your child the yam story at bath time. Use real vegetables, such as a big and a small tomato.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use the words big and small while listening to a story and acting it out" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- The big yam
- The small yam

**Picture suggestions:**
- A woman at a market with a big yam
- A baby and a small yam

**Song or rhyme:** Big yam, big yam, stretch up high. Small yam, small yam, curl up small.

**Short story:** Mama went to the market. She bought a big yam. Big, big, big! The yam was too big for her bag, so she carried it on her head. Then she bought a small yam. Small, small, small! The small yam fitted into her bag. At home she cooked the big yam for the family and the small yam for baby. Everyone clapped. Big and small, big and small.

## Day 5 (Friday): Big and small stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Finding the big one and the small one

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose the big or small object at three play stations.

### Success criteria
- Chooses correctly in most turns
- Says or signs a size word at least once

**Previous knowledge:** No prior mathematics is needed. Children have seen big and small things at home, for example an adult’s sandal and a baby’s sandal.

**Key vocabulary:** big, small, little, bigger, show me

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: big and small sandals; Station 2: big and small balls and baskets; Station 3: big and small cups; Class record sheet and pencil

### Starter
Show the three stations and sing the "Big, small" rhyme while pointing to each. Tell the children that today they will play at each station with a friend.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate children in groups of three or four through the three stations every four minutes. Say the size word only if the child is stuck. Children: Children play and choose big or small objects at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Where is the big one?" and "Where is the small one?" and watch how each child responds. Children: Children show or say their answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Photograph or note one example of each child’s work for the class record. Children: Children continue playing and tidy their station when the signal sounds.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Teacher stays at one station for observation, a helper rotates the others.
2. Children move on when the shaker rings.
3. At the end each child chooses a favourite station to visit again.

**Safety:** Keep walkways clear, supervise each station and use only large, soft items. Ensure children sit while handling objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Keep a child at one station with an adult for the whole time if a transition is difficult.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to be the "station leader" and ask a friend "Where is the big one?".

### Assessment
Observe each child at the three stations. Record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: chooses big or small on request; uses the words big and small.

- **Ask:** Where is the big one?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the larger object.
- **Ask:** Can you find the small ball?
  **Expected response:** Child picks up or points to the small ball.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Chooses big or small independently in most turns

### Conclusion
Gather the children and sing the "Big, small" rhyme one last time. Let each child hold up one big and one small object as the class lines up.

### Home connection
Send home a note: "This week we learned big and small. Please point out one big and one small thing each day."

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose the big or small object at three play stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BIG
- SMALL
- (Picture of each station)

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of the three stations
- A child pointing to a big ball
