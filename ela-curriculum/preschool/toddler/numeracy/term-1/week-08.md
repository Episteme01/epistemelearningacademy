# Toddler Numeracy — Term 1, Week 8: Shape

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Circles all around us; Rolling and not rolling; Drawing a circle in the air and in sand

## Learning objectives
- Children will find round things in the classroom and outdoors.
- Children will name the circle with support.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, round, roll, ball, wheel

**Content:** Finding, handling and naming circles and round things such as balls, plates and wheels.

**Teaching activities:** Hold round objects up and say "round, circle"; trace the edge slowly with a finger; let children roll and feel the objects before naming.

**Learner activities:** Find round things, roll and not-roll objects, trace circles with fingers and draw circles in sand or the air.

**Resources:** Balls, plates, plastic bowls, a toy wheel; Blocks and boxes that do not roll; A tray of sand or flour; Large paper circles; Chalk or a stick for outdoor drawing

**Assessment:** Note who points to round objects when asked and who says circle or round.

## Expected outcomes
- Points to round objects when asked.
- Says circle or round.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: rolling and not rolling; Literacy: shape names in a rhyme; Physical Development: tracing and arm movements

## Day 1 (Monday): Round like a ball

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Circles all around us

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to round things and say round or circle with support.

### Success criteria
- Points to a round object when asked
- Says round or circle

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three with real things and sort by colour, size and kind.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, round, roll, ball, wheel

**Instructional materials:** A ball, a plate, a bowl, a toy wheel; A block and a box; A basket

### Starter
Roll a ball slowly across the floor. Say "Round, round, round!" Show the other objects and let the children pick out which look like the ball.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold up the ball, plate and wheel and trace their edges with your finger. Say "Round. A circle." Children: Children trace the air with their fingers and say "round".

Step 2. Teacher: Hold up the block and the box. Say "Not round. Not a circle." Children: Children shake their heads or say "no".

Step 3. Teacher: Put all objects in the basket. Ask a child to pick one round object. Children: Child chooses a round object and shows the group.

Step 4. Teacher: Place all round objects on one mat and the others on another mat. Children: Children help to sort round from not round.

### Practical activity: Round thing hunt
1. Set several objects around the room, round and not round.
2. Children find one round thing and bring it to the mat.
3. The group checks by tracing the edge.

**Safety:** Remove sharp or breakable objects; use plates and bowls made of plastic. Children carry one object at a time.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects only, a ball and a box, and guide the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find three round things and name each one.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which one is round?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the ball, plate or wheel.
- **Ask:** Is the block round?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to round objects
- Says round or circle

### Conclusion
Sing the circle song and trace a big circle in the air with both arms.

### Home connection
Find round things at home, such as plates, cups, clocks and coins held by an adult, and say "round".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to round things and say round or circle with support" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- A big circle
- Round things: ball, plate, wheel

**Picture suggestions:**
- A ball, a plate and a wheel
- A box and a block (not round)

**Song or rhyme:** Round, round, round, like a ball. Round, round, round, like a plate. Circle, circle, round and round.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Roll or not roll?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Rolling and not rolling

### Learning objectives
- Children will test which objects roll and which do not.

### Success criteria
- Pushes an object and watches
- Says roll or stop with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three with real things and sort by colour, size and kind.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, round, roll, ball, wheel

**Instructional materials:** A ball, a tin or round container, a block, a box; A gentle slope such as a tilted board; A mat

### Starter
Push a ball and a block together and ask "Which one rolled? Which one stayed?" Let the children watch.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Push the ball. Say "It rolls!" Push the block and say "It does not roll." Children: Children watch and say "roll" or "no roll".

Step 2. Teacher: Let children push each object in turn along the floor. Children: Child pushes the object and watches it move or stay.

Step 3. Teacher: Set up a gentle slope and let the objects slide or roll down. Children: Children say "roll" when the object rolls.

Step 4. Teacher: Sort the objects into "rolls" and "does not roll" baskets. Children: Child places each object in the correct basket.

### Practical activity: Roll test
1. Give each pair a ball and a block.
2. They push both and see which rolls.
3. They put the round one in the basket.

**Safety:** Use a gentle slope only; keep fingers away from the end of the board. Clear the floor so children do not trip over rolling objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer only the ball and block with an adult pushing alongside.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to predict whether a new object will roll before pushing it.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does the ball roll?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** Does the block roll?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Tests roll and not roll
- Links round with rolling

### Conclusion
Roll the ball one more time and say "Round things roll."

### Home connection
At home, test which objects roll along the floor: a ball, a tin and a box.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "test which objects roll and which do not" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Roll
- No roll

**Picture suggestions:**
- A ball rolling
- A block staying still

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Drawing circles in sand

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Drawing a circle in the air and in sand

### Learning objectives
- Children will trace and draw a circle in sand or on a tray.

### Success criteria
- Traces a round shape with a finger
- Draws a circle-like shape in sand

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three with real things and sort by colour, size and kind.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, round, roll, ball, wheel

**Instructional materials:** A tray of dry sand or flour; Large paper circles; Chalk or a stick for outdoor drawing

### Starter
Draw a big circle in the air with your finger. Say "Round and round." Invite the children to draw circles in the air with you.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Draw a circle in the sand with your finger, slowly, and say "round and round". Children: Children watch and trace the circle with their own fingers.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a tray. Say "Draw a round shape." Children: Child draws in the sand with a finger.

Step 3. Teacher: Show a paper circle and match it to the one in the sand. Children: Child compares and points to the match.

Step 4. Teacher: Wipe the trays and try again with a bigger circle. Children: Child draws a larger circle and shows a friend.

### Practical activity: Sand circle trays
1. Give each child a shallow tray of sand.
2. Draw round shapes with a finger.
3. Gently shake the tray to wipe it clean.

**Safety:** Use clean, dry sand or flour; tell children not to eat it or throw it. Wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use hand-over-hand tracing around a paper circle.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to draw two circles and say which is bigger.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you draw round?
  **Expected response:** Child draws a round shape.
- **Ask:** What shape is this?
  **Expected response:** Circle or round.

### Expected outcomes
- Draws or traces a circle shape
- Names the circle

### Conclusion
Wipe the sand smooth and say "All gone circle. Goodbye."

### Home connection
Trace around a plate or a cup with your child to make a circle.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "trace and draw a circle in sand or on a tray" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Circle (large)

**Picture suggestions:**
- A circle in sand

## Day 4 (Thursday): Circles in the market

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Circles all around us

### Learning objectives
- Children will find circles in a pretend market and in the outdoor environment.

### Success criteria
- Points to circles in a play market
- Says circle or round in play

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three with real things and sort by colour, size and kind.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, round, roll, ball, wheel

**Instructional materials:** Toy oranges, tomatoes, a round tray, a plate, a bowl; A play market stall; A basket

### Starter
Set up the market and say "Today the market sells round things only. Can you help me find them?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show an orange and a tomato. Say "Round like a ball. A circle." Children: Children touch the fruits and say "round".

Step 2. Teacher: Ask a child to be the customer and buy something round from the stall. Children: Child picks a round object and gives it to the trader.

Step 3. Teacher: Swap roles and let others buy round things and name them. Children: Children buy and say "round" or "circle".

Step 4. Teacher: Take the group outdoors and point out round things such as a wheel or a tin. Children: Children find and name round things outside.

### Practical activity: Round market
1. Place a mix of round and not round objects on the stall.
2. Children buy only round things.
3. They tell the trader what they bought.

**Safety:** Use toy or washed fruits that cannot be swallowed. Hold the outdoor walk within a fenced, supervised area.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer only two items and guide the child to choose the round one.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sell something round to a friend and name it.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What round thing did you buy?
  **Expected response:** The name of the object.
- **Ask:** Is this a circle?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no depending on the object.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds circles in play
- Uses round and circle

### Conclusion
Put all round things in the basket and say "Circle, circle, round."

### Home connection
Spot round things when you go to the market, such as oranges and wheels.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find circles in a pretend market and in the outdoor environment" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Round market

**Picture suggestions:**
- A market stall with round things

## Day 5 (Friday): Round things stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Rolling and not rolling

### Learning objectives
- Children will find, roll and trace circles at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Points to round things
- Says round or circle

**Previous knowledge:** Children can count to three with real things and sort by colour, size and kind.

**Key vocabulary:** circle, round, roll, ball, wheel

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: round hunt basket; Station 2: roll and not roll; Station 3: sand circle trays; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the circle song and show the three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask each observed child "Which is round?" and "Does it roll?" Children: Child points and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Remove small items; supervise every station.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead a friend in the roll test.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: points to round objects; says circle or round; tests roll and not roll.

- **Ask:** Show me something round.
  **Expected response:** Child points to a round object.
- **Ask:** Does the box roll?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Names and finds circles

### Conclusion
Sing the circle song and draw a big circle in the air together.

### Home connection
Look for round things on the way home and name them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find, roll and trace circles at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
