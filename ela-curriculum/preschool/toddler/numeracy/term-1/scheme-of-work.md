# Toddler Numeracy — Term 1 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Big and small**: Big and small objects in the classroom; Big and small in my family and compound; Finding the big one and the small one | Children will point to the big or the small one when asked. Children will say or sign big and small with support. | Comparing two objects by size and using the words big and small in play, songs and routines. | Model big and small with exaggerated voice and gesture; hold up two objects and ask "Where is the big one?"; narrate children’s actions with the words. | Handle, carry and roll big and small objects; point, show and say big or small; match big and small in a basket game. | Adult and child sandals; Big and small plastic cups and bowls; Big and small balls; Big and small drums or tins; Two puppets or soft toys | Observe whether each child chooses the big or small object on request; record on the class record sheet. | Chooses the big or small object on request in most turns. Uses the words big and small with prompting. | Literacy: rhyme and picture book; Social Habits: tidying away in two baskets; Physical Development: carrying and rolling |
| 2 | **Same and different**: Matching identical pairs; Same colour, same kind; Finding the one that is different | Children will match two identical objects. Children will point to the one that is different in a group of three. | Matching identical objects, noticing when two things are the same colour or kind, and spotting the one that is different. | Lay out matched sets and play "Find my twin"; use the words same and different every time children match; give wait time before helping. | Match pairs of identical objects and pictures, find a friend’s same shoe or cup, and point to the one that is different. | Pairs of identical sandals, cups, spoons and socks; Pairs of picture cards (fruits, animals); Three-object sets with one odd one out; A basket and a cloth bag | Observe who matches independently, who needs a prompt and who spots the odd one; record on the class sheet. | Matches a pair of identical objects independently. Points to the odd one out with a prompt. | Literacy: picture matching and vocabulary; Science: noticing properties of objects; Social Habits: working beside a friend |
| 3 | **Sorting**: Sorting into two colour groups; Sorting three colours; Saying the colour of a group | Children will put objects of the same colour together. Children will name or point to a group by its colour. | Sorting familiar objects into groups by colour and saying the colour of a group. | Model sorting aloud ("red goes here"); give each child a basket and a sorting cloth; praise careful sorting; ask "Why does it go here?" | Pick up, sort and place objects of one colour together, name the colour or point to it, and tidy sorted objects away. | Coloured cloths or paper plates (red, yellow, green); Large safe objects in red, yellow and green (tomato, plantain, leaf, plastic cups); Baskets; A colour song | Note whether each child sorts without errors, whether they name a colour, and whether they need prompts. | Sorts into two colour groups with few errors. Names at least one colour in a group. | Literacy: colour words and rhyme; Science: noticing the colour of fruits and leaves; Social Habits: tidying into baskets |
| 4 | **Sorting**: Sorting big and small; Sorting by kind (food, clothes, toys); Telling why: "because they are fruits" | Children will sort objects by size into big and small groups. Children will sort objects by kind and give a one-word reason with support. | Sorting objects by size and by kind, with a simple reason such as "because they are fruits". | Think aloud as you sort; ask "Why does it go here?"; offer the sentence "because it is a fruit" and accept gestures. | Sort big and small objects, sort fruits from toys and clothes, and give a one-word or gesture reason. | Big and small spoons, cups and balls; Toy or large real fruits (oranges, mango); Toy animals and clothes; Three baskets and cloths | Check that each child can sort by size and by kind and note any reasons they give. | Sorts by size or by kind with growing accuracy. Offers a word or gesture as a reason. | Science: living and non-living, fruit and food; Literacy: naming kinds of things; Social Habits: tidying into the right basket |
| 5 | **Quantity**: One; Lots and a few; None: all gone | Children will take or show one when asked. Children will tell one thing from lots of things. | The idea of one, lots, a few and none, using real objects and counting games. | Use "one" firmly in routines: give one cup, one biscuit; use "lots" when there are many; use "all gone" for none. | Take one object, show lots, give one to a friend, and say "all gone" when none are left. | Baskets of beads in large sizes (wooden blocks, large buttons); Large plastic cups; Toy fruits; A shaker or drum | Observe whether each child can give or take one when asked and tell one from lots. | Gives exactly one object on request. Uses the word one or lots. | Literacy: one, lots and all gone in rhymes; Social Habits: sharing and giving one to a friend; Physical Development: picking up and passing objects |
