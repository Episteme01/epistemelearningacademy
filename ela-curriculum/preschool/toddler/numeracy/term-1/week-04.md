# Toddler Numeracy — Term 1, Week 4: Sorting

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W04 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Sorting big and small; Sorting by kind (food, clothes, toys); Telling why: "because they are fruits"

## Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects by size into big and small groups.
- Children will sort objects by kind and give a one-word reason with support.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, group, kind, food, toys, clothes

**Content:** Sorting objects by size and by kind, with a simple reason such as "because they are fruits".

**Teaching activities:** Think aloud as you sort; ask "Why does it go here?"; offer the sentence "because it is a fruit" and accept gestures.

**Learner activities:** Sort big and small objects, sort fruits from toys and clothes, and give a one-word or gesture reason.

**Resources:** Big and small spoons, cups and balls; Toy or large real fruits (oranges, mango); Toy animals and clothes; Three baskets and cloths

**Assessment:** Check that each child can sort by size and by kind and note any reasons they give.

## Expected outcomes
- Sorts by size or by kind with growing accuracy.
- Offers a word or gesture as a reason.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Science: living and non-living, fruit and food; Literacy: naming kinds of things; Social Habits: tidying into the right basket

## Day 1 (Monday): Big things, small things

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Sorting big and small

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects into a big group and a small group.

### Success criteria
- Places a big object with the big group
- Places a small object with the small group

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour with support and use some size words.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, group, kind, food, toys, clothes

**Instructional materials:** Two baskets labelled BIG and SMALL with pictures; Eight pairs of big and small objects (spoons, cups, balls, blocks); A mat

### Starter
Hold up a big spoon and a small spoon. Ask "Which one goes in the big basket?" and "Which one goes in the small basket?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sort a few objects while thinking aloud: "A big ball. Big basket." Children: Children watch and say "big" with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Hand a child an object and ask "Is it big or small?" before they place it. Children: Child decides and places it in the right basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Mix up the objects and sort again as a group, one child at a time. Children: Each child sorts one object and others check by pointing.

### Practical activity: Big and small baskets
1. Spread the mixed objects on the mat.
2. Children sort them into the two baskets.
3. Teacher and children look at each basket and say "big" or "small".

**Safety:** Use objects that are larger than a child’s fist. Supervise to keep objects out of mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer one object at a time with clear size contrast.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose a medium-sized object and decide which basket it fits best.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is this spoon big or small?
  **Expected response:** The correct size word.
- **Ask:** Which basket does the small ball go in?
  **Expected response:** The small basket.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts objects by size
- Uses big and small

### Conclusion
Look at the two baskets and say "Big things, small things." Tidy together.

### Home connection
Sort cups and plates into big and small piles at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort objects into a big group and a small group" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**
- BIG things
- SMALL things

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Fruits and toys

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Sorting by kind (food, clothes, toys)

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort fruits and toys into two groups.

### Success criteria
- Puts fruits in the fruit basket
- Puts toys in the toy basket

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour with support and use some size words.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, group, kind, food, toys, clothes

**Instructional materials:** Toy or large safe fruits (orange, mango, banana); Toys (ball, block, car); Two baskets with pictures

### Starter
Hold up a mango and a toy car. Say "This is a fruit. This is a toy. They are different kinds."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sort four objects aloud: "Mango, fruit. Car, toy." Children: Children repeat "fruit" and "toy".

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child pick an object and say whether it is a fruit or a toy. Children: Child places it in the correct basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Why is the orange in the fruit basket?" and give the sentence "because it is a fruit". Children: Child says "fruit" or points to the fruit picture.

### Practical activity: Fruit and toy basket
1. Mix fruits and toys.
2. Children take turns to sort.
3. Check each basket together.

**Safety:** Real fruit must be washed and not eaten unless offered as a snack. Use only large toys that cannot be swallowed.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two very different objects such as a ball and a banana.
- **Extension:** Introduce a third group, clothes, and sort three kinds.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is this a fruit or a toy?
  **Expected response:** The correct kind.
- **Ask:** Why does it go in this basket?
  **Expected response:** A word or gesture such as "fruit".

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts by kind
- Gives a one-word reason with support

### Conclusion
Sing "Fruits in the basket, toys on the shelf." Tidy up.

### Home connection
Sort fruit and vegetables or toys and clothes at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort fruits and toys into two groups" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FRUITS
- TOYS

**Picture suggestions:**
- A mango and a toy car

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Clothes, food and toys

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Sorting by kind (food, clothes, toys)

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects into three kinds: clothes, food and toys.

### Success criteria
- Sorts most objects correctly
- Says or points to the kind

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour with support and use some size words.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, group, kind, food, toys, clothes

**Instructional materials:** Three baskets labelled clothes, food and toys; Objects: sock, hat, orange, plantain, ball, block; A mat

### Starter
Hold a sock, an orange and a ball. Say "Which one do we wear? Which one do we eat? Which one do we play with?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sort three objects aloud using "we wear, we eat, we play". Children: Children repeat the phrases and gestures.

Step 2. Teacher: Hand out objects and ask "Where does it go?" Children: Child places it in the right basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "How do you know?" Wait and accept gestures. Children: Child shows eating, wearing or playing.

### Practical activity: Wear, eat, play sort
1. Place the baskets in a row.
2. Children take an object and act out what it does.
3. They place it in the matching basket.

**Safety:** Real food must be fresh, washed and eaten only when the teacher offers it. Use clean clothes.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two kinds only.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to think of another thing that goes in each basket.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Do we wear it, eat it or play with it?
  **Expected response:** The correct action or kind.
- **Ask:** Which basket does the hat go in?
  **Expected response:** Clothes.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts by kind
- Acts out why

### Conclusion
Sing "We wear, we eat, we play." Tidy up the baskets.

### Home connection
At bedtime, sort clean clothes and toys into different places.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort objects into three kinds: clothes, food and toys" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- We wear
- We eat
- We play

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Because they are fruits

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Telling why: "because they are fruits"

### Learning objectives
- Children will give a simple reason for how they sorted.

### Success criteria
- Says or shows why an object goes in a group
- Uses a word like "fruit" or a gesture

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour with support and use some size words.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, group, kind, food, toys, clothes

**Instructional materials:** Mixed objects from the week; Three baskets; Picture cards for reasons (apple, shirt, ball)

### Starter
Say "A magic bag! Let us sort what comes out. Then I will ask you why."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pull an object from the bag and sort it with a reason: "It goes here because it is a fruit." Children: Children repeat "because it is a fruit".

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child pull an object, decide where it goes and say why. Children: Child sorts and gives a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What else goes here?" Children: Child names or finds another object.

### Practical activity: Magic bag sorting
1. Put objects in a cloth bag.
2. Each child takes one and places it in the right basket.
3. They say one reason word.

**Safety:** Keep objects safe and large. Supervise to avoid mouthing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer picture cards for the child to point to as a reason.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to think of a new kind, such as animals, and sort.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Why does the orange go here?
  **Expected response:** Because it is a fruit.
- **Ask:** What else goes in this basket?
  **Expected response:** A suitable item.

### Expected outcomes
- Gives a reason for sorting
- Sorts with growing independence

### Conclusion
Clap a rhythm for each sorted basket. Say "Because, because!"

### Home connection
Ask your child why they put things together when tidying.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "give a simple reason for how they sorted" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Sorting stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Telling why: "because they are fruits"

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort by size and kind at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Sorts correctly by size and kind
- Offers a word or gesture as a reason

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort by colour with support and use some size words.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, group, kind, food, toys, clothes

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: big and small; Station 2: fruits and toys; Station 3: clothes, food and toys; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing "Big and small, fruits and toys" and show the stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children sort at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask observed children to sort and say why. Children: Child sorts and gives a reason.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Remove small items and supervise all stations.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner at each station.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead a friend through sorting.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: sorts by size; sorts by kind; gives a reason.

- **Ask:** Where does it go?
  **Expected response:** The correct basket.
- **Ask:** Why did you put it there?
  **Expected response:** A word or gesture reason.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Sorts and gives reasons

### Conclusion
Sing the sorting song and tidy up together.

### Home connection
Let your child sort objects around the house this week.
