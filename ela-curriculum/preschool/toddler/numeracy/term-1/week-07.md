# Toddler Numeracy — Term 1, Week 7: Counting

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Touch and count one at a time; How many?; Counting steps, claps and jumps

## Learning objectives
- Children will touch each object once while saying the number words.
- Children will say or show how many when there are up to three.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, one, two, three, touch

**Content:** Counting up to three real objects by touching each one once, and answering how many.

**Teaching activities:** Count slowly aloud with a clear touch on each object; move counted objects to one side; repeat the last number as the answer.

**Learner activities:** Touch and move objects one at a time while saying the numbers; count steps, claps and jumps; answer how many.

**Resources:** Large wooden blocks; Three plastic cups; Toy fruits; Two trays; A basket of large toy vehicles

**Assessment:** Check whether each child touches one object for each number word and says how many for groups up to three.

## Expected outcomes
- Counts up to three objects with one touch for each.
- Answers how many with the last number word, with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: number words in rhymes and chants; Physical Development: moving objects one at a time; Social Habits: taking turns to count

## Day 1 (Monday): Touch and say

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Touch and count one at a time

### Learning objectives
- Children will touch one object for each number word when counting up to three.

### Success criteria
- Touches each object once
- Says the number words in order

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join the rhyme one, two, three and show one, two or three fingers.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, one, two, three, touch

**Instructional materials:** Three large wooden blocks; Two trays; A mat

### Starter
Put three blocks on the mat. Say "I have some blocks. Let me count them. I touch, I say." Touch the first block slowly and wait for the children to watch.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Touch the first block and say "one". Slide it to the other tray. Touch the next and say "two", then "three". Children: Children watch and say the numbers with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "I touched three blocks. There are three." Point to all three on the tray. Children: Children say "three" and look at the blocks.

Step 3. Teacher: Invite a child to touch and slide each block while you say the numbers slowly. Children: Child touches and slides one block for each number.

Step 4. Teacher: Repeat with two blocks, then one block, so the numbers change. Children: Children touch and say "two" or "one" as asked.

### Practical activity: Block count
1. Give each pair a tray of three blocks and an empty tray.
2. Child one touches and slides each block while child two says the numbers.
3. Swap roles.

**Safety:** Use large blocks that do not fit in a child’s mouth. Seat children firmly on the floor.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Count only two blocks and guide the child’s finger with a hand-over-hand touch.
- **Extension:** Count four blocks with help and say "four".

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many blocks did you count?
  **Expected response:** Three, or the number counted.
- **Ask:** Can you touch each block once?
  **Expected response:** Child touches each block one time.

### Expected outcomes
- Touches one object for each number word
- Says numbers one to three in order

### Conclusion
Slide all the blocks back into the first tray, saying "one, two, three, all home."

### Home connection
Count three spoons or three shoes at home by touching each one.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "touch one object for each number word when counting up to three" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Touch, say, slide
- Three blocks

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three blocks and two trays
- A finger touching a block

**Song or rhyme:** Touch one, touch two, touch three. Blocks and me.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): How many cups?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** How many?

### Learning objectives
- Children will answer how many with the last number they say for up to three cups.

### Success criteria
- Counts the cups
- Says or shows the last number as the answer

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join the rhyme one, two, three and show one, two or three fingers.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, one, two, three, touch

**Instructional materials:** Three plastic cups; A tray; A shaker

### Starter
Shake the shaker and say "Listen! How many shakes?" Shake three times and count them out. Say "Now let us count something we can see."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put one cup on the tray. Say "How many cups? One." Add a second, then a third, asking each time. Children: Children answer "one", "two" and "three".

Step 2. Teacher: Mix the cups on the tray and count them again by touching. Children: Children touch each cup and say the numbers.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "How many?" and wait. Do not help unless needed. Children: Children say the last number or show it with fingers.

Step 4. Teacher: Add or remove a cup and ask "How many now?" Children: Children count again and say the new number.

### Practical activity: Cup count tray
1. Place one to three cups on a tray for each pair.
2. One child counts the cups, the other shows the number on fingers.
3. Swap and play again with a different number.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable cups only. Keep cups on the tray and off the floor to avoid slips.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer one or two cups only and say the number with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count three cups and then give you exactly two.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many cups are on the tray?
  **Expected response:** The correct number, up to three.
- **Ask:** Show me with your fingers.
  **Expected response:** Child shows the number of fingers.

### Expected outcomes
- Answers how many up to three
- Counts by touching

### Conclusion
Stack the cups one by one while counting, then tip them back on the tray and say "Three cups. Well done."

### Home connection
Ask "how many?" when your child has biscuits, shoes or toys, up to three.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "answer how many with the last number they say for up to three cups" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- How many?
- Cup, cup, cup

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three cups on a tray
- A child counting cups

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Counting steps, claps and jumps

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Counting steps, claps and jumps

### Learning objectives
- Children will count steps, claps or jumps up to three.

### Success criteria
- Takes one step for each number
- Stops at the number named

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join the rhyme one, two, three and show one, two or three fingers.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, one, two, three, touch

**Instructional materials:** An open floor space; Three footprints cut from paper or card; A drum

### Starter
Walk three big steps across the floor and say "One, two, three! I walked three steps." Ask "Can you walk three steps?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Place three paper footprints on the floor in a line. Step on each one and say the numbers. Children: Children watch and say the numbers with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Call out "Walk two steps" and do it. Then "Walk three steps". Children: Children walk the number of steps called and stop.

Step 3. Teacher: Change to clapping: "Clap three." then to jumping: "Jump two." Children: Children clap or jump the number called.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child choose a number and call it for the group. Children: Child calls a number from one to three and the group does it.

### Practical activity: Footprint walk
1. Lay three footprints in a line.
2. Each child steps on them while the group counts.
3. Repeat with two and then one footprint.

**Safety:** Fix paper footprints with tape so they do not slip. Children walk one at a time with an adult nearby.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk beside the child and touch the shoulder at each step.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to do three claps and two jumps in a row.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many steps did you take?
  **Expected response:** The number of steps, up to three.
- **Ask:** Can you clap two?
  **Expected response:** Child claps two times.

### Expected outcomes
- Moves the number of times called
- Stops at the right number

### Conclusion
Take three slow steps to the sitting area and count them. Sit down together.

### Home connection
Count steps to the door or the gate at home, one to three.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count steps, claps or jumps up to three" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Step 1, step 2, step 3

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three footprints on the floor

## Day 4 (Thursday): Counting toy vehicles

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** How many?

### Learning objectives
- Children will count toy vehicles in play and answer how many.

### Success criteria
- Counts vehicles by touching
- Says how many in play

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join the rhyme one, two, three and show one, two or three fingers.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, one, two, three, touch

**Instructional materials:** Toy vehicles (okada, bus, car); A mat road or drawn road; A basket

### Starter
Push a toy car along the road and say "Brum, brum, how many cars on the road? Let us count them."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Place one, two or three vehicles on the road. Ask "How many vehicles?" and touch each one as you count. Children: Children count with you and say the last number.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Park two vehicles." Move two vehicles to the car park. Children: Child moves two vehicles and counts them.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "How many are still on the road?" Let children look and answer. Children: Children count the vehicles on the road.

Step 4. Teacher: Pass the basket and ask each child to take three vehicles and count them. Children: Child takes vehicles and counts up to three.

### Practical activity: Road count
1. Put a road mat on the floor.
2. Children place up to three vehicles on the road and count them.
3. They tell a friend how many.

**Safety:** Toy vehicles must be too large to swallow and have no loose parts; keep them off the floor between turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use only two vehicles and say each number as the child touches them.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put three vehicles on the road, take one away and say how many are left.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many vehicles on the road?
  **Expected response:** The correct number, up to three.
- **Ask:** Can you park one vehicle?
  **Expected response:** Child moves one vehicle and counts it.

### Expected outcomes
- Counts toy vehicles to three
- Answers how many in play

### Conclusion
Park all the vehicles in the basket while counting them aloud.

### Home connection
Count cars or okadas you see on the road and say how many.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "count toy vehicles in play and answer how many" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- How many vehicles?

**Picture suggestions:**
- A road with three vehicles

## Day 5 (Friday): Counting stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** How many?

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can count up to three at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Touches each object once
- Says how many up to three

**Previous knowledge:** Children can join the rhyme one, two, three and show one, two or three fingers.

**Key vocabulary:** count, how many, one, two, three, touch

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: blocks and trays; Station 2: cups and a tray; Station 3: footprints and drum; Class record sheet

### Starter
Show each station and say "Count, count, count with me." Remind the children to touch each one.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children count at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "How many?" and watch whether each child touches one object for each number. Children: Child counts and answers.

Step 3. Teacher: Note each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children continue and tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Remove small items and supervise each station closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer an adult partner at each station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to count objects for a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: touches one object for each number; says how many up to three; counts steps, claps or jumps.

- **Ask:** How many blocks?
  **Expected response:** The correct number, up to three.
- **Ask:** Can you count the cups again?
  **Expected response:** Child counts again and gets the same number.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Counts up to three objects

### Conclusion
Count the children sitting on the mat together, up to three at a time.

### Home connection
Count to three each day with a real object such as fruit or shoes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show they can count up to three at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Count to 3

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of stations
