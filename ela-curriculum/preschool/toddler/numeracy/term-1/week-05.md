# Toddler Numeracy — Term 1, Week 5: Quantity

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W05 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** One; Lots and a few; None: all gone

## Learning objectives
- Children will take or show one when asked.
- Children will tell one thing from lots of things.

**Key vocabulary:** one, lots, few, none, all gone

**Content:** The idea of one, lots, a few and none, using real objects and counting games.

**Teaching activities:** Use "one" firmly in routines: give one cup, one biscuit; use "lots" when there are many; use "all gone" for none.

**Learner activities:** Take one object, show lots, give one to a friend, and say "all gone" when none are left.

**Resources:** Baskets of beads in large sizes (wooden blocks, large buttons); Large plastic cups; Toy fruits; A shaker or drum

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child can give or take one when asked and tell one from lots.

## Expected outcomes
- Gives exactly one object on request.
- Uses the word one or lots.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: one, lots and all gone in rhymes; Social Habits: sharing and giving one to a friend; Physical Development: picking up and passing objects

## Day 1 (Monday): Give me one

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** One

### Learning objectives
- Children will take one object when asked.

### Success criteria
- Takes exactly one object on request
- Says or shows "one"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort and compare by size and say some words such as big, small and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one, lots, few, none, all gone

**Instructional materials:** A basket of large blocks; A tray; Large plastic cups

### Starter
Hold up one finger and say "One!" Take one block from the basket and say "I have one block."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Say "Give me one block" and hold out your hand. Children: Child takes one block and gives it to you.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Thank you. One block." Ask another child. Children: Each child takes one block and puts it on the tray.

Step 3. Teacher: Show lots of blocks and ask "Can you take one?" Children: Child takes one and keeps the others in the basket.

### Practical activity: One for the tray
1. Place a basket of blocks in front of the group.
2. Say "Take one."
3. Each child puts one block on the tray.

**Safety:** Use large wooden or plastic blocks that cannot be swallowed. Supervise at all times.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer a basket of only two blocks and say "one" as you place the first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to take one block in each hand and say "two".

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you give me one?
  **Expected response:** Child gives exactly one object.
- **Ask:** How many blocks did you take?
  **Expected response:** One.

### Expected outcomes
- Gives one when asked
- Says the word one aloud

### Conclusion
Put all the single blocks into a line and say "One, one, one." Clap.

### Home connection
At snack time, ask your child to take one biscuit or one spoon.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "take one object when asked" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- ONE (one block)

**Picture suggestions:**
- One block
- A hand holding one finger

**Song or rhyme:** One finger, one finger, one finger up. One block, one block, one block up.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Lots and a few

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Lots and a few

### Learning objectives
- Children will tell lots from a few using real objects.

### Success criteria
- Points to the group with lots
- Says "lots" or "few"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort and compare by size and say some words such as big, small and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one, lots, few, none, all gone

**Instructional materials:** Two baskets: one with many large blocks, one with two or three; A mat

### Starter
Show a basket with lots of blocks and a basket with a few. Say "Lots! A few!" using different hand gestures.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pour out lots of blocks and say "Lots of blocks!" Pour out a few and say "A few." Children: Children point to each pile and say "lots" or "few".

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which has lots?" and "Which has a few?" Children: Children point to the right pile.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child take a few and put the rest back. Children: Child takes two or three blocks.

### Practical activity: Lots and few piles
1. Give each pair a tray.
2. Ask them to make a pile with lots and a pile with a few.
3. Check by pointing to each pile.

**Safety:** Use only large blocks and keep the area clear. Supervise to prevent mouthing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer one block and a basket full of blocks, which is an obvious difference.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell you how many are in the few pile by counting.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where are lots of blocks?
  **Expected response:** The bigger pile.
- **Ask:** Where are a few blocks?
  **Expected response:** The smaller pile.

### Expected outcomes
- Tells lots from few
- Uses the words

### Conclusion
Sweep the piles together and say "Lots of blocks, tidy up!"

### Home connection
At home, show your child lots of spoons and a few spoons and talk about them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "tell lots from a few using real objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- LOTS
- A FEW

**Picture suggestions:**
- A big pile and a small pile

## Day 3 (Wednesday): All gone!

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** None: all gone

### Learning objectives
- Children will say "all gone" when no objects are left.

### Success criteria
- Notices when a container is empty
- Says or signs "all gone"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort and compare by size and say some words such as big, small and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one, lots, few, none, all gone

**Instructional materials:** A basket of toy fruits; An empty basket; A cloth bag

### Starter
Pretend to eat toy fruits one by one. After each, say "One, gobble!" until the basket is empty. Say "All gone!" and show your empty hands.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Place a few fruits in a basket and let a child take them out one by one. Children: Child takes each fruit and puts it in the bag.

Step 2. Teacher: When the basket is empty, say "All gone!" and open your hands. Children: Children copy with open hands.

Step 3. Teacher: Hide a toy under a cloth and ask "Is it there? All gone?" Lift the cloth to reveal. Children: Children say "all gone" or "there!".

### Practical activity: Fruit basket game
1. Each child has a small basket of three toy fruits.
2. They take them out one by one.
3. They say "all gone" when the basket is empty.

**Safety:** Use toy fruits larger than a child’s fist. Do not use real small fruits or sweets.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use a basket with one object.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say how many were in the basket at the start.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is in the basket now?
  **Expected response:** Nothing or all gone.
- **Ask:** Can you say all gone?
  **Expected response:** Child says or signs all gone.

### Expected outcomes
- Understands none
- Says all gone

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the empty basket. Say "All gone!" together.

### Home connection
Say "all gone" at mealtimes when the plate is empty.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say "all gone" when no objects are left" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- ALL GONE (empty basket)

**Picture suggestions:**
- An empty basket

## Day 4 (Thursday): One for you

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** One

### Learning objectives
- Children will give one object to a friend.

### Success criteria
- Gives one object to a friend
- Says "one" or "for you"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort and compare by size and say some words such as big, small and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one, lots, few, none, all gone

**Instructional materials:** A basket of large biscuits or toy biscuits; A tray; Two or three soft toys

### Starter
Say "Teddy is hungry! Can you give Teddy one biscuit?" Hold out the basket.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Give one toy biscuit to Teddy and say "One biscuit for Teddy." Children: Children watch and say "one".

Step 2. Teacher: Invite a child to give one biscuit to each soft toy. Children: Child gives one biscuit and says "one".

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Now one for you" and give each child one. Children: Child takes one and says "thank you".

### Practical activity: One for the toys
1. Seat soft toys in a row.
2. Each child gives one toy biscuit to a toy.
3. Check that each toy has one.

**Safety:** Use toy biscuits or large safe items. If using real food, check for allergies and wash hands first.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use one toy and one biscuit.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to give one to each of three toys.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How many biscuits does Teddy have?
  **Expected response:** One.
- **Ask:** Who has no biscuit?
  **Expected response:** The toy without one.

### Expected outcomes
- Gives one to a friend
- Says the word one aloud

### Conclusion
Sing "One for you, one for me" and tidy the toys away.

### Home connection
At mealtimes, hand out one spoon or plate to each person.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "give one object to a friend" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- One for you

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 5 (Friday): One and lots stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** None: all gone

### Learning objectives
- Children will show one, lots and all gone at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Takes one when asked
- Tells lots from few and says all gone

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sort and compare by size and say some words such as big, small and same.

**Key vocabulary:** one, lots, few, none, all gone

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: one block each; Station 2: lots and few piles; Station 3: all gone basket; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the "One finger" song and show the stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask observed children to take one, show lots and say all gone. Children: Child takes one, points to lots and says all gone.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and photograph. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Remove small items and supervise all stations.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead a friend in the game.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: gives one; tells lots from few; says all gone.

- **Ask:** Can you take one?
  **Expected response:** Child takes one.
- **Ask:** Which has lots?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the larger group.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Uses one and lots

### Conclusion
Sing the "One finger" song again and tidy up.

### Home connection
Count one object a day at home and say "one".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show one, lots and all gone at three stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
