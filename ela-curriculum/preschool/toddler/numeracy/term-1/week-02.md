# Toddler Numeracy — Term 1, Week 2: Same and different

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Matching identical pairs; Same colour, same kind; Finding the one that is different

## Learning objectives
- Children will match two identical objects.
- Children will point to the one that is different in a group of three.

**Key vocabulary:** same, different, match, pair, find

**Content:** Matching identical objects, noticing when two things are the same colour or kind, and spotting the one that is different.

**Teaching activities:** Lay out matched sets and play "Find my twin"; use the words same and different every time children match; give wait time before helping.

**Learner activities:** Match pairs of identical objects and pictures, find a friend’s same shoe or cup, and point to the one that is different.

**Resources:** Pairs of identical sandals, cups, spoons and socks; Pairs of picture cards (fruits, animals); Three-object sets with one odd one out; A basket and a cloth bag

**Assessment:** Observe who matches independently, who needs a prompt and who spots the odd one; record on the class sheet.

## Expected outcomes
- Matches a pair of identical objects independently.
- Points to the odd one out with a prompt.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: picture matching and vocabulary; Science: noticing properties of objects; Social Habits: working beside a friend

## Day 1 (Monday): Find my twin sandal

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Matching identical pairs

### Learning objectives
- Children will match one sandal to its identical partner.

### Success criteria
- Brings together two identical sandals
- Says or shows "same"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sometimes choose the big or the small object and enjoy handling sandals, cups and balls.

**Key vocabulary:** same, different, match, pair, find

**Instructional materials:** Three pairs of different sandals or slippers; A cloth bag; A mat

### Starter
Hold up one blue sandal and say "I have lost my other sandal! It is the same as this one. Can you help me find it?" Look around dramatically.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Lay out three pairs of sandals mixed on the mat. Hold up one sandal and say "I need the same one." Children: Children look and one child points or brings the matching sandal.

Step 2. Teacher: Place the pair together and say "Same! These two are the same." Clap the beat. Children: Children say "same" and clap.

Step 3. Teacher: Pull a sandal from the cloth bag and ask each child in turn to find its twin. Children: Child finds the twin and puts it beside the first.

### Practical activity: Sandal pairs game
1. Give each child one sandal from a pair.
2. Children walk slowly to find the friend holding the same sandal.
3. They stand together and say "same".

**Safety:** Children should walk, not run, and sandals must be clean and dry. Check the floor for obstacles.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use pairs that differ strongly in colour and size, and sit beside the child to help look.
- **Extension:** Mix sandals that look similar but differ in one small detail and ask "Are these the same?"

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you find the sandal that is the same?
  **Expected response:** Child brings or points to the matching sandal.
- **Ask:** Are these two the same?
  **Expected response:** Child nods or says "same" when shown a true pair.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches a pair of sandals
- Says or signs same

### Conclusion
Put all matched pairs in a line. Say together, "Same, same, same!" and clap.

### Home connection
At home, pair up socks or slippers with your child and say "same" for each match.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match one sandal to its identical partner" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SAME (two matching sandals)

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three pairs of sandals
- A mother and child matching socks

**Song or rhyme:** Same, same, same. Find the same. Same, same, same. Match the same.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Same colour, same cup

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Same colour, same kind

### Learning objectives
- Children will match objects that are the same colour or the same kind.

### Success criteria
- Puts two cups of the same colour together
- Says "same" or points to show

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sometimes choose the big or the small object and enjoy handling sandals, cups and balls.

**Key vocabulary:** same, different, match, pair, find

**Instructional materials:** Six plastic cups in three colours (two each); Matching coloured paper circles or cloths; A tray

### Starter
Hold a red cup and a red cloth. Say "Red cup, red cloth. They are the same colour!" Hold a blue cup beside the red cloth and say "Different!"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a red cup and ask "Who can find another red thing?" Lay the red cloth beside it. Children: Child finds a matching red object and puts it down.

Step 2. Teacher: Do the same for blue and yellow. Say "same colour" each time. Children: Children match each cup to the cloth of the same colour.

Step 3. Teacher: Show a red cup and a blue cup and say "Different." Hold up two red cups and say "Same." Children: Children say same or different for each pair you show.

### Practical activity: Colour cup match
1. Place coloured cloths in a row.
2. Children put a cup on the cloth of the same colour.
3. Children check by lifting the cup and saying the colour.

**Safety:** Use unbreakable cups and large cloths. Make sure children sit and do not put cups in their mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two colours only and point to the correct cloth with a gesture.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to match two different kinds of object that share a colour, such as a red cup and a red ball.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which cup is the same colour as this cloth?
  **Expected response:** Child chooses the cup with the matching colour.
- **Ask:** Are these two the same or different?
  **Expected response:** Child says the correct word.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches objects by colour
- Uses same and different with support

### Conclusion
Gather the cups and say a rhyme: "Red with red, blue with blue, yellow with yellow, same, same, same."

### Home connection
Ask your child to find two things in the kitchen that are the same colour.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match objects that are the same colour or the same kind" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SAME colour
- DIFFERENT colour

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two red cups
- A red cup and a blue cup

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Which one is different?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Finding the one that is different

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to the one that is different in a group of three.

### Success criteria
- Points to the odd one out in a group of three
- Says "different" with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sometimes choose the big or the small object and enjoy handling sandals, cups and balls.

**Key vocabulary:** same, different, match, pair, find

**Instructional materials:** Three small baskets each containing two identical objects and one different (two spoons and a cup; two balls and a block; two socks and a slipper); A mat

### Starter
Show two spoons and one cup and sing "One of these things is not like the others." Ask the children to look carefully and point to the one that does not belong.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold up three objects: two spoons and one cup. Say "Look, which one is different?" Give wait time. Children: Children look and point to the cup.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Yes, the cup is different. The spoons are the same." Put the two spoons together. Children: Children say "different" while pointing.

Step 3. Teacher: Repeat with a new set. Let a child choose the next set. Children: Child gives you a set to show the group, and others point to the odd one out.

### Practical activity: Odd one out baskets
1. Each pair of children takes a basket of three objects.
2. They find the one that is different and put it in the lid.
3. They tell the teacher "different".

**Safety:** Use only large objects that cannot be swallowed. Supervise all baskets closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use extremely clear differences such as a ball and two blocks.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to explain why: "It is different because it is round."

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Show me the one that is different.
  **Expected response:** Child points to the odd object.
- **Ask:** Are the two spoons the same?
  **Expected response:** Yes, same.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to the odd one out
- Uses same and different

### Conclusion
Put all the odd ones out in one basket and the pairs back in order. Say "Same with same, different with different."

### Home connection
At home, put two shoes and one hat on a table and ask your child which one is different.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to the one that is different in a group of three" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SAME: two spoons
- DIFFERENT: one cup

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two spoons and a cup
- Three objects, one different

## Day 4 (Thursday): Same and different in the market basket

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Same colour, same kind

### Learning objectives
- Children will find things that are the same in a pretend market.

### Success criteria
- Finds two items that are the same kind
- Says "same" or "different" in play

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sometimes choose the big or the small object and enjoy handling sandals, cups and balls.

**Key vocabulary:** same, different, match, pair, find

**Instructional materials:** A play market stall with plastic or toy fruits and vegetables (two oranges, two tomatoes, one yam); A basket; A cloth for the stall

### Starter
Set up a pretend market stall. Say "Welcome to the market! I want two things that are the same. Can you help me find them?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Act as the customer. Ask a child to find two oranges that are the same and place them in the basket. Children: Child picks two oranges and shows them.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Now I want the one that is different." Show the yam with the oranges. Children: Child points to the yam.

Step 3. Teacher: Let the children take turns to be the trader and the customer. Children: Children ask for two things that are the same and hand them over.

### Practical activity: Market match
1. Spread the market items on the stall.
2. Each child fills a basket with a pair of the same fruit.
3. The class checks the baskets together and says "same".

**Safety:** Use toy or large safe fruits that cannot be swallowed. Wash hands afterwards if real fruit is used.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer only two kinds of item and guide the child’s hand to the match.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find two pairs, each pair a different kind.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Can you give me two that are the same?
  **Expected response:** Child gives two identical items.
- **Ask:** Which one is different here?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the odd item.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds pairs in a play context
- Uses same and different in role play

### Conclusion
Count the baskets out loud and tidy the stall. Say "Goodbye, market!" together.

### Home connection
When shopping or sorting laundry, ask your child to find two that are the same.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find things that are the same in a pretend market" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Same: two oranges
- Different: a yam

**Picture suggestions:**
- A market stall
- Two oranges and a yam

## Day 5 (Friday): Same and different stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Finding the one that is different

### Learning objectives
- Children will match and spot the odd one at three play stations.

### Success criteria
- Matches a pair independently
- Points to the odd one out

**Previous knowledge:** Children can sometimes choose the big or the small object and enjoy handling sandals, cups and balls.

**Key vocabulary:** same, different, match, pair, find

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: sandal pairs; Station 2: coloured cups and cloths; Station 3: odd-one-out baskets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the "Same, same, same" song and point to each station. Tell the children they will play with a friend at each one.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes while you observe one station closely. Children: Children play at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask each observed child to show you a pair and then the odd one. Children: Child shows a pair and points to the odd one.

Step 3. Teacher: Make a note of each child’s level and take a photograph. Children: Children continue and tidy up at the signal.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations with enough items for four children each.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and notes.

**Safety:** Check each station for small items and remove them. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner who points and says the word.
- **Extension:** Let a child lead the class in the odd-one-out game.

### Assessment
Observe each child and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: matches an identical pair; points to the one that is different.

- **Ask:** Can you find the same one?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the matching object.
- **Ask:** Which one is different?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the odd one.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Matches and spots the odd one with growing confidence

### Conclusion
Sing the "Same, same, same" song one last time and let children choose one pair to put back on the shelf.

### Home connection
Please let your child match socks or spoons each day this week.

- Which children could show "match and spot the odd one at three play stations" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SAME
- DIFFERENT

**Picture suggestions:**
- Photos of the stations
