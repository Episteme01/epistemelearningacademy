# Toddler Numeracy — Term 1, Week 3: Sorting

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Sorting into two colour groups; Sorting three colours; Saying the colour of a group

## Learning objectives
- Children will put objects of the same colour together.
- Children will name or point to a group by its colour.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, red, yellow, green, basket, together

**Content:** Sorting familiar objects into groups by colour and saying the colour of a group.

**Teaching activities:** Model sorting aloud ("red goes here"); give each child a basket and a sorting cloth; praise careful sorting; ask "Why does it go here?"

**Learner activities:** Pick up, sort and place objects of one colour together, name the colour or point to it, and tidy sorted objects away.

**Resources:** Coloured cloths or paper plates (red, yellow, green); Large safe objects in red, yellow and green (tomato, plantain, leaf, plastic cups); Baskets; A colour song

**Assessment:** Note whether each child sorts without errors, whether they name a colour, and whether they need prompts.

## Expected outcomes
- Sorts into two colour groups with few errors.
- Names at least one colour in a group.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: colour words and rhyme; Science: noticing the colour of fruits and leaves; Social Habits: tidying into baskets

## Day 1 (Monday): Red things go here

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Sorting into two colour groups

### Learning objectives
- Children will put red objects in one place and non-red objects in another.

### Success criteria
- Places a red object in the red basket
- Leaves other objects out

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match identical objects and sometimes say same or different.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, red, yellow, green, basket, together

**Instructional materials:** Red basket or red cloth; Four red objects (red cup, red ball, red cloth, tomato) and four non-red objects; A mat

### Starter
Show a red tomato and say "Red tomato. Red!" Put on a red hat or hold a red cloth. Tell the children "Today we will find red things."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the red cloth on the mat. Pick up a red cup and say "Red cup goes on the red cloth." Children: Children watch, then say "red".

Step 2. Teacher: Hold up a blue block and say "Blue block. Does it go on red? No." Put it aside. Children: Children shake their heads or say "no".

Step 3. Teacher: Invite each child to choose one object and decide whether it goes on the red cloth. Children: Child chooses an object and puts it on the red cloth if it is red.

### Practical activity: Find the reds
1. Spread eight objects on the mat.
2. Children take turns to put a red object in the red basket.
3. Ask the class to check by pointing and saying "red".

**Safety:** Use only large objects that cannot be swallowed. A tomato should be firm and washed; do not let children eat it during the activity.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use only red and blue objects and guide the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a red thing in the room that is not on the mat.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which one is red?
  **Expected response:** Child points to a red object.
- **Ask:** Does the blue block go here?
  **Expected response:** No, it is not red.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts red objects with support
- Names the colour red

### Conclusion
Count how many red things are in the basket by pointing, without worrying about number. Say "All red! Well done."

### Home connection
At home, look for red objects such as a cup, a tomato or a cloth and say "red".

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "put red objects in one place and non-red objects in another" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- RED (basket of red things)
- NOT RED

**Picture suggestions:**
- A red tomato
- A basket of red objects

**Song or rhyme:** Red, red, red. Find the red. Red, red, red. Put it in the basket.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Red and yellow baskets

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Sorting into two colour groups

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects into a red basket and a yellow basket.

### Success criteria
- Places objects in the correct colour basket
- Names one colour

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match identical objects and sometimes say same or different.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, red, yellow, green, basket, together

**Instructional materials:** A red and a yellow basket; Six red and six yellow objects (plastic cups, large blocks, cloths, ripe plantain toy); A mat

### Starter
Hold up a yellow plantain (or toy) and a red tomato. Say "Yellow plantain, red tomato." Ask "Which one is yellow?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Place the two baskets side by side. Put one red and one yellow object in the correct baskets while thinking aloud. Children: Children watch and say the colour names with you.

Step 2. Teacher: Hand out one object to each child. Children: Child decides which basket it belongs in and puts it there.

Step 3. Teacher: Pick up a mistake on purpose and ask "Is this right?" Children: Children say "no" and move it to the correct basket.

### Practical activity: Two-basket sort
1. Children sit around a tray of mixed red and yellow objects.
2. They take turns to sort one at a time.
3. The class checks each basket and says the colour.

**Safety:** Use blocks and cups larger than a child’s fist. Supervise to prevent mouthing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use only one object at a time and say the colour before the child places it.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count how many are in each basket by touching and say "more" or "same".

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where does the yellow cup go?
  **Expected response:** In the yellow basket.
- **Ask:** What colour is this basket?
  **Expected response:** Red or yellow depending on the basket.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts into two colour baskets
- Notices and corrects a mistake

### Conclusion
Look at the two full baskets and say "Red basket, yellow basket." Tidy by carrying them to the shelf.

### Home connection
Sort fruit in a bowl: red tomatoes and yellow plantains or lemons. Talk about the colours.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort objects into a red basket and a yellow basket" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Red basket
- Yellow basket

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two baskets, red and yellow
- Tomatoes and plantains

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Three colour sort

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Sorting three colours

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects into three colour groups with help.

### Success criteria
- Places most objects in the correct group
- Names or points to a colour on request

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match identical objects and sometimes say same or different.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, red, yellow, green, basket, together

**Instructional materials:** Three coloured plates (red, yellow, green); Nine objects in these colours (leaf, cloth, cup, block); A mat

### Starter
Sing a rainbow song with the colours red, yellow and green. Show each plate and say the colour.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Introduce the green plate with a leaf. Say "Green leaf goes on the green plate." Children: Children touch the leaf and say "green".

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child one object and ask them to find its plate. Children: Child puts the object on the matching plate.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which plate has the most?" in a simple way, pointing to piles. Children: Children point to the biggest pile.

### Practical activity: Plate sort
1. Place three plates in a row.
2. Children take turns to take an object from a bag.
3. They place it on the matching plate and name the colour.

**Safety:** Use leaves that are clean and non-toxic or use paper leaves. Make sure all objects are too big to swallow.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Keep to two colours and only two objects in each pile.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another green thing in the classroom.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What colour is the leaf?
  **Expected response:** Green.
- **Ask:** Show me the yellow plate.
  **Expected response:** Child points to the yellow plate.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts into three groups
- Names or points to red, yellow and green

### Conclusion
Sing the colour song and let each child place one object back in the bag.

### Home connection
Look for red, yellow and green foods at home and name each colour.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort objects into three colour groups with help" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Red, yellow, green plates

**Picture suggestions:**
- Three coloured plates with objects

## Day 4 (Thursday): Colour walk

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Saying the colour of a group

### Learning objectives
- Children will find and sort colourful objects outdoors.

### Success criteria
- Collects an object of a named colour
- Names the colour of their collection

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match identical objects and sometimes say same or different.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, red, yellow, green, basket, together

**Instructional materials:** Three baskets (red, yellow, green); Colour cards; A safe outdoor area or a covered corridor

### Starter
Show the colour cards and say "Today we are going on a colour walk! Can you find something green?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Walk with the children to a safe outdoor area. Name each colour card as you hold it up. Children: Children look around and point to things of that colour.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child collect one leaf, flower petal or fallen item of the colour you name. Children: Child brings the item and places it in the correct basket.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask each child to name the colour of their item. Children: Child says the colour or points to a card.

### Practical activity: Colour treasure hunt
1. Give each child a colour card.
2. They find an object that matches the card.
3. They bring it to the basket and say the colour.

**Safety:** Keep the walk within a fenced area. Do not pick unknown plants and keep hands out of mouths. Wash hands after outdoor collecting.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk beside the child and point to a suitable object.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find something that is two colours.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What colour did you find?
  **Expected response:** The colour of their item.
- **Ask:** Which basket does it go in?
  **Expected response:** The basket of that colour.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds and names a colour outdoors
- Sorts collected items

### Conclusion
Empty the baskets together and say "Red, yellow, green. Well done!" Wash hands.

### Home connection
On the way home or at the market, spot something red, yellow and green.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find and sort colourful objects outdoors" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Red, yellow, green

**Picture suggestions:**
- Leaves, flowers and cloths outdoors

## Day 5 (Friday): Colour sorting stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-NUM-T1-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Saying the colour of a group

### Learning objectives
- Children will show they can sort by colour at three stations.

### Success criteria
- Sorts correctly into two or three groups
- Names or points to a colour

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match identical objects and sometimes say same or different.

**Key vocabulary:** sort, red, yellow, green, basket, together

**Instructional materials:** Station 1: red and yellow baskets; Station 2: three-colour plates; Station 3: colour cards and objects; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the colour song and show the stations. Remind children to sort carefully.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Rotate groups every four minutes and observe one station closely. Children: Children sort at each station.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask each observed child to sort four objects and name one colour. Children: Child sorts and names a colour.

Step 3. Teacher: Note each child’s level and take a photograph. Children: Children tidy their station when the signal sounds.

### Practical activity: Station circuit
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. Teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Remove any small items and keep all objects larger than a child’s fist.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner at the station.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to sort a mixed basket into three groups without help.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: sorts objects by colour; names or points to colours.

- **Ask:** Where does the green one go?
  **Expected response:** On the green plate or in the green basket.
- **Ask:** What colour is this?
  **Expected response:** The correct colour name or a pointing response.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Sorts by colour with growing accuracy

### Conclusion
Sing the colour song and tidy the baskets together.

### Home connection
Sort toys or fruit by colour with your child this week.
