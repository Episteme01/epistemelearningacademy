# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 4: My senses

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W04 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Smelling safe things; Tasting safe foods; Sweet, sour and salty

## Learning objectives
- Children will smell safe items and say if they like the smell.
- Children will taste safe foods and describe them with a word.

**Key vocabulary:** smell, taste, nose, tongue, sweet, salty

**Content:** Smelling safe items, tasting safe foods, and noticing sweet, sour and salty.

**Teaching activities:** Check allergies and parental consent before any tasting; wash hands first; offer tiny tastes only; never force children to smell or taste; use a spoon and individual cups.

**Learner activities:** Smell safe things and taste safe foods and say one word to describe them.

**Resources:** Orange, lemon, cloves or mint leaves; Sugar, salt and a slice of lemon in small cups; Spoons; Napkins; A cloth to cover items

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names a smell and says one word about how a food tastes.

## Expected outcomes
- Names a smell with support.
- Says one word for how a food tastes.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: describing words; Social Habits: trying new foods politely; Understanding the World: food

## Day 1 (Monday): Smelling with my nose

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Smelling safe things

### Learning objectives
- Children will smell safe items and say if they like the smell.

### Success criteria
- Smells an item gently
- Says nice or not nice with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use eyes, ears and hands to explore objects and name what they find.

**Key vocabulary:** smell, taste, nose, tongue, sweet, salty

**Instructional materials:** Orange peel, mint leaves, a bar of soap, a flower; Small covered pots

### Starter
Point to your nose and say "We smell with our nose. Sniff, sniff." Smell a flower and say "Mmm."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to sniff gently and not breathe in hard. Children: Children sniff gently.

Step 2. Teacher: Pass each pot round and say its name. Children: Children smell and say the name.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Do you like the smell?" Children: Child says yes or no with a smile or shake.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a favourite smell. Children: Child chooses and shows the pot.

### Practical activity: Smell pots
1. Put safe items in covered pots.
2. Each child sniffs gently.
3. Say what the smell is and if it is nice.

**Safety:** Use only safe, non-allergenic items and check for allergies first. Never use strong chemicals or cleaning products.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the pot at a distance and let the child sniff.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find something at home that smells nice.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we smell with?
  **Expected response:** Our nose.
- **Ask:** Do you like it?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no.

### Expected outcomes
- Smells safe items gently
- Says if they like a smell

### Conclusion
Cover the pots and say "Thank you, nose."

### Home connection
Smell flowers and foods with your child and say what you smell.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "smell safe items and say if they like the smell" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Nice and not nice smells

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Smelling safe things

### Learning objectives
- Children will describe smells as nice or not nice.

### Success criteria
- Says nice or not nice
- Names one thing that smells nice

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use eyes, ears and hands to explore objects and name what they find.

**Key vocabulary:** smell, taste, nose, tongue, sweet, salty

**Instructional materials:** Safe items such as an orange, mint, soap and a ripe banana; Picture cards of rubbish and flowers

### Starter
Hold up the orange and say "Mmm, this smells nice." Show a picture of rubbish and say "This smells not nice."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pass the items round and ask "Nice or not nice?" Children: Children smell and say nice or not nice.

Step 2. Teacher: Show picture cards and ask "Does it smell nice?" Children: Child says or shows with a face.

Step 3. Teacher: Talk about why we keep rubbish away from where we eat. Children: Children listen and nod.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose their favourite smell. Children: Child chooses and says a word.

### Practical activity: Nice smells
1. Put four safe items on a tray.
2. Each child smells one.
3. Say nice or not nice.

**Safety:** Use only fresh, safe items and check allergies. Never let children smell rubbish or chemicals.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two items and ask which one is nicer.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name a smell they like at home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does the orange smell nice?
  **Expected response:** Yes, nice.
- **Ask:** What smells nice at home?
  **Expected response:** Food or flowers.

### Expected outcomes
- Describes smells as nice or not nice
- Names a nice smell

### Conclusion
Put the items away and say "Mmm."

### Home connection
Talk about nice smells when you cook at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "describe smells as nice or not nice" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- NICE
- NOT NICE

**Picture suggestions:**
- A smiling face
- A frowning face

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Tasting with my tongue

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Tasting safe foods

### Learning objectives
- Children will taste safe foods and say one word to describe them.

### Success criteria
- Tastes a small piece of food
- Says a word for the taste

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use eyes, ears and hands to explore objects and name what they find.

**Key vocabulary:** smell, taste, nose, tongue, sweet, salty

**Instructional materials:** Small pieces of banana, orange and bread on a plate; Napkins; Individual cups

### Starter
Point to your tongue and say "We taste with our tongue." Check allergy notes and wash hands together.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each food and name it. Give a tiny piece to each child. Children: Children look and take a piece.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Chew slowly" and show how. Children: Children chew and swallow.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "How does it taste?" and wait. Children: Child says a word such as nice or sweet.

Step 4. Teacher: Wipe mouths and say thank you. Children: Child says thank you.

### Practical activity: Tasting tray
1. Wash hands first.
2. Each child tastes a tiny piece of each food.
3. Say how it tastes.

**Safety:** Check every child’s allergy information first and cut food into very small pieces. Sit children down and supervise closely for choking.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer one food and let the child lick it if they do not want to bite.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name a food they like at home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we taste with?
  **Expected response:** Our tongue.
- **Ask:** How does it taste?
  **Expected response:** Sweet, nice or a word.

### Expected outcomes
- Tastes safe foods
- Says a word about the taste

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say "Thank you for the food."

### Home connection
Let your child taste small safe foods and say how they taste.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "taste safe foods and say one word to describe them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Sweet, sour and salty

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Sweet, sour and salty

### Learning objectives
- Children will taste something sweet, sour and salty and match each word to its taste.

### Success criteria
- Tastes tiny amounts
- Matches a word to the taste with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use eyes, ears and hands to explore objects and name what they find.

**Key vocabulary:** smell, taste, nose, tongue, sweet, salty

**Instructional materials:** A pinch of sugar, a drop of lemon juice and a very small pinch of salt on separate spoons; Water to sip; Napkins

### Starter
Say "Today we find sweet, sour and salty." Show three small spoons.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Check allergies and show each spoon and say its name. Children: Children look and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Give a tiny taste of sugar and say "Sweet." Children: Child tastes and says sweet.

Step 3. Teacher: Give a drop of lemon and say "Sour." Do the same with salt. Children: Child tastes and says sour or salty.

Step 4. Teacher: Offer water and ask "Which one did you like?" Children: Child says a word or points.

### Practical activity: Three tastes
1. Wash hands.
2. Each child tastes sugar, lemon and salt in tiny amounts.
3. Match the word to the taste.

**Safety:** Use only tiny amounts and check allergies first. Do not force any child. Offer water and watch closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer sweet only and let the child choose to try more.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name a sweet and a salty food they like.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is sugar sweet?
  **Expected response:** Yes, sweet.
- **Ask:** Is lemon sour?
  **Expected response:** Yes, sour.

### Expected outcomes
- Names sweet, sour and salty with support
- Tastes small amounts

### Conclusion
Sip water and say "Thank you, tongue."

### Home connection
Talk about sweet, sour and salty foods at mealtimes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "taste something sweet, sour and salty and match each word to its taste" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 4 smell and taste check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Tasting safe foods

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child uses smell and taste to explore.

### Success criteria
- Names a smell
- Says a word about a taste

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use eyes, ears and hands to explore objects and name what they find.

**Key vocabulary:** smell, taste, nose, tongue, sweet, salty

**Instructional materials:** Smell pots; Taste plates; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "Nose and tongue" and show two stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up two stations: smell pots and tasting. Children: Children rotate every six minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What do you smell?" and "How does it taste?" Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy.

### Practical activity: Smell and taste stations
1. Set up two stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Check allergies again and supervise every station closely. Cut food very small and offer water.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and offer one choice.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to describe a taste to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: smells gently; says nice or not nice; says a word about a taste.

- **Ask:** What do we smell with?
  **Expected response:** Our nose.
- **Ask:** What do we taste with?
  **Expected response:** Our tongue.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Describes smells and tastes with a word

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Talk about smells and tastes when you cook.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
