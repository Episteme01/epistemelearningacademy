# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 6: Water

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Pouring and splashing; Wet and dry; Water in my day

## Learning objectives
- Children will pour, splash and play with water safely.
- Children will say whether things are wet or dry.

**Key vocabulary:** water, wet, dry, pour, splash, drink

**Content:** Pouring and splashing water, deciding whether things are wet or dry, and noticing where water is used in our day.

**Teaching activities:** Keep water shallow and supervise every moment; use aprons and towels; name each action; let children pour from small jugs; wipe spills at once.

**Learner activities:** Pour, splash and play with water safely and say wet or dry.

**Resources:** A shallow basin; Small jugs, cups and funnels; Aprons and towels; A sponge; A cloth and a dry towel; Pictures of water uses

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child pours water safely and says wet or dry for objects.

## Expected outcomes
- Pours water from one container to another.
- Says wet or dry for objects.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Numeracy: full and empty; Social Habits: cleaning up spills; Literacy: describing words

## Day 1 (Monday): Pouring water

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Pouring and splashing

### Learning objectives
- Children will pour water from one container to another.

### Success criteria
- Pours water from a jug into a cup
- Says pour or water

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored the senses and can describe things as soft, hard, rough and smooth.

**Key vocabulary:** water, wet, dry, pour, splash, drink

**Instructional materials:** A shallow basin of water; Small jugs and cups; Aprons; Towels

### Starter
Pour a little water slowly into a cup and say "Pour, pour, pour. Look, the water goes in."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to hold a jug with two hands and pour slowly into a cup. Children: Children watch and copy holding a jug.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child pour water from the jug into a cup while you hold the cup steady. Children: Child pours water into the cup.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Where does the water go?" and "Is the cup full?" Children: Child says or points to where the water went.

Step 4. Teacher: Tip the water back into the basin. Children: Child pours the water back and says "pour".

### Practical activity: Water pouring
1. Put on aprons and set up the basin on a waterproof mat.
2. Each child pours water from a jug to a cup.
3. Wipe spills and dry hands.

**Safety:** Keep water shallow and supervise at all times. Use a waterproof mat and wipe spills at once so no one slips.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the jug together with the child and pour slowly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to fill two cups to the top.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What are you doing?
  **Expected response:** Pouring water.
- **Ask:** Where did the water go?
  **Expected response:** Into the cup.

### Expected outcomes
- Pours water into a cup
- Says pour and water

### Conclusion
Pour the water back and dry hands with a towel.

### Home connection
Let your child pour water from a small jug into a cup at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "pour water from one container to another" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- POUR
- WATER

**Picture suggestions:**
- A jug pouring water
- A cup of water

**Song or rhyme:** Pour, pour, pour the water. Splash, splash, splash.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Splash and play

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Pouring and splashing

### Learning objectives
- Children will explore water with their hands and toys and say splash.

### Success criteria
- Plays in water gently
- Says splash or wet

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored the senses and can describe things as soft, hard, rough and smooth.

**Key vocabulary:** water, wet, dry, pour, splash, drink

**Instructional materials:** A shallow basin; Plastic toys; Sponges; Aprons; Towels

### Starter
Tap the water with your hand and say "Splash, splash." Ask "Can you splash?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to splash gently with one hand. Children: Children splash gently with a hand.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a sponge and show how to squeeze it. Children: Child squeezes the sponge and says splash or drip.

Step 3. Teacher: Float plastic toys on the water and say their names. Children: Child pushes a toy through the water.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Stop" and ask children to hold their hands still. Children: Children stop and watch the water settle.

### Practical activity: Splash time
1. Put on aprons and sit by the basin.
2. Splash, squeeze and play with toys.
3. Stop and watch the water settle.

**Safety:** Keep splashing gentle and supervise every child. Wipe the floor often and keep children’s faces dry.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Play beside the child and move their hand through the water.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a big splash and a small splash.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you do with water?
  **Expected response:** Splash, pour or play.
- **Ask:** Is your hand wet?
  **Expected response:** Yes, wet.

### Expected outcomes
- Plays safely in water
- Says splash and wet

### Conclusion
Dry hands and say "Water play is finished."

### Home connection
Splash gently in the bath or in a bowl of water.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Wet and dry

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Wet and dry

### Learning objectives
- Children will say whether objects are wet or dry.

### Success criteria
- Touches and says wet or dry
- Sorts wet and dry things with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored the senses and can describe things as soft, hard, rough and smooth.

**Key vocabulary:** water, wet, dry, pour, splash, drink

**Instructional materials:** A wet cloth and a dry cloth; A wet sponge and a dry sponge; A dry towel; Two trays

### Starter
Hold a wet cloth and a dry cloth. Say "This is wet. This is dry." Let children touch both.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Dip a cloth in water and say "Now it is wet." Children: Children touch the wet cloth and say wet.

Step 2. Teacher: Touch a dry towel and say "Dry." Children: Children touch the towel and say dry.

Step 3. Teacher: Show pairs of objects and ask "Which is wet?" Children: Child points to the wet object.

Step 4. Teacher: Sort wet and dry things into two trays. Children: Child puts each object on the correct tray.

### Practical activity: Wet and dry sort
1. Put wet and dry objects on the table.
2. Each child touches each one.
3. Sort them into two trays.

**Safety:** Use only safe objects and wipe up any water. Keep electrical items away from water.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Touch both objects together and say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a dry thing wet and say what happened.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the cloth wet or dry?
  **Expected response:** Wet or dry.
- **Ask:** How do you know?
  **Expected response:** It feels wet or dry.

### Expected outcomes
- Says wet or dry for objects
- Sorts wet and dry things

### Conclusion
Hang the wet cloth in the sun and say "It will be dry soon."

### Home connection
Talk about wet and dry things at home, such as wet clothes and dry towels.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say whether objects are wet or dry" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Water in my day

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Water in my day

### Learning objectives
- Children will say what we use water for each day.

### Success criteria
- Names at least two uses of water
- Acts out a use of water

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored the senses and can describe things as soft, hard, rough and smooth.

**Key vocabulary:** water, wet, dry, pour, splash, drink

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of drinking, washing, cooking and watering plants; A cup; A bowl

### Starter
Show a cup of water and say "We drink water." Pretend to drink.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each picture and say what water is used for. Children: Children look and say the use.

Step 2. Teacher: Act out each use: drink, wash hands, wash a plate, water a plant. Children: Children copy the actions.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "What do we use water for?" and show the pictures. Children: Child points to a picture.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a picture and act it. Children: Child acts and friends guess.

### Practical activity: Water uses
1. Put four pictures on the floor.
2. Act out each use of water.
3. Say the word together.

**Safety:** Use only clean drinking water for tasting and never allow children to drink from the basin. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two pictures and act together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name a use of water at home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we drink?
  **Expected response:** Water.
- **Ask:** What else do we use water for?
  **Expected response:** Washing, cooking or plants.

### Expected outcomes
- Names uses of water
- Acts out uses of water

### Conclusion
Say "Thank you, water" and wash hands.

### Home connection
Talk about how your family uses water each day.

- Which children could show "say what we use water for each day" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 water check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Pouring and splashing

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child plays with water and uses water words.

### Success criteria
- Pours water safely
- Says wet or dry

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored the senses and can describe things as soft, hard, rough and smooth.

**Key vocabulary:** water, wet, dry, pour, splash, drink

**Instructional materials:** A basin; Jugs and cups; Wet and dry objects; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing "Pour, pour, pour the water" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: pouring, splashing and wet and dry. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is happening?" and "Is it wet or dry?" Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children dry hands and tidy.

### Practical activity: Water stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station closely and keep water shallow. Wipe spills quickly.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to explain what they did to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: pours water; says splash or wet; says wet or dry for objects.

- **Ask:** Can you pour?
  **Expected response:** Child pours water.
- **Ask:** Is it wet?
  **Expected response:** Yes, wet.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Plays safely with water

### Conclusion
Dry hands and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Let your child help with water tasks at home.
