# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 9: Weather

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Sunny and cloudy; Rain and wind; Dressing for the weather

## Learning objectives
- Children will notice and name the weather each day.
- Children will choose clothes for different weather with support.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, rain, wind, cloud, hot, cold

**Content:** Noticing and naming weather, and choosing clothes for sun and rain.

**Teaching activities:** Look at the sky every morning with the children; use a weather chart; name the weather each day; link to real experience of rain, harmattan and sun; never go outside in dangerous weather.

**Learner activities:** Name the weather and choose suitable clothes for rain or sun with support.

**Resources:** A weather chart with sun, rain, cloud and wind pictures; A raincoat, an umbrella, a sun hat and a jumper; A ribbon streamer; A bowl for rain

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names the weather today and chooses suitable clothes.

## Expected outcomes
- Names the weather today with a word or gesture.
- Chooses suitable clothes for rain or sun with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: weather songs; Numeracy: counting days; Social Habits: dressing appropriately

## Day 1 (Monday): Sunny and cloudy

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Sunny and cloudy

### Learning objectives
- Children will say if it is sunny or cloudy.

### Success criteria
- Looks at the sky
- Says sunny or cloudy with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials such as wet, dry, hard and soft and have played outdoors with water, sand and soil.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, rain, wind, cloud, hot, cold

**Instructional materials:** A weather chart; A yellow circle and a grey cloud; A sun hat

### Starter
Look out of the window together and say "What is the sky like today?" Show the yellow and grey pictures.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Look at the sky from a safe place and say what you see. Children: Children look and say sun or cloud.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the sun picture and say "Sunny" and the cloud picture and say "Cloudy". Children: Children say sunny and cloudy.

Step 3. Teacher: Put the right picture on the weather chart for today. Children: Child places the picture.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "The sun is hot. We wear a hat." Put on the hat. Children: Child puts on the hat.

### Practical activity: Weather watch
1. Go to a safe place to look at the sky.
2. Name the weather.
3. Put the picture on the chart.

**Safety:** Never look directly at the sun. Stay in the shade and use hats and water in hot weather.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the sky and say the word with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what the weather is at home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is it sunny or cloudy?
  **Expected response:** Sunny or cloudy.
- **Ask:** What do we wear in the sun?
  **Expected response:** A hat.

### Expected outcomes
- Names sunny or cloudy
- Chooses a sun hat

### Conclusion
Wave to the sun and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Look at the sky each morning with your child and say the weather.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say if it is sunny or cloudy" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**

**Picture suggestions:**

**Song or rhyme:** Sunny, sunny, hello sun. Cloudy, cloudy, grey clouds.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Rainy day

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Rain and wind

### Learning objectives
- Children will talk about rain and where it comes from.

### Success criteria
- Says rain
- Does a rain action

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials such as wet, dry, hard and soft and have played outdoors with water, sand and soil.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, rain, wind, cloud, hot, cold

**Instructional materials:** A rain picture; A watering can; A bowl; An umbrella

### Starter
Tap your fingers on the table like rain and say "Pitter, patter, rain." Show the umbrella.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Tell a short story about rain coming from clouds. Children: Children listen and say rain.

Step 2. Teacher: Pour water from a watering can into a bowl to show rain falling. Children: Children watch and say rain.

Step 3. Teacher: Tap your fingers softly and then quickly like rain. Children: Children tap their fingers.

Step 4. Teacher: Show the umbrella and say "We use an umbrella in the rain." Children: Child holds the umbrella.

### Practical activity: Rain play
1. Pour water slowly from a watering can.
2. Make rain sounds with fingers.
3. Hold an umbrella and pretend to walk in the rain.

**Safety:** Keep the water shallow and supervise closely. Wipe spills. Do not go out in thunder or lightning.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Tap the rain together and say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what we wear in the rain.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where does rain come from?
  **Expected response:** Clouds.
- **Ask:** What do we use in the rain?
  **Expected response:** An umbrella or raincoat.

### Expected outcomes
- Says the word rain
- Names an umbrella

### Conclusion
Close the umbrella and say "Rain, rain, go away."

### Home connection
Listen to the rain with your child and say rain.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "talk about rain and where it comes from" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- RAIN
- UMBRELLA

**Picture suggestions:**

- An umbrella

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Windy day

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Rain and wind

### Learning objectives
- Children will notice wind by watching things move.

### Success criteria
- Says wind
- Shows something moving in the wind

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials such as wet, dry, hard and soft and have played outdoors with water, sand and soil.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, rain, wind, cloud, hot, cold

**Instructional materials:** Ribbon streamers; A paper windmill; A feather; A fan

### Starter
Blow on a feather and say "The wind makes it move."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold a streamer outside and watch it move in the wind. Children: Children watch the streamer move.

Step 2. Teacher: Blow gently on a windmill and say "Wind." Children: Children blow on the windmill.

Step 3. Teacher: Use a fan from a safe distance and watch the feather. Children: Children watch the feather move.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What moves in the wind?" and point to objects. Children: Child points to moving objects.

### Practical activity: Wind play
1. Give each child a streamer.
2. Go outside and run gently to make the streamer fly.
3. Watch trees and leaves move.

**Safety:** Check the area is clear and run gently. Keep fans out of reach and never allow children to touch moving blades.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the streamer together and move it.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find three things that move in the wind.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What moves in the wind?
  **Expected response:** Leaves, streamers or trees.
- **Ask:** Can you feel the wind?
  **Expected response:** Yes, on my face.

### Expected outcomes
- Notices wind by what moves
- Says the word wind

### Conclusion
Fold the streamers and say "The wind is resting."

### Home connection
Fly a paper streamer outside with your child on a windy day.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "notice wind by watching things move" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- WIND
- BLOW

**Picture suggestions:**
- A streamer in the wind
- A windmill

## Day 4 (Thursday): Dressing for the weather

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Dressing for the weather

### Learning objectives
- Children will choose clothes for different weather.

### Success criteria
- Chooses a hat, a raincoat or a jumper
- Says why

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials such as wet, dry, hard and soft and have played outdoors with water, sand and soil.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, rain, wind, cloud, hot, cold

**Instructional materials:** A raincoat, a sun hat, a jumper and sandals; Weather pictures; A doll

### Starter
Show a rain picture and say "It is raining. What do we wear?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a weather picture and ask what to wear. Children: Child points to clothes.

Step 2. Teacher: Dress the doll for the weather shown. Children: Children help to choose and put on clothes.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child choose clothes for a weather card. Children: Child picks the right clothes.

Step 4. Teacher: Match clothes to weather cards in pairs. Children: Child matches the clothes and the weather.

### Practical activity: Dress for the weather
1. Put clothes and weather pictures on the mat.
2. Choose clothes for each weather.
3. Dress the doll.

**Safety:** Keep clothes clean and dry. Make sure children can reach and put on clothes safely and without rushing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two clothes and say the weather.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to choose clothes for hot and for cold weather.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we wear in the rain?
  **Expected response:** A raincoat.
- **Ask:** What do we wear in the sun?
  **Expected response:** A hat.

### Expected outcomes
- Chooses suitable clothes for weather
- Matches clothes to weather

### Conclusion
Put the clothes away and say "We are ready for any weather."

### Home connection
Talk about what to wear each morning according to the weather.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "choose clothes for different weather" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 9 weather check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Sunny and cloudy

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names the weather and chooses clothes.

### Success criteria
- Names the weather today
- Chooses suitable clothes

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials such as wet, dry, hard and soft and have played outdoors with water, sand and soil.

**Key vocabulary:** sun, rain, wind, cloud, hot, cold

**Instructional materials:** Weather chart; Clothes; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the weather song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: weather chart, dressing and wind play. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is the weather?" and "What do we wear?" Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Weather stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use safe objects only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two pictures.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell the class the weather.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names the weather today; chooses suitable clothes; notices wind or rain.

- **Ask:** What is the weather today?
  **Expected response:** Sunny, cloudy, rainy or windy.
- **Ask:** What do we wear?
  **Expected response:** Suitable clothes for the weather.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Names the weather with a word

### Conclusion
Sing the weather song and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Say the weather each day at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child names the weather and chooses clothes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
