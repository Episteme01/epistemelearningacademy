# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 1: My body

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Naming body parts; What my body can do; Songs about my body

## Learning objectives
- Children will point to and name main parts of the body.
- Children will show what different body parts can do.

**Key vocabulary:** head, hands, feet, eyes, ears, mouth

**Content:** Naming main body parts, finding out what the body can do, and singing body songs.

**Teaching activities:** Touch each body part as you name it; use a mirror so children see their own body; sing and move together; use the same words every day.

**Learner activities:** Point to and name body parts and show what each part can do.

**Resources:** A large mirror; A doll or soft toy; A body picture; A scarf; A drum

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child points to at least six body parts when named.

## Expected outcomes
- Points to at least six body parts when named.
- Says what a body part can do with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: action songs; Physical Development: body awareness; Social Habits: respecting our bodies

## Day 1 (Monday): Head, hands and feet

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Naming body parts

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to and name the head, hands and feet.

### Success criteria
- Points to the head, hands and feet when named
- Says a body part name with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they know some body parts from home and enjoy songs and movement.

**Key vocabulary:** head, hands, feet, eyes, ears, mouth

**Instructional materials:** A large mirror; A doll; A mat

### Starter
Sit in front of the mirror, touch your head and say "This is my head." Ask each child to touch their own head.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Touch your head, hands and feet in turn and say each name clearly. Children: Children touch the same part on their own body.

Step 2. Teacher: Say a body part and wait for children to find it on themselves. Children: Children touch the part named.

Step 3. Teacher: Show the parts on the doll and ask "Where are the doll’s feet?" Children: Child points to the doll’s feet.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child look in the mirror and name one part. Children: Child touches a part and says its name.

### Practical activity: Mirror body parts
1. Sit children in front of the mirror in small groups.
2. Name a body part and let them touch it.
3. Say the name together.

**Safety:** Fix the mirror firmly to the wall or hold it steady so it cannot fall. Use an unbreakable mirror and keep children seated.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Touch the part together on the child’s body and say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name a part and touch it on a friend’s doll.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is your head?
  **Expected response:** Child touches their head.
- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Hands or feet.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to head, hands and feet
- Names a body part with support

### Conclusion
Wave your hands, wiggle your feet and say "Thank you, body."

### Home connection
At bath time, touch each body part with your child and name it.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to and name the head, hands and feet" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HEAD
- HANDS
- FEET

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child’s head
- Two hands
- Two feet

**Song or rhyme:** Head, hands, feet, head, hands, feet. This is me.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Eyes, ears and mouth

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Naming body parts

### Learning objectives
- Children will point to and name the eyes, ears, nose and mouth.

### Success criteria
- Points to the eyes, ears, nose and mouth
- Says at least two names

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they know some body parts from home and enjoy songs and movement.

**Key vocabulary:** head, hands, feet, eyes, ears, mouth

**Instructional materials:** A mirror; A doll; A face picture

### Starter
Point to your eyes and say "These are my eyes." Wink and ask "Where are your eyes?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Touch each part of your face and say its name: eyes, ears, nose, mouth. Children: Children touch the same part on their own face.

Step 2. Teacher: Say a part of the face and wait. Children: Children touch the part named.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing "Eyes, ears, nose and mouth" while touching each part. Children: Children touch and sing along.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask each child to point to a part on the doll’s face. Children: Child points and says the name.

### Practical activity: Face parts
1. Sit in front of the mirror.
2. Touch each face part as it is named.
3. Make a funny face and name the parts.

**Safety:** Remind children to touch their faces gently and keep fingers away from eyes. Use a steady, unbreakable mirror.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Touch the part on your own face first and let the child copy.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name a part and say what it does.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where are your eyes?
  **Expected response:** Child touches their eyes.
- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Ears, nose or mouth.

### Expected outcomes
- Points to four face parts
- Names a face part with support

### Conclusion
Blow a kiss and say "Thank you, face."

### Home connection
Play touch your nose, ears and mouth at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "point to and name the eyes, ears, nose and mouth" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- EYES
- EARS
- NOSE
- MOUTH

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two eyes
- Two ears
- A nose
- A mouth

**Song or rhyme:** Eyes, ears, nose and mouth. This is my face.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): What can my hands do?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** What my body can do

### Learning objectives
- Children will show things that hands, feet and mouths can do.

### Success criteria
- Does an action with their hands
- Says what a body part does with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they know some body parts from home and enjoy songs and movement.

**Key vocabulary:** head, hands, feet, eyes, ears, mouth

**Instructional materials:** A ball; A scarf; A drum; A cup

### Starter
Wave your hands and say "My hands can wave." Ask "What can your hands do?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show an action for each body part: clap hands, stamp feet, blink eyes, sing with the mouth. Children: Children copy each action.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "What can hands do?" and show a ball, scarf and cup. Children: Child picks up an object and uses their hands.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "What can feet do?" and let children stamp, walk and tiptoe. Children: Children do the actions.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask each child to show you something their body can do. Children: Child shows an action.

### Practical activity: Body can do
1. Clear a space on the mat.
2. Call a body part and let children show an action.
3. Name the action together.

**Safety:** Clear the floor so children do not trip. Keep actions gentle and stop if anyone is tired.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Do the action together and say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to invent a new thing a body part can do.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What can hands do?
  **Expected response:** Clap, wave or hold.
- **Ask:** What can feet do?
  **Expected response:** Walk, stamp or jump.

### Expected outcomes
- Shows what body parts can do
- Says what a part does with support

### Conclusion
Wave, clap and stamp together and then sit still.

### Home connection
Ask your child what their hands and feet can do while you do daily tasks.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show things that hands, feet and mouths can do" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- CLAP
- WALK
- LOOK

**Picture suggestions:**
- Hands clapping
- Feet walking
- Eyes looking

## Day 4 (Thursday): The body song

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Songs about my body

### Learning objectives
- Children will sing a body song and touch each part.

### Success criteria
- Touches the right body part in the song
- Sings or hums along

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they know some body parts from home and enjoy songs and movement.

**Key vocabulary:** head, hands, feet, eyes, ears, mouth

**Instructional materials:** A drum; A doll; Body picture cards

### Starter
Say "Let us sing about our body." Touch your head and tap the drum once.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Sing "Head, shoulders, knees and toes" slowly with actions. Children: Children touch each part.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing again and speed up a little. Children: Children keep up with the actions.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing again and leave out a word. Children: Children touch the part and say the word.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child lead the song with the doll. Children: Child touches the doll and the group copies.

### Practical activity: Sing and touch
1. Stand in a circle.
2. Sing the song and touch each part.
3. Sing it quickly and then slowly.

**Safety:** Leave space around each child. Stop and sit down when children are tired.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Sing softly and touch the part together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead the song and choose the part.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where are your knees?
  **Expected response:** Child touches their knees.
- **Ask:** Where are your toes?
  **Expected response:** Child touches their toes.

### Expected outcomes
- Touches parts to a song
- Sings along with actions

### Conclusion
Sing the song quietly and sit down.

### Home connection
Sing the body song at home and touch each part.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sing a body song and touch each part" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- KNEES
- TOES
- SHOULDERS

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two knees
- Ten toes
- Two shoulders

**Song or rhyme:** Head, shoulders, knees and toes, knees and toes.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 1 body check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Naming body parts

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names body parts and shows what they can do.

### Success criteria
- Points to at least six body parts
- Shows an action

**Previous knowledge:** Children are new to the class; they know some body parts from home and enjoy songs and movement.

**Key vocabulary:** head, hands, feet, eyes, ears, mouth

**Instructional materials:** A mirror; A doll; A drum; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the body song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: mirror, doll and song. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station name a body part and ask the child to point to it. Children: Child points to the part.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Body stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use an unbreakable mirror held steady.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use three body parts.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to name parts for a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: points to body parts when named; names a part; shows what a part can do.

- **Ask:** Where is your nose?
  **Expected response:** Child touches their nose.
- **Ask:** What can your feet do?
  **Expected response:** Walk, jump or stamp.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Points to at least six body parts

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye with hands and feet.

### Home connection
Name body parts at home during dressing and bathing.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child names body parts and shows what they can do" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
