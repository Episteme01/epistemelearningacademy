# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 3: My senses

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Touching soft things; Touching hard things; Rough and smooth

## Learning objectives
- Children will touch and describe soft and hard objects.
- Children will describe rough and smooth with support.

**Key vocabulary:** soft, hard, rough, smooth, touch, feel

**Content:** Touching soft and hard objects, and describing rough and smooth.

**Teaching activities:** Let children touch with hands and say each word as they touch; use a mystery bag; never force a child to touch something they dislike; offer a tool instead.

**Learner activities:** Touch objects, say soft, hard, rough or smooth and sort objects.

**Resources:** Cotton wool, a sponge, a teddy; A stone, a wooden block, a spoon; Sandpaper, a rough mat, a smooth tile and a leaf; A cloth bag; Two baskets

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says soft or hard for an object and sorts objects with support.

## Expected outcomes
- Says soft or hard for a familiar object.
- Sorts objects into soft and hard with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: describing words; Numeracy: sorting; Creative Development: textures

## Day 1 (Monday): Touching soft things

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Touching soft things

### Learning objectives
- Children will touch soft things and say the word soft.

### Success criteria
- Touches soft things gently
- Says soft with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use their eyes and ears to explore and name what they see and hear.

**Key vocabulary:** soft, hard, rough, smooth, touch, feel

**Instructional materials:** Cotton wool, a sponge, a teddy and a soft cloth; A tray

### Starter
Stroke the teddy and say "Soft, soft, soft." Ask "Can you feel it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pass the soft objects round and say "Soft" as each child touches. Children: Children touch each object and say soft.

Step 2. Teacher: Squeeze the sponge and the teddy and show how they change. Children: Children squeeze and say soft.

Step 3. Teacher: Close your eyes and feel an object. Ask "Is it soft?" Children: Child feels an object and nods or says soft.

Step 4. Teacher: Put soft things on a tray and name each. Children: Child names a soft object.

### Practical activity: Soft things
1. Put soft objects on a tray.
2. Each child touches each object.
3. Say "soft" together.

**Safety:** Use clean items that cannot be swallowed. Watch that no child puts cotton wool in the mouth or nose.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Stroke the object with the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another soft thing in the room.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the teddy soft?
  **Expected response:** Yes, soft.
- **Ask:** What else is soft?
  **Expected response:** Sponge, cloth or cotton wool.

### Expected outcomes
- Touches and names soft things
- Says soft with support

### Conclusion
Put the soft things in a basket and say "Soft."

### Home connection
Find soft things at home, such as a pillow, and say soft.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "touch soft things and say the word soft" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**

**Picture suggestions:**

**Song or rhyme:** Soft, soft, soft. Teddy is soft.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Touching hard things

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Touching hard things

### Learning objectives
- Children will touch hard things and say the word hard.

### Success criteria
- Touches hard things safely
- Says hard with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use their eyes and ears to explore and name what they see and hear.

**Key vocabulary:** soft, hard, rough, smooth, touch, feel

**Instructional materials:** A smooth large stone; A wooden block; A metal spoon; A plastic cup

### Starter
Tap the wooden block on the table and say "Hard." Ask "Is it hard or soft?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pass the hard objects round and say "Hard" as each child touches. Children: Children touch each and say hard.

Step 2. Teacher: Tap objects on the table and listen to the sound. Children: Children tap and listen.

Step 3. Teacher: Show a soft and a hard object together and ask which is hard. Children: Child points to the hard object.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose a hard thing and show a friend. Children: Child shows the object and says hard.

### Practical activity: Hard things
1. Put hard objects on a tray.
2. Each child touches and taps them.
3. Say "hard" together.

**Safety:** Use stones larger than a child’s fist and smooth wood without splinters. Supervise closely and keep objects out of mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Tap the object together and say hard.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a hard thing outside.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the block hard?
  **Expected response:** Yes, hard.
- **Ask:** Which is hard?
  **Expected response:** The stone or block.

### Expected outcomes
- Touches and names hard things
- Says hard with support

### Conclusion
Put the hard things back and say "Hard."

### Home connection
Find hard things at home, such as a table, and say hard.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "touch hard things and say the word hard" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Rough and smooth

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Rough and smooth

### Learning objectives
- Children will feel rough and smooth surfaces and use the words.

### Success criteria
- Touches rough and smooth surfaces
- Says rough or smooth with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use their eyes and ears to explore and name what they see and hear.

**Key vocabulary:** soft, hard, rough, smooth, touch, feel

**Instructional materials:** Fine sandpaper; A rough mat; A smooth tile; A smooth leaf; A cloth bag

### Starter
Rub the sandpaper and say "Rough." Rub the tile and say "Smooth." Ask "Which is smooth?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pass the rough and smooth objects round and say each word. Children: Children touch and say the word.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Is it rough or smooth?" for each object. Children: Child says rough or smooth.

Step 3. Teacher: Put one object in the bag and let a child feel it. Children: Child feels and says rough or smooth.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child find a rough and a smooth thing in the room. Children: Child finds and touches objects.

### Practical activity: Rough and smooth
1. Put rough and smooth objects on the table.
2. Each child touches each one.
3. Sort them into two baskets.

**Safety:** Use fine sandpaper only and watch that no child rubs it hard or near the face. Wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Guide the child’s finger across the surface and say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a rough and a smooth thing on the playground.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the tile smooth?
  **Expected response:** Yes, smooth.
- **Ask:** Is the sandpaper rough?
  **Expected response:** Yes, rough.

### Expected outcomes
- Names rough and smooth with support
- Sorts rough and smooth things

### Conclusion
Put the objects back and say "Rough and smooth."

### Home connection
Feel rough and smooth things at home, such as a wall and a plate.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "feel rough and smooth surfaces and use the words" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- ROUGH
- SMOOTH

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Sort soft and hard

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Touching soft things

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects into soft and hard.

### Success criteria
- Puts a soft object in the soft basket
- Puts a hard object in the hard basket

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use their eyes and ears to explore and name what they see and hear.

**Key vocabulary:** soft, hard, rough, smooth, touch, feel

**Instructional materials:** Two baskets labelled soft and hard; Six objects: three soft, three hard

### Starter
Show two baskets and say "One is for soft things and one is for hard things."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pick up a teddy, feel it and put it in the soft basket. Children: Children watch and say soft.

Step 2. Teacher: Pick up a stone, feel it and put it in the hard basket. Children: Children watch and say hard.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pick an object, feel it and choose a basket. Children: Child feels and sorts the object.

Step 4. Teacher: Check each basket together. Children: Children touch and say soft or hard.

### Practical activity: Soft and hard baskets
1. Put six objects on the mat.
2. Each child feels an object.
3. Put it in the right basket.

**Safety:** Use large objects that cannot be swallowed. Supervise sorting and turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use two objects and feel them together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find one more soft and one more hard thing.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which basket does the teddy go in?
  **Expected response:** The soft basket.
- **Ask:** Which basket does the stone go in?
  **Expected response:** The hard basket.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts soft and hard objects with support
- Says the words

### Conclusion
Count the objects in each basket and say well done.

### Home connection
Sort soft and hard toys at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort objects into soft and hard" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- SOFT
- HARD

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two baskets

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 touch check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Rough and smooth

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child uses touch to describe objects.

### Success criteria
- Says soft or hard for an object
- Sorts objects with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can use their eyes and ears to explore and name what they see and hear.

**Key vocabulary:** soft, hard, rough, smooth, touch, feel

**Instructional materials:** Soft and hard objects; Rough and smooth objects; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "We touch with our hands" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: soft, hard and rough and smooth. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Is it soft or hard?" or "Is it rough or smooth?" Children: Child answers with a word.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Touch stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use safe objects only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two objects.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to teach a friend the words.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says soft or hard; says rough or smooth; sorts objects.

- **Ask:** Is this soft or hard?
  **Expected response:** Soft or hard.
- **Ask:** Is this rough or smooth?
  **Expected response:** Rough or smooth.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Describes objects by touch

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye with soft hands.

### Home connection
Describe things at home as soft, hard, rough or smooth.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child uses touch to describe objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

- Touch
