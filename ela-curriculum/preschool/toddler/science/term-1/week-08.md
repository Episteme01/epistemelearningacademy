# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 8: Earth materials

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Playing with sand; Digging in soil; Wet sand and dry sand

## Learning objectives
- Children will play with sand and soil using hands and tools.
- Children will notice that wet sand and dry sand feel different.

**Key vocabulary:** sand, soil, dig, wet, dry, scoop

**Content:** Playing with sand and soil, digging, and noticing that wet and dry sand feel different.

**Teaching activities:** Let children dig and scoop freely; name each action; use small tools; keep sand away from eyes; model washing hands after play.

**Learner activities:** Dig and scoop sand and soil with tools and say wet or dry for sand.

**Resources:** A sand tray; A tray of soil; Spoons, scoops, small buckets and trowels; A watering can; Aprons; Towels

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child digs or scoops with a tool and says wet or dry for sand.

## Expected outcomes
- Digs or scoops sand or soil with a tool.
- Says wet or dry for sand.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Numeracy: full and empty; Physical Development: scooping and digging; Social Habits: washing hands

## Day 1 (Monday): Playing with sand

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Playing with sand

### Learning objectives
- Children will play with dry sand using hands and tools.

### Success criteria
- Scoops sand into a bucket
- Says the word sand

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with water and say wet and dry, float and sink.

**Key vocabulary:** sand, soil, dig, wet, dry, scoop

**Instructional materials:** A tray of dry sand; Scoops and small buckets; Aprons

### Starter
Let sand run through your fingers and say "Sand. It is soft and it falls."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to scoop sand into a bucket and pour it out. Children: Children scoop and pour.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children feel the sand with their hands. Children: Child touches the sand and says sand.

Step 3. Teacher: Fill a small bucket and turn it upside down to make a sand shape. Children: Child makes a shape in the sand.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What can sand do?" Children: Child says pour, fall or scoop.

### Practical activity: Sand tray
1. Set up a sand tray on a mat.
2. Scoop and pour sand with tools.
3. Make a sand shape.

**Safety:** Keep sand away from eyes and mouths. Use clean sand and wash hands afterwards. Sweep up spilled sand to avoid slipping.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Scoop together with a hand over the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to fill two buckets and say which has more.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Sand.
- **Ask:** What can you do with sand?
  **Expected response:** Scoop or pour.

### Expected outcomes
- Scoops and pours sand
- Says the word sand

### Conclusion
Tidy the sand and wash hands.

### Home connection
Let your child play in sand at the compound or park.

- Which children could show "play with dry sand using hands and tools" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Digging in soil

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Digging in soil

### Learning objectives
- Children will dig in soil and look for small creatures with a spoon.

### Success criteria
- Digs in soil with a tool
- Says soil or dig

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with water and say wet and dry, float and sink.

**Key vocabulary:** sand, soil, dig, wet, dry, scoop

**Instructional materials:** A tray of soil; Spoons and trowels; A magnifying glass; Aprons

### Starter
Hold a handful of soil and say "This is soil. Plants grow in soil." Let it fall.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to dig with a spoon and put soil in a bucket. Children: Children dig and fill the bucket.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children touch the soil and describe it. Children: Child touches the soil and says soft or dark.

Step 3. Teacher: Look for small stones or roots in the soil with a magnifying glass. Children: Child looks and points.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Wash hands" after digging. Children: Children wash hands with soap.

### Practical activity: Digging soil
1. Set up a tray of clean soil.
2. Dig with spoons and trowels.
3. Look at what is in the soil.

**Safety:** Use clean potting soil and wash hands after every session. Do not allow children to put soil in their mouths and cover any cuts.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Dig together and name the tool.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find three different things in the soil.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Soil.
- **Ask:** What do plants need?
  **Expected response:** Soil and water.

### Expected outcomes
- Digs in soil with a tool
- Says soil and dig

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say "Thank you, soil."

### Home connection
Dig in the garden with your child and wash hands afterwards.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "dig in soil and look for small creatures with a spoon" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Wet sand and dry sand

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Wet sand and dry sand

### Learning objectives
- Children will feel the difference between wet and dry sand.

### Success criteria
- Touches wet and dry sand
- Says wet or dry

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with water and say wet and dry, float and sink.

**Key vocabulary:** sand, soil, dig, wet, dry, scoop

**Instructional materials:** Two trays: dry sand and wet sand; A watering can; Small buckets; Towels

### Starter
Pour dry sand through your fingers, then wet sand. Say "Dry sand falls. Wet sand sticks."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pour dry sand and let it run through your fingers. Children: Children feel the dry sand.

Step 2. Teacher: Pour a little water on sand and mix. Children: Children watch and feel the wet sand.

Step 3. Teacher: Try to make a sandcastle with dry sand and then wet sand. Children: Child tries both and sees which works.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Which sand makes a shape?" Children: Child points to the wet sand.

### Practical activity: Sandcastles
1. Fill buckets with wet sand.
2. Turn them over to make castles.
3. Try with dry sand and compare.

**Safety:** Keep sand away from eyes and wash hands afterwards. Use shallow water and wipe spills.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Feel both sands together and say the words.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to wet and mix a tray of sand.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which sand makes a shape?
  **Expected response:** Wet sand.
- **Ask:** Is this sand wet or dry?
  **Expected response:** Wet or dry.

### Expected outcomes
- Says wet or dry for sand
- Makes a shape with wet sand

### Conclusion
Tidy the sand trays and wash hands.

### Home connection
Make a sandcastle with wet sand at home or at the park.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "feel the difference between wet and dry sand" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Sand and soil play

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Playing with sand

### Learning objectives
- Children will explore sand and soil together in play.

### Success criteria
- Uses sand and soil in play
- Says what they are making

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with water and say wet and dry, float and sink.

**Key vocabulary:** sand, soil, dig, wet, dry, scoop

**Instructional materials:** Trays of sand and soil; Plastic animals; Small buckets and spoons; Towels

### Starter
Show plastic animals and say "Let us make a farm in the sand."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to make a road in the sand with a finger. Children: Children make roads.

Step 2. Teacher: Show how to make a garden with soil. Children: Children dig a small garden.

Step 3. Teacher: Put animals on the farm and ask "What are you making?" Children: Child tells you what they are making.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How is sand different from soil?" Children: Child says a word such as dark or light.

### Practical activity: Sand farm
1. Set up trays of sand and soil.
2. Make roads, gardens and a farm.
3. Tell a friend what you made.

**Safety:** Keep sand and soil away from faces and wash hands afterwards. Supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Play beside the child and name what they make.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a long road and a small garden.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you make?
  **Expected response:** A farm, road or garden.
- **Ask:** What colour is the soil?
  **Expected response:** Dark or brown.

### Expected outcomes
- Plays creatively with sand and soil
- Says what they made

### Conclusion
Tidy the trays and wash hands.

### Home connection
Play with sand or soil outdoors and talk about what you make.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 sand and soil check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Wet sand and dry sand

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child plays with sand and soil.

### Success criteria
- Digs or scoops with a tool
- Says wet or dry for sand

**Previous knowledge:** Children can play with water and say wet and dry, float and sink.

**Key vocabulary:** sand, soil, dig, wet, dry, scoop

**Instructional materials:** Sand and soil trays; Tools; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing "Scoop, scoop, scoop the sand" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: dry sand, wet sand and soil. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What are you doing?" and "Is it wet or dry?" Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy.

### Practical activity: Sand and soil stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station closely. Keep sand away from eyes and wash hands after each station.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to explain what they made.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: digs or scoops; says wet or dry for sand; says soil and sand.

- **Ask:** Is the sand wet or dry?
  **Expected response:** Wet or dry.
- **Ask:** What did you do?
  **Expected response:** Dig, scoop or pour.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Plays safely with sand and soil

### Conclusion
Wash hands and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Play with sand or soil at home and wash hands afterwards.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child plays with sand and soil" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
