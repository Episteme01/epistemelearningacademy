# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 5: My senses

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W05 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Mystery bag; Sense stations; Which sense do I use?

## Learning objectives
- Children will use different senses at stations to explore objects.
- Children will link a sense to a body part.

**Key vocabulary:** see, hear, touch, smell, taste, senses

**Content:** Using different senses at stations, a mystery bag and linking each sense to a body part.

**Teaching activities:** Run stations with simple rules; ask which sense the child used; point to the body part each time; use the same five picture cards daily.

**Learner activities:** Use different senses to explore objects and link each sense to a body part.

**Resources:** Five sense picture cards: eye, ear, hand, nose, tongue; A mystery bag; Objects for each station; A doll

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names at least three senses and points to the right body part.

## Expected outcomes
- Names at least three senses with support.
- Points to the body part for each sense.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: describing words; Numeracy: counting five senses; Physical Development: sensory play

## Day 1 (Monday): Five senses song

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Which sense do I use?

### Learning objectives
- Children will sing a song about the five senses and touch the matching body part.

### Success criteria
- Touches the body part for each sense
- Sings or hums along

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored seeing, hearing, touch, smell and taste separately.

**Key vocabulary:** see, hear, touch, smell, taste, senses

**Instructional materials:** Five sense picture cards; A doll; A mat

### Starter
Hold up the eye card and say "We see with our eyes." Touch your eyes.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each card and touch the body part: eyes, ears, hands, nose, tongue. Children: Children touch the same part.

Step 2. Teacher: Sing "I see with my eyes, I hear with my ears" and show each card. Children: Children listen and touch each part.

Step 3. Teacher: Sing again and let children hold the cards. Children: Child holds up the card at the right word.

Step 4. Teacher: Let a child lead with the doll. Children: Child touches the doll and friends copy.

### Practical activity: Five senses
1. Sit in a circle with five cards.
2. Sing the song and touch each body part.
3. Hold up the card at the right word.

**Safety:** Keep actions gentle and fingers away from eyes. Leave space between children.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Touch the part together and say the sense.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to lead and choose the sense.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do we see with?
  **Expected response:** Eyes.
- **Ask:** What do we smell with?
  **Expected response:** Nose.

### Expected outcomes
- Names senses and body parts with support
- Sings the song

### Conclusion
Sing quietly and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Sing the five senses song at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sing a song about the five senses and touch the matching body part" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Sense stations

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Sense stations

### Learning objectives
- Children will use different senses at stations to explore objects.

### Success criteria
- Uses the right sense at each station
- Says what they found

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored seeing, hearing, touch, smell and taste separately.

**Key vocabulary:** see, hear, touch, smell, taste, senses

**Instructional materials:** Five stations: look, listen, touch, smell and a picture tasting station with a lemon picture; Objects for each station

### Starter
Show the five stations and say "Today we use all our senses."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Visit each station with the whole group and say which sense to use. Children: Children look, listen, touch and smell as shown.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children explore at their own pace at each station. Children: Child explores and talks.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which sense did you use?" at each. Children: Child points to the body part.

Step 4. Teacher: Come together and name one thing from each station. Children: Child names an object.

### Practical activity: Sense stations
1. Set up five stations.
2. Children visit each in turn.
3. Name the sense and the body part.

**Safety:** Use only safe objects and supervise each station. Keep the tasting station as pictures only unless allergies have been checked.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Stay with the child at each station and name the sense.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach a friend a station.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Which sense did you use?
  **Expected response:** Look, listen, touch or smell.
- **Ask:** What did you find?
  **Expected response:** The name of an object.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses different senses
- Names objects found

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say "Thank you, senses."

### Home connection
Use all your senses on a walk at home and talk about them.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use different senses at stations to explore objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): The big mystery bag

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Mystery bag

### Learning objectives
- Children will use touch and smell to guess what is in the bag.

### Success criteria
- Feels an object in a bag
- Names the object or says a word about it

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored seeing, hearing, touch, smell and taste separately.

**Key vocabulary:** see, hear, touch, smell, taste, senses

**Instructional materials:** A cloth bag; A sponge, an orange, a spoon and a toy car; A tray

### Starter
Show the bag and say "There is something in my bag. Use your hand to feel it."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the sponge in the bag. Show a child how to feel it without looking. Children: Child feels the object.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Is it soft or hard?" and "What do you think it is?" Children: Child says a word or guesses.

Step 3. Teacher: Take it out and say "It is a sponge." Children: Children look and check.

Step 4. Teacher: Repeat with the orange, spoon and toy car. Children: Each child feels and guesses.

### Practical activity: Mystery bag
1. Put one object in the bag.
2. Each child feels and guesses.
3. Show the object.

**Safety:** Use clean, large objects only and make sure no object is sharp. Supervise the bag.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two choices and describe the object first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put a secret object in for a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is it soft or hard?
  **Expected response:** Soft or hard.
- **Ask:** What is it?
  **Expected response:** The object’s name.

### Expected outcomes
- Guesses an object by touch
- Uses describing words

### Conclusion
Put the objects back and say thank you.

### Home connection
Play the mystery bag game at home with safe objects.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use touch and smell to guess what is in the bag" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FEEL
- GUESS

**Picture suggestions:**
- A cloth bag
- A sponge and an orange

## Day 4 (Thursday): Which sense?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Which sense do I use?

### Learning objectives
- Children will match activities to the sense they use.

### Success criteria
- Names the sense for an action
- Points to the matching body part

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored seeing, hearing, touch, smell and taste separately.

**Key vocabulary:** see, hear, touch, smell, taste, senses

**Instructional materials:** Five sense picture cards; Action pictures: looking, listening, touching, sniffing, eating; A mat

### Starter
Say "I am looking at a book. Which sense?" and point to your eyes.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show an action picture and say what the child is doing. Children: Children look and listen.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which sense?" and wait for children to point to the card or body part. Children: Child points to the right card.

Step 3. Teacher: Do an action yourself and ask which sense. Children: Children point to the matching body part.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child do an action for friends to match. Children: Child acts and friends match.

### Practical activity: Match the sense
1. Put five sense cards on the floor.
2. Show an action picture.
3. Children stand by the matching card.

**Safety:** Keep the floor clear and children seated between turns. Supervise movement.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two sense cards and say the sense first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make up an action for a sense.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What sense do you use to listen?
  **Expected response:** Hearing, with the ears.
- **Ask:** What sense do you use to eat?
  **Expected response:** Taste, with the tongue.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches actions to senses with support
- Points to the body part

### Conclusion
Put the cards away and say "Five senses."

### Home connection
Ask your child which sense they use at home during the day.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match activities to the sense they use" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 senses check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Sense stations

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child uses the senses and links them to body parts.

### Success criteria
- Names at least three senses
- Points to the body part for each sense

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored seeing, hearing, touch, smell and taste separately.

**Key vocabulary:** see, hear, touch, smell, taste, senses

**Instructional materials:** Sense cards; Stations; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the five senses song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: look and listen, touch and smell, sense cards. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Which sense?" and watch what the child does. Children: Child answers with a word or point.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Senses stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use safe objects only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use three senses.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to name all five senses.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names senses; points to the matching body part; uses senses to describe objects.

- **Ask:** What do we see with?
  **Expected response:** Our eyes.
- **Ask:** What do we smell with?
  **Expected response:** Our nose.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Names senses and body parts with support

### Conclusion
Sing the senses song once more and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Talk about senses at home during daily routines.
