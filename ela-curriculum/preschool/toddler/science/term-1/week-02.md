# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 2: My senses

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Looking with my eyes; Listening with my ears; Spot and guess games

## Learning objectives
- Children will use their eyes to find, match and name objects.
- Children will use their ears to find and name sounds.

**Key vocabulary:** see, look, hear, listen, eyes, ears

**Content:** Using eyes to find, match and name objects, using ears to find and name sounds, and playing spot and guess games.

**Teaching activities:** Ask children to look and listen slowly; give time; use real objects and everyday sounds; never test with loud sounds.

**Learner activities:** Use eyes to find hidden objects and ears to name sounds.

**Resources:** A tray of objects; A cloth; Pictures hidden around the room; A bell, a rattle and a spoon with a cup; A cloth bag

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child finds a hidden object by looking and names a sound by listening.

## Expected outcomes
- Finds a hidden object by looking.
- Names a sound heard in the room.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: listening games; Numeracy: matching; Physical Development: moving carefully

## Day 1 (Monday): Looking with my eyes

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Looking with my eyes

### Learning objectives
- Children will use their eyes to find and name objects.

### Success criteria
- Finds an object when asked
- Says the name of what they see

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name main body parts including eyes and ears.

**Key vocabulary:** see, look, hear, listen, eyes, ears

**Instructional materials:** A tray of five familiar objects; A cloth

### Starter
Point to your eyes and say "We see with our eyes." Look around the room and say "I see a door."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the tray and name each object slowly. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Find the ball" and wait. Children: Child looks and points to the ball.

Step 3. Teacher: Cover the tray and take one object away, then show it again. Ask "What is missing?" Children: Child looks and says or points.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose an object and tell what they see. Children: Child picks and names it.

### Practical activity: What do I see?
1. Place five objects on a tray.
2. Ask each child to find a named object.
3. Take one away and ask what is missing.

**Safety:** Use large safe objects only. Keep the tray at child height and supervise handling.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use three objects and point to help.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find two objects in one go.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do you see?
  **Expected response:** The name of an object.
- **Ask:** Where is the ball?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the ball.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds named objects by looking
- Names what they see

### Conclusion
Cover the tray and say "Thank you, eyes."

### Home connection
Play I spy with your child at home and name what you see.

### Teacher reflection (private)

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Spot the hidden picture

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Spot and guess games

### Learning objectives
- Children will find hidden pictures around the room using their eyes.

### Success criteria
- Finds a hidden picture
- Says what is on the picture

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name main body parts including eyes and ears.

**Key vocabulary:** see, look, hear, listen, eyes, ears

**Instructional materials:** Six picture cards hidden around the room; A basket

### Starter
Say "Some pictures are hiding. Use your eyes to find them." Show one picture so children know what to find.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the six picture cards and name each one. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Hide the pictures in easy, low places. Children: Children watch where you hide them.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Look, look, look" and let children search. Children: Children look and find pictures.

Step 4. Teacher: Put the pictures in the basket and name each one. Children: Child brings a picture and names it.

### Practical activity: Hide and find
1. Hide six pictures in easy places.
2. Children find one each.
3. Bring the picture to the basket and name it.

**Safety:** Hide pictures only in clear, low places away from hazards. Supervise the search and keep children together.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk with the child and point to the area.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to hide a picture for a friend to find.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you find?
  **Expected response:** The name of the picture.
- **Ask:** Where was it?
  **Expected response:** Under, on or by something.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds hidden pictures
- Names the pictures

### Conclusion
Count the pictures in the basket and say well done.

### Home connection
Hide a small toy at home and let your child find it using their eyes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find hidden pictures around the room using their eyes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Listening with my ears

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Listening with my ears

### Learning objectives
- Children will use their ears to name sounds.

### Success criteria
- Listens quietly
- Names or points to a sound source

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name main body parts including eyes and ears.

**Key vocabulary:** see, look, hear, listen, eyes, ears

**Instructional materials:** A bell, a rattle and a spoon with a cup; A screen or cloth

### Starter
Point to your ears and say "We hear with our ears. Shh, listen." Ring the bell.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each sound-maker and make its sound. Children: Children listen and name each object.

Step 2. Teacher: Hide behind a screen and make a sound. Children: Children listen and point to the object.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "What can you hear?" and wait in silence for ten seconds. Children: Children listen and say or point.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child make a sound for friends to name. Children: Child makes a sound and friends name it.

### Practical activity: Listening game
1. Put three sound-makers on the mat.
2. Hide behind a screen and make one sound.
3. Children point to the matching object.

**Safety:** Use gentle sounds only. Do not use very loud sounds near children’s ears.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two sound-makers and make the sound twice.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to close their eyes and name the sound.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What can you hear?
  **Expected response:** Bell, rattle or the cup.
- **Ask:** What do we hear with?
  **Expected response:** Our ears.

### Expected outcomes
- Names sounds by listening
- Points to the sound source

### Conclusion
Cover the sound-makers and say "Thank you, ears."

### Home connection
Listen for sounds at home and name them with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Mystery sound bag

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Spot and guess games

### Learning objectives
- Children will guess an object by its sound.

### Success criteria
- Guesses what is in the bag by listening
- Says the name of the object

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name main body parts including eyes and ears.

**Key vocabulary:** see, look, hear, listen, eyes, ears

**Instructional materials:** A cloth bag; A bell, a spoon, a small shaker and a key ring; A tray

### Starter
Shake the bag gently and say "What is in my bag? Listen."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put the bell in the bag, shake it and ask "What is it?" Children: Children listen and guess.

Step 2. Teacher: Take it out to show them. Children: Children look and check.

Step 3. Teacher: Repeat with the spoon, shaker and key ring. Children: Children listen and guess each one.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child shake the bag and ask the group. Children: Child shakes and friends guess.

### Practical activity: Mystery bag
1. Put one object in a bag.
2. Shake the bag and ask what it is.
3. Show the object.

**Safety:** Use only large soft objects and never put anything in the mouth. Supervise the bag handling.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects and say the names.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put an object in the bag for a friend to guess.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is in the bag?
  **Expected response:** The object’s name.
- **Ask:** How do you know?
  **Expected response:** By listening.

### Expected outcomes
- Guesses an object by its sound
- Says what they heard

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say thank you.

### Home connection
Play guess the sound with a cup and spoon at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "guess an object by its sound" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 senses check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Spot and guess games

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child uses eyes and ears to explore.

### Success criteria
- Finds an object by looking
- Names a sound by listening

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name main body parts including eyes and ears.

**Key vocabulary:** see, look, hear, listen, eyes, ears

**Instructional materials:** Objects; Picture cards; Sound-makers; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "Eyes and ears" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: spot, listen and mystery bag. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What do you see?" or "What do you hear?" Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Eyes and ears stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use gentle sounds.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two choices.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to run a listening game.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: finds an object by looking; names a sound by listening; says which body part is used.

- **Ask:** What do we see with?
  **Expected response:** Our eyes.
- **Ask:** What do we hear with?
  **Expected response:** Our ears.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Uses eyes and ears to explore

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye with eyes and ears.

### Home connection
Play look and listen games at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child uses eyes and ears to explore" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
