# Toddler Science — Term 1, Week 7: Water

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Things that float; Things that sink; Floating boats

## Learning objectives
- Children will put objects in water and see which float or sink.
- Children will say float or sink with support.

**Key vocabulary:** float, sink, boat, heavy, light, water

**Content:** Putting objects in water to see which float and which sink, and making boats that float.

**Teaching activities:** Let children predict by saying or pointing; put each object in the water together and say "float" or "sink"; use large objects only; keep a tray of towels ready.

**Learner activities:** Put objects in water, say float or sink and put a floating object on the water.

**Resources:** A clear basin of water; A cork, a leaf, a plastic cup, a wooden spoon; A stone, a metal spoon, a key, a coin-sized large washer; A paper boat; Towels

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says float or sink after seeing an object in water.

## Expected outcomes
- Says float or sink after seeing an object in water.
- Puts a floating object on the water.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Numeracy: heavy and light; Literacy: describing words; Creative Development: making boats

## Day 1 (Monday): Things that float

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Things that float

### Learning objectives
- Children will see objects float on water and say the word float.

### Success criteria
- Watches objects floating
- Says float with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can pour and play with water and say wet and dry.

**Key vocabulary:** float, sink, boat, heavy, light, water

**Instructional materials:** A clear basin of water; A cork, a leaf, a plastic cup and a wooden spoon; Towels

### Starter
Put a leaf on the water and say "The leaf floats. It stays on top."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Put each object on the water one at a time and say "It floats." Children: Children watch and say float.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child put one object on the water. Children: Child puts an object in the water and watches.

Step 3. Teacher: Push a floating object down and let it go. Children: Child watches it come back up and says float.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What floats?" and point to the objects. Children: Child points to floating things.

### Practical activity: Floating things
1. Set up a clear basin on a mat.
2. Put objects on the water.
3. Say "float" for each one that stays on top.

**Safety:** Use only objects larger than a child’s mouth that are not sharp. Keep water shallow and supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Put the object in the water together and say float.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another floating thing in the room.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does the leaf float?
  **Expected response:** Yes, it floats.
- **Ask:** What floats?
  **Expected response:** Cork, cup or leaf.

### Expected outcomes
- Says float for floating objects
- Puts an object on the water

### Conclusion
Take the objects out and dry them with a towel.

### Home connection
Float a leaf or a plastic cup in a bowl of water at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "see objects float on water and say the word float" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FLOAT
- TOP

**Picture suggestions:**
- A leaf on water
- A floating cup

**Song or rhyme:** Float, float, float on top. Float, float, float.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Things that sink

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Things that sink

### Learning objectives
- Children will see objects sink in water and say the word sink.

### Success criteria
- Watches objects sinking
- Says sink with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can pour and play with water and say wet and dry.

**Key vocabulary:** float, sink, boat, heavy, light, water

**Instructional materials:** A clear basin of water; A large smooth stone, a metal spoon, a large key and a large washer; Towels

### Starter
Drop a large stone in the water and say "Splash! The stone sinks. It goes to the bottom."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Drop each heavy object in the water one at a time and say "It sinks." Children: Children watch and say sink.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child drop a heavy object in the water. Children: Child drops an object and watches it go down.

Step 3. Teacher: Pick the object up and let children feel how heavy it is. Children: Child holds the object and says heavy.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What sinks?" and point to the objects. Children: Child points to sinking things.

### Practical activity: Sinking things
1. Set up a clear basin on a mat.
2. Drop heavy objects in the water.
3. Say "sink" for each one that goes down.

**Safety:** Use only large, smooth objects that cannot be swallowed. Keep water shallow and do not let children put objects in their mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Drop the object together and watch it sink.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess before dropping each object.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does the stone sink?
  **Expected response:** Yes, it sinks.
- **Ask:** What sinks?
  **Expected response:** Stone, spoon or key.

### Expected outcomes
- Says sink for sinking objects
- Drops an object in the water

### Conclusion
Take the objects out and dry them with a towel.

### Home connection
Drop a spoon and a leaf in a bowl of water and see what happens.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "see objects sink in water and say the word sink" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Float or sink?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Things that sink

### Learning objectives
- Children will guess and test if objects float or sink.

### Success criteria
- Guesses float or sink
- Checks the guess in water

**Previous knowledge:** Children can pour and play with water and say wet and dry.

**Key vocabulary:** float, sink, boat, heavy, light, water

**Instructional materials:** A basin of water; Six objects: three float and three sink; Two hoops or trays labelled float and sink; Towels

### Starter
Show a cork and ask "Will it float or sink? Let us find out."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Hold up an object and ask the children to guess float or sink. Children: Children guess with a word or point.

Step 2. Teacher: Put it in the water and watch. Children: Children watch and say float or sink.

Step 3. Teacher: Put floating things on the float tray and sinking things on the sink tray. Children: Child puts the object on the right tray.

Step 4. Teacher: Check each tray together. Children: Children say float or sink for each object.

### Practical activity: Test and sort
1. Choose an object and guess.
2. Test in the water.
3. Sort into float and sink trays.

**Safety:** Use large objects only and supervise closely. Keep the water shallow and wipe spills.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects and say the guess together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a new object and test it.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Will the cork float or sink?
  **Expected response:** Float.
- **Ask:** Did you guess right?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no.

### Expected outcomes
- Guesses and tests float and sink
- Sorts objects into float and sink

### Conclusion
Dry the objects and put them away.

### Home connection
Test float and sink with safe objects in a bowl at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "guess and test if objects float or sink" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FLOAT
- SINK

**Picture suggestions:**
- Two trays

## Day 4 (Thursday): Floating boats

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Floating boats

### Learning objectives
- Children will make a boat and see that it floats.

### Success criteria
- Helps make a boat
- Puts the boat on the water

**Previous knowledge:** Children can pour and play with water and say wet and dry.

**Key vocabulary:** float, sink, boat, heavy, light, water

**Instructional materials:** Paper or foam boats; A plastic tub; A large leaf; A straw as a sail; Towels

### Starter
Show a paper boat and say "This is a boat. Let us put it on the water."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a boat made from foam or folded paper. Children: Children look and touch.

Step 2. Teacher: Put the boat on the water and say "It floats." Children: Children watch it float.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child push their own boat with a gentle puff or hand. Children: Child pushes the boat across the water.

Step 4. Teacher: Try a large leaf as a boat. Children: Child puts a leaf on the water and watches.

### Practical activity: Boat race
1. Fill a long tub with water.
2. Put boats at one end.
3. Gently blow or push the boats across.

**Safety:** Use a shallow tub and supervise closely. Remove boats and wipe spills afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the boat together and place it on the water.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to put a small toy on the boat.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does the boat float?
  **Expected response:** Yes, it floats.
- **Ask:** What pushes the boat?
  **Expected response:** Our hand or a puff of air.

### Expected outcomes
- Floats a boat on water
- Says boat and float

### Conclusion
Dry the boats on a towel and say "Boats float."

### Home connection
Make a boat from foil or paper and float it in a bowl.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make a boat and see that it floats" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- BOAT
- FLOAT

**Picture suggestions:**
- A paper boat on water

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 float and sink check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T1-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Things that float

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child describes objects in water.

### Success criteria
- Says float or sink after seeing an object in water
- Puts an object on the water

**Previous knowledge:** Children can pour and play with water and say wet and dry.

**Key vocabulary:** float, sink, boat, heavy, light, water

**Instructional materials:** A basin; Float and sink objects; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the float song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: float, sink and boats. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Float or sink?" Children: Child says float or sink.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children dry hands and tidy.

### Practical activity: Float and sink stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station closely and keep water shallow. Wipe spills quickly.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one object.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to explain float and sink to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says float or sink after seeing; puts an object on the water; sorts floaters and sinkers.

- **Ask:** Does it float or sink?
  **Expected response:** Float or sink.
- **Ask:** Can you put it in the water?
  **Expected response:** Child does it.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Uses the words float and sink

### Conclusion
Dry hands and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Try float and sink at bath time.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child describes objects in water" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- Float and sink

**Picture suggestions:**
