# Toddler Science — Term 3, Week 1: Movement

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Pushing a toy; Pulling a toy; Push and pull games

## Learning objectives
- Children will push and pull toys and say push or pull.
- Children will notice that pushing moves things away and pulling brings them closer.

**Key vocabulary:** push, pull, toy, cart, move, stop

**Content:** Pushing and pulling toys and objects, noticing that pushing moves things away and pulling brings them closer, and push and pull games.

**Teaching activities:** Use large light objects that children can move easily; say push or pull as the action happens; let children feel the effort; link to everyday actions such as opening a door.

**Learner activities:** Push and pull toys on request and say push or pull with support.

**Resources:** Toy carts and boxes with string; Large soft balls; A light box; A rope; A door that opens both ways

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child pushes and pulls a toy on request and says push or pull.

## Expected outcomes
- Pushes and pulls a toy on request.
- Says push or pull with support.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Physical Development: strength and control; Literacy: action words; Numeracy: near and far

## Day 1 (Monday): Pushing a toy

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Pushing a toy

### Learning objectives
- Children will push a toy and see it move away.

### Success criteria
- Pushes a toy along the floor
- Says push with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore materials, name animals and plants and care for living things.

**Key vocabulary:** push, pull, toy, cart, move, stop

**Instructional materials:** A toy cart or box; A soft ball; A clear floor space

### Starter
Push a toy cart across the floor and say "Push. The cart moves away."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to push a cart with both hands and say "Push, push, push." Children: Children watch and say push.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child push the cart gently across the floor. Children: Child pushes the cart and watches it move.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which way did it go?" and point away. Children: Child points away from themselves.

Step 4. Teacher: Push a soft ball and watch it roll. Children: Child pushes the ball and says push.

### Practical activity: Pushing play
1. Clear a long space on the floor.
2. Push a cart or box across it.
3. Say push and watch it go.

**Safety:** Clear the floor and keep children well apart. Use light carts and push at a walking pace. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Push the cart together with a hand over the child’s hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to push the cart as far as they can.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What are you doing?
  **Expected response:** Pushing.
- **Ask:** Which way does it go?
  **Expected response:** Away from me.

### Expected outcomes
- Pushes a toy on request
- Says the word push

### Conclusion
Park the cart and say "Push, push, stop."

### Home connection
Let your child push a small cart or a toy car at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "push a toy and see it move away" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- PUSH
- AWAY

**Picture suggestions:**
- A child pushing a cart
- An arrow pointing away

**Song or rhyme:** Push, push, push the cart. Push it far away.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Pulling a toy

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Pulling a toy

### Learning objectives
- Children will pull a toy on a string and see it come closer.

### Success criteria
- Pulls a toy along the floor
- Says pull with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore materials, name animals and plants and care for living things.

**Key vocabulary:** push, pull, toy, cart, move, stop

**Instructional materials:** A toy on a string; A rope; A light box with a handle

### Starter
Pull a toy on a string towards you and say "Pull. The toy comes to me."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to hold the string and pull the toy. Children: Children watch and say pull.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child pull a toy along the floor. Children: Child pulls the toy.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which way did it come?" and point to yourself. Children: Child points towards themselves.

Step 4. Teacher: Pull a light box with a handle. Children: Child pulls the box and says pull.

### Practical activity: Pulling play
1. Give each child a toy on a string.
2. Pull it along a clear path.
3. Say pull and see it come closer.

**Safety:** Use short strings and watch for tripping. Remove strings from around necks and keep strings out of reach after play.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Pull the string together and say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to pull a heavier box with a friend.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What are you doing?
  **Expected response:** Pulling.
- **Ask:** Which way does it come?
  **Expected response:** Closer to me.

### Expected outcomes
- Pulls a toy on request
- Says the word pull

### Conclusion
Put the toys away and say "Pull, pull, stop."

### Home connection
Let your child pull a toy on a string at home.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Push or pull?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Push and pull games

### Learning objectives
- Children will decide if an action is a push or a pull.

### Success criteria
- Says push or pull for an action
- Does the action

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore materials, name animals and plants and care for living things.

**Key vocabulary:** push, pull, toy, cart, move, stop

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of pushing a door, pulling a wagon, pushing a swing and pulling a drawer; A toy cart; A string toy

### Starter
Show a door and say "To open this door we push." Pull a drawer and say "To open this drawer we pull."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each picture and do the action. Children: Children look and copy the action.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Is it a push or a pull?" Children: Child says push or pull.

Step 3. Teacher: Let children try pushing and pulling real objects. Children: Child pushes a door or pulls a drawer.

Step 4. Teacher: Match each picture to push or pull. Children: Child places the picture in the right group.

### Practical activity: Push or pull game
1. Put pictures on the mat.
2. Do each action.
3. Sort pictures into push and pull.

**Safety:** Keep fingers away from door hinges and drawers. Use only light doors and supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Do the action together and say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a push and a pull in the room.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Do we push or pull a swing?
  **Expected response:** Push.
- **Ask:** Do we push or pull a wagon?
  **Expected response:** Pull.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts actions into push and pull
- Says push or pull with support

### Conclusion
Put away the cards and say "Push and pull."

### Home connection
Notice pushes and pulls at home, such as opening a door.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "decide if an action is a push or a pull" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- PUSH
- PULL

**Picture suggestions:**
- A swing being pushed
- A wagon being pulled

## Day 4 (Thursday): Tug of war and trolley

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Push and pull games

### Learning objectives
- Children will use push and pull in a team game.

### Success criteria
- Pushes or pulls with a friend
- Takes turns

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore materials, name animals and plants and care for living things.

**Key vocabulary:** push, pull, toy, cart, move, stop

**Instructional materials:** A soft rope; A light trolley or box; Cones

### Starter
Say "Today we push and pull together. We are a team."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how two children can push a trolley together. Children: Children watch and say push.

Step 2. Teacher: Let pairs push the trolley to a cone. Children: Pair pushes the trolley together.

Step 3. Teacher: Show how to pull a rope gently with a friend, with no tugging. Children: Pair pulls the rope gently.

Step 4. Teacher: Talk about how it is easier together. Children: Child says easy or together.

### Practical activity: Team push and pull
1. Set up a short path with cones.
2. Push a light trolley in pairs.
3. Pull a rope gently with a friend.

**Safety:** Never allow tugging or letting go suddenly. Use a soft rope and supervise every pair. Clear the floor.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Work with the child and a friend and guide gently.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count how many pushes it takes to reach the cone.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is it easier alone or together?
  **Expected response:** Together.
- **Ask:** What do we do with a trolley?
  **Expected response:** Push it.

### Expected outcomes
- Pushes and pulls with a friend
- Works with a partner

### Conclusion
Put the equipment away and say "We worked together."

### Home connection
Push and pull things together at home as a family.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use push and pull in a team game" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- TEAM
- TOGETHER

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 1 push and pull check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Push and pull games

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child pushes and pulls and uses the words.

### Success criteria
- Pushes and pulls a toy on request
- Says push or pull

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore materials, name animals and plants and care for living things.

**Key vocabulary:** push, pull, toy, cart, move, stop

**Instructional materials:** Carts; String toys; Picture cards; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the push and pull song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: pushing, pulling and sorting. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Is it push or pull?" Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Push and pull stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use light objects only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one action.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to show a friend push and pull.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: pushes on request; pulls on request; says push or pull; sorts actions.

- **Ask:** What is this action?
  **Expected response:** Push or pull.
- **Ask:** Can you push the cart?
  **Expected response:** Child pushes the cart.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Pushes and pulls with confidence

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye with a push and a pull.

### Home connection
Talk about push and pull at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child pushes and pulls and uses the words" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
