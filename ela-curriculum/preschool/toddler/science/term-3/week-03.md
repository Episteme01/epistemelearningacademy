# Toddler Science — Term 3, Week 3: Light

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Light and dark; Making shadows; Shadow puppets

## Learning objectives
- Children will notice that light comes from the sun and a torch.
- Children will make and watch shadows.

**Key vocabulary:** light, dark, shadow, sun, torch, shade

**Content:** Noticing light and dark, making shadows with the sun and a torch, and shadow puppets.

**Teaching activities:** Never let children look at the sun; use a torch with a firm cover and shine it on a wall; darken the room only as much as needed; stay close in dim light; use simple words.

**Learner activities:** Say light or dark for a place, make a shadow and play with shadow puppets.

**Resources:** A torch; A white wall or sheet; A dark box or tent; Cardboard shapes on sticks; Chalk for outdoor shadows

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says light or dark for a place and makes a shadow with a hand or toy.

## Expected outcomes
- Says light or dark for a place.
- Makes a shadow with a hand or toy.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: stories with shadows; Creative Development: puppets; Understanding the World: sun and sky

## Day 1 (Monday): Light and dark

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Light and dark

### Learning objectives
- Children will say if a place is light or dark.

### Success criteria
- Says light or dark
- Points to a source of light

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe movement and test objects with simple words.

**Key vocabulary:** light, dark, shadow, sun, torch, shade

**Instructional materials:** A torch; A dark box or tent; A picture of the sun and a lamp

### Starter
Switch off the room light a little and say "It is dark." Switch on a torch and say "Now there is light."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a dark box and look inside. Shine a torch in and say "Light." Children: Children look inside and say dark and light.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the sun and a lamp picture and say "They give us light." Children: Children point to the pictures.

Step 3. Teacher: Go to a bright place and a shaded place and say light and dark. Children: Child says light or dark.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Where is it dark?" and "What gives light?" Children: Child points or says a word.

### Practical activity: Light and dark
1. Look inside a dark box or tent.
2. Shine a torch inside.
3. Say light and dark.

**Safety:** Stay close to children in dim light and never leave a child alone in a dark space. Do not shine a torch in anyone’s eyes.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Look together and say the word.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a dark place in the school.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is it light or dark?
  **Expected response:** Light or dark.
- **Ask:** What gives us light?
  **Expected response:** The sun or a torch.

### Expected outcomes
- Says light or dark
- Names a source of light

### Conclusion
Switch the lights back on and say "Light!"

### Home connection
Notice light and dark places at home with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say if a place is light or dark" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Making shadows

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Making shadows

### Learning objectives
- Children will make shadows with their bodies and objects.

### Success criteria
- Makes a shadow with a hand or toy
- Says shadow

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe movement and test objects with simple words.

**Key vocabulary:** light, dark, shadow, sun, torch, shade

**Instructional materials:** A torch; A white wall or sheet; A toy and a cup; Chalk

### Starter
Shine the torch on the wall and make a hand shadow. Say "Look, a shadow."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show a hand shadow on the wall and ask "What is this?" Children: Children look and say shadow.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child hold their hand or a toy in front of the torch. Children: Child makes a shadow.

Step 3. Teacher: Move the toy closer and further and see the shadow change. Children: Children watch and say big or small.

Step 4. Teacher: Go outside on a sunny day and look at shadows on the ground. Children: Child looks at their shadow.

### Practical activity: Shadow play
1. Shine a torch on a wall.
2. Make shadows with hands and toys.
3. Draw round a shadow with chalk outside.

**Safety:** Never look at the sun or shine a torch at eyes. Draw shadows with chalk on a safe paved area.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the toy together in front of the light.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make a big and a small shadow.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** A shadow.
- **Ask:** How do you make a shadow?
  **Expected response:** Put something in front of the light.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a shadow
- Says shadow

### Conclusion
Switch off the torch and say "Goodbye, shadow."

### Home connection
Make hand shadows on a wall with a torch at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "make shadows with their bodies and objects" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Shadow puppets

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Shadow puppets

### Learning objectives
- Children will use shadow puppets to tell a short story.

### Success criteria
- Moves a puppet in front of the light
- Says what the puppet is

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe movement and test objects with simple words.

**Key vocabulary:** light, dark, shadow, sun, torch, shade

**Instructional materials:** Cardboard puppets on sticks: a bird, a cat, a fish; A torch; A white sheet

### Starter
Show a bird puppet in front of the torch and say "Look, a bird shadow on the wall."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Move the puppets behind a sheet in front of the light. Children: Children watch the shadows.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "What animal is it?" Children: Child names the animal.

Step 3. Teacher: Tell a short story with the puppets. Children: Children listen and watch.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child hold a puppet behind the sheet. Children: Child moves the puppet.

### Practical activity: Shadow show
1. Hang a sheet in front of a torch.
2. Hold puppets behind it.
3. Tell a short story.

**Safety:** Secure the sheet firmly and keep cables out of reach. Use a battery torch only and stay close to children in the dim light.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the puppet together and move it.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make up a short story for a puppet.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What animal is this?
  **Expected response:** A bird, cat or fish.
- **Ask:** Where is the shadow?
  **Expected response:** On the sheet.

### Expected outcomes
- Uses puppets to make shadows
- Names the shadow animals

### Conclusion
Bow to the audience and put the puppets away.

### Home connection
Make shadow puppets at home with a torch and paper shapes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "use shadow puppets to tell a short story" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Shadows outside

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Making shadows

### Learning objectives
- Children will find shadows outside and see how they change.

### Success criteria
- Finds their own shadow
- Says shadow

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe movement and test objects with simple words.

**Key vocabulary:** light, dark, shadow, sun, torch, shade

**Instructional materials:** Chalk; A sunny paved area; Toys to make shadows

### Starter
Go outside on a sunny day and say "Where is your shadow? Can you find it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Stand in the sun and look at your shadow. Children: Children look and say shadow.

Step 2. Teacher: Move and see the shadow move. Children: Children move and watch their shadow.

Step 3. Teacher: Draw round a friend’s shadow with chalk. Children: Child draws round a shadow.

Step 4. Teacher: Stand in the shade and see that there is no shadow. Children: Child notices the missing shadow.

### Practical activity: Shadow hunt
1. Go to a sunny paved area.
2. Find and draw shadows with chalk.
3. Compare sun and shade.

**Safety:** Never look at the sun. Use hats, shade and water in hot weather and stay away from roads.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk together and point to the shadow.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make their shadow tall and short.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is your shadow?
  **Expected response:** On the ground.
- **Ask:** Is there a shadow in the shade?
  **Expected response:** No.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds shadows outside
- Notices that shadows move with them

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to your shadow and go inside.

### Home connection
Look for your shadow outside on a sunny day.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "find shadows outside and see how they change" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?

- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 light check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Light and dark

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child describes light and makes shadows.

### Success criteria
- Says light or dark
- Makes a shadow

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe movement and test objects with simple words.

**Key vocabulary:** light, dark, shadow, sun, torch, shade

**Instructional materials:** Torch; Puppets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the light and dark song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: light and dark, shadows and puppets. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Light or dark?" and "What makes a shadow?" Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Light stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep children safe in dim light. Do not shine torches in eyes.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to explain shadows to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says light or dark; makes a shadow; names a source of light.

- **Ask:** Is it light or dark?
  **Expected response:** Light or dark.
- **Ask:** What makes a shadow?
  **Expected response:** Something in front of light.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Describes light and shadows

### Conclusion
Switch off the torch and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Talk about light and shadows at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child describes light and makes shadows" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
