# Toddler Science — Term 3, Week 6: Materials

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W06 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Things from nature; Things people make; Sorting natural and made

## Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects into natural and made with support.
- Children will name materials such as wood, cloth and plastic.

**Key vocabulary:** wood, cloth, plastic, stone, leaf, made

**Content:** Things from nature, things people make, and sorting natural and made things.

**Teaching activities:** Use real objects such as leaves, stones, wood, cloth and plastic; say what each thing is made of; keep the sorting simple with two baskets; link to what children see around the school.

**Learner activities:** Sort objects into natural and made and name materials such as wood, cloth and plastic.

**Resources:** Leaves, stones, shells, sticks, a flower; A plastic cup, a cloth, a wooden spoon, a metal key, a toy car; Two baskets; Pictures of natural and made things

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child sorts objects into natural and made with support and names two materials.

## Expected outcomes
- Sorts objects into natural and made with support.
- Names two materials.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Numeracy: sorting; Literacy: describing words; Creative Development: making with materials

## Day 1 (Monday): Things from nature

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W06-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Things from nature

### Learning objectives
- Children will name things that come from nature.

### Success criteria
- Names a leaf, a stone and a stick
- Says nature with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials as wet, dry, soft, hard, rough and smooth and know about plants and animals.

**Key vocabulary:** wood, cloth, plastic, stone, leaf, made

**Instructional materials:** Leaves, stones, shells and sticks; A basket; A picture of a forest

### Starter
Hold up a leaf and say "This is from nature. A tree made it. Nobody made it in a factory."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each natural object and say its name. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "These come from nature: trees, rocks, shells, water." Children: Children touch each object.

Step 3. Teacher: Go outside and find natural things in the yard. Children: Child finds a leaf, stone or stick.

Step 4. Teacher: Put the natural things in a basket and name them. Children: Child puts an object in the basket.

### Practical activity: Nature basket
1. Collect natural things outdoors.
2. Put them in a basket.
3. Name each one together.

**Safety:** Check the area for sharp objects, thorns and insects first. Use only objects larger than a child’s fist and wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the object together and say the name.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find five natural things.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is a leaf from nature?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** Name a natural thing.
  **Expected response:** Leaf, stone, shell or stick.

### Expected outcomes
- Names natural things
- Says that natural things come from nature

### Conclusion
Put the objects in the basket and say "Thank you, nature."

### Home connection
Collect safe natural things on a walk with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name things that come from nature" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Things people make

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W06-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Things people make

### Learning objectives
- Children will name things that people make.

### Success criteria
- Names a cup, a toy car and a cloth
- Says made with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials as wet, dry, soft, hard, rough and smooth and know about plants and animals.

**Key vocabulary:** wood, cloth, plastic, stone, leaf, made

**Instructional materials:** A plastic cup, a toy car, a cloth, a wooden spoon and a pencil; A basket; A picture of a factory or a tailor

### Starter
Hold up a plastic cup and say "People made this cup. It is not from nature."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each made object and say its name. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "People make these things from materials." Children: Children touch each object.

Step 3. Teacher: Look around the classroom to find made things. Children: Child finds a chair or book.

Step 4. Teacher: Put the made things in a basket and name them. Children: Child puts an object in the basket.

### Practical activity: Made things basket
1. Find made things in the room.
2. Put them in a basket.
3. Name each one together.

**Safety:** Use large safe objects only and keep objects out of mouths. Supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the object together and say the name.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what a made thing is used for.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Did people make the cup?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** Name a made thing.
  **Expected response:** Cup, car, chair or book.

### Expected outcomes
- Names made things
- Says that people make things

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say "People make things."

### Home connection
Look for made things at home and say who might have made them.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Natural or made?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W06-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Sorting natural and made

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort objects into natural and made.

### Success criteria
- Puts an object in the correct basket
- Says natural or made

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials as wet, dry, soft, hard, rough and smooth and know about plants and animals.

**Key vocabulary:** wood, cloth, plastic, stone, leaf, made

**Instructional materials:** Two baskets labelled natural and made; Eight objects; A tray

### Starter
Show two baskets and say "One is for natural things. One is for made things."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pick up a stone and put it in the natural basket. Children: Children watch and say natural.

Step 2. Teacher: Pick up a cup and put it in the made basket. Children: Children watch and say made.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pick an object and choose a basket. Children: Child sorts the object.

Step 4. Teacher: Check each basket together. Children: Children name the objects.

### Practical activity: Natural and made sort
1. Put two baskets on the mat.
2. Each child picks an object.
3. Put it in the natural or made basket.

**Safety:** Use only large safe objects. Supervise turns and keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two objects and say where each comes from.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find one more object for each basket.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is a stone natural or made?
  **Expected response:** Natural.
- **Ask:** Is a toy car natural or made?
  **Expected response:** Made.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts objects into natural and made
- Says natural or made with support

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say "Natural and made."

### Home connection
Sort things at home into natural and made.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort objects into natural and made" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): What is it made of?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W06-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Sorting natural and made

### Learning objectives
- Children will name materials such as wood, cloth, plastic and metal.

### Success criteria
- Names at least two materials
- Matches an object to its material

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials as wet, dry, soft, hard, rough and smooth and know about plants and animals.

**Key vocabulary:** wood, cloth, plastic, stone, leaf, made

**Instructional materials:** A wooden spoon, a cloth, a plastic cup and a metal spoon; Material cards: wood, cloth, plastic, metal

### Starter
Hold up a wooden spoon and say "This is made of wood. Wood comes from trees."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each object and say its material. Children: Children look and say the materials.

Step 2. Teacher: Let children touch each object and describe it. Children: Child says hard, soft or smooth.

Step 3. Teacher: Match each object to a material card. Children: Child places the object on the card.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "What is it made of?" Children: Child says the material.

### Practical activity: Material match
1. Put material cards on the mat.
2. Pick an object.
3. Match it to its material.

**Safety:** Use only large smooth objects with no sharp edges. Supervise handling.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two cards and say the material first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find three objects made of the same material.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is the spoon made of?
  **Expected response:** Wood or metal.
- **Ask:** What is the cup made of?
  **Expected response:** Plastic.

### Expected outcomes
- Names materials
- Matches objects to materials with support

### Conclusion
Put the objects away and say "Wood, cloth, plastic, metal."

### Home connection
Talk about what things are made of at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name materials such as wood, cloth, plastic and metal" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 materials check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W06-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Sorting natural and made

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child sorts natural and made things and names materials.

### Success criteria
- Sorts objects into natural and made
- Names two materials

**Previous knowledge:** Children can describe materials as wet, dry, soft, hard, rough and smooth and know about plants and animals.

**Key vocabulary:** wood, cloth, plastic, stone, leaf, made

**Instructional materials:** Objects; Baskets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the natural and made song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: natural, made and materials. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Natural or made?" and "What is it made of?" Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Materials stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use only safe objects.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two objects.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to teach a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: sorts natural and made; names materials; says where objects come from.

- **Ask:** Is a leaf natural or made?
  **Expected response:** Natural.
- **Ask:** What is the cup made of?
  **Expected response:** Plastic.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 6
- Sorts natural and made things

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye and put the baskets away.

### Home connection
Talk about natural and made things at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child sorts natural and made things and names materials" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
