# Toddler Science — Term 3, Week 5: Materials

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W05 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** What gets wet; What stays dry; Drying in the sun

## Learning objectives
- Children will test which materials get wet and which stay dry.
- Children will notice that wet things dry in the sun.

**Key vocabulary:** wet, dry, cloth, sun, umbrella, rain

**Content:** Testing which materials get wet and which stay dry, and noticing that wet things dry in the sun.

**Teaching activities:** Let children test with a spray bottle or a dripping sponge; use the words wet and dry for every result; hang cloths in the sun and look again later; keep water use small and controlled.

**Learner activities:** Test which materials get wet and which stay dry and see that wet things dry in the sun.

**Resources:** A spray bottle or sponge; Paper, cloth, plastic, foil and a leaf; An umbrella and a raincoat; A washing line; A tray

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child says wet or dry after testing and notices a wet cloth drying in the sun.

## Expected outcomes
- Says wet or dry for materials after testing.
- Notices that a wet cloth dries in the sun.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Numeracy: sorting; Literacy: describing words; Understanding the World: weather

## Day 1 (Monday): What gets wet?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** What gets wet

### Learning objectives
- Children will test which materials get wet.

### Success criteria
- Sprinkles water on a material
- Says wet

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored water and know wet and dry, and have noticed the sun and rain.

**Key vocabulary:** wet, dry, cloth, sun, umbrella, rain

**Instructional materials:** Paper, cloth and a sponge; A spray bottle or a cup of water; A tray

### Starter
Sprinkle a few drops on a piece of paper and say "Look, it is wet."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to put a few drops of water on each material. Children: Children watch and say wet.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child test a material. Children: Child puts water on a material and watches.

Step 3. Teacher: Feel the wet paper and the wet cloth. Children: Child touches and says wet.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Which things get wet?" Children: Child points to the materials.

### Practical activity: Wet test
1. Put materials on a tray.
2. Put a few drops of water on each.
3. Say wet or dry.

**Safety:** Use only a few drops of water and wipe the tray. Supervise closely.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Drop the water together and feel the material.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another thing that gets wet.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does the paper get wet?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** How does it feel?
  **Expected response:** Wet.

### Expected outcomes
- Tests materials for wetness
- Says wet for materials that get wet

### Conclusion
Wipe the tray and hang the wet things to dry.

### Home connection
Test what gets wet in the sink at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "test which materials get wet" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): What stays dry?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** What stays dry

### Learning objectives
- Children will find materials that stay dry in water.

### Success criteria
- Tests plastic and foil
- Says dry

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored water and know wet and dry, and have noticed the sun and rain.

**Key vocabulary:** wet, dry, cloth, sun, umbrella, rain

**Instructional materials:** Plastic, foil, a raincoat and a plastic bag; A spray bottle or a cup of water; A tray

### Starter
Sprinkle water on a raincoat and say "The water rolls off. It stays dry."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how water rolls off the raincoat. Children: Children watch and say dry.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child test plastic and foil with a few drops of water. Children: Child puts water on the material and watches.

Step 3. Teacher: Feel the material under the water. Children: Child touches and says dry.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "Raincoats keep us dry." Children: Children say the sentence.

### Practical activity: Dry test
1. Put materials on a tray.
2. Put a few drops on each.
3. See which stay dry.

**Safety:** Use only a few drops of water and wipe the tray. Do not allow children to put plastic bags near their faces.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Drop the water together and look at the material.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess before testing.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does the raincoat get wet?
  **Expected response:** No, it stays dry.
- **Ask:** Why do we wear a raincoat?
  **Expected response:** To stay dry.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds materials that stay dry
- Says dry for materials that keep water out

### Conclusion
Wipe the tray and put the materials away.

### Home connection
Test which materials keep water out at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Drying in the sun

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Drying in the sun

### Learning objectives
- Children will see that wet cloths dry in the sun.

### Success criteria
- Hangs a wet cloth in the sun
- Says dry after waiting

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored water and know wet and dry, and have noticed the sun and rain.

**Key vocabulary:** wet, dry, cloth, sun, umbrella, rain

**Instructional materials:** Small cloths; A washing line or a rack; Clothes pegs; A bowl of water

### Starter
Hold a wet cloth and say "This cloth is wet. How can we make it dry?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Dip a cloth in water and squeeze it. Children: Child squeezes the cloth.

Step 2. Teacher: Hang the cloth on the line in the sun. Children: Child hangs the cloth with help.

Step 3. Teacher: Feel the cloth after a short time. Children: Child feels the cloth and says wet or dry.

Step 4. Teacher: Compare with a cloth left in the shade. Children: Child compares the two cloths.

### Practical activity: Drying cloths
1. Dip a small cloth in water.
2. Hang it in the sun.
3. Feel it again later.

**Safety:** Keep the washing line low and clear of faces. Supervise water and pegs and keep children out of the sun at midday.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hang the cloth together and touch it later.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess how long it will take to dry.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is the cloth wet or dry now?
  **Expected response:** Wet at first and dry later.
- **Ask:** What dries the cloth?
  **Expected response:** The sun.

### Expected outcomes
- Notices that wet cloths dry in the sun
- Says wet and dry correctly

### Conclusion
Take down the dry cloths and fold them.

### Home connection
Hang wet clothes with your child and check when they are dry.

## Day 4 (Thursday): Wet and dry sort

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** What gets wet

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort materials into those that get wet and those that stay dry.

### Success criteria
- Sorts materials after testing
- Says wet or dry

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored water and know wet and dry, and have noticed the sun and rain.

**Key vocabulary:** wet, dry, cloth, sun, umbrella, rain

**Instructional materials:** Two hoops labelled gets wet and stays dry; Materials tested this week; A tray

### Starter
Show two hoops and say "One is for things that get wet. One is for things that stay dry."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pick up a material we tested and ask "Does it get wet?" Children: Children say yes or no.

Step 2. Teacher: Put it in the right hoop. Children: Children watch and say wet or dry.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pick a material and sort it. Children: Child sorts the material.

Step 4. Teacher: Check each hoop together. Children: Children name the materials.

### Practical activity: Sort by testing
1. Put two hoops on the floor.
2. Pick a material and remember the test.
3. Sort into the right hoop.

**Safety:** Keep materials dry for sorting. Supervise turns and keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two materials and say the result.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a new material and test it.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Does cloth get wet?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** Does plastic stay dry?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts materials into gets wet and stays dry
- Uses wet and dry

### Conclusion
Put the materials away and say "Wet and dry."

### Home connection
Sort things that get wet and stay dry at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort materials into those that get wet and those that stay dry" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 wet and dry check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T3-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** What stays dry

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child tests and describes wet and dry.

### Success criteria
- Says wet or dry after testing
- Notices a wet cloth drying in the sun

**Previous knowledge:** Children have explored water and know wet and dry, and have noticed the sun and rain.

**Key vocabulary:** wet, dry, cloth, sun, umbrella, rain

**Instructional materials:** Materials; Cloths; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the wet and dry song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: wet test, dry test and drying cloths. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Wet or dry?" Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Wet and dry stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep water to a few drops. Wipe spills quickly.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one material.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to explain wet and dry to a friend.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: says wet or dry after testing; sorts materials; notices drying in the sun.

- **Ask:** Is it wet or dry?
  **Expected response:** Wet or dry.
- **Ask:** What dries the cloth?
  **Expected response:** The sun.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Describes wet and dry with a word

### Conclusion
Wipe up and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Talk about wet and dry things at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child tests and describes wet and dry" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
