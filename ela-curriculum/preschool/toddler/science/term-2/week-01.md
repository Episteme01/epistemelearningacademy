# Toddler Science — Term 2, Week 1: Animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W01 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Pets and farm animals; Wild animals in pictures; Animal movements

## Learning objectives
- Children will name familiar animals and say where they see them.
- Children will move like different animals.

**Key vocabulary:** dog, cat, goat, hen, cow, lion

**Content:** Naming familiar animals, learning where we see them, and moving like animals.

**Teaching activities:** Use real animals where safe and large pictures or toys otherwise; make the sound and name each animal; link animals to what children see at home and in the compound; never force a child to touch an animal.

**Learner activities:** Name familiar animals, say where we see them and move like different animals.

**Resources:** Large animal pictures: dog, cat, goat, hen, cow, lion; Soft toy animals; A basket; A recording of animal sounds if available

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names at least six animals and moves like an animal on request.

## Expected outcomes
- Names at least six animals.
- Moves like an animal on request.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: animal songs and sounds; Physical Development: moving like animals; Social Habits: kindness to animals

## Day 1 (Monday): Pets and farm animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W01-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Pets and farm animals

### Learning objectives
- Children will name a dog, a cat, a goat, a hen and a cow.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to at least three animals
- Makes the sound of an animal

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore with their senses, play with water, sand and soil and name the weather.

**Key vocabulary:** dog, cat, goat, hen, cow, lion

**Instructional materials:** Large pictures of a dog, cat, goat, hen and cow; Soft toy animals; A basket

### Starter
Take the dog picture from the basket and say "Woof, woof. This is a dog. Where do we see dogs?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each picture, say the animal name and make its sound. Children: Children look, point and copy the sound.

Step 2. Teacher: Say a name and ask "Where is the goat?" Children: Child points to the goat.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Which animal lives at home with us?" and show the pictures. Children: Child points to a pet such as a dog or cat.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child hold a toy animal and say its name. Children: Child holds the toy and names it.

### Practical activity: Animal basket
1. Put toy animals in a basket.
2. Each child takes one out and names it.
3. The group makes its sound together.

**Safety:** Use clean soft toys and wash hands after real animal contact. Never put animals near children’s faces.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two animals and say the name with the child.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say where they see the animal.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this animal?
  **Expected response:** Dog, cat, goat, hen or cow.
- **Ask:** What does the cow say?
  **Expected response:** Moo.

### Expected outcomes
- Names at least three animals
- Makes an animal sound

### Conclusion
Put the animals back in the basket and say each name.

### Home connection
Name the animals you see at home or on the street with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name a dog, a cat, a goat, a hen and a cow" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Wild animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W01-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Wild animals in pictures

### Learning objectives
- Children will name wild animals in pictures and say they live outside.

### Success criteria
- Names or points to a lion, an elephant and a monkey
- Says that wild animals live outside

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore with their senses, play with water, sand and soil and name the weather.

**Key vocabulary:** dog, cat, goat, hen, cow, lion

**Instructional materials:** Large pictures of a lion, an elephant, a monkey and a giraffe; A basket; A big book of animals

### Starter
Show the lion picture and say "Roar! This is a lion. It lives in the wild."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each picture and say the name and what it looks like. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Which animal is big?" and "Which animal has a long neck?" Children: Child points to the elephant or giraffe.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Lions and elephants live in the wild. Dogs and goats live with people." Children: Children listen and nod.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick a picture and say a word about it. Children: Child shows the picture and says a word.

### Practical activity: Wild animal pictures
1. Put pictures on the mat.
2. Each child picks a picture and names it.
3. Make the animal’s sound or movement.

**Safety:** Use pictures or soft toys only. Explain that wild animals are not to be touched and that we look from far away.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the animal and say the name for the child to copy.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend which animal is the biggest.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What animal is this?
  **Expected response:** Lion, elephant, monkey or giraffe.
- **Ask:** Where do wild animals live?
  **Expected response:** Outside, in the wild.

### Expected outcomes
- Names at least three wild animals
- Says that wild animals live outside

### Conclusion
Put the pictures away and say "Goodbye, wild animals."

### Home connection
Look at pictures of wild animals with your child and say their names.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name wild animals in pictures and say they live outside" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Moving like animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W01-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Animal movements

### Learning objectives
- Children will move like different animals.

### Success criteria
- Moves like at least two animals
- Names the animal they are being

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore with their senses, play with water, sand and soil and name the weather.

**Key vocabulary:** dog, cat, goat, hen, cow, lion

**Instructional materials:** A clear space; Animal picture cards; A drum

### Starter
Show the frog picture and say "Hop like a frog." Hop gently on the spot.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each animal card and demonstrate its movement: hop, crawl, fly, stomp. Children: Children copy each movement.

Step 2. Teacher: Call an animal name and let children move like it. Children: Children move like the animal named.

Step 3. Teacher: Play the drum for each animal and stop it for freeze. Children: Children move and freeze on the signal.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose an animal card and lead the group. Children: Child leads and friends copy.

### Practical activity: Animal moves
1. Clear a space on the mat.
2. Call out an animal and move like it.
3. Freeze when the drum stops.

**Safety:** Clear the floor and keep children apart. Keep movements gentle and stop if children are tired.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move beside the child and copy together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to guess which animal a friend is.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** How does a frog move?
  **Expected response:** It hops.
- **Ask:** How does a bird move?
  **Expected response:** It flies.

### Expected outcomes
- Moves like animals on request
- Names the animal they are being

### Conclusion
Settle down like a sleeping animal.

### Home connection
Play animal moves at home and guess each other’s animals.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "move like different animals" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- HOP
- FLY
- CRAWL

**Picture suggestions:**
- A frog hopping
- A bird flying
- A snail crawling

## Day 4 (Thursday): Animal sort

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W01-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Pets and farm animals

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort animals into pets and wild animals.

### Success criteria
- Puts an animal in the correct group
- Says pet or wild with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore with their senses, play with water, sand and soil and name the weather.

**Key vocabulary:** dog, cat, goat, hen, cow, lion

**Instructional materials:** Two hoops labelled pets and wild; Eight animal pictures; A basket

### Starter
Show two hoops and say "One is for animals that live with us. One is for animals that live in the wild."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pick up a dog picture and put it in the pets hoop. Children: Children watch and say pet.

Step 2. Teacher: Pick up a lion picture and put it in the wild hoop. Children: Children watch and say wild.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pick a picture and choose a hoop. Children: Child places the picture.

Step 4. Teacher: Check each hoop together. Children: Children name the animals in each hoop.

### Practical activity: Animal hoops
1. Put two hoops on the floor.
2. Each child picks a picture.
3. Place it in the pet or wild hoop.

**Safety:** Use large pictures. Keep the floor clear and supervise turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two pictures and say whether they live with us.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another animal for each hoop.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is a dog a pet?
  **Expected response:** Yes, a pet.
- **Ask:** Is a lion a pet?
  **Expected response:** No, it is wild.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts animals into pets and wild animals
- Says pet or wild with support

### Conclusion
Put the pictures away and say "Pets and wild animals."

### Home connection
Talk about pets and wild animals when you see pictures at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort animals into pets and wild animals" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- PETS
- WILD

**Picture suggestions:**

- A lion

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 1 animals check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W01-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Pets and farm animals

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names animals and moves like them.

### Success criteria
- Names at least six animals
- Moves like an animal

**Previous knowledge:** Children can explore with their senses, play with water, sand and soil and name the weather.

**Key vocabulary:** dog, cat, goat, hen, cow, lion

**Instructional materials:** Animal pictures; Soft toys; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the animal song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: naming, sorting and moving. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What animal is this?" and "How does it move?" Children: Child names the animal or moves.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Animal stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use soft toys and large pictures.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use three animals.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to name all the animals.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names animals; says where animals live; moves like an animal.

- **Ask:** What animal is this?
  **Expected response:** The animal’s name.
- **Ask:** How does it move?
  **Expected response:** Hop, fly, crawl or walk.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 1
- Names at least six animals

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the animals.

### Home connection
Name animals together at home or on a walk.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child names animals and moves like them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
