# Toddler Science — Term 2, Week 9: Caring

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W09 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Watering plants; Feeding and caring for pets; Being kind to animals

## Learning objectives
- Children will water plants and say that plants need water.
- Children will show kind and gentle handling of animals.

**Key vocabulary:** water, feed, kind, gentle, care, pet

**Content:** Watering plants, feeding and caring for pets, and being kind and gentle to animals.

**Teaching activities:** Give each child a real job such as watering; talk about what each living thing needs; model gentle voices and hands; never leave children alone with real animals.

**Learner activities:** Water plants, say what pets need and handle toy animals gently.

**Resources:** Potted plants; A watering can; A toy pet with a bowl; Pictures of caring actions; A pet visit if possible

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child waters a plant with support and handles a toy animal gently.

## Expected outcomes
- Waters a plant with support.
- Handles a toy animal gently.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Social Habits: kindness and responsibility; Literacy: caring words; Numeracy: counting scoops of food

## Day 1 (Monday): Watering plants

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W09-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Watering plants

### Learning objectives
- Children will water plants and say that plants need water.

### Success criteria
- Waters a plant gently
- Says plants need water

**Previous knowledge:** Children know that plants and animals are alive and need food, water and care.

**Key vocabulary:** water, feed, kind, gentle, care, pet

**Instructional materials:** Two potted plants, one dry and one watered; A small watering can; A tray

### Starter
Show a droopy plant and say "This plant is thirsty. It needs a drink."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Water the droopy plant slowly and say "Drink, plant." Children: Children watch and say water.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child water a small plant with the can. Children: Child waters the plant gently.

Step 3. Teacher: Compare the two plants and ask "Which one is happy?" Children: Child points to the watered plant.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "We look after plants." Children: Children say the sentence.

### Practical activity: Water the plants
1. Give each child a turn with the can.
2. Water a plant gently.
3. Wipe up spills.

**Safety:** Use small watering cans and shallow trays. Wipe spills straight away so no one slips.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the can together and pour slowly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to water two plants and say which needs more.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do plants need?
  **Expected response:** Water.
- **Ask:** Is the plant happy?
  **Expected response:** Yes, after a drink.

### Expected outcomes
- Waters a plant gently
- Says plants need water

### Conclusion
Put the plants in the sun and say "Grow, plants."

### Home connection
Help to water a plant at home every day.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "water plants and say that plants need water" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Looking after a pet

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W09-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Feeding and caring for pets

### Learning objectives
- Children will say what a pet needs and pretend to care for it.

### Success criteria
- Names two things a pet needs
- Feeds a toy pet gently

**Previous knowledge:** Children know that plants and animals are alive and need food, water and care.

**Key vocabulary:** water, feed, kind, gentle, care, pet

**Instructional materials:** A toy dog or cat; A bowl, a water dish and a blanket; Pictures of food, water, a bed and a walk

### Starter
Hold a toy dog and say "This is Rex. He is hungry and thirsty. What does he need?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the pictures and say what a pet needs: food, water, a bed. Children: Children look and say the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Feed the toy pet and give it water. Children: Child feeds and gives water to the toy.

Step 3. Teacher: Put the pet to bed with a blanket. Children: Child tucks the pet in.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "How do we look after a pet?" Children: Child says or shows one way.

### Practical activity: Pet care play
1. Set up a bowl, water dish and bed.
2. Care for the toy pet.
3. Say what the pet needs.

**Safety:** Use soft toys only and wash hands after any real animal contact. Never leave children alone with a real pet.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Feed the toy together and name each need.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what a pet needs every day.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does a pet need?
  **Expected response:** Food, water and a bed.
- **Ask:** Who looks after the pet?
  **Expected response:** We do.

### Expected outcomes
- Names what pets need
- Cares for a toy pet gently

### Conclusion
Put the pet to bed and say "Goodnight, Rex."

### Home connection
Talk about how your family cares for a pet or an animal.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say what a pet needs and pretend to care for it" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Kind and gentle

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W09-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Being kind to animals

### Learning objectives
- Children will show kind and gentle handling of animals.

### Success criteria
- Strokes a toy gently
- Says kind or gentle

**Previous knowledge:** Children know that plants and animals are alive and need food, water and care.

**Key vocabulary:** water, feed, kind, gentle, care, pet

**Instructional materials:** Soft toy animals; Pictures of kind and unkind actions; A basket

### Starter
Stroke a soft toy slowly and say "Gentle hands. Be kind."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show kind actions: stroke, feed, speak softly. Children: Children copy with a toy.

Step 2. Teacher: Show pictures and ask "Is it kind?" Children: Child says yes or no.

Step 3. Teacher: Practise holding a toy with two hands. Children: Child holds the toy gently.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "We are kind to animals" together. Children: Children say the sentence.

### Practical activity: Gentle hands
1. Give each child a soft toy.
2. Stroke and hold it gently.
3. Say "kind".

**Safety:** Use soft toys and remind children never to pull tails or ears. Supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Stroke the toy together with a gentle hand.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to teach a friend how to be gentle.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is it kind to pull a tail?
  **Expected response:** No.
- **Ask:** How do we touch animals?
  **Expected response:** Gently.

### Expected outcomes
- Handles toy animals gently
- Says kind and gentle

### Conclusion
Put the toys away and say "We are kind."

### Home connection
Show gentle hands with pets or toys at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "show kind and gentle handling of animals" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Our caring chart

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W09-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Watering plants

### Learning objectives
- Children will use a caring chart for the class plants and toy pet.

### Success criteria
- Does a caring job
- Marks the chart

**Previous knowledge:** Children know that plants and animals are alive and need food, water and care.

**Key vocabulary:** water, feed, kind, gentle, care, pet

**Instructional materials:** A caring chart with jobs: water, feed, tidy; Stickers; The class plants

### Starter
Show the chart and say "Every day we care for our plants. Today it is your turn."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the chart and say each job. Children: Children look and say the jobs.

Step 2. Teacher: Give each child a job for the day. Children: Child chooses a job.

Step 3. Teacher: Let them do their job. Children: Child waters or tidies.

Step 4. Teacher: Put a sticker on the chart. Children: Child puts a sticker on the chart.

### Practical activity: Caring jobs
1. Choose a job on the chart.
2. Do the job.
3. Put a sticker on the chart.

**Safety:** Supervise jobs closely and wipe spills. Use child-safe tools only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Do the job together and point to the chart.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to remind a friend of their job.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is your job today?
  **Expected response:** Water the plants or tidy.
- **Ask:** Did you do it?
  **Expected response:** Yes, with a smile.

### Expected outcomes
- Does a caring job
- Uses a chart with support

### Conclusion
Look at the chart and say "Well done."

### Home connection
Give your child a small caring job at home.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 9 caring check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W09-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Being kind to animals

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child cares for living things.

### Success criteria
- Waters a plant with support
- Handles a toy animal gently

**Previous knowledge:** Children know that plants and animals are alive and need food, water and care.

**Key vocabulary:** water, feed, kind, gentle, care, pet

**Instructional materials:** Plants; Toy animals; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the caring song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: plants, pets and kind hands. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What does it need?" and watch how the child cares. Children: Child answers with a word or action.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy.

### Practical activity: Caring stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors dry. Use soft toys only.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use one station.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell a friend how to care.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: waters a plant; names what a pet needs; handles toys gently; does a caring job.

- **Ask:** What does a plant need?
  **Expected response:** Water.
- **Ask:** How do we touch animals?
  **Expected response:** Gently.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 9
- Cares for living things gently

### Conclusion
Wash hands and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Keep caring for plants and pets at home.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
