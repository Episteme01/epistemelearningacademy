# Toddler Science — Term 2, Week 3: Animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W03 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Mothers and babies; Eggs and chicks; Caring for babies

## Learning objectives
- Children will match animal babies to their mothers.
- Children will say that mothers care for their babies.

**Key vocabulary:** baby, chick, kid, calf, egg, mother

**Content:** Matching animal babies to their mothers, eggs and chicks, and how mothers care for babies.

**Teaching activities:** Use the baby names chick, kid, calf, puppy and kitten with real or picture examples; link to human babies and mothers; use gentle language and soft toys.

**Learner activities:** Match animal babies to their mothers and say that mothers care for babies.

**Resources:** Picture pairs: hen and chick, goat and kid, cow and calf, dog and puppy, cat and kitten; Soft toy mothers and babies; A toy egg; A basket

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child matches at least three animal babies to their mothers.

## Expected outcomes
- Matches at least three animal babies to their mothers.
- Says "baby" and the animal name.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: names and songs; Social Habits: caring for others; Numeracy: matching pairs

## Day 1 (Monday): Mothers and babies

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W03-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Mothers and babies

### Learning objectives
- Children will match animal babies to their mothers.

### Success criteria
- Matches a baby to its mother
- Says the baby name with support

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match animals to their homes and name many animals.

**Key vocabulary:** baby, chick, kid, calf, egg, mother

**Instructional materials:** Picture pairs: hen and chick, goat and kid, cow and calf; A basket

### Starter
Show a hen and say "This is a mother hen. Where is her baby?" Show the chick.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each mother and baby pair and say both names. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Mix up the cards and show a mother. Ask "Where is the baby?" Children: Child points to the baby.

Step 3. Teacher: Say the baby names together: chick, kid, calf. Children: Children say the names.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child take a card and find its pair. Children: Child finds the pair.

### Practical activity: Mother and baby
1. Put cards on the mat.
2. Each child picks a mother and finds its baby.
3. Say both names.

**Safety:** Use large soft cards. Supervise sharing and keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two cards and say the mother and baby names.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name another baby animal.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is the baby of a hen?
  **Expected response:** A chick.
- **Ask:** What is the baby of a cow?
  **Expected response:** A calf.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches baby animals to mothers
- Names baby animals with support

### Conclusion
Put the babies with their mothers and say goodnight.

### Home connection
Talk about baby animals when you see them or in pictures.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "match animal babies to their mothers" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Eggs and chicks

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W03-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Eggs and chicks

### Learning objectives
- Children will learn that chicks hatch from eggs.

### Success criteria
- Says egg and chick
- Acts out a chick hatching

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match animals to their homes and name many animals.

**Key vocabulary:** baby, chick, kid, calf, egg, mother

**Instructional materials:** A toy egg that opens; Pictures of an egg, a cracking egg and a chick; A cloth nest

### Starter
Show the toy egg and say "There is a baby inside. Crack, crack. Out comes a chick."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the three pictures in order and tell the story of a chick hatching. Children: Children watch and say egg, crack, chick.

Step 2. Teacher: Open the toy egg to show the chick inside. Children: Children look and say chick.

Step 3. Teacher: Act out hatching by curling up and then stretching out. Children: Children curl up and stretch out.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child put the three pictures in order. Children: Child places the pictures in order with support.

### Practical activity: Hatching chick
1. Curl up like an egg on the floor.
2. Crack open and say "cheep".
3. Stand up like a chick.

**Safety:** Clear the floor and keep children apart so they do not bump. Do not use real eggs.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Curl up together and stretch out slowly.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell what comes first and last.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What comes out of an egg?
  **Expected response:** A chick.
- **Ask:** Who looks after the chick?
  **Expected response:** The mother hen.

### Expected outcomes
- Says that chicks come from eggs
- Orders egg, crack and chick

### Conclusion
Put the chick back in the nest and whisper goodnight.

### Home connection
Look at a picture of a hen and chicks with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "learn that chicks hatch from eggs" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

- CRACK
- CHICK

**Picture suggestions:**

- A chick

## Day 3 (Wednesday): How mothers care

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W03-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Caring for babies

### Learning objectives
- Children will say how animal and human mothers care for their babies.

### Success criteria
- Says one way a mother cares for a baby
- Acts out caring for a baby

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match animals to their homes and name many animals.

**Key vocabulary:** baby, chick, kid, calf, egg, mother

**Instructional materials:** Soft toy mothers and babies; Pictures: feeding, keeping warm, protecting; A doll and a blanket

### Starter
Hold a mother toy and baby toy and say "Mother cares for the baby. She gives food and keeps it warm."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each picture and say what the mother does: feeds, keeps warm, protects. Children: Children look and say the words.

Step 2. Teacher: Act out feeding and keeping the baby warm with the toys. Children: Children copy with their own toys.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "How does a mother care for her baby?" Children: Child says or shows one way.

Step 4. Teacher: Talk about how we care for a baby at home. Children: Child shares an idea.

### Practical activity: Caring play
1. Give each child a toy baby and a blanket.
2. Pretend to feed and keep it warm.
3. Rock the baby gently.

**Safety:** Use soft toys and be gentle. Remind children that real baby animals are touched only with an adult.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Act out caring together and say the words.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say how mothers keep babies safe.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does a mother give her baby?
  **Expected response:** Food or milk.
- **Ask:** How do we keep a baby warm?
  **Expected response:** With a blanket.

### Expected outcomes
- Says how mothers care for babies
- Acts out caring gently

### Conclusion
Put the babies to bed and say "Sleep well."

### Home connection
Talk about how your family cares for a baby.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "say how animal and human mothers care for their babies" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- FEED
- WARM
- SAFE

**Picture suggestions:**
- A mother feeding a baby
- A baby with a blanket

## Day 4 (Thursday): Baby animal game

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W03-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Mothers and babies

### Learning objectives
- Children will play a matching and naming game with baby animals.

### Success criteria
- Matches five baby animals to mothers
- Names at least three baby animals

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match animals to their homes and name many animals.

**Key vocabulary:** baby, chick, kid, calf, egg, mother

**Instructional materials:** Five mother and five baby cards; A bag; A mat

### Starter
Say "Mothers and babies are mixed up. Can you help them find each other?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Spread the mother cards out and give each child a baby card. Children: Children hold a baby card.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Find your mother" and let children match. Children: Child puts the baby on the right mother.

Step 3. Teacher: Check each pair and say the names together. Children: Children say the pair names.

Step 4. Teacher: Swap cards and play again. Children: Child matches a new pair.

### Practical activity: Find your mother
1. Put mother cards on the floor.
2. Give each child a baby card.
3. Find the mother and place the card.

**Safety:** Use large soft cards and keep the floor clear. Supervise turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use three pairs and name the baby first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to match all five pairs alone.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Who is the mother of the kid?
  **Expected response:** The goat.
- **Ask:** Who is the baby of the dog?
  **Expected response:** A puppy.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches five baby animals to mothers
- Names baby animals

### Conclusion
Put the cards away and say "Mothers and babies together."

### Home connection
Look at animal families in pictures or videos at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "play a matching and naming game with baby animals" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- MOTHER
- BABY

**Picture suggestions:**
- Mother and baby pairs

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 babies check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W03-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Mothers and babies

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child matches baby animals to mothers.

### Success criteria
- Matches at least three baby animals to mothers
- Says that mothers care for babies

**Previous knowledge:** Children can match animals to their homes and name many animals.

**Key vocabulary:** baby, chick, kid, calf, egg, mother

**Instructional materials:** Pair cards; Soft toys; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the mother and baby song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: pairs, eggs and chicks, and caring. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Who is the baby?" and "How does the mother care?" Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Baby stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use soft toys and large cards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two pairs.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell a friend about animal babies.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: matches babies to mothers; names a baby animal; says how a mother cares.

- **Ask:** What is the baby of a hen?
  **Expected response:** A chick.
- **Ask:** What does a mother do?
  **Expected response:** Feeds and cares for the baby.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 3
- Matches baby animals to mothers

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the mothers and babies.

### Home connection
Talk about mothers and babies at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child matches baby animals to mothers" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
