# Toddler Science — Term 2, Week 7: Plants

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W07 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Leaves of different shapes; Flowers and their colours; Fruits we eat

## Learning objectives
- Children will sort leaves by size and shape with support.
- Children will name fruits and say they come from plants.

**Key vocabulary:** leaf, flower, petal, fruit, mango, big

**Content:** Leaves of different shapes and sizes, flowers and their colours, and fruits that grow on plants.

**Teaching activities:** Use real leaves, flowers and fruits from the area; let children touch and sort; name colours and shapes; ask "Where does this fruit come from?"; check allergies before any tasting.

**Learner activities:** Sort leaves by size and shape, name flower colours and name fruits that come from plants.

**Resources:** Leaves of different sizes and shapes; Fresh flowers or pictures; Mango, orange, banana and pawpaw; Baskets; A tray of paint colours

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child sorts leaves into big and small and names two fruits that grow on trees.

## Expected outcomes
- Sorts leaves into big and small.
- Names two fruits that grow on trees.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Numeracy: sorting by size; Creative Development: flower painting; Literacy: colour words

## Day 1 (Monday): Big and small leaves

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W07-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Leaves of different shapes

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort leaves into big and small.

### Success criteria
- Sorts leaves into big and small
- Says big or small

**Previous knowledge:** Children have planted seeds and know that plants need water, soil and sun.

**Key vocabulary:** leaf, flower, petal, fruit, mango, big

**Instructional materials:** Big and small leaves; Two baskets labelled big and small; A tray

### Starter
Hold up a big leaf and a small leaf and say "This is big. This is small."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pass the leaves round and ask "Is it big or small?" Children: Children touch and say big or small.

Step 2. Teacher: Put one leaf in each basket to show. Children: Children watch and say the word.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pick a leaf and choose a basket. Children: Child sorts the leaf.

Step 4. Teacher: Look at the shapes of the leaves and talk about round and long. Children: Child says round or long.

### Practical activity: Leaf sort
1. Put leaves on a tray.
2. Each child picks one.
3. Put it in the big or small basket.

**Safety:** Use leaves from safe plants and wash hands afterwards. Do not allow leaves in mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Compare two leaves together and say big or small.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a leaf that is long and thin.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is this leaf big or small?
  **Expected response:** Big or small.
- **Ask:** What shape is it?
  **Expected response:** Round or long.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts leaves by size
- Describes leaf shapes with support

### Conclusion
Put the leaves back and wash hands.

### Home connection
Collect two safe leaves and compare their size.

- Which children could show "sort leaves into big and small" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Flower colours

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W07-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Flowers and their colours

### Learning objectives
- Children will name the colours of flowers.

### Success criteria
- Names at least three flower colours
- Matches a flower to a colour card

**Previous knowledge:** Children have planted seeds and know that plants need water, soil and sun.

**Key vocabulary:** leaf, flower, petal, fruit, mango, big

**Instructional materials:** Fresh flowers or large flower pictures in red, yellow, pink and white; Colour cards; A basket

### Starter
Show a red hibiscus and say "This flower is red. What colour is it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each flower and name its colour. Children: Children look and say the colour.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Find the yellow flower" and wait. Children: Child points to the yellow flower.

Step 3. Teacher: Match the flowers to the colour cards. Children: Child places a flower on a colour card.

Step 4. Teacher: Smell the flowers gently. Children: Child smells a flower and says nice.

### Practical activity: Flower colours
1. Put flowers on a tray.
2. Match each to a colour card.
3. Smell gently.

**Safety:** Check for allergies and pollen sensitivity first. Do not allow children to eat petals and wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two flowers and say the colour first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find a flower with two colours.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What colour is this flower?
  **Expected response:** Red, yellow, pink or white.
- **Ask:** Does it smell nice?
  **Expected response:** Yes or no.

### Expected outcomes
- Names flower colours
- Matches flowers to colours

### Conclusion
Put the flowers in a vase and say "Thank you, flowers."

### Home connection
Look at flowers outside with your child and say their colours.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Fruits from trees

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W07-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Fruits we eat

### Learning objectives
- Children will name fruits and say they grow on trees and plants.

### Success criteria
- Names at least three fruits
- Says that fruits grow on plants

**Previous knowledge:** Children have planted seeds and know that plants need water, soil and sun.

**Key vocabulary:** leaf, flower, petal, fruit, mango, big

**Instructional materials:** A mango, an orange, a banana and a pawpaw; A picture of each tree; A basket

### Starter
Hold up a mango and say "A mango grows on a mango tree. It comes from a plant."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each fruit and its tree picture. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "Where does the mango come from?" Children: Child points to the tree picture.

Step 3. Teacher: Cut a fruit safely in half to show the seed inside. Children: Children look at the seed.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick a fruit and match it to its tree. Children: Child matches the fruit to a tree.

### Practical activity: Fruit and tree
1. Put fruit and tree pictures on the mat.
2. Each child picks a fruit.
3. Match it to its tree.

**Safety:** An adult cuts all fruit; check allergies first. Wash fruit and hands. Do not let children taste unless allergies are cleared.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two fruits and show the tree first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find the seed inside a fruit.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this fruit?
  **Expected response:** Mango, orange, banana or pawpaw.
- **Ask:** Where does it grow?
  **Expected response:** On a tree or plant.

### Expected outcomes
- Names fruits that grow on plants
- Matches fruits to trees

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say "Thank you, trees."

### Home connection
Talk about where fruits come from when you eat them.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 4 (Thursday): Fruit salad

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W07-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Fruits we eat

### Learning objectives
- Children will make a fruit salad and say what each fruit is.

### Success criteria
- Names the fruits in the salad
- Shares with friends

**Previous knowledge:** Children have planted seeds and know that plants need water, soil and sun.

**Key vocabulary:** leaf, flower, petal, fruit, mango, big

**Instructional materials:** Washed fruit pieces; A bowl; Spoons; Small cups

### Starter
Say "Today we make a fruit salad." Show the fruits and ask "Which fruit is this?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Wash hands together and say why. Children: Children wash hands with soap.

Step 2. Teacher: Show how to put pieces of fruit into the bowl. Children: Children add fruit pieces to the bowl.

Step 3. Teacher: Stir the fruit together gently. Children: Child stirs and says the names.

Step 4. Teacher: Share the fruit salad in small cups. Children: Child says thank you and tastes.

### Practical activity: Fruit salad
1. Wash hands.
2. Add fruit pieces to a bowl.
3. Share in small cups.

**Safety:** An adult cuts fruit into very small pieces. Check allergies first and supervise closely for choking. Use clean spoons and bowls.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the bowl together and add the fruit.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name each fruit in the salad.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is in the fruit salad?
  **Expected response:** Mango, banana, orange or pawpaw.
- **Ask:** Does fruit grow on plants?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Makes a fruit salad
- Names fruits from plants

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say "Thank you for the fruit."

### Home connection
Make a simple fruit salad at home with your child.

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 plants check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W07-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Fruits we eat

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child sorts leaves and names fruits and flowers.

### Success criteria
- Sorts leaves by size
- Names two fruits that grow on trees

**Previous knowledge:** Children have planted seeds and know that plants need water, soil and sun.

**Key vocabulary:** leaf, flower, petal, fruit, mango, big

**Instructional materials:** Leaves; Flowers; Fruits; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the plant song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: leaves, flowers and fruits. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is this?" and "What colour or size?" Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy.

### Practical activity: Plant stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use safe plants and wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two items.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to teach a friend the fruit names.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: sorts leaves by size; names flower colours; names fruits that grow on trees.

- **Ask:** Is this leaf big or small?
  **Expected response:** Big or small.
- **Ask:** Where does a mango grow?
  **Expected response:** On a tree.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 7
- Sorts leaves and names fruits

### Conclusion
Wash hands and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Talk about leaves, flowers and fruits on walks.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
