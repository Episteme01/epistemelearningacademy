# Toddler Science — Term 2, Week 4: Animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W04 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Ants and beetles; Butterflies and bees; Looking carefully and not touching

## Learning objectives
- Children will look closely at small creatures with a magnifying glass.
- Children will say that small creatures are looked at and not hurt.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, bee, butterfly, beetle, look, tiny

**Content:** Looking closely at ants, beetles, butterflies and bees, and learning to look without touching or hurting.

**Teaching activities:** Use magnifying glasses and a clear bug box with a lid; keep creatures only for a short time and return them; avoid stinging insects; always say "we look, we do not hurt"; wash hands after.

**Learner activities:** Name small creatures, look at them carefully and be kind to them.

**Resources:** A magnifying glass; A clear bug box with a lid; Pictures of an ant, a bee, a butterfly and a beetle; A garden or school yard area; Soap and water

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names two small creatures and looks gently without touching.

## Expected outcomes
- Names two small creatures.
- Looks at a small creature gently without touching.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: song about bees and ants; Numeracy: counting legs and wings; Social Habits: being gentle

## Day 1 (Monday): Ants and beetles

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W04-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Ants and beetles

### Learning objectives
- Children will name ants and beetles and look at them with a magnifying glass.

### Success criteria
- Names an ant and a beetle
- Looks closely without touching

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, their homes and babies and handle toys gently.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, bee, butterfly, beetle, look, tiny

**Instructional materials:** A magnifying glass; Pictures of an ant and a beetle; A clear bug box with a lid

### Starter
Show the ant picture and say "This is an ant. It is very small. Let us look carefully."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the pictures and say how many legs and what colour they are. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Show how to hold the magnifying glass and look closely. Children: Children look at a picture through the glass.

Step 3. Teacher: Go outside and find a small creature in the garden with an adult. Children: Children look from a safe distance.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "We look and we do not hurt" and wave goodbye to the creature. Children: Children say "bye bye" and wave.

### Practical activity: Look with a magnifier
1. Give each child a turn to look through the glass.
2. Look at an ant or beetle without touching.
3. Say what you see.

**Safety:** Never allow children to touch or squash creatures. Avoid stinging insects, and wash hands after looking. An adult leads the search.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Hold the magnifying glass together and point to what you see.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count the legs on a picture of an ant.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** An ant or a beetle.
- **Ask:** Do we hurt small creatures?
  **Expected response:** No, we look gently.

### Expected outcomes
- Names ants and beetles
- Looks at creatures gently

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say "We are kind to small creatures."

### Home connection
Look for ants or beetles with your child and watch without touching.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Butterflies and bees

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W04-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Butterflies and bees

### Learning objectives
- Children will name a butterfly and a bee and say what they do.

### Success criteria
- Names a butterfly and a bee
- Says that they fly

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, their homes and babies and handle toys gently.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, bee, butterfly, beetle, look, tiny

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a butterfly and a bee; A paper butterfly on a stick; A flower picture; A scarf

### Starter
Fly a paper butterfly in the air and say "Flutter, flutter. This is a butterfly."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the butterfly and bee pictures and say the names and colours. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Butterflies and bees visit flowers." Children: Children point to the flower.

Step 3. Teacher: Move the scarf like a butterfly and like a bee. Children: Children flutter and buzz.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child be a butterfly or a bee and visit a flower. Children: Child moves to the flower and back.

### Practical activity: Butterflies and bees
1. Put a flower picture on the floor.
2. Children fly like butterflies and bees to the flower.
3. Say flutter and buzz.

**Safety:** Clear the floor so children can move safely. Do not touch real bees and stay away from hives.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Fly together and say flutter or buzz.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say why bees visit flowers.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What does a butterfly do?
  **Expected response:** Flies or flutters.
- **Ask:** What does a bee say?
  **Expected response:** Buzz.

### Expected outcomes
- Names a butterfly and a bee
- Says that they fly

### Conclusion
Settle like a butterfly on a flower.

### Home connection
Look for butterflies on flowers with your child and stay still to watch.

### Teacher reflection (private)

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Garden hunt

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W04-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Looking carefully and not touching

### Learning objectives
- Children will go on a garden hunt for small creatures and look without touching.

### Success criteria
- Finds a small creature or its home
- Says "look, not touch"

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, their homes and babies and handle toys gently.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, bee, butterfly, beetle, look, tiny

**Instructional materials:** Magnifying glasses; A clipboard with creature pictures; A school garden area

### Starter
Say "Today we go on a creature hunt. We have kind eyes. We look but we do not touch."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Walk to the garden in a line with an adult at each end. Children: Children walk in a line.

Step 2. Teacher: Search near plants, stones and leaves for small creatures. Children: Children look closely with magnifiers.

Step 3. Teacher: When a creature is found, ask "What is it?" Children: Child names the creature or says small.

Step 4. Teacher: Come back and talk about what we saw. Children: Child tells one thing they saw.

### Practical activity: Creature hunt
1. Go to the garden in a line.
2. Search quietly for small creatures.
3. Look without touching and tell what you see.

**Safety:** Check the area for stinging insects, sharp objects and snakes before the hunt. Keep children together and wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Walk with the child and point to creatures.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to count how many different creatures they saw.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you find?
  **Expected response:** An ant, a beetle or a leaf.
- **Ask:** Did you touch it?
  **Expected response:** No, we looked.

### Expected outcomes
- Finds small creatures in the garden
- Looks without touching

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say "We were kind."

### Home connection
Look in your garden or compound for small creatures with your child.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "go on a garden hunt for small creatures and look without touching" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Be kind to small creatures

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W04-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Looking carefully and not touching

### Learning objectives
- Children will say and show how to be kind to small creatures.

### Success criteria
- Says one way to be kind
- Shows gentle hands

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, their homes and babies and handle toys gently.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, bee, butterfly, beetle, look, tiny

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of kind and unkind actions; A toy ant; A leaf

### Starter
Hold a toy ant and say "Little ant. How can we be kind to him?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show pictures of gentle hands and ask "Is it kind?" Children: Children say yes or no.

Step 2. Teacher: Show pictures of squashing or throwing and ask "Is it kind?" Children: Children shake their heads and say no.

Step 3. Teacher: Show how to move a toy ant gently on a leaf. Children: Children move the ant gently.

Step 4. Teacher: Say "We look and we are gentle." Children: Children say the sentence.

### Practical activity: Kind hands
1. Give each child a toy creature and a leaf.
2. Move the creature gently.
3. Say "gentle".

**Safety:** Use soft toy creatures and remind children never to squash real creatures. Supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Move the toy together with gentle hands.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend why we are kind to small creatures.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is it kind to squash an ant?
  **Expected response:** No.
- **Ask:** What can we do?
  **Expected response:** Look gently.

### Expected outcomes
- Says and shows how to be kind to creatures
- Uses gentle hands

### Conclusion
Put the toys away and say "We are kind."

### Home connection
Show your child how to watch small creatures gently.

### Teacher reflection (private)

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 4 creatures check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W04-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Ants and beetles

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names small creatures and looks gently.

### Success criteria
- Names two small creatures
- Looks without touching

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, their homes and babies and handle toys gently.

**Key vocabulary:** ant, bee, butterfly, beetle, look, tiny

**Instructional materials:** Creature pictures; Magnifiers; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the creature song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: pictures, magnifiers and kind hands. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is this?" and "Are we kind?" Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy.

### Practical activity: Creature stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Wash hands after each activity.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two pictures.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell a friend how to look at creatures.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names small creatures; looks closely; says we do not hurt creatures.

- **Ask:** What is this creature?
  **Expected response:** Ant, bee, beetle or butterfly.
- **Ask:** Do we hurt it?
  **Expected response:** No, we look gently.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 4
- Names small creatures

### Conclusion
Wash hands and say goodbye.

### Home connection
Talk about being kind to small creatures.

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
