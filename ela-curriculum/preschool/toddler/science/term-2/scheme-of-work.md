# Toddler Science — Term 2 scheme of work

| Week | Topic and subtopics | Learning objectives | Content | Teaching activities | Learner activities | Resources | Assessment | Expected outcomes | Cross-curricular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Animals**: Pets and farm animals; Wild animals in pictures; Animal movements | Children will name familiar animals and say where they see them. Children will move like different animals. | Naming familiar animals, learning where we see them, and moving like animals. | Use real animals where safe and large pictures or toys otherwise; make the sound and name each animal; link animals to what children see at home and in the compound; never force a child to touch an animal. | Name familiar animals, say where we see them and move like different animals. | Large animal pictures: dog, cat, goat, hen, cow, lion; Soft toy animals; A basket; A recording of animal sounds if available | Observe whether each child names at least six animals and moves like an animal on request. | Names at least six animals. Moves like an animal on request. | Literacy: animal songs and sounds; Physical Development: moving like animals; Social Habits: kindness to animals |
| 2 | **Animals**: Homes on the farm; Homes in the wild; Matching animals to homes | Children will match animals to their homes. Children will say simple words for animal homes. | Homes of farm animals, homes of wild animals, and matching animals to their homes. | Show the home picture first, then the animal; use words nest, pen, kennel and hole; build a small farm with boxes; link to what children see near the school. | Match animals to their homes and say a word for each home. | Pictures of a nest, a pen, a kennel, a hole and a tree; Animal pictures: hen, goat, dog, rabbit, monkey; Boxes for homes; Toy animals | Observe whether each child matches at least three animals to homes and names one animal home. | Matches at least three animals to their homes. Names one animal home. | Literacy: where questions; Numeracy: matching; Social Habits: caring for animals |
| 3 | **Animals**: Mothers and babies; Eggs and chicks; Caring for babies | Children will match animal babies to their mothers. Children will say that mothers care for their babies. | Matching animal babies to their mothers, eggs and chicks, and how mothers care for babies. | Use the baby names chick, kid, calf, puppy and kitten with real or picture examples; link to human babies and mothers; use gentle language and soft toys. | Match animal babies to their mothers and say that mothers care for babies. | Picture pairs: hen and chick, goat and kid, cow and calf, dog and puppy, cat and kitten; Soft toy mothers and babies; A toy egg; A basket | Observe whether each child matches at least three animal babies to their mothers. | Matches at least three animal babies to their mothers. Says "baby" and the animal name. | Literacy: names and songs; Social Habits: caring for others; Numeracy: matching pairs |
| 4 | **Animals**: Ants and beetles; Butterflies and bees; Looking carefully and not touching | Children will look closely at small creatures with a magnifying glass. Children will say that small creatures are looked at and not hurt. | Looking closely at ants, beetles, butterflies and bees, and learning to look without touching or hurting. | Use magnifying glasses and a clear bug box with a lid; keep creatures only for a short time and return them; avoid stinging insects; always say "we look, we do not hurt"; wash hands after. | Name small creatures, look at them carefully and be kind to them. | A magnifying glass; A clear bug box with a lid; Pictures of an ant, a bee, a butterfly and a beetle; A garden or school yard area; Soap and water | Observe whether each child names two small creatures and looks gently without touching. | Names two small creatures. Looks at a small creature gently without touching. | Literacy: song about bees and ants; Numeracy: counting legs and wings; Social Habits: being gentle |
| 5 | **Plants**: Trees and flowers; Leaves and grass; The school garden | Children will name trees, flowers and leaves and say that plants are alive. Children will explore the school garden with their senses. | Naming trees, flowers, leaves and grass, noticing that plants are alive, and exploring the school garden with the senses. | Use real plants from the compound; let children touch, smell and look; name parts such as leaf, flower and trunk; link to the plants children know such as mango, pawpaw and hibiscus. | Name a tree, a flower and a leaf and say that plants are alive and grow. | Real leaves, flowers and a small potted plant; Pictures of a mango tree, a hibiscus, grass and a palm; A basket; Magnifying glasses | Observe whether each child names a tree, a flower and a leaf and says that plants grow. | Names a tree, a flower and a leaf. Says that plants grow. | Literacy: plant songs; Numeracy: counting petals and leaves; Creative Development: leaf rubbing |
| 6 | **Plants**: Looking at seeds; Planting a bean seed; Watering and waiting | Children will plant a seed and water it. Children will notice and talk about changes as the seed grows. | Looking at seeds, planting a bean seed, and watering and waiting while it grows. | Use large bean seeds that are easy to handle; plant in clear cups so children can see roots; keep a growth chart with pictures; talk about waiting; never let children eat seeds. | Plant a seed, water it and notice and talk about changes as it grows. | Large bean seeds; Clear plastic cups; Soil; A small watering can; Cotton wool; A growth chart; Pictures of seed, root and shoot | Observe whether each child plants a seed with support and says grow or points to change. | Plants a seed with support. Says "grow" or points to change. | Numeracy: counting days; Literacy: story of a seed; Social Habits: patience and care |
| 7 | **Plants**: Leaves of different shapes; Flowers and their colours; Fruits we eat | Children will sort leaves by size and shape with support. Children will name fruits and say they come from plants. | Leaves of different shapes and sizes, flowers and their colours, and fruits that grow on plants. | Use real leaves, flowers and fruits from the area; let children touch and sort; name colours and shapes; ask "Where does this fruit come from?"; check allergies before any tasting. | Sort leaves by size and shape, name flower colours and name fruits that come from plants. | Leaves of different sizes and shapes; Fresh flowers or pictures; Mango, orange, banana and pawpaw; Baskets; A tray of paint colours | Observe whether each child sorts leaves into big and small and names two fruits that grow on trees. | Sorts leaves into big and small. Names two fruits that grow on trees. | Numeracy: sorting by size; Creative Development: flower painting; Literacy: colour words |
| 8 | **Food**: Foods from plants; Foods from animals; Healthy foods | Children will say which foods come from plants and which from animals. Children will name foods that help them grow. | Foods that come from plants, foods that come from animals, and healthy foods that help us grow. | Use real foods or large pictures; sort into two groups; speak positively about all foods; check family diets and allergies; link to the market and the kitchen. | Sort foods into those from plants and those from animals and name healthy foods. | Pictures or toy foods: yam, rice, beans, tomato, egg, milk, fish, meat; Two baskets; Plate pictures of a healthy meal | Observe whether each child names two plant foods and two animal foods and says healthy food helps us grow. | Names two plant foods and two animal foods. Says that healthy food helps us grow. | Social Habits: table manners; Literacy: food words; Numeracy: sorting |
| 9 | **Caring**: Watering plants; Feeding and caring for pets; Being kind to animals | Children will water plants and say that plants need water. Children will show kind and gentle handling of animals. | Watering plants, feeding and caring for pets, and being kind and gentle to animals. | Give each child a real job such as watering; talk about what each living thing needs; model gentle voices and hands; never leave children alone with real animals. | Water plants, say what pets need and handle toy animals gently. | Potted plants; A watering can; A toy pet with a bowl; Pictures of caring actions; A pet visit if possible | Observe whether each child waters a plant with support and handles a toy animal gently. | Waters a plant with support. Handles a toy animal gently. | Social Habits: kindness and responsibility; Literacy: caring words; Numeracy: counting scoops of food |
| 10 | **Revision and assessment**: Revising animals and homes; Revising plants and seeds; Revising caring and food | Children will show Term 2 learning through play stations. Teachers will gather evidence for every child’s Term 2 record. | Revising animals and homes, plants and seeds, and caring and food through play stations and gathering Term 2 evidence. | Keep every activity familiar and playful; observe quietly and record without interrupting; offer help only when a child is stuck; celebrate what each child can do. | Show what they know about animals, plants, seeds, food and caring through play. | Animal and home cards; Baby animal pairs; Plants and seeds; Food pictures; Class record sheets | Observe and record each child’s Term 2 science learning using the four-level scale. | Completes familiar tasks with growing independence. Each child has an observation record for Term 2. | Literacy: songs and describing words; Numeracy: matching and sorting; Social Habits: kindness |
