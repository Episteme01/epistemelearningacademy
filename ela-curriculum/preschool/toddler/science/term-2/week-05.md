# Toddler Science — Term 2, Week 5: Plants

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W05 · **Week type:** consolidation · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Trees and flowers; Leaves and grass; The school garden

## Learning objectives
- Children will name trees, flowers and leaves and say that plants are alive.
- Children will explore the school garden with their senses.

**Key vocabulary:** tree, flower, leaf, grass, plant, green

**Content:** Naming trees, flowers, leaves and grass, noticing that plants are alive, and exploring the school garden with the senses.

**Teaching activities:** Use real plants from the compound; let children touch, smell and look; name parts such as leaf, flower and trunk; link to the plants children know such as mango, pawpaw and hibiscus.

**Learner activities:** Name a tree, a flower and a leaf and say that plants are alive and grow.

**Resources:** Real leaves, flowers and a small potted plant; Pictures of a mango tree, a hibiscus, grass and a palm; A basket; Magnifying glasses

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names a tree, a flower and a leaf and says that plants grow.

## Expected outcomes
- Names a tree, a flower and a leaf.
- Says that plants grow.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: plant songs; Numeracy: counting petals and leaves; Creative Development: leaf rubbing

## Day 1 (Monday): Trees and flowers

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W05-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Trees and flowers

### Learning objectives
- Children will name trees and flowers and say that plants are alive.

### Success criteria
- Names a tree and a flower
- Says plants grow

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, babies and small creatures and have been in the garden.

**Key vocabulary:** tree, flower, leaf, grass, plant, green

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a mango tree and a hibiscus; A potted plant; A basket

### Starter
Show a potted plant and say "This plant is alive. It needs water and sun to grow."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show the tree and flower pictures and name them. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Touch the potted plant and talk about its parts. Children: Children touch the leaf and stem gently.

Step 3. Teacher: Say "Plants are alive. They grow." and show growth with your hands. Children: Children grow tall like a plant.

Step 4. Teacher: Go outside and find a tree and a flower. Children: Child points to a tree and a flower.

### Practical activity: Plant walk
1. Walk to the garden in a line.
2. Find a tree and a flower.
3. Touch the leaves gently and name them.

**Safety:** Check the garden for thorns, poisonous plants and insects first. Remind children not to pick plants or eat any part.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the plant and say the name together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find two different plants.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** A tree or a flower.
- **Ask:** Do plants grow?
  **Expected response:** Yes, they grow.

### Expected outcomes
- Names a tree and a flower
- Says that plants grow

### Conclusion
Stretch up tall like a tree and then sit down.

### Home connection
Name trees and flowers on the way home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name trees and flowers and say that plants are alive" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**

- GROW

**Picture suggestions:**

**Song or rhyme:** A seed grows into a plant. Grow, grow, grow.

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Leaves and grass

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W05-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Leaves and grass

### Learning objectives
- Children will look closely at leaves and grass and describe them.

### Success criteria
- Names a leaf and grass
- Says a word for the colour or shape

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, babies and small creatures and have been in the garden.

**Key vocabulary:** tree, flower, leaf, grass, plant, green

**Instructional materials:** Different leaves; A patch of grass or a tray of grass; Magnifying glasses; Paper and crayons

### Starter
Hold up a leaf and say "This is a leaf. It is green." Ask "What colour is it?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pass leaves round and let children touch them. Children: Children touch and say leaf.

Step 2. Teacher: Look closely at a leaf with a magnifying glass and point out the lines. Children: Children look and point.

Step 3. Teacher: Touch the grass and say "The grass is green and soft." Children: Children touch the grass and say green.

Step 4. Teacher: Make a leaf rubbing with paper and a crayon. Children: Child rubs a crayon over a leaf under paper.

### Practical activity: Leaf rubbing
1. Put a leaf under a sheet of paper.
2. Rub a crayon gently over it.
3. Look at the leaf pattern.

**Safety:** Use leaves from safe plants only and wash hands afterwards. Do not allow leaves to go in mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Rub the crayon together and look at the pattern.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to make three rubbings and compare the shapes.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What colour is the leaf?
  **Expected response:** Green.
- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** Grass or a leaf.

### Expected outcomes
- Describes leaves and grass
- Makes a leaf rubbing

### Conclusion
Put the leaves back in the basket and wash hands.

### Home connection
Collect safe leaves with your child and look at their shapes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "look closely at leaves and grass and describe them" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Our school garden

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W05-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** The school garden

### Learning objectives
- Children will explore the school garden using their senses.

### Success criteria
- Looks, touches or smells a plant safely
- Says one thing about the garden

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, babies and small creatures and have been in the garden.

**Key vocabulary:** tree, flower, leaf, grass, plant, green

**Instructional materials:** The school garden; Magnifying glasses; Gloves; A clipboard with picture cards

### Starter
Say "Today we visit our garden. We use our eyes, nose and hands gently."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Walk to the garden in a line and sit together. Children: Children sit and look.

Step 2. Teacher: Ask "What do you see?" and "What do you smell?" Children: Child says a word or points.

Step 3. Teacher: Touch leaves gently and smell flowers safely. Children: Child touches and smells.

Step 4. Teacher: Talk about what plants need: water, sun and soil. Children: Children say water, sun or soil.

### Practical activity: Garden senses
1. Walk to the garden in a line.
2. Use eyes, nose and hands to explore.
3. Name what you find.

**Safety:** Check the garden for thorns, ants and poisonous plants beforehand. Do not allow children to pick or eat plants and wash hands afterwards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Explore beside the child and name what they touch.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to tell a friend what plants need.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What do plants need?
  **Expected response:** Water, sun and soil.
- **Ask:** What do you see?
  **Expected response:** A plant, flower or leaf.

### Expected outcomes
- Explores a garden using senses
- Says what plants need

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the garden and wash hands.

### Home connection
Visit a garden or a farm with your child and talk about what you see.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "explore the school garden using their senses" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

### Learner-facing content (safe to turn into slides)
**Board or slide content:**
- GARDEN
- SUN
- WATER

**Picture suggestions:**

## Day 4 (Thursday): Plant parts

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W05-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** consolidate · **Subtopic:** Trees and flowers

### Learning objectives
- Children will name parts of a plant: leaf, flower, stem and root.

### Success criteria
- Points to the leaf, the flower and the stem
- Names at least two plant parts

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, babies and small creatures and have been in the garden.

**Key vocabulary:** tree, flower, leaf, grass, plant, green

**Instructional materials:** A real plant with roots washed clean; A plant picture with parts; Cards: leaf, flower, stem, root

### Starter
Hold a plant and say "A plant has parts. Here is a leaf. Here is a flower."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Point to each part on the plant and say its name. Children: Children point to the same part.

Step 2. Teacher: Show the root and say "The root is under the soil." Children: Children look and point down.

Step 3. Teacher: Say a part and ask children to find it on the plant. Children: Child points to the part.

Step 4. Teacher: Match the part cards to the plant picture. Children: Child places a card on the picture.

### Practical activity: Plant parts
1. Look at a real plant.
2. Name each part.
3. Match the part cards to the picture.

**Safety:** Wash roots clean before handling and wash hands afterwards. Do not allow plant parts in mouths.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Point to the part together and say its name.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what each part does.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where is the leaf?
  **Expected response:** Child points to the leaf.
- **Ask:** What is under the soil?
  **Expected response:** The root.

### Expected outcomes
- Names plant parts with support
- Points to leaf, flower and stem

### Conclusion
Put the plant back and say "Thank you, plant."

### Home connection
Look at a plant at home and name its parts.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name parts of a plant: leaf, flower, stem and root" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

**Board or slide content:**

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 plants check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W05-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Leaves and grass

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names plants and plant parts.

### Success criteria
- Names a tree, a flower and a leaf
- Says that plants grow

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name animals, babies and small creatures and have been in the garden.

**Key vocabulary:** tree, flower, leaf, grass, plant, green

**Instructional materials:** Real plants; Pictures; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the plant song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: leaves, flowers and plant parts. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is this?" and "Does it grow?" Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children wash hands and tidy.

### Practical activity: Plant stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use safe plants and wash hands after.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two plants.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to name three plant parts.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names a tree, a flower and a leaf; says that plants grow; names plant parts.

- **Ask:** What is this?
  **Expected response:** A leaf, flower or tree.
- **Ask:** Do plants grow?
  **Expected response:** Yes, they grow.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 5
- Names plants and parts

### Conclusion
Wash hands and wave goodbye.

### Home connection
Talk about plants during walks and at mealtimes.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child names plants and plant parts" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
