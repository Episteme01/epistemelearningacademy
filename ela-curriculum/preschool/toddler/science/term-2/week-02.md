# Toddler Science — Term 2, Week 2: Animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W02 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Homes on the farm; Homes in the wild; Matching animals to homes

## Learning objectives
- Children will match animals to their homes.
- Children will say simple words for animal homes.

**Key vocabulary:** nest, pen, kennel, hole, water, tree

**Content:** Homes of farm animals, homes of wild animals, and matching animals to their homes.

**Teaching activities:** Show the home picture first, then the animal; use words nest, pen, kennel and hole; build a small farm with boxes; link to what children see near the school.

**Learner activities:** Match animals to their homes and say a word for each home.

**Resources:** Pictures of a nest, a pen, a kennel, a hole and a tree; Animal pictures: hen, goat, dog, rabbit, monkey; Boxes for homes; Toy animals

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child matches at least three animals to homes and names one animal home.

## Expected outcomes
- Matches at least three animals to their homes.
- Names one animal home.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Literacy: where questions; Numeracy: matching; Social Habits: caring for animals

## Day 1 (Monday): Homes on the farm

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W02-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Homes on the farm

### Learning objectives
- Children will match farm animals to their homes.

### Success criteria
- Matches the hen to the nest and the dog to the kennel
- Says the name of one home

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals and say if they are pets or wild.

**Key vocabulary:** nest, pen, kennel, hole, water, tree

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a nest, pen and kennel; Pictures of a hen, a goat and a dog; Boxes for homes

### Starter
Show a kennel picture and say "This is a kennel. A dog sleeps here." Ask "Where does the dog live?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each home picture and say its name. Children: Children look and say the name.

Step 2. Teacher: Show an animal picture and ask "Where does it live?" Children: Child points to the home.

Step 3. Teacher: Put each animal in its home box. Children: Child places the animal in the right box.

Step 4. Teacher: Ask "Who lives in the nest?" Children: Child says hen or bird.

### Practical activity: Farm homes
1. Put three home boxes on the mat.
2. Each child picks an animal toy.
3. Put it in its home.

**Safety:** Use clean soft toys and large boxes. Keep the floor clear and supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two homes and show the animal first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what the animal does at home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where does the hen live?
  **Expected response:** In a nest or coop.
- **Ask:** Where does the dog live?
  **Expected response:** In a kennel.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches farm animals to their homes
- Names one animal home

### Conclusion
Put the animals in their homes and say goodnight.

### Home connection
Look at where animals live near your home and name the homes.

- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Homes in the wild

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W02-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Homes in the wild

### Learning objectives
- Children will match wild animals to their homes.

### Success criteria
- Matches a monkey to a tree and a rabbit to a hole
- Says the name of one wild home

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals and say if they are pets or wild.

**Key vocabulary:** nest, pen, kennel, hole, water, tree

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of a tree, a hole, water and a cave; Pictures of a monkey, a rabbit, a fish and a bat

### Starter
Show a tree picture and say "A monkey lives in a tree." Ask "Who lives in a tree?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each home picture and say its name. Children: Children look and say the name.

Step 2. Teacher: Show an animal and ask "Where does it live?" Children: Child points to the home.

Step 3. Teacher: Talk about how animals find food and water in their homes. Children: Children listen and point to food and water pictures.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child match one animal to its home. Children: Child places an animal on its home.

### Practical activity: Wild homes
1. Put home pictures on the floor.
2. Each child takes an animal card.
3. Stand the animal on its home.

**Safety:** Use large cards on a clear floor. Supervise movement and turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two homes and say the animal first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find where fish live and say why.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where does the monkey live?
  **Expected response:** In a tree.
- **Ask:** Where do fish live?
  **Expected response:** In water.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches wild animals to homes
- Names one wild home

### Conclusion
Put all the animals back in their homes.

### Home connection
Look for animal homes outside, such as nests and holes.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Matching homes game

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W02-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Matching animals to homes

### Learning objectives
- Children will play a matching game with animals and homes.

### Success criteria
- Matches at least three animals to homes
- Says the home name

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals and say if they are pets or wild.

**Key vocabulary:** nest, pen, kennel, hole, water, tree

**Instructional materials:** Six animal cards; Six home cards; A bag

### Starter
Say "Let us help the animals find their homes. Who is missing a home?"

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Spread out the home cards. Pick an animal and ask "Where does it live?" Children: Child points to the home.

Step 2. Teacher: Match the card and say the animal and the home. Children: Children say both words.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pick an animal from the bag and match it. Children: Child picks and matches.

Step 4. Teacher: Check all the pairs together. Children: Children name the pairs.

### Practical activity: Home match
1. Put six home cards on the mat.
2. Each child picks an animal card.
3. Match it to a home.

**Safety:** Use large soft cards. Supervise turns and keep the floor clear.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Use three pairs only and say the home first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to match all six pairs alone.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where does the rabbit live?
  **Expected response:** In a hole.
- **Ask:** Where does the goat live?
  **Expected response:** In a pen.

### Expected outcomes
- Matches animals to homes
- Names the animal and home

### Conclusion
Put the cards away and say "All the animals are home."

### Home connection
Play animal home matching with pictures at home.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "play a matching game with animals and homes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Building a farm

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W02-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Homes on the farm

### Learning objectives
- Children will build a model farm with homes for animals.

### Success criteria
- Builds a home for an animal
- Says what they made

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals and say if they are pets or wild.

**Key vocabulary:** nest, pen, kennel, hole, water, tree

**Instructional materials:** Boxes, blocks and cloth; Toy farm animals; Green paper for grass

### Starter
Show the toy animals and say "The animals need homes. Let us build a farm."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show how to build a pen with blocks and a nest with cloth. Children: Children watch and choose a block.

Step 2. Teacher: Let each child build a home for an animal. Children: Child builds a home with blocks or boxes.

Step 3. Teacher: Put the animals in their new homes. Children: Child places an animal in a home.

Step 4. Teacher: Walk round and ask "What did you build?" Children: Child says the name of the home.

### Practical activity: Farm building
1. Set out blocks, boxes and cloth.
2. Build homes for the animals.
3. Put the animals inside.

**Safety:** Use soft blocks and keep towers low. Clear the floor and supervise sharing.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Build beside the child and name each part.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to build a home with a roof and a door.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What did you build?
  **Expected response:** A pen, nest or house.
- **Ask:** Who lives there?
  **Expected response:** An animal’s name.

### Expected outcomes
- Builds a home for an animal
- Says what they built

### Conclusion
Look at the farm together and say "Well done."

### Home connection
Build a home for a toy animal at home with boxes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "build a model farm with homes for animals" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 homes check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W02-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Matching animals to homes

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child matches animals to homes.

### Success criteria
- Matches at least three animals to homes
- Names one animal home

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name familiar animals and say if they are pets or wild.

**Key vocabulary:** nest, pen, kennel, hole, water, tree

**Instructional materials:** Animal and home cards; Toy animals; Class record sheet

### Starter
Say "Where do animals live?" and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: farm homes, wild homes and building. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "Where does the animal live?" Children: Child points to or names the home.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Home stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use soft toys and large cards.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two homes.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell a friend where animals live.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: matches animals to homes; names one home; builds a home.

- **Ask:** Where does the hen live?
  **Expected response:** In a nest.
- **Ask:** Where do fish live?
  **Expected response:** In water.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 2
- Matches animals to homes

### Conclusion
Say goodnight to the animals in their homes.

### Home connection
Talk about animal homes whenever you see animals.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child matches animals to homes" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
