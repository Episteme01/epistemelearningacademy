# Toddler Science — Term 2, Week 8: Food

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W08 · **Week type:** new-learning · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft

**Subtopics:** Foods from plants; Foods from animals; Healthy foods

## Learning objectives
- Children will say which foods come from plants and which from animals.
- Children will name foods that help them grow.

**Key vocabulary:** yam, rice, egg, milk, fish, healthy

**Content:** Foods that come from plants, foods that come from animals, and healthy foods that help us grow.

**Teaching activities:** Use real foods or large pictures; sort into two groups; speak positively about all foods; check family diets and allergies; link to the market and the kitchen.

**Learner activities:** Sort foods into those from plants and those from animals and name healthy foods.

**Resources:** Pictures or toy foods: yam, rice, beans, tomato, egg, milk, fish, meat; Two baskets; Plate pictures of a healthy meal

**Assessment:** Observe whether each child names two plant foods and two animal foods and says healthy food helps us grow.

## Expected outcomes
- Names two plant foods and two animal foods.
- Says that healthy food helps us grow.

**Cross-curricular connections:** Social Habits: table manners; Literacy: food words; Numeracy: sorting

## Day 1 (Monday): Foods from plants

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W08-D01 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** introduce · **Subtopic:** Foods from plants

### Learning objectives
- Children will name foods that come from plants.

### Success criteria
- Names at least three plant foods
- Says that these foods grow

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name fruits and know that plants grow from seeds.

**Key vocabulary:** yam, rice, egg, milk, fish, healthy

**Instructional materials:** Toy or real yam, rice, beans and tomato; A basket; A picture of a farm

### Starter
Hold up a yam and say "A yam grows in the ground. It comes from a plant."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each food and name it. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "Yams grow under the soil. Tomatoes grow on plants." Children: Children listen and point to the pictures.

Step 3. Teacher: Ask "Which food comes from a plant?" Children: Child points to a plant food.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child pick a food and say where it grows. Children: Child shows the food and says a word.

### Practical activity: Plant foods
1. Put plant foods on a tray.
2. Each child picks one and names it.
3. Say where it grows.

**Safety:** If real foods are used, wash them and check allergies. Keep food for looking unless tasting is planned and cleared.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two foods and say the name first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to name a plant food they eat at home.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where does yam grow?
  **Expected response:** Under the soil.
- **Ask:** Does rice come from a plant?
  **Expected response:** Yes.

### Expected outcomes
- Names foods from plants
- Says that plant foods grow

### Conclusion
Put the foods in the basket and say "Thank you, plants."

### Home connection
Talk about plant foods when you cook, such as yam and beans.

### Teacher reflection (private)

- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 2 (Tuesday): Foods from animals

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W08-D02 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** explore · **Subtopic:** Foods from animals

### Learning objectives
- Children will name foods that come from animals.

### Success criteria
- Names at least three animal foods
- Says which animal gives each food

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name fruits and know that plants grow from seeds.

**Key vocabulary:** yam, rice, egg, milk, fish, healthy

**Instructional materials:** Pictures or toys of an egg, milk, fish and meat; Animal pictures: hen, cow, fish, goat; A basket

### Starter
Show an egg and say "An egg comes from a hen." Show milk and say "Milk comes from a cow."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each food and the animal it comes from. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say a food and ask "Which animal gives us this?" Children: Child points to the animal.

Step 3. Teacher: Match the foods to the animals. Children: Child places the food next to its animal.

Step 4. Teacher: Talk about how animals give us food and need our care. Children: Children listen and nod.

### Practical activity: Animal foods
1. Put foods and animal pictures on the mat.
2. Each child picks a food.
3. Match it to the animal.

**Safety:** Use pictures or toy foods and be sensitive to families who do not eat certain foods. Check allergies before any tasting.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two pictures and say the food first.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say which animal gives us eggs.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Where do eggs come from?
  **Expected response:** A hen.
- **Ask:** Where does milk come from?
  **Expected response:** A cow.

### Expected outcomes
- Names foods from animals
- Matches foods to animals

### Conclusion
Put the cards away and say "Thank you, animals."

### Home connection
Talk about foods from animals at mealtimes.

## Day 3 (Wednesday): Plant or animal food?

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W08-D03 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** practise · **Subtopic:** Foods from plants

### Learning objectives
- Children will sort foods into plant foods and animal foods.

### Success criteria
- Sorts a food into the right basket
- Says plant or animal

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name fruits and know that plants grow from seeds.

**Key vocabulary:** yam, rice, egg, milk, fish, healthy

**Instructional materials:** Two baskets labelled plant and animal; Eight food pictures; A mat

### Starter
Show two baskets and say "One is for foods from plants. One is for foods from animals."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Pick up a rice picture and put it in the plant basket. Children: Children watch and say plant.

Step 2. Teacher: Pick up an egg picture and put it in the animal basket. Children: Children watch and say animal.

Step 3. Teacher: Let each child pick a picture and choose a basket. Children: Child sorts the picture.

Step 4. Teacher: Check each basket together. Children: Children name the foods.

### Practical activity: Food sort
1. Put two baskets on the mat.
2. Each child picks a food picture.
3. Put it in the plant or animal basket.

**Safety:** Use large pictures. Keep the floor clear and supervise turns.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two pictures and say where the food comes from.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to find another food for each basket.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** Is rice a plant food?
  **Expected response:** Yes.
- **Ask:** Is egg a plant food?
  **Expected response:** No, it is from an animal.

### Expected outcomes
- Sorts foods into plant and animal
- Says plant or animal with support

### Conclusion
Put the pictures away and say "Plants and animals give us food."

### Home connection
Sort foods in the kitchen into plant and animal foods.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "sort foods into plant foods and animal foods" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 4 (Thursday): Healthy food helps me grow

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W08-D04 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** apply · **Subtopic:** Healthy foods

### Learning objectives
- Children will name healthy foods and say they help us grow.

### Success criteria
- Names two healthy foods
- Says that healthy food helps us grow

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name fruits and know that plants grow from seeds.

**Key vocabulary:** yam, rice, egg, milk, fish, healthy

**Instructional materials:** Pictures of fruit, vegetables, beans, rice, fish and water; A plate picture; A doll

### Starter
Show a plate with healthy foods and say "These foods help us grow strong."

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Show each healthy food and say its name. Children: Children look and say the names.

Step 2. Teacher: Say "We eat these to grow tall and strong." Stretch up tall. Children: Children stretch up and say strong.

Step 3. Teacher: Show foods that we only have sometimes, such as sweets. Children: Children listen and nod.

Step 4. Teacher: Let each child choose foods for a healthy plate. Children: Child puts pictures on a plate.

### Practical activity: Healthy plate
1. Put food pictures on the mat.
2. Each child picks foods for a plate.
3. Say "healthy".

**Safety:** Be sensitive to family circumstances and food availability. Focus on foods children know and never shame any child.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Offer two foods and choose the healthy one together.
- **Extension:** Ask the child to say what they eat to grow strong.

### Assessment
Observe each child during the practical activity and note Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding on the class record. Ask the questions below one to one or in a small group.

- **Ask:** What helps us grow strong?
  **Expected response:** Healthy food and water.
- **Ask:** Name a healthy food.
  **Expected response:** Fruit, beans or rice.

### Expected outcomes
- Names healthy foods
- Says healthy food helps us grow

### Conclusion
Stretch up tall and say "I will grow strong."

### Home connection
Talk about healthy foods at mealtimes.

### Teacher reflection (private)
- Which children could show "name healthy foods and say they help us grow" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?

## Day 5 (Friday): Week 8 food check

**ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI-T2-W08-D05 · **Duration:** 20 minutes · **Day type:** review-assess · **Subtopic:** Healthy foods

### Learning objectives
- Teachers will observe how each child names and sorts foods.

### Success criteria
- Names two plant foods and two animal foods
- Says that healthy food helps us grow

**Previous knowledge:** Children can name fruits and know that plants grow from seeds.

**Key vocabulary:** yam, rice, egg, milk, fish, healthy

**Instructional materials:** Food pictures; Baskets; Class record sheet

### Starter
Sing the food song and show three stations.

### Step-by-step lesson presentation
Step 1. Teacher: Set up three stations: plant foods, animal foods and the healthy plate. Children: Children rotate every four minutes.

Step 2. Teacher: At each station ask "What is this food?" and "Where does it come from?" Children: Child answers with a word or gesture.

Step 3. Teacher: Record each child’s level and take photographs. Children: Children tidy at the signal.

### Practical activity: Food stations
1. Set up three stations.
2. Children rotate on the signal.
3. The teacher observes and records.

**Safety:** Supervise each station and keep floors clear. Use pictures or toy foods.

### Differentiation
- **Support:** Provide an adult partner and use two foods.
- **Extension:** Ask a confident child to tell a friend about healthy food.

### Assessment
Observe and record Emerging, Developing, Secure or Exceeding for: names plant foods; names animal foods; sorts foods; says healthy food helps us grow.

- **Ask:** Where does rice come from?
  **Expected response:** A plant.
- **Ask:** Where do eggs come from?
  **Expected response:** A hen.

### Expected outcomes
- Has an observation record for Week 8
- Sorts foods and names healthy foods

### Conclusion
Wave goodbye to the food pictures.

### Home connection
Talk about food sources at home.

- Which children could show "Teachers will observe how each child names and sorts foods" without help, and who needs another turn tomorrow?
- What did the children say or do that I did not expect, and how will I use it in the next lesson?
- Which resource or instruction worked best, and what would I change before I teach this again?
