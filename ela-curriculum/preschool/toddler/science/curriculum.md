# Toddler Science

**Programme ID:** ELA-PS-TOD-SCI · **Version:** 1.0.0 · **Status:** draft (not yet approved by an ELA reviewer)

## Subject philosophy
Science for toddlers is wonder, noticing and handling real things. Children learn about their bodies, the world around them and how things behave by looking, listening, touching, smelling, pouring, pushing and asking "what is it?" ELA’s Toddler Science keeps every experience hands-on, safe and short, and builds the words children need to talk about what they discover.

## Rationale
Early science is the foundation for later enquiry. Toddlers who handle water, sand, soil, plants and animals, and who hear the right words while they explore, build the vocabulary and curiosity that later science lessons depend on. This programme uses the familiar Nigerian environment (the market, the compound, the school garden, the rain and the harmattan) so that learning is rooted in what children already see and know.

## Aims
- To encourage curiosity by letting children explore real objects, materials and living things.
- To teach the names of body parts and the five senses through safe, playful activities.
- To introduce simple ideas about materials, movement, living things and weather through play.
- To build early science vocabulary such as soft, hard, wet, dry, push, pull, float and grow.
- To teach caring habits: caring for living things, keeping clean and looking after the environment.

## Learning outcomes by the end of the year
- Names and points to main parts of the body and says what they are used for.
- Uses the senses to explore and describes things with simple words such as soft, hard, hot and cold.
- Explores water, sand and soil and notices that some things float and some sink.
- Names familiar animals and plants and says where they live and what they need.
- Describes the weather today with a word or gesture.
- Pushes and pulls objects and notices that some things roll and slide.
- Sorts everyday objects by simple properties such as colour, size or texture with support.
- Shows care for living things and for the environment, for example by watering plants and putting litter in a bin.
- Follows simple hygiene routines such as washing hands.

## Developmental expectations
- Toddlers learn through their senses and through movement, so every lesson starts with real objects.
- Attention is short; each activity is brief and repeated across the week.
- Language grows with experience; adults name what children touch and see.
- Toddlers do not yet explain why things happen; they notice, handle and describe.
- Safety is essential: nothing small enough to swallow, no sharp or hot items, and adult supervision at all times.

## Key competencies
- Observing with all the senses.
- Naming and describing objects and materials.
- Exploring and asking questions.
- Caring for living things and the environment.
- Following safe routines.

## Curriculum strands
- **Me, my body and my senses** (BS): Parts of the body, the five senses and keeping healthy.
- **Materials and water** (MA): Soft and hard, wet and dry, water, sand, soil, float and sink.
- **Living things** (LF): Animals, plants, growing, and caring for living things.
- **Movement and forces** (MF): Push, pull, roll and slide, in simple play.
- **Earth, weather and light** (EW): Weather, light and shadow, natural and made things.
- **Caring for our world** (EN): Hygiene, tidiness, litter and looking after the environment.

## Prior knowledge
None assumed. Children bring many experiences of the home and the outdoors. Observe during the first two weeks how each child explores objects and records it as the baseline.

## Progression across the year
- **Term 1:** Term 1 focuses on me and my world: the body, the senses, water, sand and soil, and weather.
- **Term 2:** Term 2 focuses on living things: animals, their homes and babies, insects, plants, seeds, food and caring for living things.
- **Term 3:** Term 3 focuses on how things work: push and pull, rolling and sliding, light and shadow, wet and dry, natural and made things, colours in nature, health and caring for the environment.

## Assessment principles
- Assessment is by watching, listening and recording what children say and do during play. There are no written tests.
- Levels Emerging, Developing, Secure and Exceeding follow the lesson success criteria (see ASSESSMENT_FRAMEWORK.md).
- Note the words children use to describe what they find, as evidence of learning.
- Share with parents in plain language, always with a strength, a next step and a home idea.

## Inclusion and differentiation
- Every lesson has a support and an extension version.
- Offer several ways to explore the same thing: look, touch, listen and move.
- For children who dislike messy materials, offer a tool such as a spoon or a glove and never force.
- Accept home-language words and repeat them in English in a natural way.

## Cross-curricular connections
- Literacy: stories, rhymes and songs about animals, weather and plants.
- Numeracy: sorting, counting objects and comparing size, weight and capacity.
- Social Habits: hygiene, sharing equipment and caring for living things.
- EYFS mapping: Understanding the World and Physical Development, with Communication and Language (see STANDARDS_ALIGNMENT.md).

## Parent connection
- Every lesson ends with a short home idea that uses safe household objects and the outdoors.
- Parents are invited to share local plants, animals, foods and weather words from their home language.
- Families are invited to help with the school garden and the nature table.
