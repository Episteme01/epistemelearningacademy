# Assessment framework

Assessment in preschool is observation-led, positive and light. Written examinations are never the main method, and are not used for Toddler.

## Four developmental levels

| Level | Meaning | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emerging | The child shows the first signs. Needs adult modelling or hand-over-hand help. | Joins in after watching; responds when prompted |
| Developing | The child can do it with a prompt, partner or familiar objects, but not yet every time. | Succeeds in some turns; needs a reminder |
| Secure | The child does it reliably and independently in familiar situations. | Does it in most turns without help |
| Exceeding | The child does it confidently in new situations, explains or helps others. | Uses it in a new game; explains; leads a friend |

Apply the level to the lesson's **success criteria**, not to the child as a person. Use evidence from at least two occasions before recording Secure.

## Methods

- **Baseline:** observations in the first two weeks of Term 1, recorded on the class record.
- **Formative:** every lesson ends with 2 to 4 questions or tasks and the teacher notes quick evidence.
- **Practical and performance tasks:** play stations on Day 5 each week and in Week 10.
- **Portfolio:** dated photographs, drawings and teacher notes, 2 to 3 per child per term.
- **End of term:** each child's level for each strand, written from the evidence, discussed with parents.

## Progress tracking

Class record sheet columns: child, date, lesson ID, criterion, level, note. A summary per strand is written at the end of each term. Level changes between terms show progress.

## Parent communication

Share one strength, one next step and one home idea each term, in plain language and without comparing children. Say "is developing" rather than "is behind".
